## Q1 — Noninvasive Cardiac Testing (p. 577)
A 44-year-old man has 3 months of nonspecific sharp left-sided chest pain at rest and exertion; HTN and dyslipidemia, no known CAD; CAD Consortium 2 pretest probability is low. Which one of the following is most appropriate to evaluate possible CAD?

- A. Coronary artery calcium scoring.
- B. Stress testing with echocardiography.
- C. Stress testing with myocardial perfusion imaging.
- D. Stress testing with cardiac MRI.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Coronary artery calcium scoring.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> With low pretest probability and nonspecific symptoms, CAC scoring effectively reclassifies risk and rules out significant CAD without functional stress testing (reserved for intermediate/high pretest probability).

## Q2 — Noninvasive Cardiac Testing (p. 577)
Which one of the following is a contraindication to exercise stress testing?

- A. Resting hypertension greater than 160/90 mm Hg.
- B. Having acute coronary syndrome in the past year.
- C. Inability to safely exercise to 5 metabolic equivalents.
- D. Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Inability to safely exercise to 5 METs.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> A patient who cannot exercise to a meaningful workload should have pharmacologic (not exercise) stress testing. The HTN threshold for contraindication is higher (~200/110), an ACS contraindication is within ~2 days (not a year), and compensated HFpEF alone isn't a contraindication. **⚠️ Verify against print key.**

## Q3 — Mildly Elevated Liver Transaminase Levels (p. 585)
Which one of the following statements about MASLD is correct?

- A. An AST:ALT ratio greater than 0.8 rules out MASLD.
- B. Ultrasonography is the most accurate method to differentiate MASLD from alcoholic liver disease.
- C. Weight loss of more than 10% in patients with MASLD is associated with improvement in fibrosis.
- D. GLP-1 receptor agonists have been FDA-approved for treatment of MASLD.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Weight loss >10% is associated with improvement in fibrosis.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> ≥10% weight loss drives fibrosis regression in steatotic liver disease. A is false (ratio doesn't rule it out); B is false (US can't distinguish etiology); D is false (resmetirom — not a GLP-1 RA — was the agent approved for MASH in 2024).

## Q4 — Mildly Elevated Liver Transaminase Levels (p. 585)
A 36-year-old was diagnosed with drug-induced liver injury 2 days ago. Which one of the following statements about DILI is correct?

- A. Idiosyncratic DILI is predictably dose-related.
- B. Elevated transaminase levels typically take up to 3 months to resolve.
- C. DILI invariably occurs within the first 2 weeks of medication use.
- D. All patients with DILI should undergo liver biopsy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Elevated transaminases typically take up to 3 months to resolve.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Recovery after stopping the offending drug is gradual (weeks to months). A is false (idiosyncratic DILI is *not* dose-dependent); C is false (onset can be delayed weeks–months); D is false (biopsy is reserved, not universal). **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q5 — Bone Stress Injuries (p. 592)
A 21-year-old has 3 weeks of foot pain while running. Which one of the following findings is most indicative of a bone stress injury?

- A. Bony tenderness to palpation.
- B. Foot edema.
- C. Pain with application of a vibrating tuning fork.
- D. Pes cavus.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Focal bony tenderness to palpation.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Localized bony tenderness over the involved site is the most useful clinical finding. The tuning-fork test has poor accuracy, edema is nonspecific, and pes cavus is a *risk factor*, not a diagnostic finding. **⚠️ Verify against print key.**

## Q6 — Bone Stress Injuries (p. 592)
A 21-year-old runner has hip pain progressing to pain at rest. Radiography shows periosteal reaction and an indistinct fracture line along the *superior* femoral neck. Which one of the following management plans is most appropriate?

- A. Activity limitation.
- B. Extracorporeal shock wave therapy.
- C. Immobilization followed by gradually increased activity.
- D. Immobilization and orthopedic referral.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Immobilization and orthopedic referral.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> A superior (tension-side) femoral neck stress fracture is HIGH-RISK — it can complete and displace. It needs protected weight-bearing and urgent orthopedic referral (possible surgical fixation), not a gradual return-to-activity plan.

## Q7 — Management of Keloids and Hypertrophic Scars (p. 605)
A 21-year-old with a history of keloid formation needs a cholecystectomy. Which one of the following interventions during healing is most likely to reduce scar formation?

- A. Corticosteroid ointment application.
- B. Gel sheets over the incision.
- C. Imiquimod application.
- D. Massaging the scar.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Silicone gel sheeting over the incision.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Silicone gel sheets are the best-evidenced *preventive* measure for keloid/hypertrophic scarring in high-risk patients. Topical corticosteroid ointment, imiquimod, and massage are not effective primary prevention.

## Q8 — Management of Keloids and Hypertrophic Scars (p. 605)
A 28-year-old has an earlobe keloid from a piercing. Which one of the following interventions is *least* likely to be effective for improving the scar?

- A. OnabotulinumtoxinA injections.
- B. Corticosteroid injections.
- C. Fluorouracil injections.
- D. Topical vitamin E.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Topical vitamin E.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Topical vitamin E has no proven benefit (and can cause contact dermatitis). Intralesional corticosteroids and 5-FU are mainstays; botulinum toxin has emerging evidence.

## Q9 — Anemia in Infants and Children (p. 612)
A 9-month-old boy on WIC screening has Hb 10.2 g/dL and MCV 76 fL (normal for age 70–86). Which one of the following tests should be obtained first?

- A. Bilirubin level.
- B. Iron studies including ferritin.
- C. Peripheral smear.
- D. Reticulocyte count.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Iron studies including ferritin.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Mild microcytic anemia in a 9-month-old is most often iron deficiency; ferritin/iron studies confirm it (an empiric iron trial is the common alternative first step). Bilirubin/reticulocyte/smear target hemolysis, which is less likely here. **⚠️ Verify** (reticulocyte count is a defensible alternative "first step").

## Q10 — Anemia in Infants and Children (p. 612)
A 4-year-old girl with poor concentration, conjunctival pallor, microcytic anemia, low iron, ferritin 14 ng/mL. Which one of the following initial treatments is most appropriate?

- A. Intravenous ferric gluconate.
- B. Oral ferrous sulfate.
- C. Oral iron polysaccharide.
- D. Increased intake of iron-rich foods.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Oral ferrous sulfate.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Oral ferrous sulfate is first-line for iron-deficiency anemia — effective, cheap, best-studied. IV iron is reserved for intolerance/malabsorption; iron polysaccharide is less effective; diet alone won't correct established IDA.

## Q11 — Acute Abdominal Pain in Children (p. 621)
An 8-year-old girl has acute abdominal pain, vomiting, fever, leukocytosis, non-focal exam. Which one of the following is the most appropriate initial imaging to evaluate for appendicitis?

- A. Radiography.
- B. Ultrasonography.
- C. Computed tomography.
- D. MRI.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Ultrasonography.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> US is first-line for pediatric appendicitis — no radiation, good when an experienced operator is available. CT/MRI are reserved for equivocal US.

## Q12 — Acute Abdominal Pain in Children (p. 621)
A previously healthy 9-month-old has abdominal pain, vomiting, and bloody mucoid stools. Which one of the following is the most likely cause?

- A. Intussusception.
- B. Child abuse.
- C. Pyloric stenosis.
- D. Urinary tract infection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Intussusception.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> "Currant-jelly" (bloody mucoid) stools with intermittent pain and vomiting in a 6–12-month-old is classic intussusception. Pyloric stenosis presents earlier with non-bloody projectile vomiting.

## Q13 — Cochrane: Nonmedical Interventions to Enhance Return to Work After Cancer (p. 574)
Which one of the following interventions is likely to increase the ability to return to work?

- A. Physical interventions (e.g., walking, resistance/strength training, yoga).
- B. Counseling alone.
- C. Coping skills alone.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Physical interventions (exercise-based).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Physical/exercise (and multidisciplinary) interventions improve return-to-work after cancer; psychological counseling or coping-skills training *alone* did not show clear benefit. **⚠️ Verify** vs "all of the above."

## Q14 — Cochrane: Psychological Interventions for Depression/Anxiety in CHD or HF (p. 575)
A 69-year-old with coronary heart disease has mental-health concerns. Which one of the following statements about psychological therapy (vs none) in CHD or HF is correct?

- A. It is safe and effective for managing depression.
- B. It is safe and effective for managing anxiety.
- C. It is safe and effective for improving mental health–related quality of life.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **All of the above.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The review supports psychological therapy as safe and beneficial across depression, anxiety, and mental-health QoL in CHD/HF patients. **⚠️ Verify** vs a single-domain answer.

## Q15 — Lown Right Care: The Overdiagnosis of Myocardial Infarction (p. 632)
Which one of the following statements about troponin measurement in the acute care setting is correct?

- A. Elevated levels are highly specific for type 1 MI.
- B. People older than 70 years have higher baseline levels.
- C. The positive predictive value is high in people without classic cardiac symptoms.
- D. Because troponin is highly sensitive, levels are not affected by noncardiac conditions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **People older than 70 years have higher baseline troponin levels.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Baseline troponin rises with age and comorbidity. A is false (elevation is sensitive, not specific — many type 2/non-MI causes); C is false (low PPV without symptoms); D is false (renal disease, sepsis, HF, PE all raise troponin).

## Q16 — Point-of-Care Guide: Bleeding Risk on DOACs in AF (p. 639)
Which one of the following is *not* a risk factor for major bleeding in patients taking a DOAC for atrial fibrillation?

- A. Increasing age.
- B. Female sex.
- C. Comorbid diabetes or hypertension.
- D. Body mass index less than 18.5 kg/m².

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Female sex.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Increasing age, comorbid DM/HTN, and low BMI all increase DOAC major-bleeding risk; female sex is not an established independent risk factor. **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q17 — Practice Guidelines: Premenstrual Disorders (ACOG) (p. 647)
A 21-year-old has depression, anxiety, and irritability starting ~5 days before menses and resolving after onset. Per ACOG, which one of the following is most effective for her affective symptoms?

- A. Increasing dietary calcium to 2,000 mg.
- B. Initiating an intermittent NSAID.
- C. Initiating an intermittent SSRI.
- D. Placing a 52-mg levonorgestrel IUD.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Intermittent SSRI.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> SSRIs are first-line and most effective for the affective symptoms of PMS/PMDD and can be dosed intermittently in the luteal phase. NSAIDs target physical symptoms; the LNG-IUD doesn't treat affective symptoms; calcium is adjunctive. *(No entry in the embedded official key.)*

## Q18 — Practice Guidelines: Acute Bacterial Arthritis in Children (PIDS/IDSA) (p. 650)
A 3-year-old has 1 day of limp, pain on right hip movement, fever 102.8°F, and tachycardia. Per PIDS/IDSA, which one of the following should be obtained *before* starting antibiotics?

- A. Blood culture.
- B. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate.
- C. Culture of hip joint synovial fluid.
- D. Procalcitonin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **Culture of hip joint synovial fluid.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Synovial fluid aspiration with culture (and Gram stain/cell count) before antibiotics maximizes pathogen yield and is diagnostic for septic arthritis. Blood cultures are also recommended, but the joint aspirate is the key pre-antibiotic test. *(No entry in the embedded official key.)*
