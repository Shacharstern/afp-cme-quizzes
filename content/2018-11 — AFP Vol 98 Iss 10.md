## Q1 — Braces and Splints for Common Musculoskeletal Conditions (p. 570)

Which one of the following statements about the patellar stabilizing brace is correct?

- A. It is made of a rigid material.
- B. It has a large benefit for patellofemoral pain syndrome compared with exercise therapy alone.
- C. It maintains proper patellar alignment.
- D. It is not typically used for patients with patellar tracking abnormalities.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — It maintains proper patellar alignment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Patellar braces are typically **soft/flexible** (A wrong), add **little** over exercise therapy (B wrong), and are used **precisely for tracking abnormalities** (D wrong).

## Q2 — Braces and Splints (p. 570)

Which one of the following statements about wrist splints for carpal tunnel syndrome is correct?

- A. They reduce symptoms over three months.
- B. They are superior to other nonsurgical interventions.
- C. Long-term effectiveness is the same as with surgery.
- D. They do not improve short-term functional outcomes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — They reduce symptoms over a three-month period.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Night splinting helps CTS symptoms short-to-medium term. They aren't clearly superior to other conservative options (B), don't match **surgery** long-term (C), and **do** improve short-term function (D wrong).

## Q3 — Care of Incarcerated Patients (p. 577)

Which one of the following groups is disproportionately represented in the U.S. inmate population?

- A. Middle-aged men.
- B. Military veterans.
- C. Women.
- D. Black and Hispanic men.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Black and Hispanic men.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The US incarcerated population is disproportionately **young Black and Hispanic men**.

## Q4 — Care of Incarcerated Patients (p. 577)

Which one of the following conditions is as prevalent in inmates as in the general population?

- A. Human immunodeficiency virus infection.
- B. Latent tuberculosis infection.
- C. Diabetes mellitus.
- D. Mental health disorders.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Diabetes mellitus.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Diabetes prevalence is similar to the general population, whereas **HIV, latent TB, and mental health disorders are markedly more prevalent** among inmates (A, B, D).

## Q5 — Conduct Disorder: Recognition and Management (p. 584)

Which one of the following statements about evaluating an adolescent for conduct disorder is correct?

- A. The Vanderbilt Assessment Scales are limited in usefulness because they evaluate only comorbid conditions.
- B. Three of 15 relevant criteria from the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th ed., must have been present in the past 12 months.
- C. The presence of antisocial personality disorder virtually rules out conduct disorder.
- D. Laboratory tests should always be conducted to rule out comorbid conditions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Three of 15 DSM-5 criteria within the past 12 months (with one in the last 6).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> That's the DSM-5 conduct disorder threshold. The Vanderbilt scales are useful (A wrong); conduct disorder often **precedes** antisocial personality disorder (C wrong); routine labs aren't required (D wrong).

## Q6 — Conduct Disorder (p. 584)

Which one of the following medications is the most appropriate first step in the pharmacologic treatment of a patient with comorbid attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder and conduct disorder when psychosocial interventions alone are ineffective?

- A. Quetiapine (Seroquel).
- B. Methylphenidate.
- C. Haloperidol.
- D. Carbamazepine (Tegretol).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Methylphenidate (treat the comorbid ADHD first).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> When ADHD coexists, **stimulants** are first-line and often reduce aggression/conduct symptoms. Antipsychotics (quetiapine, haloperidol) and anticonvulsants are later-line (A, C, D).

## Q7 — Conduct Disorder (p. 584)

Which one of the following statements about the use of risperidone (Risperdal) in treating conduct disorder is correct?

- A. It should be avoided in children with an average IQ who display aggressive or disruptive behavior.
- B. It has no known adverse effects.
- C. Doses that have been studied are similar to the target doses listed in the prescribing information for adolescents with schizophrenia or bipolar mania.
- D. It cannot be used if the patient has concomitant attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Studied doses are similar to the target doses for adolescent schizophrenia/bipolar mania.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Risperidone can reduce severe aggression (including in average-IQ children, A wrong), but has **metabolic/EPS adverse effects** (B wrong) and **can** be combined with ADHD treatment (D wrong).

## Q8 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Osteoporosis to Prevent Fractures (p. 593)

A 57-year-old postmenopausal woman with no significant problems, a remote childhood forearm fracture, and current smoking presents for a wellness visit. Based on the USPSTF recommendations on screening for osteoporosis, which one of the following is an appropriate next step?

- A. She should be screened with a bone mineral density examination.
- B. She should be evaluated with a risk assessment tool to determine her need for a bone mineral density examination.
- C. She should not be screened with a bone mineral density examination because she is younger than 65 years.
- D. She should not be screened with a bone mineral density examination because she has no risk factors for an osteoporotic fracture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Use a formal fracture-risk assessment tool (e.g., FRAX) to decide on DXA.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For **postmenopausal women under 65 with risk factors** (here, smoking), the USPSTF advises a clinical risk-assessment tool to determine who warrants DXA. All women ≥65 are screened directly; she does have a risk factor (so C, D wrong).

## Q9 — Practice Guidelines: Dysphonia (Hoarseness) — AAO-HNSF Updated Clinical Guideline (p. 606)

According to the AAO-HNSF, which one of the following statements about managing dysphonia is correct?

- A. Antireflux medications should be prescribed routinely in patients with dysphonia.
- B. Corticosteroids should be offered before laryngoscopy.
- C. Computed tomography should not be performed before laryngoscopy.
- D. Antimicrobial therapy should be started if dysphonia symptoms do not improve in four weeks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Don't obtain CT/imaging before visualizing the larynx (laryngoscopy).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Visualize the larynx first (especially if hoarseness persists >4 weeks or red flags). Routine **antireflux meds**, **steroids**, and **antibiotics** are recommended against for uncomplicated dysphonia (A, B, D wrong).
