## Q1 — Annular Lesions: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 283)

Which one of the following most accurately describes the skin lesions of plaque psoriasis?

- A. Annular, erythematous patch or plaque that grows centrifugally.
- B. Annular and raised with central target-like erythema.
- C. Sharply demarcated, erythematous, round or ovoid, with silver plaques that bleed easily with trauma.
- D. Planar, purple, polygonal, pruritic, and papular.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Sharply demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery scale that bleed with trauma (Auspitz sign).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A centrifugally spreading annular plaque suggests **tinea** (A); central target erythema suggests **erythema multiforme** (B); the "6 Ps" (planar, purple, polygonal, pruritic, papular) describe **lichen planus** (D).

## Q2 — Annular Lesions (p. 283)

Which one of the following conditions is associated with skin lesions that are often mistaken for tinea corporis and are typically treated with topical corticosteroids?

- A. Urticaria.
- B. Nummular eczema.
- C. Erythema migrans.
- D. Leprosy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Nummular eczema.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Coin-shaped nummular eczema mimics tinea but is **eczematous** and responds to **topical steroids** (which would worsen true tinea — "tinea incognito").

## Q3 — Annular Lesions (p. 283)

Which one of the following conditions is associated with a herald patch?

- A. Pityriasis rosea.
- B. Lichen planus.
- C. Immunoglobulin A vasculitis.
- D. Sarcoidosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Pityriasis rosea.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A single **herald patch** precedes the "Christmas-tree"–distributed truncal eruption of pityriasis rosea by days to weeks.

## Q4 — Common Foot Problems: Over-the-Counter Treatments and Home Care (p. 298)

Which one of the following statements about plantar fasciitis treatment is correct?

- A. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs improve outcomes by treating inflammation.
- B. Steroid injections are significantly more effective than heel cups.
- C. Over-the-counter (OTC) shoe inserts are as effective as custom orthotics for managing pain.
- D. Less than 50% of cases resolve with OTC and home therapies alone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — OTC shoe inserts are as effective as custom orthotics.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Plantar fasciitis is **degenerative**, so NSAID benefit isn't from anti-inflammation (A); steroid injections aren't clearly superior to inserts and carry risk (B); **most cases resolve** with conservative care (D wrong).

## Q5 — Common Foot Problems (p. 298)

Which one of the following OTC treatments for tinea pedis is most effective?

- A. Miconazole.
- B. Terbinafine (Lamisil).
- C. Clotrimazole.
- D. Undecylenic acid.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Terbinafine.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Topical **allylamines (terbinafine)** have higher cure rates and shorter courses than azoles (miconazole, clotrimazole) or undecylenic acid for tinea pedis.

## Q6 — Common Foot Problems (p. 298)

Which one of the following statements about hallux valgus is correct?

- A. It is more prevalent in men.
- B. Wide toe box shoes can relieve symptoms.
- C. Interdigital pads improve pain and function.
- D. Surgery is superior to orthotics after two years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Wide toe-box shoes relieve symptoms.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Hallux valgus is more prevalent in **women** (A wrong); the data don't show interdigital pads improve function (C) or clear surgical superiority over orthotics at 2 years (D); footwear modification is the practical first step.

## Q7 — Long-Acting Reversible Contraception: Difficult Insertions and Removals (p. 304)

You are inserting an intrauterine device (IUD) in a nulliparous woman. However, the IUD inserter will not pass through the cervical os. Which one of the following is the next best step?

- A. Use an IUD with a wider inserter.
- B. Apply misoprostol (Cytotec) to soften the cervix.
- C. Use a uterine sound/dilator to dilate the os.
- D. Use a longer speculum.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Use a uterine sound/dilator to dilate the os.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mechanical dilation is the next step for a tight os. **Routine misoprostol does not ease insertion** and increases cramping (B); a wider inserter or longer speculum doesn't solve a stenotic os (A, D).

## Q8 — Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (p. 304)

A transgender man using testosterone would like to use an IUD for birth control. Which one of the following is advised to facilitate the insertion?

- A. Temporarily stop testosterone supplementation.
- B. Use vaginal estrogen for one to two weeks before the procedure.
- C. Avoid using a tenaculum for this procedure.
- D. Use general anesthesia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Pretreat with vaginal estrogen for one to two weeks.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Testosterone causes vaginal/cervical **atrophy**; short-course vaginal estrogen improves tissue for a more comfortable insertion. Testosterone need not be stopped (A); a tenaculum is still useful (C); general anesthesia is unnecessary (D).

## Q9 — Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (p. 304)

A patient presents for progestin IUD removal, but the strings are not visible on inspection. Assuming the IUD is in place, which one of the following is correct?

- A. The IUD cannot be removed if the patient is pregnant.
- B. Abdominal radiography to confirm the IUD position is the next step.
- C. When grasped, the IUD must be removed right side up.
- D. An IUD hook or alligator forceps may be used for removal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — An IUD hook or alligator forceps may be used to remove it.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> With missing strings (IUD confirmed intrauterine by **ultrasound**, not abdominal x-ray first, B), retrieval instruments are used. The IUD can be removed in pregnancy if strings are visible (A overstated); orientation during removal isn't a rule (C).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Feed Thickener for Newborn Infants with Gastroesophageal Reflux (p. 275)

Which one of the following is an appropriate first-line treatment for a formula-fed infant showing signs and symptoms of gastroesophageal reflux?

- A. Using smaller, more frequent feeds.
- B. Using thickeners such as rice cereal.
- C. Providing parental reassurance.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Uncomplicated infant reflux is managed conservatively: **reassurance**, smaller/more-frequent feeds, and feed **thickening** — before any acid-suppressing medication.

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Blood Pressure Targets for Patients with Hypertension and Cardiovascular Disease (p. 276)

In a woman with hypertension and a history of angina, which one of the following statements about treating to a target blood pressure of 135/85 mm Hg or less instead of 140 to 160/90 to 100 mm Hg is correct?

- A. It increases the chances that she will discontinue her medication.
- B. It lowers the risk of total mortality.
- C. It lowers the risk of cardiovascular-related mortality.
- D. It lowers the risk of total cardiovascular events.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — The lower target increases the likelihood of medication discontinuation (adverse effects).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In this Cochrane analysis, a tighter target did **not** reduce total mortality, cardiovascular mortality, or total cardiovascular events (B, C, D) but increased adverse-effect-related withdrawals.

## Q12 — AHRQ Review: Management of Insomnia Disorder in Adults (p. 319)

Which one of the following medications has the strongest evidence of effectiveness for the treatment of insomnia disorder in adults 55 years and older?

- A. Zolpidem.
- B. Melatonin.
- C. Lorazepam.
- D. Doxepin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Doxepin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Low-dose **doxepin** had the strongest evidence in older adults (and avoids the falls/cognitive risks of benzodiazepines like lorazepam and Z-drugs like zolpidem). Melatonin's evidence is weak.

## Q13 — Practice Guidelines: Antibiotics for Uncomplicated Skin Abscesses After Incision and Drainage — BMJ Rapid Recommendation (p. 323)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of uncomplicated skin abscesses is correct?

- A. Cephalosporins are recommended in combination with incision and drainage to reduce treatment failure.
- B. Clindamycin is preferred over trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) to reduce risk of diarrhea.
- C. TMP-SMX has a significantly reduced risk of abscess recurrence compared with clindamycin.
- D. TMP-SMX and clindamycin are associated with decreases in pain, hospitalizations, and infections in household contacts.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — TMP-SMX and clindamycin (added to I&D) reduce treatment failure, recurrence, and household spread.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cephalosporins don't cover MRSA, so they don't reduce failure (A); **clindamycin causes more diarrhea** than TMP-SMX (B reversed); the two agents are broadly comparable for recurrence (C overstated).
