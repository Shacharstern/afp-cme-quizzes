## Q1 — Common Foot Fractures (p. 119)
Which one of the following complications is most likely to occur following a zone 2 fracture of the fifth metatarsal?

- A. Arthritis.
- B. Infection.
- C. Nonunion.
- D. Compartment syndrome.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Nonunion.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Zone 2 (Jones fracture) at the metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction has a watershed blood supply → high rate of delayed/nonunion. This is the hallmark teaching point distinguishing it from zone 1 (avulsion) fractures.

## Q2 — Common Foot Fractures (p. 119)
When considering imaging for a suspected foot or toe fracture, which one of the following radiographic views should be ordered initially?

- A. Anteroposterior and lateral radiography of the foot.
- B. Weight-bearing anteroposterior, lateral, and oblique radiography of the foot, and dedicated toe radiography.
- C. CT of the foot and ankle.
- D. MRI of the affected area.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Weight-bearing AP, lateral, and oblique foot films + dedicated toe views.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Plain three-view radiography (weight-bearing where tolerated) is the initial study; dedicated toe views improve sensitivity for phalangeal fractures. CT/MRI are reserved for occult or complex injuries.

## Q3 — Managing Selected Chronic Conditions in Hospitalized Patients (p. 134)
A 50-year-old man admitted for CAP, BP 175/100 mm Hg, no history of hypertension. Which one is recommended?

- A. IV labetalol to <140/80 mm Hg.
- B. Oral clonidine (0.2 mg) to <160/90 mm Hg.
- C. Do not treat unless he has pain from an acute back injury.
- D. Do not treat with medications during admission, but refer to PCP for outpatient follow-up.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Don't treat acutely; refer for outpatient follow-up.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Asymptomatic inpatient hypertension (no end-organ damage) should not be treated aggressively — acute BP lowering risks harm. Elevated BP during acute illness is often transient; arrange outpatient confirmation and management.

## Q4 — Managing Selected Chronic Conditions in Hospitalized Patients (p. 134)
A 60-year-old woman with heart failure and remote DVT, admitted for pneumonia. Most appropriate VTE prevention?

- A. Mechanical prophylaxis (intermittent pneumatic compression).
- B. Oral aspirin daily.
- C. Subcutaneous LMWH, 40 mg daily.
- D. Subcutaneous fondaparinux, 2.5 mg daily.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Subcutaneous LMWH 40 mg daily.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Acutely ill medical inpatients with VTE risk factors (HF, prior DVT) warrant pharmacologic prophylaxis; LMWH is first-line. Mechanical prophylaxis is for patients with bleeding contraindications. Fondaparinux is a niche alternative (e.g., HIT).

## Q5 — Acute Medication Poisoning (p. 143)
25-year-old: acute mental status change, agitation, tachycardia, hypertension, dilated pupils, diaphoresis. Most likely toxidrome?

- A. Opioid overdose.
- B. Sedative-hypnotic overdose.
- C. Sympathomimetic overdose.
- D. Antimuscarinic overdose.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Sympathomimetic.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Agitation + tachycardia + HTN + mydriasis + **diaphoresis** = sympathomimetic. The discriminator vs. antimuscarinic: anticholinergic toxidrome causes **dry** skin ("dry as a bone"); sympathomimetics are sweaty.

## Q6 — Acute Medication Poisoning (p. 143)
36-year-old: altered mental status, diminished breath sounds, hypotension, bradycardia. Priority?

- A. Contact poison control.
- B. Support airway, breathing, and circulation, and possibly administer naloxone.
- C. Perform electrocardiography.
- D. Obtain serum acetaminophen and salicylate levels.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Support ABCs, ± naloxone.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Resuscitation always precedes diagnostics. The picture (CNS depression, hypoventilation, bradycardia/hypotension) is an opioid toxidrome → ABCs plus empiric naloxone. Labs and ECG follow stabilization.

## Q8 — Treatment of Chronic Insomnia in Adults (p. 154)

## Q9 — Abnormal Uterine Bleeding With Hormonal Contraception (p. 161)
33-year-old, irregular bleeding one month after LNG-IUD placement. Statement most consistent with evidence?

- A. Bleeding is common over the first three months after placement.
- B. Bleeding rates after placement do not change over time.
- C. Most patients experience amenorrhea within 12 months.
- D. This bleeding indicates nonfundal placement.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Irregular bleeding is common in the first 3 months.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Unscheduled bleeding/spotting is expected early after LNG-IUD insertion and improves with time (counsel reassurance). Amenorrhea occurs in a minority, not "most," by 12 months.

## Q10 — Abnormal Uterine Bleeding With Hormonal Contraception (p. 161)
21-year-old, 16 days of bleeding per cycle + breakthrough bleeding on a monophasic COC with 20 mcg estradiol. Most likely to improve her bleeding?

- A. Switch to 10 mcg estradiol.
- B. Increase the hormone-free interval.
- C. Shift to a progestin-only pill.
- D. Start a different formulation with 30 mcg estradiol.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Increase to a 30 mcg estradiol formulation.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Low estrogen dose is a common cause of breakthrough bleeding; increasing the estrogen component stabilizes the endometrium. Lowering estrogen (A) or switching to progestin-only (C) typically worsens unscheduled bleeding.

## Q11 — Acute Uncomplicated UTIs in Adults: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 167)
Which is a sign/symptom of a complicated UTI?

- A. Nocturia.
- B. Fever.
- C. Dysuria.
- D. Suprapubic pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Fever.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Fever signals systemic/upper-tract involvement (pyelonephritis) → complicated UTI. Dysuria, suprapubic pain, and nocturia are lower-tract/uncomplicated features.

## Q12 — Acute Uncomplicated UTIs in Adults (p. 167)
Urine culture with susceptibility should be ordered for which group?

- A. All women younger than 65 with suspected UTI.
- B. All women with any previous UTIs.
- C. All women prescribed antibiotics.
- D. Women with ≥2 UTIs in the past 6 months or ≥3 in the past year.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — ≥2 UTIs in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Uncomplicated cystitis is treated empirically without culture; culture is reserved for recurrent, complicated, treatment-failure, or atypical cases.

## Q14 — Cochrane: Cannabis Use for Cancer-Related Pain (p. 117)
Which statement about cannabis/synthetic cannabis for cancer pain is correct?

- A. Cannabis improves the patient's global impression of change.
- B. Cannabis improves mean pain intensity scores.
- C. Cannabis is associated with an increased risk of CNS adverse events.

## Q15 — AHRQ: Management of Postpartum Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy (online)
Which statement about postpartum home BP monitoring is correct?

- A. It increases the number of people who have their BP checked at recommended intervals.
- B. It leads to lower patient satisfaction than in-office monitoring.

## Q16 — Practice Guidelines: Treating Eating Disorders (APA) (p. 185)
19-year-old with bulimia nervosa. In addition to CBT, which medication does the APA recommend?

- A. Bupropion.
- B. Fluoxetine.
- C. Lisdexamfetamine (Vyvanse).
- D. Topiramate.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Fluoxetine.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Fluoxetine is the only FDA-approved and APA-recommended medication for bulimia nervosa. **Bupropion is contraindicated** (seizure risk with purging). Lisdexamfetamine is for binge-eating disorder, not bulimia.

## Q17 — Practice Guidelines: Management of Anal Fissures (ASCRS) (p. 188)
32-year-old with migraines, two months of defecation-triggered anal pain, single apical fissure. Treatment most likely to resolve it without causing headaches?

- A. Oral diltiazem (CCB).
- B. Oral nitroglycerin (nitrate).
- C. Topical diltiazem (CCB).
- D. Topical nitroglycerin (nitrate).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Topical diltiazem.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Topical CCBs are first-line for chronic anal fissure with efficacy comparable to nitrates but **fewer headaches** — important in a migraineur. Nitrates (oral or topical) commonly cause headache; topical minimizes systemic effect vs. oral.
