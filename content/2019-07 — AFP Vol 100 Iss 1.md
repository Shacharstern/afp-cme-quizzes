## Q1 — Lead Poisoning in Children (p. 24)

Which one of the following statements about the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's recommendations for screening Medicaid-eligible children for elevated blood lead levels is correct?

- A. Screening is not recommended.
- B. Screen all children at six and 12 months.
- C. Screen all children at 12 and 24 months.
- D. Wait to screen all children until 48 months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Screen Medicaid-eligible children at 12 and 24 months.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Medicaid mandates blood lead testing at **12 and 24 months** (and 24–72 months if not previously tested) — these higher-risk children warrant universal screening.

## Q2 — Lead Poisoning in Children (p. 24)

Which one of the following blood lead levels should initiate an environmental lead investigation in the case management of asymptomatic children?

- A. 2 mcg per dL (0.10 μmol per L).
- B. 5 mcg per dL (0.24 μmol per L).
- C. 10 mcg per dL (0.48 μmol per L).
- D. Any detectable blood lead level.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — 5 mcg/dL.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The CDC reference value of **≥5 mcg/dL** triggers case management, including an **environmental investigation** and education. (No lead level is truly "safe," but 5 is the action threshold.)

## Q3 — Polypharmacy: Evaluating Risks and Deprescribing (p. 32)

Which one of the following is a patient-related risk factor for polypharmacy?

- A. Younger age.
- B. Automated medication refills.
- C. Cognitive impairment.
- D. Prescribing to meet disease-specific quality metrics.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Cognitive impairment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Among **patient** factors, cognitive impairment (with multiple comorbidities and older age) raises polypharmacy risk. Automated refills and quality-metric-driven prescribing are **system/prescriber** factors (B, D); younger age lowers risk (A).

## Q4 — Polypharmacy (p. 32)

Which one of the following statements about assessment tools for polypharmacy is correct?

- A. The Beers criteria are superior to other tools in improving polypharmacy-related patient outcomes.
- B. Explicit tools have rigid standards and clear criteria.
- C. Implicit tools are more reliable than explicit tools.
- D. The Medication Appropriateness Index is quick to use.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Explicit tools have rigid standards and clear criteria.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Explicit (criterion-based) tools like **Beers/STOPP** are standardized; **implicit** tools (e.g., the Medication Appropriateness Index) rely on judgment, are **less reliable**, and are **time-consuming** (C, D wrong). No single tool clearly improves outcomes best (A).

## Q5 — Amenorrhea: A Systematic Approach to Diagnosis and Management (p. 39)

Which one of the following statements about primary ovary insufficiency is correct?

- A. These patients should be offered testing for the FMR1 gene.
- B. Hormone therapy is typically continued until 60 years of age.
- C. Contraception is not necessary.
- D. Transdermal estrogen is associated with an increased risk of venous thromboembolism.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Offer FMR1 (fragile X premutation) gene testing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Primary ovarian insufficiency warrants **FMR1** premutation and karyotype testing. Hormone therapy is continued to the **average age of menopause (~51)** (B); intermittent ovulation means **contraception is still needed** (C); **transdermal** estrogen carries lower VTE risk than oral (D reversed).

## Q6 — Amenorrhea (p. 39)

Which one of the following statements about amenorrhea is correct?

- A. Any history of menses rules out Turner syndrome.
- B. A single follicle-stimulating hormone level in the menopausal range meets the criteria for primary ovarian failure.
- C. Outflow obstruction is ruled out in any women with a history of vaginal delivery.
- D. Luteinizing hormone, prolactin, follicle-stimulating hormone, and thyroid-stimulating hormone levels should be obtained as part of the initial evaluation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Obtain LH, prolactin, FSH, and TSH in the initial workup (plus a pregnancy test).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mosaic Turner syndrome can present with some menses (A wrong); POI requires **two** elevated FSH levels ≥1 month apart (B wrong); obstruction can develop **after** vaginal delivery (e.g., Asherman, C wrong).

## Q7 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Noninvasive Diagnostic Tests for Helicobacter pylori Infection (p. 16)

Assuming a fixed prevalence of 53.7% and a specificity of 90%, which one of the following statements about testing for Helicobacter pylori infection is correct?

- A. Urea breath tests (¹³C or ¹⁴C) have a greater than 90% positive predictive value (PPV) and false-negative rates of less than 10%.
- B. Serology tests have a greater than 90% PPV and false-negative rates of less than 10%.
- C. Stool antigen tests have a greater than 90% PPV and false-negative rates of less than 10%.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> At this (high) prevalence and 90% specificity, urea breath, serology, and stool antigen tests all reach >90% PPV with <10% false-negative rates. (Real-world choice favors **urea breath/stool antigen** for active infection over serology.)

## Q8 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Medication Management for Chronic Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (p. 17)

Which one of the following medication classes may decrease cardiovascular mortality in patients who have chronic heart failure with preserved ejection fraction?

- A. Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors.
- B. Angiotensin receptor blockers.
- C. Beta blockers.
- D. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Beta blockers.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In HFpEF (where no therapy has robustly improved survival), this review found **beta blockers** may reduce cardiovascular mortality. ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and MRAs did not clearly lower mortality (mostly reduce hospitalizations).

## Q9 — Practice Guidelines: Physical Activity — Updated Recommendations from HHS (p. 58)

According to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, which one of the following is a result of increasing physical activity in older adults?

- A. No change in cognition.
- B. Increased osteoarthritis pain.
- C. Uncertain benefits in frail patients.
- D. Reduction in falls and fall-related injuries.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Reduction in falls and fall-related injuries.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Physical activity (especially **balance + strength**) reduces falls and injuries in older adults, **improves cognition**, **reduces** OA pain, and benefits even **frail** patients (A, B, C wrong).
