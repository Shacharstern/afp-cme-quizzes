## Q1 — Chronic Asthma Treatment (p. 358)
Which one of the following medications is first-line therapy for control (i.e., maintenance) of asthma?

- A. Inhaled corticosteroids.
- B. Long-acting muscarinic antagonists.
- C. Leukotriene receptor antagonists.
- D. Long-acting beta 2 agonists.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Inhaled corticosteroids.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> ICS are the cornerstone controller therapy at every step of persistent asthma. LABAs are never used as monotherapy; LTRAs and LAMAs are add-ons or alternatives, not first-line maintenance.

## Q2 — Chronic Asthma Treatment (p. 358)
Which one of the following patients should be considered for single maintenance and reliever therapy (SMART)?

- A. A 10-year-old child with moderately severe asthma.
- B. A 28-year-old man with exercise-induced asthma.
- C. A 35-year-old woman with moderate asthma.
- D. A 31-year-old man with mild asthma who is adherent with his rescue inhalers.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A 35-year-old woman with moderate asthma.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High (Corrected) 
> While SMART has been studied in children aged 4-11 years and reduced exacerbations compared with ICS monotherapy, **data are insufficient to make this the preferred therapy for children**.  The guidelines specifically recommend SMART as preferred therapy for **adolescents (≥12 years) and adults**, not younger children.**

## Q3 — Outpatient Management of COVID-19 (p. 370)
Which one of the following factors is not a known predictor of severe COVID-19 disease?

- A. Presence of comorbidities.
- B. Myocardial injury.
- C. Elevated platelet count.
- D. Elevated C-reactive protein.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Elevated platelet count.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Severe COVID-19 predictors include comorbidities, myocardial injury (elevated troponin), and elevated CRP. **Thrombocytopenia** (low platelets), not thrombocytosis, marks severe disease — so an elevated platelet count is the odd one out.

## Q5 — Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (p. 383)
A 25-year-old woman presents with fatigue, weight loss, and joint pain. Which one of the following statements about the presentation and diagnosis of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is correct?

- A. Joint pain is the most prevalent symptom.
- B. An antinuclear antibody titer should be the initial laboratory test.
- C. Involvement of multiple organ systems is not typical.
- D. A renal biopsy is required for all patients.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — An antinuclear antibody titer should be the initial laboratory test.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> ANA is the most sensitive screening test and the recommended first lab in suspected SLE (a negative ANA makes SLE very unlikely). Fatigue — not joint pain — is the most prevalent symptom; multiorgan involvement is typical; renal biopsy is reserved for suspected lupus nephritis, not all patients.

> [!success]- Reveal answer

## Q7 — Leukemia (p. 397)
Which one of the following statements about the diagnosis, evaluation, and treatment of chronic lymphocytic leukemia is correct?

- A. It primarily affects children.
- B. The Philadelphia chromosome (reciprocal translocation of chromosomes 9 and 22) is found in 90% to 95% of cases.
- C. Bone marrow biopsy is required for diagnosis.
- D. Asymptomatic, early-stage disease may be monitored without treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Asymptomatic, early-stage disease may be monitored without treatment.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> CLL is a disease of older adults (watch-and-wait for early/asymptomatic Rai/Binet stages). The Philadelphia chromosome defines CML, not CLL. CLL is diagnosed by peripheral-blood flow cytometry — marrow biopsy is not required.

## Q8 — Leukemia (p. 397)
In patients who survive childhood leukemia, which one of the following is recommended?

- A. Routine age- and sex-specific screenings and counseling.
- B. Periodic screening for endocrinopathies.
- C. Periodic complete blood count monitoring.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Childhood leukemia survivors need lifelong surveillance: routine age/sex-appropriate screening + counseling, periodic endocrinopathy screening (treatment-related), and periodic CBC monitoring for relapse and secondary malignancy.

## Q9 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2022 (p. 406)
Which one of the following statements about behavioral health treatments is correct?

- A. Monotherapy is preferred over combination drug therapy for acute severe depression.
- B. Tricyclic antidepressants are the preferred therapy for panic disorder.
- C. Relapse is common after discontinuing antidepressants.
- D. Hypnotic agents are first-line therapy for insomnia in adults.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Relapse is common after discontinuing antidepressants.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The ANTLER trial (a 2022 top study) showed high relapse rates after stopping long-term antidepressants. SSRIs (not TCAs) are first-line for panic disorder; CBT-I (not hypnotics) is first-line for insomnia; combination therapy can outperform monotherapy in severe depression.

## Q10 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2022 (p. 406)
Which one of the following statements about prediabetes or diabetes mellitus is correct?

- A. In patients with prediabetes, an intensive lifestyle intervention has been shown to improve long-term cardiovascular risk.
- B. Most older adults with prediabetes do not develop diabetes.
- C. Drug-induced hypoglycemia is uncommon in frail older adults.
- D. Combination therapy for diabetic peripheral neuropathy does not improve pain more effectively than monotherapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Most older adults with prediabetes do not develop diabetes.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> In older adults, prediabetes much more often regresses to normoglycemia or remains stable than it progresses to diabetes. Intensive lifestyle (DPP follow-up) reduced diabetes incidence but not long-term CV events; hypoglycemia is *common* in frail elders; and the OPTION-DM trial showed combination therapy *did* improve neuropathic pain.

## Q11 — Bell Palsy (p. 415)
Which one of the following statements about oral corticosteroid therapy for patients with Bell palsy is correct?

- A. It has been shown to improve rates of complete recovery in adults.
- B. It is contraindicated in pregnant people.
- C. In children, antivirals are preferred over corticosteroids.
- D. It should be used only for patients with severe symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — It has been shown to improve rates of complete recovery in adults.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Early oral corticosteroids (within 72 h) improve complete-recovery rates in adults — the cornerstone of Bell palsy treatment. They are not contraindicated in pregnancy, are recommended regardless of severity, and antivirals are at most an adjunct (not preferred) in children.

## Q12 — Bell Palsy (p. 415)
Which one of the following statements about the diagnosis of Bell palsy is correct?

- A. Symptoms are bilateral in about 20% of patients.
- B. Onset of symptoms is typically gradual, occurring over one to two weeks.
- C. Patients with Bell palsy are still able to wrinkle their forehead on the affected side.
- D. Patients with Bell palsy have weakness of both the upper and lower facial muscles on the affected side.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Weakness of both the upper and lower facial muscles on the affected side.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Bell palsy is a *peripheral* CN VII lesion, so the forehead is involved (cannot wrinkle) — distinguishing it from a central lesion, which spares the forehead. It is almost always unilateral and acute (<72 h), not bilateral or gradual.

## Q16 — Practice Guidelines: FIGO Postpartum Hemorrhage (p. 438)
A 32-year-old patient delivers her sixth child vaginally after a pregnancy complicated by gestational diabetes. After delivery, brisk bleeding and poor uterine tone are noted. Bleeding responds to oxytocin, misoprostol, tranexamic acid, and uterine balloon tamponade. Blood pressure has decreased from 115/82 to 95/63 mm Hg and heart rate has increased from 88 to 110 beats per minute. According to FIGO, which one of the following suggests a need for transfusion in this patient?

- A. Administration of oxytocin.
- B. Administration of tranexamic acid.
- C. Rule of 30.
- D. Shock index.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Shock index.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Shock index (HR ÷ SBP) here = 110 ÷ 95 ≈ 1.16; a value ≥1 signals hemodynamic compromise and the likely need for transfusion, and FIGO emphasizes it as a more reliable early marker than isolated vital signs. The "Rule of 30" (option C) is a competing PPH transfusion heuristic — this is the residual ambiguity. **To verify against print key.**
