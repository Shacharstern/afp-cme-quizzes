## Q1 — Dermatologic Conditions in Skin of Color (p. 26)
Which statement about dermatologic health disparities in patients with skin of color is correct?

- A. Textbooks lack representative images of skin of color relative to the population proportion.
- B. Clinical trials lack adequate representation of patients with skin of color.
- C. These patients are less likely to be treated for common conditions (e.g., acne) and more likely to present with advanced skin cancer than White patients.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> All three disparities are documented: under-representation in textbooks and trials, undertreatment of common conditions, and later-stage skin cancer presentation (worse melanoma survival).

## Q2 — Dermatologic Conditions in Skin of Color (p. 26)
A teenager having a mole removed wants to avoid a keloid like their sibling's. Which statement about keloids is correct?

- A. Intralesional triamcinolone + fluorouracil should be given weekly for six weeks after excision.
- B. The patient has no increased risk despite the sibling's history.
- C. Compression therapy can decrease keloid formation.
- D. Postsurgical onabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) is proven to prevent keloids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Compression therapy can decrease keloid formation.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> A family history *does* raise keloid risk (genetic predisposition, more common in skin of color). Compression (and silicone sheeting) help prevent recurrence; Botox is unproven.

## Q3 — Dermatologic Conditions in Skin of Color (p. 26)
Which statement about postinflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) is correct?

- A. Laser surgery is first-line treatment.
- B. Sunscreen should be avoided in skin of color to prevent PIH.
- C. Hydroquinone monotherapy is superior to triple combination (fluocinolone/hydroquinone/tretinoin).
- D. Acne vulgaris is a common underlying cause.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Acne vulgaris is a common underlying cause.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Any inflammatory dermatosis (acne above all) can trigger PIH. Sunscreen is *essential*; triple-combination therapy outperforms hydroquinone alone; laser is reserved for refractory cases (and can worsen PIH).

## Q4 — Tonsillitis and Tonsilloliths (p. 35)
A 10-year-old with sore throat, left tonsillar exudate, no lymphadenopathy, mild cough, no fever. Appropriate next step?

- A. Empiric penicillin.
- B. Symptomatic treatment only.
- C. Testing for ASO and anti-DNase B antibodies.
- D. Contrast-enhanced CT of the neck.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Symptomatic treatment only.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Low modified Centor score — *cough present*, no fever, no anterior nodes — points to viral pharyngitis. Empiric antibiotics aren't warranted; ASO/anti-DNase B are for suspected post-streptococcal sequelae, not acute diagnosis; CT is for suspected deep-space abscess.

## Q5 — Tonsillitis and Tonsilloliths (p. 35)
A 32-year-old with halitosis is found to have tonsilloliths. Which statement is correct?

- A. Best treated with surgery.
- B. Cannot be visualized on dental panoramic radiographs.
- C. Complications are common.
- D. Usually managed expectantly.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Usually managed expectantly.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Tonsilloliths are benign and typically need only reassurance/conservative care (gargling, manual expression). Surgery is reserved for refractory/recurrent cases; complications are rare; they can appear incidentally on panoramic radiographs.

## Q6 — Polyarticular Joint Pain (p. 42)
A 45-year-old with polyarticular pain. Which finding is most associated with rheumatoid arthritis?

- A. Bony enlargement at the DIP joints.
- B. Dactylitis.
- C. Symmetrical inflammation of the PIP joints.
- D. High titers of antinuclear antibodies.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Symmetrical inflammation of the PIP joints.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> RA is symmetric and spares the DIPs (MCP/PIP/wrist involvement). DIP bony enlargement = OA (Heberden); dactylitis = psoriatic/reactive arthritis; high ANA = SLE.

## Q7 — Polyarticular Joint Pain (p. 42)
Which finding is most suggestive of psoriatic arthritis?

- A. Positive (elevated) erythrocyte sedimentation rate.
- B. Pencil-in-cup erosion on radiography.
- C. Double contour sign on ultrasonography.
- D. Diffuse osteoporosis and large subchondral erosions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Pencil-in-cup erosion.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The "pencil-in-cup" deformity is the classic radiographic hallmark of psoriatic arthritis (arthritis mutilans). The double-contour sign is specific for *gout* (urate over cartilage).

What is the recommended daily dietary potassium intake for adults without advanced CKD?

- C. Less than 3,510 mg per day.

## Q11 — Potassium Disorders (p. 59)
Which statement about diagnostic testing in potassium disorders is correct?

- A. A repeated serum potassium is not necessary to confirm hypokalemia.
- B. All hypokalemic patients need renin, aldosterone, and urine potassium/sodium/chloride/creatinine.
- C. If hyperkalemia is unexpected, repeat the serum potassium to exclude pseudohyperkalemia.
- D. A 24-hour urine potassium should be measured in all hypokalemic patients.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Repeat an unexpected hyperkalemia to exclude pseudohyperkalemia.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Hemolysis, fist-clenching, and delayed processing cause spurious (pseudo) hyperkalemia — always recheck an unexpected result. Extensive renin/aldosterone/urine workups are reserved for selected, not all, patients.

## Q12 — Infectious Mononucleosis (p. 71)
Which sign is most suggestive of infectious mononucleosis?

- A. Tonsillar exudate.
- B. Tonsillar inflammation.
- C. Posterior cervical lymphadenopathy.
- D. Anterior cervical lymphadenopathy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Posterior cervical lymphadenopathy.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Posterior cervical (and especially symmetric/generalized) lymphadenopathy is the most discriminating sign for mono; anterior nodes and exudate overlap heavily with streptococcal pharyngitis.

## Q13 — Infectious Mononucleosis (p. 71)
Which is the recommended initial diagnostic test for suspected infectious mononucleosis?

- A. Viral capsid antigen IgG.
- B. Viral capsid antigen IgM.
- C. Viral capsid antigen IgG and IgM.
- D. Heterophile antibody test and CBC with differential.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Heterophile (Monospot) antibody test + CBC with differential.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Initial testing pairs the heterophile antibody with a CBC showing lymphocytosis and atypical lymphocytes. EBV-specific serologies (VCA IgM/IgG) are reserved for heterophile-negative cases or early presentation in children.

## Q14 — Infectious Mononucleosis (p. 71)
Which statement about splenomegaly in infectious mononucleosis is correct?

- A. It is relatively uncommon, occurring in about 10% of patients.
- B. It occurs in almost all patients.
- C. Ultrasonography is recommended when splenomegaly is clinically suspected.
- D. Ultrasound confirmation of resolution is recommended before returning to sports.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ultrasonography is recommended when splenomegaly is clinically suspected.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Physical exam is unreliable for spleen size, so US helps when splenomegaly is suspected. Routine imaging to confirm *resolution* before return to play is **not** standard (return to contact sport is time-based, ~3–4 weeks). Splenomegaly occurs in roughly half of patients, not 10% or "almost all." Flagged to verify (C vs. D).
