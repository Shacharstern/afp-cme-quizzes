## Q1 — Sarcoidosis: Evaluation and Treatment (p. 19)
A 45-year-old woman with a family history of sarcoidosis, dry cough and fatigue. Which approach to diagnosis/treatment of pulmonary sarcoidosis is correct?

- A. Serum ACE testing is the diagnostic standard.
- B. Patients with asymptomatic or mild disease should be observed without treatment.
- C. Methotrexate is a first-line treatment.
- D. Several biomarkers can aid in the assessment of treatment response.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Observe asymptomatic/mild disease without treatment.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Many patients (especially stage I) remit spontaneously → watchful waiting. Serum ACE is neither sensitive nor specific (not diagnostic standard). Corticosteroids are first-line when treatment is needed; methotrexate is steroid-sparing/second-line. No biomarker reliably tracks response.

## Q2 — Sarcoidosis: Evaluation and Treatment (p. 19)
Patient with dyspnea, cutaneous lesions suspicious for sarcoidosis, ECG with RBBB. Which treatment statement is correct?

- A. Lupus pernio requires tissue biopsy for confirmation.
- B. Glucocorticoids are preferred in the treatment of lupus pernio.
- C. Azathioprine, cyclophosphamide, and hydroxychloroquine are recommended second-line treatments for pulmonary sarcoidosis.
- D. Treatment of cardiac sarcoidosis with corticosteroids may improve long-term clinical outcomes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Corticosteroids for cardiac sarcoidosis may improve long-term outcomes.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The RBBB hints at cardiac involvement, which is life-threatening; immunosuppression (steroids) is used to preserve function. Lupus pernio is a clinical diagnosis often not requiring biopsy and is frequently refractory to steroids alone. ⚠️ Verify against print key.

## Q3 — Acute Coronary Syndrome (p. 34)
Which ECG finding is most predictive of ischemia causing ACS?

- A. Peaked T waves.
- B. Absence of Q waves.
- C. ST-segment depression.
- D. Left bundle branch block.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — ST-segment depression.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Dynamic ST-segment depression (and T-wave inversion) is the most reliable ECG marker of ischemia in NSTE-ACS. New LBBB is less specific; peaked T waves suggest hyperkalemia/hyperacute MI but are nonspecific.

## Q4 — Acute Coronary Syndrome (p. 34)
Recommended initial management for patients en route to the hospital with suspected ACS?

- A. Monitor and, if symptoms evolve, encourage the patient to drive to the ED.
- B. Administer 81 mg of enteric-coated aspirin as soon as available.
- C. Administer fibrinolytics before transfer to the ED.
- D. Administer supplemental oxygen if saturation is less than 90%.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Supplemental oxygen only if SpO₂ <90%.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Routine oxygen is no longer recommended in non-hypoxic ACS (no benefit, possible harm) — give it only when SpO₂ <90%. Aspirin is recommended, but the correct form/dose is **chewed, non–enteric-coated 162–325 mg**, so option B as written is wrong. Patients should never drive themselves; prehospital fibrinolytics are not standard. ⚠️ Verify against print key (B vs D).

## Q5 — Acute Asthma Exacerbations (p. 43)
13-year-old, PEF >70% predicted, gradually worsening with activity. Most appropriate?

- A. Administer oxygen to achieve saturation >95%.
- B. Administer a short-acting beta₂ agonist (SABA).
- C. Order chest radiography.
- D. Transfer to an acute care facility.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — SABA.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> PEF >70% = mild exacerbation → inhaled SABA is first-line. No need for routine CXR, transfer, or oxygen targeting >95% in a mild attack.

## Q6 — Acute Asthma Exacerbations (p. 43)
25-year-old with worsening asthma at urgent care. Which may reduce likelihood of hospitalization?

- A. IV instead of oral corticosteroids.
- B. Intramuscular epinephrine.
- C. High-dose inhaled corticosteroid within the first hour of presentation.
- D. Expedited chest radiography.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — High-dose ICS within the first hour.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Early high-dose ICS in the first hour can reduce admissions. IV and oral steroids are equivalent (no advantage to IV); IM epinephrine is reserved for anaphylaxis; CXR doesn't change disposition. ⚠️ Verify against print key.

## Q7 — Mental Status Examination in Primary Care (p. 51)
68-year-old worried about forgetfulness; given time constraints, which test is efficient and reliable for detecting cognitive impairment in primary care?

- A. Mini-Addenbrooke's cognitive examination.
- B. Rapid cognitive screen.
- C. Mini-Cog.
- D. Mini-Mental State Examination.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Mini-Cog.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The Mini-Cog (3-item recall + clock draw) takes ~3 minutes, is validated, and is free of copyright restrictions — ideal for time-limited primary care. The MMSE is longer and copyrighted.

## Q8 — Mental Status Examination in Primary Care (p. 51)
Healthy older patient requests dementia screening before a cruise. Which statement is accurate?

- B. Screening includes distracting the patient to test mental agility.

## Q9 — Injections of the Hip and Knee (p. 61)
Which statement about knee injections is correct?

- A. Point-of-care ultrasonography has no advantage over landmark-guided injections.
- B. The superior lateral approach is the most accurate technique.
- C. The patient should be positioned with the knee fully extended.
- D. Knee injections are performed primarily for diagnostic purposes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — The superolateral approach is the most accurate.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The superolateral approach to the suprapatellar pouch has the highest accuracy. Ultrasound guidance *does* improve accuracy (A false); the knee is positioned slightly flexed/supported, not fully extended; injections are mainly therapeutic. ⚠️ Verify against print key.

## Q10 — Injections of the Hip and Knee (p. 61)
61-year-old receives a steroid injection into the right pes anserine bursa. Which aftercare instruction is appropriate?

- A. Return in three days for reevaluation.
- B. A steroid flare typically occurs one to two hours after injection.
- C. Some strenuous activity should be encouraged within the first two days.
- D. Soreness lasting more than 72 hours may be a sign of infection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Soreness >72 hours may indicate infection.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Safety-net instruction: persistent/worsening pain beyond ~72 hours raises concern for septic complication → seek care. A steroid flare occurs at ~24–48 hours (not 1–2), strenuous activity should be *avoided* early, and routine 3-day follow-up isn't needed. ⚠️ Verify against print key.

## Q11 — Medications for Alcohol Use Disorder (p. 71)
Which medication for reducing heavy-drinking days in AUD is supported by the strongest evidence?

- A. Disulfiram.
- B. Oral naltrexone (Revia).
- C. Topiramate.
- D. Varenicline (Chantix).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Oral naltrexone.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Naltrexone (and acamprosate) have the strongest evidence base. Naltrexone particularly reduces heavy-drinking days and craving. Disulfiram requires high adherence; topiramate/varenicline are off-label/second-line.

## Q12 — Medications for Alcohol Use Disorder (p. 71)
Which statement about naltrexone for AUD is correct?

- D. It commonly causes irritability.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — May be combined with acamprosate, topiramate, or gabapentin.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High

## Q15 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: Screening for Syphilis in Nonpregnant Adolescents and Adults (p. 79)
42-year-old MSM, new partner, inconsistent condoms, recent gonorrhea, presenting with sciatic neuritis. Per USPSTF, what is recommended?

- A. Screen for syphilis at this visit because he has symptoms of the infection.
- B. Screen for syphilis at this visit because he is at increased risk and has a new sex partner.
- C. Screen for syphilis at least once every two years.
- D. Do not screen because his partner does not have symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Screen because he is at increased risk with a new partner.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> USPSTF (A recommendation) advises screening *asymptomatic* persons at increased risk (MSM, recent STI, inconsistent condom use). His back pain/sciatica is unrelated to syphilis (so A's premise is wrong). Screening frequency is individualized by ongoing risk, not a fixed 2-year interval.

## Q16 — Practice Guidelines: Diagnosis and Management of Celiac Disease (ACG) (p. 92)
31-year-old already on a gluten-free diet, wants testing for celiac **without** resuming gluten. Per ACG, most appropriate test?

- A. Tissue transglutaminase IgG levels.
- B. Endoscopy with biopsies.
- C. HLA DQ2 and DQ8.
- D. Tissue transglutaminase IgA levels.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — HLA DQ2/DQ8 typing.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Serology (tTG-IgA) and biopsy require active gluten ingestion and become unreliable on a gluten-free diet. HLA DQ2/DQ8 testing is gluten-independent; a *negative* result effectively rules out celiac disease (high negative predictive value).

## Q17 — IAS-USA: Treatment and Prevention of HIV Infection (Online)
28-year-old MSM on daily TDF/FTC (Truvada) PrEP, struggles with daily dosing, no injection drug use. Per IAS-USA, which regimen is recommended?

- A. A double dose 2–24 hours before sexual activity, then a single dose daily until 48 hours after sexual activity.
- B. A double dose starting 48 hours before, then a single dose daily until 72 hours after.
- C. A double dose 24 hours after sexual activity.
- D. A single dose 48 hours before, then maintenance dosing for seven days after.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — On-demand "2-1-1" dosing.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Event-driven PrEP (2-1-1): two pills 2–24 h before sex, one pill 24 h after the first dose, and one pill 48 h after. Validated for cisgender MSM with TDF/FTC; not recommended for cisgender women.
