## Q1 — Back Pain in Children and Adolescents (p. 19)

Which one of the following tests is used to assess for spondylolysis in children?

- A. Stork test.
- B. Patrick (FABER) test.
- C. Slump test.
- D. Thomas test.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Stork test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The single-leg hyperextension ("stork") test reproduces pain in **spondylolysis** (pars defect). FABER tests the SI joint/hip (B); the slump test screens neural tension/radiculopathy (C); the Thomas test assesses hip flexor contracture (D).

## Q2 — Back Pain in Children and Adolescents (p. 19)

Which one of the following imaging modalities is the appropriate initial step for a child with suspected spondylolysis?

- A. Plain radiography with anteroposterior and lateral views.
- B. Plain radiography with oblique view.
- C. Computed tomography.
- D. Bone scan.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Plain radiography with AP and lateral views.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Start with **AP and lateral** plain films. Oblique views add radiation without improving yield (B); CT and MRI/bone scan are reserved for ongoing suspicion with normal radiographs (C, D).

## Q3 — Back Pain in Children and Adolescents (p. 19)

Which one of the following statements about treatments for back pain in children is correct?

- A. Bracing accelerates healing in children with spondylolysis.
- B. Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation can worsen outcomes in patients with acute fractures.
- C. Pilates is potentially helpful for improving quality of life in patients with juvenile idiopathic arthritis.
- D. Patients with suspected syringomyelia can generally be treated conservatively.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pilates may improve quality of life in juvenile idiopathic arthritis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Bracing has **not** been shown to speed spondylolysis healing (A wrong); TENS doesn't worsen fracture outcomes (B wrong); suspected **syringomyelia** is a red flag needing imaging/referral, not conservative care (D wrong).

## Q4 — Blood Product Transfusion in Adults (p. 30)

A 67-year-old woman with osteoarthritis is admitted for lower-extremity cellulitis. Her hemoglobin is 7.5 g/dL and her leg is painful. She is otherwise asymptomatic. Which one of the following statements about a possible RBC transfusion is correct?

- A. She should be offered a transfusion because her hemoglobin level is less than 8 g/dL.
- B. She should be offered a transfusion because her hemoglobin level is less than 10 g/dL.
- C. There is no indication for transfusion at this time.
- D. Transfusing 1 unit of RBCs should raise her hemoglobin level to more than 9.5 g/dL.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — No indication for transfusion at this time.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The restrictive threshold for a stable, asymptomatic patient is **7 g/dL**; at 7.5 with no cardiac symptoms she doesn't meet it (A, B wrong). One unit raises hemoglobin ~**1 g/dL** (to ~8.5, not >9.5 — D wrong).

## Q5 — Blood Product Transfusion in Adults (p. 30)

Which one of the following blood product modifications can prevent transfusion-associated graft-versus-host disease?

- A. Leukoreduction.
- B. Irradiation.
- C. Washing.
- D. Volume reduction.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Irradiation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Irradiation** inactivates donor T lymphocytes, preventing TA-GVHD in immunocompromised recipients. Leukoreduction reduces febrile reactions/CMV/HLA-alloimmunization (A); washing removes plasma proteins (C); neither prevents GVHD.

## Q6 — Blood Product Transfusion in Adults (p. 30)

A 37-year-old healthy woman received 2 units of blood for postoperative hemorrhage. Three hours later she developed fever, dyspnea, and bilateral infiltrates; transfusion-related acute lung injury is diagnosed. Which one of the following is the most appropriate initial therapy?

- A. Intravenous diuretics.
- B. Intravenous steroids.
- C. Immunosuppressants.
- D. Supportive care.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Supportive care.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> TRALI is treated with **supportive/respiratory care** (oxygen, ventilation as needed); it usually resolves in 48–96 hours. Diuretics are for circulatory overload (TACO), not TRALI (A); steroids/immunosuppressants aren't effective (B, C).

## Q7 — Cervical Colposcopy: Indications and Risk Assessment (p. 39)

Which one of the following statements about cervical cancer is correct?

- A. The most common type is squamous cell carcinoma of the cervix.
- B. The most common type is adenocarcinoma of the cervix.
- C. The link between human papillomavirus (HPV) infection and cancer is weaker for squamous cell carcinoma compared with adenocarcinoma.
- D. There is no association between squamous cell carcinoma of the cervix and HPV.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Squamous cell carcinoma is the most common type.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SCC accounts for ~70% of cervical cancers (adenocarcinoma is less common, B wrong). **HPV drives both** histologies (C, D wrong) — persistent high-risk HPV (16/18) is the key carcinogen.

## Q8 — Cervical Colposcopy (p. 39)

Which one of the following patients is at highest risk of cervical cancer?

- A. A 50-year-old with a normal Pap result and high-risk HPV (not HPV-16 or HPV-18).
- B. A 29-year-old with a Pap showing a low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion.
- C. A 30-year-old with a Pap showing a high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL) who also has HPV-16.
- D. A 22-year-old with a Pap showing atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance and high-risk HPV (subtype unknown).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The 30-year-old with HSIL plus HPV-16.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **HSIL + HPV-16** carries the highest risk of CIN 3+/cancer. LSIL and ASC-US carry lower immediate risk (B, D); a normal Pap with non-16/18 high-risk HPV is the lowest of these (A).

## Q9 — Cervical Colposcopy (p. 39)

Which one of the following options is appropriate for a 45-year-old woman with HSIL Pap results who also tests positive for HPV-18?

- A. Hysterectomy if the patient does not desire fertility.
- B. Colposcopy with loop electrosurgical excision procedure at the time of colposcopy.
- C. Repeat cytology and HPV testing in six months.
- D. Cryotherapy with repeat cytology and HPV testing in 12 months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Colposcopy with loop electrosurgical excision (LEEP) at the time of colposcopy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> HSIL + HPV-16/18 has a high enough CIN 3+ risk that **immediate excisional treatment ("see-and-treat" colposcopy/LEEP)** is acceptable. Hysterectomy is not first-line for preinvasive disease (A); deferring with repeat testing is inappropriate for HSIL (C, D).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Role of Mucolytics in Chronic Bronchitis or COPD (p. 16)

A 55-year-old with two COPD hospitalizations in the past year is on a LAMA and a LABA. Which one of the following statements about adding mucolytics is correct?

- A. They significantly increase the forced vital capacity.
- B. They should be used cautiously because of the significant risk of adverse effects.
- C. They reduce hospitalizations and days of disability.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Mucolytics reduce hospitalizations and days of disability.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In chronic bronchitis/COPD, mucolytics modestly reduce exacerbations, hospitalizations, and disability days. They do **not** meaningfully change FVC (A wrong) and are **well tolerated** (B wrong).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Nicotine Replacement Therapy for Smoking Cessation (p. 17)

A 61-year-old wants to quit smoking. Which one of the following treatments is most likely to be effective?

- A. Nicotine gum, 2 mg every two hours as needed.
- B. Nicotine lozenge, 2 mg every two hours as needed.
- C. Nicotine patch plus any short-acting nicotine agent.
- D. All of the above are equally effective.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A nicotine patch combined with a short-acting agent.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Combination NRT** (long-acting patch for baseline + a short-acting gum/lozenge for cravings) is more effective than any single form. Single short-acting agents alone (A, B) are less effective.

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: ADHD — AAP Updated Guideline (p. 58)

A five-year-old girl is having difficulty concentrating in kindergarten and her mother worries about ADHD. According to the AAP, which one of the following is the best next step?

- A. Reassure the mother that her child is too young to be diagnosed with ADHD.
- B. Start low-dose methylphenidate (Ritalin) and reassess in one week.
- C. Recommend parent training in behavior management.
- D. Recommend training interventions to improve organization and interpersonal skills.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Recommend parent training in behavior management.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For preschool/kindergarten-aged children (4–5 years), **behavioral parent training is first-line** before medication. ADHD **can** be diagnosed at this age (A wrong); methylphenidate is added only if behavioral therapy is insufficient (B); organizational training suits older children (D).
