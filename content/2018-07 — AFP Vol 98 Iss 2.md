## Q1 — Stimulant and Designer Drug Use: Primary Care Management (p. 85)

For how long is cocaine typically detectable in the urine?

- A. Less than 24 hours.
- B. Two to three days.
- C. Four to seven days.
- D. One to two weeks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Two to three days.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cocaine (its metabolite benzoylecgonine) is usually detectable for **~2–3 days** (longer with heavy/chronic use).

## Q2 — Stimulant and Designer Drug Use (p. 85)

Which one of the following statements about treatment of cocaine use disorder is correct?

- A. Anticonvulsants are effective for long-term abstinence.
- B. Antidepressants are effective for long-term abstinence.
- C. Psychosocial therapy is effective for short-term abstinence.
- D. Auricular acupuncture is effective for short-term abstinence.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Psychosocial therapy helps achieve short-term abstinence.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> No medication is reliably effective for cocaine use disorder — anticonvulsants and antidepressants don't sustain abstinence (A, B), and acupuncture is ineffective (D). **Psychosocial/behavioral** interventions are the mainstay.

## Q3 — Stimulant and Designer Drug Use (p. 85)

Which one of the following statements about synthetic cannabinoids is correct?

- A. Withdrawal symptoms may include headaches, anxiety, depression, and irritability.
- B. Antidepressants are effective for long-term treatment.
- C. They have the same chemical structure as marijuana-derived cannabinoids.
- D. They are easily detectable with drug tests.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Withdrawal can cause headaches, anxiety, depression, and irritability.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Synthetic cannabinoids ("K2/Spice") are **structurally different** and far more potent than THC (C wrong), are **not detected** by routine assays (D wrong), and have no proven drug treatment (B wrong).

## Q4 — Top POEMs of 2017 (Choosing Wisely) (p. 93)

Which one of the following statements about musculoskeletal conditions is correct?

- A. Arthroscopic meniscectomy for a degenerative tear is superior to nonoperative therapy.
- B. Radial extracorporeal shock wave therapy improves subacromial shoulder pain when added to supervised exercise therapy.
- C. The American Academy of Family Physicians and the American College of Physicians recommend treating adults who have osteoporosis and no known fragility fracture with a bisphosphonate for no more than five years.
- D. In persons with acute low back pain, adding diazepam (Valium) to naproxen improves disability scores.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — AAFP/ACP: treat osteoporosis (no prior fragility fracture) with a bisphosphonate for no more than five years.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Arthroscopic meniscectomy is **not superior** to conservative care for degenerative tears (A); shock wave therapy adds no benefit (B); **adding diazepam to naproxen doesn't help** acute LBP (D wrong).

## Q5 — Top POEMs of 2017 (p. 93)

In patients with nonpurulent uncomplicated cellulitis, adding trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole to cephalexin (Keflex) results in which one of the following benefits compared with cephalexin alone?

- A. No additional benefits.
- B. Eradication of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus.
- C. Higher clinical cure rates.
- D. Improved mortality.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — No additional benefit.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Nonpurulent cellulitis is usually **streptococcal**, so adding MRSA coverage (TMP-SMX) to cephalexin doesn't improve cure rates — a Choosing Wisely "less is more" point.

## Q6 — Top POEMs of 2017 (p. 93)

Which one of the following statements about primary prevention of cardiovascular disease (CVD) in select high-risk patients 40 to 75 years of age is correct?

- A. Most individuals younger than 40 years will have an estimated 10-year CVD risk greater than 10% using the Framingham calculator.
- B. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends that at least two CVD risk factors be present before starting a low- to moderate-dose statin.
- C. Niacin is indicated in addition to statin therapy.
- D. A post-hoc analysis of the lipid-lowering trial component of the Antihypertensive and Lipid-Lowering Treatment to Prevent Heart Attack Trial showed no benefit to statins for primary prevention in persons 65 to 74 years of age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — ALLHAT-LLT post-hoc analysis found no statin primary-prevention benefit in adults 65–74.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Few people <40 reach >10% 10-year risk (A); the USPSTF criteria are ≥1 risk factor **plus** a calculated ≥10% risk, not simply "two risk factors" (B); **niacin adds no benefit** to statins (C wrong).

## Q7 — Delivering Bad or Life-Altering News (p. 99)

Which one of the following statements about patient preferences for receiving bad news is correct?

- A. Older patients generally want to hear more detailed information about their diagnosis.
- B. Patients' ethnicity does not affect how they prefer to receive bad news.
- C. Patients want to hear simple, clear explanations that they can understand instead of medical jargon.
- D. Patients prefer that physicians overestimate survival so that they are more hopeful about possible treatment and cure.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Patients want simple, clear, jargon-free explanations.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Older patients often prefer **less** detail (A); **culture/ethnicity does influence** preferences (B wrong); patients want **honest, realistic** prognoses, not over-optimistic ones (D wrong).

## Q8 — Delivering Bad or Life-Altering News (p. 99)

Which one of the following is a common theme among the models used for guiding the delivery of bad news?

- A. Physicians should provide detailed medical information regarding the patient's prognosis.
- B. Physicians should work to establish rapport with the patient.
- C. The setting in which the news is delivered is less important than giving the bad news as quickly as possible.
- D. Physicians should encourage the patient to take a few days to process the news before asking questions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Establishing rapport with the patient.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Frameworks like **SPIKES** emphasize rapport, a private **setting** (so C is wrong), assessing what the patient wants to know (not dumping all detail, A), and inviting questions (D wrong).

## Q9 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Behavioral Counseling to Prevent Skin Cancer (p. 105)

A 25-year-old Irish American woman with fair skin, red hair, freckles, a history of tanning bed use, and multiple sunburns presents before a Caribbean vacation. Based on the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommendation, which one of the following approaches to skin cancer counseling would you consider for her?

- A. She should be counseled because the benefit of counseling in adults with fair skin types substantially outweighs the harms.
- B. She should be considered for counseling because the benefits and harms are similar in magnitude for all adults.
- C. She should be considered for counseling because of her risk factors for skin cancer.
- D. She should not be counseled because there are known harms from counseling, and there is a lack of evidence that counseling affects sun-protection behaviors.
- E. She should be counseled because the studies evaluating the financial impacts of counseling confirmed that it is not only noninvasive but also an inexpensive method of preventing melanoma.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Consider counseling because of her skin cancer risk factors.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends behavioral counseling for **fair-skinned people aged 6 months–24 years** (and individualized for adults >24 based on risk factors). At 25 with strong risk factors, counseling is reasonable; the benefit isn't uniform across all adults (A, B overstate).

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: Hepatitis B Virus Vaccination and Screening — ACP and CDC (p. 121)

Which one of the following statements about hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection is correct?

- A. Persons diagnosed with HBV infection should be monitored for hepatocellular carcinoma.
- B. Screening for HBV is warranted for all international travelers.
- C. Adverse effects from HBV vaccination are common.
- D. Pregnant women require screening for HBV infection only if they are at increased risk (e.g., injection drug user, treated for a sexually transmitted infection).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Monitor patients with chronic HBV for hepatocellular carcinoma.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Travelers are screened/vaccinated based on **destination/risk**, not universally (B); HBV vaccine adverse effects are **uncommon/mild** (C); **all pregnant women** are screened for HBsAg (D wrong).
