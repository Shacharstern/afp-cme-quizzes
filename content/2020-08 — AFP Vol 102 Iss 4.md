## Q1 — House Calls (p. 211)

Which one of the following strategies is recommended to improve the efficiency of house calls?

- A. Schedule the visit during the lunch hour.
- B. Conduct visits to the same area over several days.
- C. Have only one member of the care team be responsible for visits to any given patient.
- D. Use a template for documentation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Use a template for documentation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A structured template streamlines documentation and ensures completeness. Efficiency improves by **clustering visits geographically on the same day** (not over several days, B) and using a team approach (C wrong).

## Q2 — House Calls (p. 211)

Which one of the following is recommended to ensure physician safety during a house call?

- A. When calling to remind a patient of the visit, provide only an estimated time of arrival.
- B. Wear a white coat during house calls.
- C. Use alert codes to notify designated persons of emergencies.
- D. Avoid traveling with a medical student or other team member in unsafe areas.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Use alert codes to notify designated persons of emergencies.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Pre-arranged "duress" codes let a clinician discreetly summon help. Safety is improved by traveling **with** a colleague in unsafe areas (D wrong) and a white coat is not a safety measure (B).

## Q3 — House Calls (p. 211)

Which one of the following statements about house calls is correct?

- A. Many direct primary care and concierge practices routinely offer house calls.
- B. House calls do not decrease 30-day readmissions.
- C. Grab bars and shower chairs are typically covered by insurance.
- D. Patients with terminal cancer generally prefer to die in the hospital.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Many direct primary care and concierge practices routinely offer house calls.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> House calls **reduce** readmissions (B wrong); most home-safety equipment (grab bars, shower chairs) is **not** typically covered (C wrong); terminally ill patients generally prefer to die **at home** (D wrong).

## Q4 — Paget Disease of Bone for Primary Care (p. 224)

Which one of the following statements about the clinical presentation of Paget disease of bone is correct?

- A. The disease is usually diagnosed early in its course.
- B. The initial presentation in symptomatic individuals is pain.
- C. Symptoms often improve at night.
- D. Symptoms are usually relieved by rest.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — In symptomatic patients, the initial presentation is pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most cases are found **incidentally** (elevated alkaline phosphatase or x-ray), so diagnosis is often late (A wrong). When symptomatic, **bone pain** predominates — and it characteristically **worsens** at night and with rest (C, D wrong).

## Q5 — Paget Disease of Bone (p. 224)

Which one of the following statements about treatment approaches to metabolically active Paget disease of bone is correct?

- A. It should be treated with the anabolic bone agent teriparatide (Forteo).
- B. Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates are contraindicated.
- C. A single dose of intravenous zoledronic acid (Reclast) is the treatment of choice.
- D. Treatment with antipagetic agents is curative.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A single IV dose of zoledronic acid is the treatment of choice.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Potent **nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates** (especially one IV zoledronic acid infusion) are first-line and produce durable remission (B reversed). Teriparatide is not used (A); therapy controls but does **not cure** (D wrong).

## Q6 — Paget Disease of Bone (p. 224)

Which one of the following approaches to monitoring initial treatment response in patients with metabolically active Paget disease of bone is most appropriate?

- A. Measurement of total serum alkaline phosphatase.
- B. Evaluation of urinary cross-linked N-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen.
- C. Evaluation of procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide.
- D. No laboratory evaluation is helpful.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Total serum alkaline phosphatase.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Total ALP is the inexpensive, recommended marker to track treatment response in typical Paget disease. Specialized bone-turnover markers (B, C) are reserved for cases with confounding liver disease or focal monostotic disease.

## Q7 — Henoch-Schönlein Purpura (IgA Vasculitis): Rapid Evidence Review (p. 229)

Which one of the following statements about the diagnosis of IgA vasculitis is correct?

- A. Abnormal levels of IgA are required for the diagnosis.
- B. Risk of long-term renal complications is highest in children younger than 10 years.
- C. The characteristic rash may start as erythematous papules that develop into crops of petechiae and palpable purpura.
- D. Joint involvement and abdominal pain are more common in adults than children.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The rash may begin as erythematous papules evolving into petechiae and palpable purpura.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Diagnosis is **clinical** — serum IgA need not be abnormal (A wrong). Renal complications are worse in **older children/adults** (B wrong); joint and GI involvement are more common in **children** (D wrong).

## Q8 — Henoch-Schönlein Purpura (p. 229)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of IgA vasculitis is correct?

- A. Severe abdominal pain that does not improve with supportive care and NSAIDs should be treated with systemic corticosteroids.
- B. A rash should be treated with a corticosteroid cream.
- C. Corticosteroids have shown benefit in preventing renal disease over placebo.
- D. Spontaneous improvement in IgA vasculitis is uncommon.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Severe abdominal pain refractory to supportive care/NSAIDs warrants systemic corticosteroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most IgA vasculitis is **self-limited** (D wrong); the rash needs no topical steroid (B wrong); steroids do **not** prevent nephritis (C wrong) but **do** help severe GI pain or arthritis.

## Q9 — Henoch-Schönlein Purpura (p. 229)

Which one of the following statements about the signs and symptoms of IgA vasculitis is correct?

- A. Abdominal pain is rarely severe.
- B. Gastrointestinal bleeding is rare, occurring in less than 5% of patients.
- C. Low-grade fever and fatigue are common.
- D. Headaches and behavior changes are common.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Low-grade fever and fatigue are common.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Constitutional symptoms accompany the tetrad (purpura, arthralgia, abdominal pain, renal disease). Abdominal pain **can be severe** (intussusception risk) and GI bleeding is **not** rare (A, B wrong); CNS symptoms are uncommon (D wrong).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Prevention and Cessation of Tobacco Use in Children and Adolescents (p. 241)

According to the USPSTF recommendations, which one of the following populations should be provided with information to prevent initiation of tobacco use?

- A. Children and adolescents at increased risk for tobacco use because they live in a rural area.
- B. Adolescent White males because they have the highest smoking rates.
- C. School-aged children at increased risk of tobacco use because they have parents who smoke.
- D. Adolescents at increased risk because they have friends who vape.
- E. All youth, whether they have risk factors for tobacco use or not.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **E — All school-aged children and adolescents, regardless of risk factors.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF **B** recommendation is to provide tobacco-prevention interventions to **all** youth — not just those with specific risk factors — because initiation is unpredictable and prevention is broadly effective.

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Colorectal Cancer Screening — ACP Guidance Statements (p. 250)

According to the ACP, which one of the following statements about fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) in patients at average risk of colorectal cancer is correct?

- A. The risk of false-positive results is higher than with guaiac fecal occult blood testing (FOBT), leading to more unnecessary colonoscopies.
- B. The risk of false-negative results is higher than with guaiac FOBT, leading to more missed cancers.
- C. Multiple samples are required for FIT.
- D. Dietary restrictions are required before obtaining the sample for FIT.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — FIT yields more positives than guaiac FOBT, leading to more follow-up colonoscopies.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Because FIT is **more sensitive**, it produces more positive results and therefore more downstream colonoscopies. It requires only a **single sample** (C wrong) and **no dietary restrictions** (D wrong); it has **fewer** false negatives, not more (B wrong). *(Teaching nuance: FIT is generally also more specific than guaiac; "false positives" here reflects the higher overall positive/colonoscopy rate. Confirmed by official key.)*

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Colorectal Cancer Screening — BMJ Rapid Recommendation (p. 253)

A 60-year-old at average risk for CRC has an estimated 15-year CRC risk of 4% using the QCancer calculator. According to the BMJ/MAGIC Group, which one of the following statements about screening this patient is correct?

- A. CRC screening is not recommended.
- B. Colonoscopy is the only screening method that will reduce CRC mortality in this patient.
- C. If the patient wants to avoid colonoscopy, annual fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) provides similar mortality benefit.
- D. Biennial FIT is the best way to reduce CRC incidence.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Annual FIT offers a similar mortality benefit for patients who prefer to avoid colonoscopy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The BMJ/MAGIC rapid recommendation supports screening at this risk level and treats **annual FIT, colonoscopy, sigmoidoscopy, and FIT** as acceptable options with comparable benefit — colonoscopy is not the only effective method (A, B, D wrong).
