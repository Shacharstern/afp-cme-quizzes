## Q1 — Shoulder Dystocia: Managing an Obstetric Emergency (p. 84)

Which one of the following statements about risk factors for shoulder dystocia is correct?

- A. Fetal macrosomia has little relationship to the incidence of shoulder dystocia.
- B. Women with a history of shoulder dystocia have more than an eightfold risk of shoulder dystocia in future deliveries.
- C. Gestational diabetes mellitus, but not preexisting maternal diabetes, is associated with shoulder dystocia.
- D. Low maternal body mass index is a significant risk factor for shoulder dystocia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A prior shoulder dystocia confers more than an eightfold recurrence risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Macrosomia strongly raises risk (A wrong); **both** gestational and pregestational diabetes increase risk (C wrong); **high**, not low, maternal BMI is a risk factor (D wrong). Still, most shoulder dystocias occur without identifiable risk factors.

## Q2 — Shoulder Dystocia (p. 84)

A 24-year-old primigravida is in labor. After delivery of the fetal head, gentle downward traction does not deliver the anterior shoulder. Which one of the following statements about delivery is correct?

- A. Attempting at least one maneuver before calling this a shoulder dystocia is recommended to prevent maternal distress.
- B. A single preferred algorithm of maneuvers is supported by definitive research findings.
- C. Flexing the maternal hips and bringing both knees toward her chest (the McRoberts maneuver) is the most widely recommended first maneuver to aid delivery.
- D. Delivery of the posterior arm starts with sliding a hand along the baby's back until the posterior hand is located.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The McRoberts maneuver is the recommended first maneuver.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> McRoberts (± suprapubic pressure) resolves most dystocias and is first-line. Recognize/announce the dystocia **immediately** (A wrong); no single algorithm is definitively superior (B wrong); posterior-arm delivery follows the **anterior** aspect to the antecubital fossa (D wrong).

## Q3 — Shoulder Dystocia (p. 84)

Which one of the following statements about complications of shoulder dystocia is correct?

- A. Postpartum hemorrhage is the most common complication of shoulder dystocia.
- B. Brachial plexus injuries noted immediately after birth rarely resolve.
- C. The time from delivery of the head to delivery of the body is linearly associated with increased risk of fetal asphyxia and death.
- D. Fundal pressure during shoulder dystocia is not associated with fetal or maternal complications.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Postpartum hemorrhage is the most common complication.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most **brachial plexus injuries resolve** (B wrong); the head-to-body interval is not simply linear with asphyxia (C wrong); **fundal pressure is harmful** (worsens impaction, uterine rupture) and should be avoided (D wrong).

## Q4 — Syphilis: Far from Ancient History (p. 91)

Which one of the following statements about screening for syphilis is correct?

- A. Patients with HIV infection should be screened annually.
- B. Men 30 years and older should be screened more often than younger men.
- C. Pregnant women should be screened for syphilis at the first prenatal visit.
- D. Patients with a history of syphilis are immune and do not need to be screened.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Screen all pregnant women at the first prenatal visit.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Universal first-visit screening (repeated in the third trimester/at delivery for high-risk) prevents congenital syphilis. HIV patients are screened **at least annually, often more** (A understates); prior infection confers **no immunity** (D wrong).

## Q5 — Syphilis (p. 91)

Which one of the following statements about the stages of untreated syphilis is correct?

- A. Untreated infections rarely progress beyond the secondary stage.
- B. All stages present with lesions on the palms and soles.
- C. Patients in the early latent stage may redevelop symptoms of secondary syphilis.
- D. Neurosyphilis develops only during the tertiary stage.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Early latent patients may relapse with secondary-stage symptoms.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Untreated infection can progress to tertiary disease (A wrong); the palm/sole rash is characteristic of **secondary** syphilis only (B wrong); **neurosyphilis can occur at any stage** (D wrong).

## Q6 — Syphilis (p. 91)

Which one of the following statements about testing for syphilis is correct?

- A. A positive rapid plasma reagin (RPR) result should be followed by a quantitative titer.
- B. Nontreponemal test results remain positive even after successful treatment.
- C. The false-positive rate of RPR testing is zero.
- D. Nontreponemal test results usually become positive within 48 hours of primary chancre development.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A positive RPR should be confirmed/followed by a quantitative titer.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Titers track treatment response. Nontreponemal tests usually **decline/serorevert** after cure (B wrong); RPR has **false positives** (pregnancy, autoimmune, infections — C wrong) and takes **1–4 weeks** to turn positive (D wrong).

## Q7 — Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (p. 99)

Current guidelines recommend screening for asymptomatic bacteriuria in which one of the following patients?

- A. A middle-aged man with a long-term indwelling catheter.
- B. A middle-aged woman with diabetes mellitus.
- C. An older man scheduled for diagnostic cystoscopy.
- D. A pregnant woman in the first trimester.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A pregnant woman in the first trimester.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Screening (and treating) ASB is recommended **only in pregnancy** and before urologic procedures with expected **mucosal bleeding** (diagnostic cystoscopy alone doesn't qualify, C). It's not screened in catheterized patients or diabetics (A, B).

## Q8 — Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (p. 99)

Which one of the following statements about patients with indwelling catheters is correct?

- A. Guidelines recommend treating asymptomatic bacteriuria if it is present at the time of catheter removal.
- B. If asymptomatic bacteriuria is present, it should be treated based on results of culture and sensitivity testing.
- C. Urinary tract infections are common in those with long-term, but not short-term, catheters.
- D. Virtually all patients with long-term indwelling catheters will develop bacteriuria.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Virtually all long-term indwelling catheter patients develop bacteriuria.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Because colonization is near-universal, **asymptomatic** catheter bacteriuria should **not** be treated (A, B wrong); catheter-associated UTIs occur with both short- and long-term catheters (C wrong).

## Q9 — Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (p. 99)

A patient with dementia in a long-term care facility develops an acute change in mental status over 24 hours, with confusion and fluctuating orientation. Vital signs are normal and there are no urinary symptoms. Which one of the following would be the next step in his evaluation?

- A. Obtain a clean-catch urine specimen to rule out infection.
- B. Obtain a catheterized urine specimen to rule out infection.
- C. Evaluate the patient for other causes of delirium.
- D. Begin empiric antimicrobial therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Evaluate for other causes of delirium.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Without urinary symptoms or systemic signs, mental-status change should **not** be attributed to a UTI. Reflexively culturing/treating leads to overtreatment of asymptomatic bacteriuria — look for medications, metabolic, and other delirium causes first.

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: Community-Acquired Pneumonia — ATS/IDSA (p. 121)

A 51-year-old man with no history of resistant infections or recent illness has community-acquired pneumonia and no criteria for severe infection. Based on ATS/IDSA recommendations, which one of the following tests would be most helpful in guiding treatment?

- A. Influenza nucleic acid amplification test when influenza is known to be circulating in the community.
- B. Blood culture.
- C. Urinary antigen testing for Legionella or pneumococcus.
- D. Sputum culture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Influenza NAAT when influenza is circulating.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In **non-severe** outpatient CAP, routine blood cultures, sputum cultures, and urinary antigens are **not** recommended (B, C, D); influenza testing during flu season changes management (antivirals/isolation).
