## Q1 — Diets for Health: Goals and Guidelines (p. 721)

Which one of the following recommendations is consistent with the Harvard University Healthy Eating Plate guidelines?

- A. Any nonalcoholic beverage is considered healthy.
- B. One-fourth of your plate at each meal should be fruits and vegetables.
- C. One-half of your plate at each meal should be whole grains.
- D. One-fourth of your plate at each meal should be healthy protein.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — One-fourth of the plate should be healthy protein.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Healthy Eating Plate is **½ vegetables/fruits, ¼ whole grains, ¼ healthy protein** (so B and C have the wrong fractions); water is the recommended beverage, not "any nonalcoholic" drink (A).

## Q2 — Diets for Health (p. 721)

Which one of the following statements about legumes is correct?

- A. Consuming as little as four servings per week has been shown to decrease the risk of cardiovascular disease.
- B. They are high in insoluble fiber.
- C. Eating them is associated with a slight increase in colorectal polyp recurrence.
- D. They typically have a high glycemic index.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Four servings of legumes per week lowers cardiovascular disease risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Legumes are high in **soluble** fiber (B wrong), have a **low** glycemic index (D wrong), and **reduce** (not increase) polyp recurrence (C wrong).

## Q3 — Diets for Health (p. 721)

Which one of the following dietary recommendations about beverages is correct?

- A. The type and quantity of beverages have minimal impact on patients' overall diet quality and caloric intake.
- B. Unsweetened green tea has been associated with decreased levels of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol.
- C. Fruit juices are generally as healthy as water.
- D. Patients who do not drink alcoholic beverages should be encouraged to add a moderate amount of red wine to their diets.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Unsweetened green tea is associated with lower LDL cholesterol.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Beverages substantially affect calories/diet quality (A wrong); **fruit juice is not equivalent to water** (sugar load, C wrong); nondrinkers should **not** be encouraged to start alcohol (D wrong).

## Q4 — Hyperhidrosis: Management Options (p. 729)

Which one of the following supports the diagnosis of primary hyperhidrosis?

- A. Visible and excessive sweating that awakens the patient from sleep.
- B. Visible and excessive sweating that has an asymmetric presentation.
- C. Onset of visible and excessive sweating occurs after 25 years of age.
- D. Visible and excessive sweating that is present for more than six months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Excessive sweating present for more than six months.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Primary (focal) hyperhidrosis is chronic (>6 months), **symmetric**, daytime, and starts **before age 25**. **Night sweats, asymmetry, and late onset** are red flags for a **secondary** cause (A, B, C).

## Q5 — Hyperhidrosis (p. 729)

A 23-year-old woman presents with mild underarm sweating that has occurred at least weekly for many years. Which one of the following is the most appropriate initial treatment?

- A. Iontophoresis.
- B. Aluminum chloride (Drysol).
- C. Topical glycopyrrolate.
- D. Oral oxybutynin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Topical aluminum chloride.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Topical **aluminum chloride** antiperspirant is first-line for focal hyperhidrosis. Iontophoresis, topical anticholinergics, oral anticholinergics, and botulinum toxin are later steps (A, C, D).

## Q6 — Hyperhidrosis (p. 729)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of hyperhidrosis is correct?

- A. Botulinum toxin injections should be used only in the most severe cases.
- B. Radical excision of the sweat glands is recommended for craniofacial hyperhidrosis.
- C. A trial of tap water iontophoresis is appropriate for plantar symptoms.
- D. Use of iontophoresis is limited because it must be performed in the physician's office.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Tap-water iontophoresis is appropriate for palmar/plantar hyperhidrosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Botulinum toxin is used across severities (A overstated); radical gland excision isn't recommended for craniofacial sweating (B); **home iontophoresis devices exist**, so it isn't office-restricted (D wrong).

## Q7 — Oncologic Emergencies: Recognition and Initial Management (p. 741)

Which one of the following conditions is a common complication of chemotherapy?

- A. Hypercalcemia of malignancy.
- B. Superior vena cava syndrome.
- C. Febrile neutropenia.
- D. Malignant epidural spinal cord compression.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Febrile neutropenia.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Febrile neutropenia is a common, life-threatening **chemotherapy** complication requiring urgent empiric antibiotics. The others result from the **tumor** itself, not chemotherapy (A, B, D).

## Q8 — Oncologic Emergencies (p. 741)

A patient with breast cancer presents with weakness and anorexia, and a calcium level of 11 mg/dL. The optimal treatment approach includes which one of the following?

- A. Rehydration and furosemide (Lasix).
- B. Plasmapheresis.
- C. Antibiotics.
- D. Pericardiocentesis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Aggressive IV rehydration (and furosemide if needed) for hypercalcemia of malignancy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Hypercalcemia is treated with **IV normal saline** (± loop diuretic once volume-replete) plus **bisphosphonates/calcitonin**. Plasmapheresis (hyperviscosity), antibiotics (infection), and pericardiocentesis (tamponade) treat other emergencies (B, C, D).

## Q9 — Oncologic Emergencies (p. 741)

A patient with a history of esophageal cancer presents acutely with dyspnea, chest pain, and palpitations. Which one of the following findings is most likely associated with these symptoms?

- A. Testing positive for Clostridium difficile infection.
- B. Low-amplitude wave forms and electrical alternans on electrocardiography.
- C. Elevated uric acid and electrolyte levels.
- D. Serum viscosity greater than 4 cP.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Low-voltage QRS with electrical alternans (pericardial effusion/tamponade).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The presentation suggests **malignant pericardial effusion/cardiac tamponade** — classic ECG findings are low voltage and electrical alternans. Elevated uric acid/electrolytes suggest tumor lysis (C); high viscosity suggests hyperviscosity syndrome (D).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Cerebrospinal Fluid Biomarkers for Detection of Alzheimer Disease in Patients with Mild Cognitive Impairment (p. 714)

Which one of the following statements about the use of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers to predict progression to Alzheimer disease in patients with mild cognitive impairment is correct?

- A. CSF p-tau:β-amyloid ratio is the best for ruling in conversion to Alzheimer disease.
- B. CSF t-tau:β-amyloid ratio is the best for ruling out conversion to Alzheimer disease.
- C. There is insufficient evidence to support the routine use of CSF biomarkers to detect progression to Alzheimer disease.
- D. CSF biomarkers have better specificity than sensitivity, with little risk of overdiagnosis or overtreatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Insufficient evidence to support routine CSF biomarker use for predicting progression.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The biomarkers lack the accuracy for routine clinical prediction; their **modest specificity raises overdiagnosis concerns** (D wrong), and no single ratio reliably rules conversion in or out (A, B overstated).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Atypical Antipsychotics for Disruptive Behavior Disorders in Children and Adolescents (p. 715)

Which one of the following statements about the use of risperidone (Risperdal) for patients with oppositional defiant disorder is correct?

- A. It decreases aggression but does not improve conduct.
- B. It improves conduct but does not reduce aggression.
- C. Adverse effects include an average weight gain of 2 to 2.5 kg (4.4 to 5.5 lb).
- D. Guidelines from the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence recommend the routine use of psychotropic medications such as risperidone for disruptive behavior disorders in children and adolescents.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Risperidone causes an average weight gain of about 2 to 2.5 kg.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Risperidone reduces **both** aggression and conduct problems (A, B wrong), but its metabolic adverse effects (weight gain) are significant — and **NICE does not recommend routine** psychotropic use for disruptive behavior disorders (D wrong); behavioral interventions are first-line.

## Q12 — Point-of-Care Guides: Ruling Out Pulmonary Embolism in the Primary Care Setting (p. 750)

Which one of the following statements about clinical decision rules for the diagnosis of pulmonary embolism is correct?

- A. The Wells and Geneva scores are preferred in the primary care setting.
- B. None of the rules have been studied in the primary care setting.
- C. The Wells and Geneva scores both require a subjective judgment by the physician.
- D. The Wells and Geneva scores both require an electrocardiogram.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — The Wells and Geneva scores are preferred in primary care.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Both have been validated in primary care (B wrong). The **Geneva score is fully objective** (no clinical-gestalt item), unlike Wells (C wrong), and neither requires an ECG (D wrong).

## Q13 — Practice Guidelines: Treatment of Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis — AAAAI/ACAAI Joint Task Force on Practice Parameters (p. 756)

Which one of the following treatments is preferred for seasonal allergic rhinitis symptoms in patients 12 years or older?

- A. Combination therapy with intranasal corticosteroids and oral antihistamines.
- B. Monotherapy with montelukast (Singulair).
- C. Monotherapy with intranasal antihistamines.
- D. Monotherapy with intranasal corticosteroids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Monotherapy with intranasal corticosteroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For patients ≥12, **intranasal corticosteroid monotherapy is preferred** over adding an oral antihistamine (no added benefit, A) and over montelukast (B). Intranasal antihistamines are an alternative but generally less effective than intranasal steroids (C).
