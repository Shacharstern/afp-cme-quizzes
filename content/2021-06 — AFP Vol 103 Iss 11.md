## Q1 — Tinnitus: Diagnosis and Management (p. 663)

In a patient with tinnitus, which one of the following indicates that imaging is needed regardless of the absence of other findings?

- A. Duration greater than six months.
- B. Unilateral location.
- C. Symmetric hearing loss.
- D. Nonpulsatile nature.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Unilateral location.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Unilateral (or pulsatile) tinnitus and asymmetric hearing loss are red flags for retrocochlear pathology (e.g., vestibular schwannoma) → MRI. Symmetric loss and chronicity alone do not mandate imaging.

## Q2 — Tinnitus (p. 663)

Which one of the following is most likely to identify the cause of pulsatile tinnitus?

- A. Tinnitus Handicap Inventory.
- B. Audiometry.
- C. Laboratory testing for thyroid disease.
- D. Temporal-bone computed tomography without contrast media.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Temporal-bone CT without contrast.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Pulsatile tinnitus is usually vascular/structural; temporal-bone CT (± CT/MR angiography) identifies causes such as glomus tumors, dural fistulas, and dehiscence. The THI quantifies impact, not cause.

## Q3 — Tinnitus (p. 663)

For a patient with bilateral, bothersome tinnitus associated with hearing loss and a normal physical examination, which one of the following treatments has the best evidence for benefit?

- A. Cognitive behavior therapy.
- B. Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation.
- C. Anticonvulsant therapy.
- D. Microvascular decompression of cranial nerve VIII.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Cognitive behavior therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CBT has the strongest evidence for reducing tinnitus-related distress/quality-of-life impact. rTMS, anticonvulsants, and surgical decompression lack supporting evidence and are not recommended.

## Q4 — Out-of-Hospital Birth (p. 672)

For which one of the following pregnant patients would it be reasonable, if desired, to plan to deliver at home?

- A. A 22-year-old nulliparous patient at 41 weeks, 2 days.
- B. A 20-year-old with prior cesarean now desiring vaginal birth.
- C. A 35-year-old with suspected twins.
- D. A 30-year-old with a singleton pregnancy in vertex position.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Singleton, vertex presentation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ACOG considers home birth reasonable only for low-risk singleton, vertex, term (37–41 wk) pregnancies. Post-term (A), prior cesarean/TOLAC (B), and multiple gestation (C) are contraindications to planned out-of-hospital birth.

## Q5 — Out-of-Hospital Birth (p. 672)

A patient with a previous healthy term vaginal delivery asks about delivering outside a hospital. Which recommendation is most consistent with evidence and guidelines?

- A. An accredited birthing center may lead to more interventions/complications vs. hospital.
- B. A planned out-of-hospital birth poses no increased risk to the mother.
- C. Delivery at home is associated with an increased risk of perinatal death.
- D. They should consider having the baby at home without assistance.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Home delivery is associated with increased risk of perinatal death.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Planned home birth roughly doubles the (still low) absolute risk of perinatal death versus hospital. Accredited birth centers reduce intervention rates (A wrong); unassisted birth is never advised (D wrong).

## Q6 — Out-of-Hospital Birth (p. 672)

Which one of the following accurately reflects appropriate midwife training and practice?

- A. Certified nurse midwives are trained in nursing and have received formal midwifery training in a university-based system.
- B. Certified midwives have a nursing degree but are not required to have formal midwifery training.
- C. Certified professional midwives must be graduates of an accredited program.
- D. All types of midwives are licensed to practice in all states.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — CNMs are nurses with formal university-based midwifery training.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CNMs hold a nursing degree + graduate midwifery education. Certified midwives (CM) are non-nurses with midwifery training (B mis-states); CPMs need not graduate an accredited program (C wrong); scope/licensure varies by state (D wrong).

## Q7 — Polycythemia Vera: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 680)

Which one of the following is a risk factor for polycythemia vera?

- A. Female sex.
- B. Age younger than 40 years.
- C. Non-White race.
- D. European descent.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — European (Ashkenazi Jewish/European) descent.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PV is more common in people of European descent, in males, and with older age (peak 60s). So female sex, young age, and non-White race are *not* risk factors.

## Q8 — Polycythemia Vera (p. 680)

Which one of the following symptoms is present in more than one-half of patients with polycythemia vera?

- A. Difficulty concentrating.
- B. Splenomegaly.
- C. Venous thrombosis.
- D. Major hemorrhage.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Difficulty concentrating.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Per the review, neurocognitive/constitutional symptoms (difficulty concentrating, fatigue) are reported by >50%. Splenomegaly (~36%), thrombosis, and hemorrhage are important but less than half. (Answer confirmed against official key; the symptom-prevalence figures are the teaching nuance here.)

## Q9 — Polycythemia Vera (p. 680)

Which one of the following statements about treatment of patients with polycythemia vera is correct?

- A. Phlebotomy is not recommended based on randomized trials.
- B. Anticoagulation with warfarin or a DOAC is routinely recommended.
- C. Patients without contraindications should take low-dose aspirin daily.
- D. Ruxolitinib (Jakafi) is first-line cytoreductive therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Low-dose aspirin daily for patients without contraindications.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PV management: therapeutic phlebotomy (target Hct <45%) + low-dose aspirin for all without contraindication. Routine anticoagulation is not standard (B); hydroxyurea is first-line cytoreduction (ruxolitinib is second-line, D wrong).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Pessaries for Managing Pelvic Organ Prolapse in Women (p. 660)

Which one of the following statements about the use of pessaries to treat pelvic organ prolapse is correct?

- A. It is unclear if they improve symptoms when used alone.
- B. When combined with PFMT, pessaries can improve symptoms and quality of life compared with PFMT alone.
- C. When combined with PFMT, pessaries can increase the risk of vaginal bleeding compared with PFMT alone.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found uncertain benefit of pessaries alone (low-certainty evidence), modest symptom/QoL improvement when added to PFMT, and a signal of increased adverse events such as vaginal bleeding/discharge with combined use.

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Psychological Therapies for Women Who Experience Intimate Partner Violence (p. 661)

A 32-year-old who has experienced IPV has depression and anxiety symptoms. Which statement about psychological therapies for IPV is correct?

- A. They clearly decrease the risk of reexposure to IPV.
- B. They improve depression and anxiety symptoms associated with IPV.
- C. They carry a significant risk of harm, shown by dropout rates at 6 and 12 months.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — They improve depression and anxiety symptoms associated with IPV.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cochrane evidence supports improvement in depression/anxiety. There is *no* clear effect on reexposure (A wrong) and no evidence of significant harm/excess dropout (C wrong).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Chronic Kidney Disease in Diabetes — KDIGO (p. 698)

A 57-year-old with type 2 diabetes (A1C 7.5%) and hypertension has eGFR 49 mL/min/1.73 m². On extended-release metformin 1,000 mg daily and lisinopril 40 mg daily. According to KDIGO, which medication should be added if possible?

- A. Angiotensin receptor blocker.
- B. Mineralocorticoid receptor agonist.
- C. Glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonist.
- D. Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — SGLT2 inhibitor.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> KDIGO recommends an SGLT2 inhibitor for T2DM + CKD with eGFR ≥20–30 for kidney/cardiovascular protection, on top of metformin and a maximally tolerated ACEi/ARB. Adding an ARB to an ACEi is contraindicated (A); MRA is an *antagonist* not agonist (B); GLP-1 RA is an alternative but SGLT2i is the priority add-on here.
