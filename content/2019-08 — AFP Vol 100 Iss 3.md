## Q1 — Management of Chronic Tendon Injuries (p. 147)

Which one of the following recommendations for the treatment of tendinopathies is correct?

- A. Cryotherapy is expensive and not widely used.
- B. Topical nitroglycerin should not be used because of severe adverse effects.
- C. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs are beneficial with short-term use, even in the absence of inflammation.
- D. Tendon fenestration is effective only in conjunction with corticosteroid injections.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — NSAIDs help short-term, even though tendinopathy is degenerative (not inflammatory).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs provide short-term analgesia despite the histology being **tendinosis** rather than tendinitis. Cryotherapy is cheap/common (A); topical nitroglycerin is useful (headache is the main side effect, B); fenestration works without steroids (D).

## Q2 — Management of Chronic Tendon Injuries (p. 147)

Which one of the following statements about rotator cuff tendinopathy is correct?

- A. Clinic-based exercise programs are more effective than home-based programs.
- B. Eccentric exercise is superior to conventional exercise for treatment of rotator cuff tendinopathy.
- C. Subacromial corticosteroid injections have been found to offer long-term pain relief.
- D. Scapular stabilization to decrease subacromial impingement is the mainstay of treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Scapular stabilization (to reduce subacromial impingement) is the mainstay.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Home and clinic programs are comparable (A); eccentric exercise isn't clearly superior for the rotator cuff (B); subacromial steroids give only **short-term** relief (C wrong).

## Q3 — Management of Chronic Tendon Injuries (p. 147)

Which one of the following findings is most helpful in diagnosing patellar tendinopathy?

- A. Positive findings on the decline squat test.
- B. Nonradicular pain along the lateral thigh.
- C. Pain with resisted hip abduction.
- D. A lack of response to eccentric exercises.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A positive single-leg decline squat test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The decline squat loads the patellar tendon and reproduces anterior knee pain ("jumper's knee"). Lateral thigh pain and pain with resisted hip abduction point to **greater trochanteric/hip** pathology (B, C).

## Q4 — Ingrown Toenail Management (p. 158)

Which one of the following statements about matrixectomy for ingrown toenail is correct?

- A. Antibiotic therapy is always necessary after matrixectomy.
- B. Phenolization is the preferred approach to matrixectomy.
- C. Electrosurgical ablation of the nail bed is not recommended because of the risk of tissue injury.
- D. Matrixectomy reduces the likelihood of recurrence.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Matrixectomy reduces recurrence.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Destroying the nail matrix (chemically or surgically) gives the **lowest recurrence**. Antibiotics aren't routinely required after the procedure (A); phenol is one effective option among several (B); electrosurgical ablation is acceptable (C wrong).

## Q5 — Ingrown Toenail Management (p. 158)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of ingrown toenails is correct?

- A. Nonsurgical treatments are not recommended because of lack of effectiveness.
- B. Nonsurgical treatments are recommended for patients with mild or moderate disease.
- C. Topical steroids worsen outcomes.
- D. Topical antibiotics are recommended after surgical treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Nonsurgical measures are recommended for mild-to-moderate disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cotton-wisp/gutter splinting, taping, and proper trimming help **mild-moderate** cases (A wrong). Topical steroids can help inflammation (C wrong); routine post-op topical antibiotics aren't recommended (D wrong).

## Q6 — Ingrown Toenail Management (p. 158)

Which one of the following statements about post-surgical management of an ingrown toenail is correct?

- A. A short course of cephalexin (Keflex) or amoxicillin should be prescribed to prevent infection.
- B. Patients should apply a topical antibiotic after each dressing change.
- C. Normal ambulation may resume after 24 to 48 hours.
- D. Early ambulation within the first 12 hours is recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Normal ambulation may resume after 24 to 48 hours.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Brief rest/elevation then normal walking at 24–48 hours. Routine prophylactic oral or topical antibiotics aren't recommended after uncomplicated nail surgery (A, B), and immediate (12-hour) ambulation isn't advised (D).

## Q7 — Hirsutism in Women (p. 168)

Which one of the following conditions is the most common cause of hirsutism?

- A. Idiopathic hyperandrogenism.
- B. Adrenal hyperplasia.
- C. Polycystic ovary syndrome.
- D. Androgen-secreting tumors.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Polycystic ovary syndrome.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PCOS causes the large majority of hirsutism. Idiopathic hyperandrogenism and nonclassic adrenal hyperplasia are less common; rapidly progressive hirsutism with virilization suggests a rare **androgen-secreting tumor**.

## Q8 — Hirsutism in Women (p. 168)

Which one of the following medications is first-line therapy for women with hirsutism who do not desire fertility?

- A. Spironolactone.
- B. Combined oral contraceptives.
- C. Glucocorticoids.
- D. Metformin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Combined oral contraceptives.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> COCs are first-line (lower androgens, raise SHBG) for hirsutism when fertility isn't desired; an **antiandrogen (spironolactone)** can be added after ~6 months if needed. Metformin/glucocorticoids treat specific underlying causes.

## Q9 — Hirsutism in Women (p. 168)

Which one of the following statements about hair removal methods is correct?

- A. Electrolysis is a good option for removing hair over large areas of skin.
- B. Photoepilation is up to 60 times faster than electrolysis.
- C. Blonde or white-haired women should not use electrolysis.
- D. Photoepilation is more than 90% effective.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Photoepilation (laser/IPL) is up to ~60 times faster than electrolysis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Electrolysis is slow and best for **small** areas (A wrong) but works on **all hair colors**, including light hair where **laser is less effective** (C reversed). No method is >90% permanently effective (D overstated).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Vaccines for Preventing Influenza (p. 143)

Which one of the following statements about the use of the influenza vaccine in a moderate-risk population is correct?

- A. It prevents laboratory-confirmed influenza as well as influenza-like illness in children younger than 16 years.
- B. It prevents laboratory-confirmed influenza as well as influenza-like illness in patients 16 to 65 years of age.
- C. It prevents laboratory-confirmed influenza as well as influenza-like illness in adults older than 65 years.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Across children, adults, and older adults, the vaccine reduces both **laboratory-confirmed influenza and influenza-like illness** (with modest absolute effect sizes), supporting broad vaccination.

## Q11 — AHRQ Review: Chronic Neck Pain — Nonpharmacologic Treatment (p. 180)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of chronic neck pain is correct?

- A. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs improve pain more than acupuncture.
- B. Low-level laser therapy is ineffective at improving short-term function.
- C. No single type of exercise improves pain or function.
- D. Massage improves short-term function.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — No single type of exercise is clearly superior for pain or function.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Exercise helps chronic neck pain but **no specific modality is best** — pick what the patient will do. Low-level laser **does** help short term (B wrong); massage evidence is weak/short-lived (D); NSAID-vs-acupuncture superiority isn't established (A).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Infantile Hemangioma — AAP (p. 186)

According to the American Academy of Pediatrics, which one of the following infantile hemangiomas is considered high risk and requires immediate evaluation?

- A. Single 2-cm hemangioma on the upper abdomen.
- B. Two 1-cm hemangiomas on the upper back.
- C. Single 1-cm hemangioma over the angle of the right mandible.
- D. Three 1- to 1.5-cm hemangiomas scattered on the chest and abdomen.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A hemangioma over the angle of the mandible (the "beard" distribution).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> "Beard-area" hemangiomas (mandible/neck/lower face) signal possible **airway hemangioma** → urgent evaluation. Face/perineal, large segmental, ≥5 lesions (hepatic risk), and periocular lesions are also high-risk; small truncal lesions are lower risk (A, B, D).
