## Q1 — Prevention of Unintentional Childhood Injury (p. 411)

Which one of the following statements about vehicle safety restraints is correct?

- A. A three-year-old child who has outgrown the weight or height limit of a rear-facing car seat can ride in a forward-facing seat.
- B. Infants should move from a rear-facing to a front-facing car seat position at 12 months of age.
- C. An eight-year-old child whose weight and height are under the limits of a forward-facing car seat can sit in a booster seat.
- D. It is generally safe for children to sit in the front passenger seat by nine years of age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A 3-year-old who has outgrown the rear-facing limits may move to a forward-facing seat.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Progression is **by size, not age**: stay rear-facing until the seat's height/weight limit, then forward-facing with harness, then booster until the seat belt fits (~4'9"), and back seat until age 13 (B, C, D wrong).

## Q2 — Prevention of Unintentional Childhood Injury (p. 411)

Which one of the following statements about interventions to prevent drowning is correct?

- A. Children should wear Coast Guard–approved life jackets when swimming.
- B. Swimming lessons are recommended for all children by four years of age.
- C. Cardiopulmonary resuscitation should be administered only by trained medical personnel.
- D. Pool fences should allow rapid access to the home.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Swimming lessons are recommended for all children by four years of age.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Formal swim lessons reduce drowning risk. Life jackets are for **boating**, not a substitute for supervision while swimming (A); bystander CPR saves lives (C wrong); pool fencing must be **isolation (4-sided)** fencing that separates the pool from the house (D wrong).

## Q3 — Prevention of Unintentional Childhood Injury (p. 411)

Which one of the following interventions is recommended to prevent unintentional injury in children?

- A. Vomiting should be induced if a toxic ingestion is suspected.
- B. Guns should be stored with their ammunition in a locked location.
- C. Toddler walkers can help prevent falls.
- D. Infants should sleep in the same room as a caregiver for the first six to 12 months of life.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Infants should room-share (not bed-share) with a caregiver for the first 6–12 months.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Room-sharing reduces SIDS risk. **Do not induce vomiting** (A); firearms should be stored **unloaded and locked separately from ammunition** (B wrong); infant walkers **increase** fall/injury risk (C wrong).

## Q4 — Pregnancy Myths and Practical Tips (p. 420)

Which one of the following supplements or combinations should pregnant women be encouraged to take?

- A. A prenatal vitamin, a calcium supplement, and a DHA supplement.
- B. A folic acid supplement, a vitamin B12 supplement, and a calcium supplement.
- C. A prenatal vitamin that includes folic acid and iron.
- D. A calcium supplement, a DHA supplement, and a vitamin B12 supplement.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A prenatal vitamin containing folic acid and iron.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A standard prenatal vitamin (with **folic acid** for neural-tube prevention and **iron**) covers routine needs; extra calcium/DHA/B12 aren't routinely required for most healthy pregnant women.

## Q5 — Pregnancy Myths and Practical Tips (p. 420)

Which one of the following statements about exercise during pregnancy is correct?

- A. Pregnant women should not do yoga during the third trimester.
- B. Pregnant women should engage in 20 to 30 minutes of moderate aerobic activity on most days of the week.
- C. Bed rest is recommended in the third trimester to decrease the risk of preterm delivery.
- D. Pregnant women should not engage in resistance training.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — 20–30 minutes of moderate aerobic activity on most days.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Regular moderate exercise is encouraged in uncomplicated pregnancy. Yoga and resistance training are safe (A, D wrong); **bed rest is not recommended** and carries harms (VTE, deconditioning) without reducing preterm birth (C wrong).

## Q6 — Pregnancy Myths and Practical Tips (p. 420)

Which one of the following statements about coffee consumption during pregnancy is correct?

- A. Pregnant women should limit caffeine intake to 300 mg per day.
- B. Consumption of up to six cups of coffee per day does not increase the risk of early pregnancy loss, preterm delivery, or low birth weight.
- C. Coffee with artificial sweeteners may increase the risk of infants who are large for gestational age.
- D. Infants born to women who drink unsweetened black coffee during pregnancy are at increased risk of metabolic syndrome when they are older.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Artificially sweetened coffee may increase the risk of large-for-gestational-age infants.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Observational data link artificial sweeteners in pregnancy to higher infant adiposity/LGA. Caffeine should generally be kept **<200 mg/day** (A overstates the limit); high intake **does** raise pregnancy-loss/low-birth-weight risk (B wrong). *(Confirmed by official key.)*

## Q7 — Persistent Night Sweats: Diagnostic Evaluation (p. 427)

Which one of the following statements about persistent night sweats is correct?

- A. Malignancy and infection are the most common causes.
- B. An extensive laboratory and radiologic evaluation is usually required.
- C. Common causes include vasomotor symptoms of menopause, panic attacks, alcohol and tobacco use, and obesity.
- D. Medication use is not associated with patient-reported night sweats.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Common causes include menopausal vasomotor symptoms, panic attacks, alcohol/tobacco use, and obesity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most night sweats are benign and common. Malignancy/infection are **important but uncommon** causes (A wrong); extensive testing isn't routinely needed (B wrong); many **medications** cause night sweats (D wrong).

## Q8 — Persistent Night Sweats (p. 427)

Which one of the following statements about the initial evaluation of persistent night sweats is correct?

- A. It should include a history and physical examination for common causes.
- B. It should include a thyroid-stimulating hormone level, tuberculosis testing, and blood cultures.
- C. The use of a symptom diary to monitor response to treatment is not recommended.
- D. A review of medications is not recommended because they are rarely the cause.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A history and physical exam targeting common causes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Start with a focused **history and exam**; targeted testing follows red flags. A broad TB/blood-culture workup isn't first-line (B); symptom diaries are useful (C wrong); a medication review is essential (D wrong).

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: CT for Diagnosis of Acute Appendicitis in Adults (p. 403)

Which one of the following statements about the sensitivity and specificity of CT for diagnosing acute appendicitis in nonpregnant adults is correct?

- A. The specificity is less than 80%.
- B. The sensitivity is less than 80%.
- C. The sensitivity and specificity are both greater than 90%.
- D. It is neither sensitive nor specific for use in diagnosing appendicitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Both sensitivity and specificity exceed 90%.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CT is highly accurate (sensitivity/specificity >90%) for appendicitis in nonpregnant adults — the imaging test of choice (ultrasound/MRI preferred in pregnancy and children to limit radiation).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Effects of Inhaled Corticosteroids on Growth in Children with Persistent Asthma (p. 404)

Which one of the following statements about the effects of inhaled corticosteroids on growth in children with asthma is correct?

- A. They have no effect on growth velocity.
- B. Fluticasone (Flovent) reduces growth velocity compared with an equivalent dose of beclomethasone.
- C. Beclomethasone reduces growth velocity compared with an equivalent dose of fluticasone.
- D. The risks of using inhaled corticosteroids outweigh the benefits.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Beclomethasone reduces growth velocity more than an equivalent dose of fluticasone.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ICS cause a small (~0.5 cm/yr) reduction in growth velocity (A wrong), and the effect varies by molecule — **fluticasone/ciclesonide** affect growth less than beclomethasone/budesonide. Benefits of asthma control still outweigh this small effect (D wrong).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Patellofemoral Pain — American Physical Therapy Association (p. 442)

A 26-year-old training for a marathon has three weeks of vague anterior knee pain worse climbing or descending stairs. According to the APTA, which one of the following interventions is most likely to be beneficial?

- A. An exercise program focused on calf and ankle strengthening.
- B. An exercise program focused on posterior hip and quadriceps strengthening.
- C. Dry needling treatments.
- D. Therapies involving blood flow restriction.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Posterior hip and quadriceps strengthening.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Combined **hip (abductor/external rotator) + quadriceps** strengthening is the strongest evidence-based therapy for patellofemoral pain. Dry needling is recommended **against** (C); blood-flow-restriction and isolated calf/ankle work aren't first-line (A, D).
