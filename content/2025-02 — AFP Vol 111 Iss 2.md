## Q1 — Lower Extremity Abnormalities in Children (p. 125)
A mother is concerned that her 3-year-old son's feet turn inward when running. Exam shows a thigh-foot angle of 12 degrees of internal rotation. Which one of the following is appropriate management?

- A. Parental reassurance and repeat measurements at follow-up.
- B. Referral to a pediatric orthopedist.
- C. Use of custom foot orthotics.
- D. Radiography of the knee and hip.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Reassurance and repeat measurement.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Mild internal tibial torsion is physiologic in toddlers and resolves spontaneously. Orthotics/bracing don't help, imaging isn't indicated, and referral is reserved for severe, asymmetric, or non-resolving deformity.

> [!success]- Reveal answer

## Q5 — Acid-Base Interpretation (p. 148)
A 72-year-old with O₂-dependent COPD and pneumonia presents with acute mental status change. ABG: pH 7.30, PaCO₂ 60 mm Hg, HCO₃ 26 mEq/L. Which one of the following primary acid-base disorders should be diagnosed?

- A. Respiratory alkalosis.
- B. Respiratory acidosis.
- C. Metabolic alkalosis.
- D. Metabolic acidosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — (Acute) respiratory acidosis.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Acidemia (pH 7.30) with elevated PaCO₂ (60) and a near-normal HCO₃ (26) = acute respiratory acidosis with minimal metabolic compensation — fits CO₂ retention from COPD/pneumonia.

## Q6 — Acid-Base Interpretation (p. 148)
A 24-year-old with type 1 diabetes and presumed DKA has an anion-gap metabolic acidosis. Gap-gap analysis shows ΔAG is 8 mEq/L *less* than ΔHCO₃ (−8). This suggests a mixed disturbance with which one of the following?

- A. An additional respiratory alkalosis.
- B. An additional respiratory acidosis.
- C. An additional metabolic alkalosis.
- D. An additional metabolic acidosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — An additional (non-anion-gap) metabolic acidosis.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> When HCO₃ falls *more* than the anion gap rises (delta ratio <1), a concurrent normal-anion-gap (hyperchloremic) metabolic acidosis is present — common in DKA with saline resuscitation/GI losses. If ΔAG had exceeded ΔHCO₃, you'd suspect a coexisting metabolic alkalosis.

## Q9 — Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (p. 164)
A 2-week-old sleeps on her back in a bassinet in the parents' bedroom, swaddled, wearing a hat, with a pacifier. Which one of the following changes reduces SIDS risk?

- A. Removing the pacifier.
- B. Removing the hat while sleeping.
- C. Not swaddling.
- D. Not using a bassinet.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Remove the hat during sleep.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Head covering/hats indoors risk overheating, a SIDS risk factor. Pacifier use is protective (don't remove), room-sharing in a bassinet is recommended, and swaddling is acceptable at 2 weeks (stop once the infant shows signs of rolling).

## Q10 — Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (p. 164)
A 4-month-old vomits frequently when laid down to sleep; parents also want to prevent SIDS. Which one of the following sleep positions is recommended?

- A. On his back with a flat mattress.
- B. On his back with an inclined mattress.
- C. On his stomach with a flat mattress.
- D. On his stomach with an inclined mattress.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Supine on a flat, firm mattress.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Back-to-sleep on a flat, firm surface is recommended for ALL infants, including those with reflux/vomiting. Inclined sleep surfaces are unsafe (banned), and prone positioning markedly increases SIDS risk.

## Q13 — Practice Guidelines: Atrial Fibrillation (ACC/AHA) (p. 183)
A 62-year-old woman, 1 week after AF diagnosis and starting apixaban, has bothersome daily rapid irregular palpitations. Per ACC/AHA, which one of the following is most likely to improve her symptoms?

- A. Caffeine abstinence.
- B. Electrical cardioversion.
- C. Percutaneous occlusion of the left atrial appendage.
- D. Surgical exclusion of the left atrial appendage.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Electrical cardioversion (rhythm control).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Symptom relief in AF comes from rate/rhythm control; cardioversion restores sinus rhythm and relieves symptoms. LAA occlusion/exclusion (C, D) target stroke prevention, not symptoms. Caffeine abstinence (A) has no strong guideline support for symptom control. **⚠️ Verify** (note: cardioversion normally requires ≥3 weeks anticoagulation or TEE first — the question asks what relieves symptoms, not timing).
