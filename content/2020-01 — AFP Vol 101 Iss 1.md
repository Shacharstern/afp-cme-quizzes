## Q1 — Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis: Common Questions and Answers (p. 19)

Which one of the following statements about adolescent idiopathic scoliosis is correct?

- A. Most patients are symptomatic.
- B. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force and the American Academy of Family Physicians recommend screening for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis.
- C. Back pain is more common in patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis.
- D. Increased spinal curvature correlates with increased severity of back pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Back pain is more common in patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most patients are otherwise **asymptomatic** (A wrong); the **USPSTF gives an I statement** (insufficient evidence) and AAFP doesn't recommend routine screening (B wrong); back pain occurs more often but does **not correlate with curve magnitude** (D wrong).

## Q2 — Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis (p. 19)

Which one of the following statements about the clinical examination for scoliosis is correct?

- A. The forward bend test is more reliable for lumbar than thoracic scoliosis.
- B. A scoliometer directly measures the Cobb angle.
- C. A patient with a 4-degree angle of trunk rotation should undergo radiography.
- D. In normal weight patients, a 7-degree angle of trunk rotation corresponds with a Cobb angle of 20 degrees.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A 7-degree angle of trunk rotation roughly corresponds to a 20-degree Cobb angle.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Adams forward-bend test is more sensitive for **thoracic** curves (A reversed); a scoliometer measures **trunk rotation, not the Cobb angle** (B wrong); radiography is generally obtained at an ATR of **~5–7 degrees**, not 4 (C).

## Q3 — Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis (p. 19)

Which one of the following statements about scoliosis treatments is correct?

- A. Surgical referral is generally recommended at a Cobb angle of 40 degrees or more.
- B. Compared with observation, bracing improves quality of life.
- C. Compared with observation, bracing has no effect on curve progression.
- D. Scoliosis-specific therapy is no more effective than typical physical therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Surgical referral at a Cobb angle ≥40 degrees.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Observation <20°, bracing ~20–40° (which **does slow progression**, C wrong), and surgery at ≥40–50°. Bracing can reduce progression but doesn't clearly improve quality of life (B); scoliosis-specific exercises **do** help (D wrong).

## Q4 — Benign Anorectal Conditions: Evaluation and Management (p. 24)

Which one of the following therapies for hemorrhoids carries the most risk of causing bacteremia?

- A. Hemorrhoid artery ligation.
- B. Infrared coagulation.
- C. Rubber band ligation.
- D. Sclerotherapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Sclerotherapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Injection sclerotherapy carries the greatest risk of **bacteremia/sepsis** (a concern in immunocompromised patients) among office hemorrhoid procedures.

## Q5 — Benign Anorectal Conditions (p. 24)

A patient with perianal pain has multiple anal fissures, several located lateral to the midline. In addition to treating symptoms, additional workup should exclude which one of the following conditions?

- A. Crohn disease.
- B. HIV infection.
- C. Human papillomavirus infection.
- D. Proctalgia fugax.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Crohn disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Atypical fissures** — multiple, lateral (not posterior/anterior midline), or non-healing — should prompt evaluation for **Crohn disease** (and other causes: malignancy, TB, HIV-associated ulcers). Typical fissures sit in the posterior midline.

## Q6 — Benign Anorectal Conditions (p. 24)

Which one of the following is considered a first-line therapy for patients with fecal incontinence?

- A. Antidiarrheal agents.
- B. Biofeedback.
- C. Fiber supplementation.
- D. Scheduled toileting.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Biofeedback.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Pelvic floor **biofeedback** is a recommended first-line therapy for fecal incontinence in this article. *(Teaching nuance: dietary **fiber** and antidiarrheal/antimotility agents are also widely used first-line conservative measures; the official key selects biofeedback.)*

## Q7 — Lymphoma: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 34)

Which one of the following is the most common clinical presentation of lymphoma?

- A. Fever, unexplained weight loss, and night sweats.
- B. Painless adenopathy.
- C. Superior vena cava syndrome.
- D. Dermatomyositis or polymyositis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Painless lymphadenopathy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The classic presentation is **painless, persistent adenopathy**. "B symptoms" (fever, weight loss, night sweats) are important but less universal (A); SVC syndrome and paraneoplastic myositis are uncommon (C, D).

## Q8 — Lymphoma (p. 34)

Which one of the following can increase the risk of developing lymphoma?

- A. Fibromyalgia.
- B. Osteoarthritis.
- C. Systemic lupus erythematosus.
- D. A body mass index of 25 kg/m².

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Systemic lupus erythematosus.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Chronic immune activation/autoimmune disease — **SLE**, Sjögren, rheumatoid arthritis — and immunosuppression/infections (EBV, HIV, H. pylori) increase lymphoma risk. Fibromyalgia, osteoarthritis, and a normal BMI do not.

## Q9 — Lymphoma (p. 34)

Which one of the following is a potential complication of treatment for lymphoma?

- A. Secondary malignancies (i.e., breast, lung, skin, colon).
- B. Infertility.
- C. Heart disease.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Chemotherapy/radiation survivors face long-term risks of **secondary cancers, infertility, and cardiovascular disease** — warranting survivorship surveillance.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Positional Therapy for Obstructive Sleep Apnea (p. 16)

Which one of the following statements about the use of positional therapy for the treatment of obstructive sleep apnea is correct?

- A. It reduces apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) scores more than continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP).
- B. It reduces AHI scores compared with no treatment.
- C. It reduces Epworth Sleepiness Scale scores more than CPAP.
- D. Patients tolerate CPAP better than positional therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Positional therapy reduces AHI compared with no treatment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Positional therapy helps **position-dependent** OSA but is **inferior to CPAP** for lowering AHI (A wrong) and sleepiness (C wrong); patients generally **tolerate positional therapy better** than CPAP (D reversed).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Intrapartum Omega-3 Fatty Acid Intake and Length of Gestation (p. 17)

Which one of the following statements about the use of omega-3 fatty acid supplements during pregnancy is correct?

- A. They reduce the risk of preterm birth.
- B. They reduce the risk of prelabor rupture of membranes.
- C. They reduce the risk of low birth weight.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found omega-3 (LC-PUFA) supplementation in pregnancy reduces **preterm and early preterm birth, prelabor rupture of membranes, and low birth weight** (while slightly increasing post-term pregnancy).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Beers Criteria for Inappropriate Medication Use in Older Patients — AGS (p. 56)

Which one of the following medications should be avoided in patients with heart failure?

- A. Cilostazol (Pletal).
- B. Thiazolidinediones.
- C. Dronedarone (Multaq).
- D. Cyclooxygenase-2 inhibitors.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Cilostazol (Pletal).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cilostazol is **contraindicated in heart failure** (a phosphodiesterase III inhibitor — this drug class increased mortality in HF). The other agents carry cautions, but cilostazol is the clearest "avoid" in HF per Beers.
