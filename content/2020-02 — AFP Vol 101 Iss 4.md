## Q1 — Acute Lower Gastrointestinal Bleeding (p. 206)

When evaluating a patient with lower gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, which one of the following suspected conditions should prompt measurement of fecal calprotectin?

- A. Diverticulitis.
- B. Ischemic colitis.
- C. Inflammatory bowel disease.
- D. Angiodysplasia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Inflammatory bowel disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Fecal calprotectin** is a marker of intestinal inflammation and helps identify/monitor IBD. It is not useful for diverticular, ischemic, or angiodysplastic bleeding.

## Q2 — Acute Lower Gastrointestinal Bleeding (p. 206)

A 79-year-old patient who has had a triple bypass presents with lower GI bleeding. Which one of the following findings should prompt suspicion for ischemic colitis?

- A. The pain is out of proportion to the physical examination findings.
- B. Weight loss and fever.
- C. The patient has recently traveled to Latin America.
- D. The history indicates repeated painless episodes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Pain out of proportion to the examination findings.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> In an older vasculopath, abdominal pain that exceeds exam findings (with bloody stool) suggests **ischemia**. Weight loss/fever point to malignancy/IBD (B), travel to infectious colitis (C), and painless recurrent bleeding to diverticular/angiodysplastic sources (D). *(Teaching nuance: "pain out of proportion" is the classic phrase for acute mesenteric ischemia; here it flags the ischemic etiology — confirmed by official key.)*

## Q3 — Acute Lower Gastrointestinal Bleeding (p. 206)

Which one of the following statements about lower GI bleeding is correct?

- A. Early colonoscopy (within 24 hours of presentation) is recommended for hemodynamically unstable patients.
- B. Normal saline and balanced crystalloids (e.g., lactated Ringer solution) are equally effective in critically ill patients in terms of mortality rates, acute kidney injury, and need for renal replacement therapy.
- C. Percutaneous catheter embolization is most effective once diverticular bleeding is well controlled.
- D. Anticoagulation therapy must be discontinued promptly in patients with lower GI bleeding.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Normal saline and balanced crystalloids are comparable in critically ill patients.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Unstable patients need **resuscitation/upper-source evaluation first**, not immediate colonoscopy (A); embolization works best on **active** bleeding (C); anticoagulation decisions are **individualized**, balancing thrombotic risk, not always stopped (D).

## Q4 — Cerebral Palsy: An Overview (p. 213)

Which one of the following is the most common type of movement disorder in people with cerebral palsy?

- A. Ataxia.
- B. Dyskinesia.
- C. Mixed.
- D. Spasticity.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Spasticity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Spastic** CP is by far the most common type (~80%), followed by dyskinetic, ataxic, and mixed forms.

## Q5 — Cerebral Palsy (p. 213)

If imaging is needed in the evaluation of a child with cerebral palsy and perinatal imaging studies do not show a cause, which one of the following is the recommended modality?

- A. Computed tomography with contrast media.
- B. Computed tomography without contrast media.
- C. Magnetic resonance imaging.
- D. Skull radiography.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Magnetic resonance imaging.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **MRI** is the preferred neuroimaging study in CP — higher yield for structural/white-matter abnormalities and no ionizing radiation.

## Q6 — Cerebral Palsy (p. 213)

Which one of the following statements about common complications in patients with cerebral palsy is correct?

- A. Vision screening should occur at six months and annually thereafter to evaluate for strabismus and hemianopia.
- B. Impaired oral-motor function may require special diets, different positioning or feeding techniques, or anticholinergics.
- C. Diarrhea is a common effect of delayed gastric emptying.
- D. Screening for hearing impairment should start after three years of age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Impaired oral-motor function may need diet/positioning changes or anticholinergics (for drooling).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Oral-motor dysfunction causes feeding difficulty and sialorrhea (treated with anticholinergics). Delayed gastric emptying causes **constipation/reflux**, not diarrhea (C); vision and hearing screening begin **early/at diagnosis**, not deferred (A, D).

## Q7 — Coccidioidomycosis (Valley Fever) in Primary Care (p. 221)

In which one of the following ways is coccidioidomycosis infection acquired?

- A. Animal-to-person transmission of mold spores.
- B. Inhalation of fungal spores in dust.
- C. Person-to-person transmission of mold spores.
- D. Transplacental transmission of mold spores.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Inhalation of fungal spores in dust.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Coccidioides is acquired by **inhaling arthroconidia** from soil/dust in endemic arid regions (US Southwest). It is **not** transmitted person-to-person, animal-to-person, or transplacentally.

## Q8 — Coccidioidomycosis (p. 221)

Which one of the following is the most common presentation of coccidioidomycosis?

- A. Skin lesions.
- B. Erythema nodosum.
- C. Headache and fever.
- D. Symptoms and findings consistent with community-acquired pneumonia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A community-acquired pneumonia–like illness.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most symptomatic infections present as a **subacute pneumonia** ("Valley fever"). Erythema nodosum is a classic but less common immune sign; disseminated skin/CNS disease is uncommon.

## Q9 — Coccidioidomycosis (p. 221)

Which one of the following groups of patients are at high risk of coccidioidomycosis dissemination and generally should receive antifungal medications?

- A. Hispanics.
- B. People with well-controlled HIV infection.
- C. Pregnant women.
- D. Children.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pregnant women.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> High dissemination risk warranting treatment includes **pregnancy** (especially 2nd/3rd trimester), immunosuppression/uncontrolled HIV, and certain ethnicities (e.g., Filipino, African) — but not well-controlled HIV, otherwise healthy children, or Hispanic ethnicity alone (A, B, D).

## Q10 — Point-of-Care Guides: Diagnosis of Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (p. 230)

Which one of the following is **not** a predictor of heart failure in the MICE score?

- A. Acute dyspnea.
- B. History of myocardial infarction.
- C. Basal crepitations on lung examination.
- D. Cough.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cough.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The **MICE** rule uses **M**ale sex, prior myocardial **I**nfarction, and basal **C**repitations (plus presenting symptoms like dyspnea/**E**dema). Cough is not part of the score.

## Q11 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Risk Assessment, Genetic Counseling, and Genetic Testing for BRCA-Related Cancer (p. 239)

A 31-year-old presents for a well-woman exam. Her mother had tubal cancer at 40, her 42-year-old sister was recently diagnosed with breast cancer, and her maternal aunt had breast cancer at 45. Her exam is normal. Based on the USPSTF recommendation statement, which one of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Refer her to a geneticist for genetic testing.
- B. Assess her risk for a potentially harmful BRCA1/2 gene mutation using an appropriate brief familial risk assessment tool.
- C. Refer her for breast cancer screening using mammography, breast ultrasonography, or both.
- D. Recommend monthly breast self-examinations.
- E. Recommend yearly clinical breast examinations.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Use a brief familial risk-assessment tool first.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF pathway for women with concerning family history is **risk-assessment tool → genetic counseling → (if indicated) testing**. Direct testing skips a step (A); screening mammography/self-exam aren't the next step here (C, D, E).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: ACIP Approves 2020 Adult and Childhood/Adolescent Immunization Schedules (p. 251)

According to ACIP, which one of the following statements about immunization recommendations in adults is correct?

- A. Human papillomavirus vaccine is not recommended after age 26.
- B. Pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV13) is routinely recommended for patients 65 years and older.
- C. Hepatitis A vaccine is contraindicated in patients with active HIV infection.
- D. Either the Tdap or the Td vaccine is now recommended without preference for the 10-year booster.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Either Tdap or Td may be used (no preference) for the decennial booster.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ACIP allows Tdap **or** Td for routine boosters/wound care. HPV is now shared decision-making up to age 45 (A wrong); PCV13 in ≥65 became **shared decision-making**, not routine (B wrong); hepatitis A vaccine is **recommended**, not contraindicated, in HIV (C wrong).
