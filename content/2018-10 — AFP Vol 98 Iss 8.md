## Q1 — High Blood Pressure in Children and Adolescents (p. 486)

Which one of the following statements about the evaluation of children and adolescents with elevated blood pressure is correct?

- A. Oscillometric devices underestimate both systolic and diastolic pressure.
- B. Coarctation of the aorta leads to falsely low blood pressure readings in the right arm.
- C. Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring should be used if blood pressure has been elevated for one year or more.
- D. A home blood pressure cuff or wrist monitor is the recommended method of confirming blood pressure measurement.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Use ambulatory blood pressure monitoring if BP has been elevated for ≥1 year.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ABPM confirms hypertension and identifies white-coat/masked HTN. Oscillometric devices tend to **overestimate** (A); coarctation causes higher pressures in the **arms** with diminished leg pulses (B); auscultation (not wrist monitors) confirms readings (D).

## Q2 — High Blood Pressure in Children and Adolescents (p. 486)

Which one of the following might suggest mineralocorticoid excess as the cause of hypertension in children and adolescents?

- A. Ambiguous genitalia.
- B. Diminished femoral pulses.
- C. Blood pressure that is higher in the leg than in the arm.
- D. Abdominal bruit.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Ambiguous genitalia (e.g., congenital adrenal hyperplasia).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mineralocorticoid-excess states (some forms of CAH) can present with ambiguous genitalia + hypertension. Diminished femoral pulses and arm-leg gradients point to **coarctation** (B, C); an abdominal bruit suggests **renovascular** disease (D).

## Q3 — High Blood Pressure in Children and Adolescents (p. 486)

Which one of the following lifestyle interventions is the most effective for lowering blood pressure in children and adolescents?

- A. Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension diet.
- B. Sustained, regular exercise.
- C. Complementary stress reduction techniques.
- D. Substance use avoidance.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Sustained, regular (aerobic) exercise.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Regular physical activity has the strongest evidence for lowering pediatric blood pressure (the DASH diet also helps, but the article emphasizes exercise).

## Q4 — Urinary Retention in Adults: Evaluation and Initial Management (p. 496)

Which one of the following statements about postoperative urinary retention is correct?

- A. Older age increases the risk.
- B. Preoperative alpha-blocker use increases the risk.
- C. Preoperative anticholinergic use decreases the risk.
- D. The type of anesthetic used in the procedure does not affect the risk.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Older age increases the risk of postoperative urinary retention.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Alpha blockers **reduce** retention risk (B reversed); **anticholinergics increase** it (C reversed); spinal/epidural anesthesia and certain drugs **do** affect risk (D wrong).

## Q5 — Urinary Retention in Adults (p. 496)

Which one of the following statements about catheterization for the management of acute urinary retention is correct?

- A. Silver alloy-coated catheters prevent catheter-acquired urinary tract infections.
- B. Starting an alpha blocker at the time of catheter insertion increases the likelihood of a successful voiding trial.
- C. Suprapubic catheters are uncomfortable for patients and increase bacteriuria and the need for recatheterization.
- D. Antibiotic-impregnated catheters produce clinically significant reductions in urinary tract infections.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Starting an alpha blocker at catheterization improves the success of a voiding trial.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Silver-alloy and antibiotic-impregnated catheters don't produce clinically meaningful CAUTI reductions (A, D); **suprapubic** catheters are generally more comfortable with **less** bacteriuria than urethral catheters (C reversed).

## Q6 — Depression: Screening and Diagnosis (p. 508)

Which one of the following statements about screening for depression in adults is correct?

- A. It should be performed only in adults with risk factors for depression.
- B. One approach is to screen during routine health visits.
- C. Screening should occur regardless of whether there are systems in place to ensure accurate diagnosis, effective treatment, and appropriate follow-up.
- D. If the Patient Health Questionnaire-2 is positive for depression, no further evaluation is necessary.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Screening during routine health visits is one acceptable approach.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends screening **all adults** (not just high-risk, A) — but **only when systems exist** for diagnosis/treatment/follow-up (C reversed). A positive **PHQ-2 requires** a full PHQ-9/clinical assessment (D wrong).

## Q7 — Depression: Screening and Diagnosis (p. 508)

Which one of the following statements about postpartum depression is correct?

- A. The most convenient screening tool is the Postpartum Depression Screening Scale.
- B. Onset rarely occurs before delivery.
- C. Screening should begin four months postpartum.
- D. Screening performed during infant hospitalizations is an effective way to identify previously unscreened mothers.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Screening during infant hospitalizations helps identify unscreened mothers.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The **Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale** is the common tool (A); onset can be **antenatal** ("perinatal" depression, B wrong); screening starts **early** (not waiting to 4 months, C wrong).

## Q8 — Depression: Screening and Diagnosis (p. 508)

Which one of the following statements about screening for depression in older patients is correct?

- A. Somatic issues are the most important indicators of depression in this population.
- B. The Geriatric Depression Scale is the best screening instrument for patients with dementia.
- C. The Patient Health Questionnaire-2 can be used in this population.
- D. Low rates of depression in nursing home patients warrant more detailed screening instruments.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The PHQ-2 can be used in older patients.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The PHQ-2/PHQ-9 are validated in older adults. Somatic symptoms are nonspecific (A); the **Cornell Scale** is preferred in dementia (B); nursing-home depression rates are **high**, not low (D wrong).

## Q9 — Cerumen Impaction: Diagnosis and Management (p. 525)

Which one of the following statements about cerumen impaction is correct?

- A. It is defined as complete occlusion of the external auditory canal.
- B. Tinnitus is an uncommon symptom.
- C. It may cause reversible cognitive impairment in older adults if associated with hearing loss.
- D. Cerumen should not be removed if the patient does not report symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — It can cause reversible cognitive impairment in older adults with associated hearing loss.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Impaction doesn't require **complete** occlusion (A); **tinnitus is common** (B wrong); asymptomatic cerumen **should be removed** when it prevents needed exam or affects hearing-aid use (D wrong).

## Q10 — Cerumen Impaction (p. 525)

Which one of the following home treatments may be effective for adults with cerumen impaction?

- A. Ear candling.
- B. Bulb syringing.
- C. Clearing cerumen with cotton-tipped swabs.
- D. Olive oil drops.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Bulb syringing (home irrigation).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Bulb-syringe irrigation can clear cerumen at home. **Ear candling is dangerous/ineffective** (A); **cotton swabs push wax deeper** (C); olive oil softens wax but isn't a definitive removal method (D).

## Q11 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Prostate Cancer (p. 537)

A 57-year-old Black man with no family history of prostate cancer and no symptoms wants to discuss the PSA test. According to the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommendation, which one of the following would be the next step?

- A. You should request a PSA test and then discuss other concerns.
- B. He should be screened with a baseline PSA test because of his higher risk of prostate cancer.
- C. You should discuss the benefits and harms of screening, his values, and other health needs and then order a PSA test if he expresses a preference for screening.
- D. He should not be screened because harms outweigh benefits.
- E. He should be screened because the harms are minimal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Engage in shared decision-making, then test if he prefers screening after discussing benefits/harms.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For men **55–69**, the USPSTF gives a **C** recommendation: individualized, shared decision. Don't reflexively order or withhold the PSA (A, B, D, E).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Influenza Vaccination Recommendations for 2018-2019 — ACIP (p. 541)

According to the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP), which one of the following scenarios is an inappropriate and contraindicated administration of influenza vaccine?

- A. Live attenuated influenza vaccine in a patient with a history of urticaria associated with egg ingestion.
- B. Live attenuated influenza vaccine in a three-year-old child with asthma.
- C. A 0.5-mL dose of Fluarix Quadrivalent in a one-year-old infant.
- D. Any licensed quadrivalent inactivated influenza vaccine in a patient with recurrent emesis from egg ingestion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — LAIV in a three-year-old with asthma is contraindicated.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> LAIV is **contraindicated in children 2–4 with asthma/recent wheezing**. Egg-related urticaria/emesis allows any age-appropriate flu vaccine (A, D acceptable), and Fluarix Quadrivalent is licensed from 6 months at 0.5 mL (C acceptable).
