## Q1 — HIV Infection in Adults: Initial Management (p. 407)

Which one of the following laboratory test results demonstrates optimal HIV treatment and a low risk of transmission to a seronegative partner?

- A. A negative HIV drug-resistance test result.
- B. An undetectable quantitative plasma HIV RNA (viral load).
- C. A CD4 count greater than 200 cells/μL.
- D. A negative tuberculin skin test.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Undetectable plasma HIV RNA (viral load).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> "Undetectable = Untransmittable" (U=U): a sustained undetectable viral load reflects optimal treatment and essentially no sexual transmission risk. CD4 count tracks immune status, not transmissibility.

## Q2 — HIV Infection in Adults (p. 407)

After initial diagnosis of HIV infection, in which one of the following instances should immediate initiation of antiretroviral therapy *not* be considered?

- A. Resistance testing results are not yet available.
- B. The partner's HIV status is unknown.
- C. The patient's insurance status is unknown.
- D. The patient has untreated cryptococcal meningitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Untreated cryptococcal meningitis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Rapid ART start is favored, but with certain opportunistic infections (notably cryptococcal meningitis and TB meningitis) ART is briefly delayed to avoid immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS). Pending resistance results/insurance/partner status do not delay starting.

## Q3 — HIV Infection in Adults (p. 407)

A 42-year-old with HIV and hepatitis B coinfection should be counseled about which one of the following?

- A. The infections cannot be treated simultaneously due to drug interactions.
- B. Hepatocellular carcinoma screening is not indicated with HIV coinfection.
- C. Stopping HIV medications that also have anti-hepatitis B activity can cause a hepatitis B flare.
- D. Hepatitis A vaccination should be avoided.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Stopping HIV drugs with anti-HBV activity can trigger a hepatitis B flare.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Tenofovir/lamivudine/emtricitabine treat both HIV and HBV; abrupt discontinuation risks an HBV reactivation flare. HCC surveillance is *more* important in coinfection (B wrong); HAV vaccination is recommended (D wrong).

## Q4 — The Suicidal Patient: Evaluation and Management (p. 417)

Which one of the following statements about suicide is correct?

- A. Rates of completed suicides have been decreasing over time.
- B. Men are more likely to attempt suicide, but women are more likely to die by suicide.
- C. Comorbid psychiatric illness increases the risk of suicide.
- D. Firearms are an uncommon means of committing suicide.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Comorbid psychiatric illness increases suicide risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Psychiatric comorbidity (depression, substance use, etc.) is a major risk factor. US suicide rates have *risen* (A wrong); **women attempt more, men die more** (B is reversed); firearms are the most common method of suicide death (D wrong).

## Q5 — The Suicidal Patient (p. 417)

Which one of the following patients may be considered for outpatient therapy?

- A. A patient with a specific suicidal plan.
- B. A patient with no close social support.
- C. A patient who recently purchased a firearm.
- D. A patient with suicidal ideation but no specific plan.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Suicidal ideation but no specific plan.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Lower-risk patients — ideation without a plan, intent, or access to lethal means, and with support — can be managed as outpatients with safety planning. A specific plan, recent means acquisition, and lack of support all push toward higher-level/inpatient care.

## Q6 — The Suicidal Patient (p. 417)

A patient with a mood disorder and suicidal ideation may benefit from which one of the following first-line treatments?

- A. Ketamine.
- B. Clozapine (Clozaril).
- C. Citalopram (Celexa).
- D. Lithium.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Lithium.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Lithium has the strongest evidence for reducing suicide risk in mood disorders (and clozapine for schizophrenia). Ketamine is emerging/adjunctive; citalopram treats depression but lacks lithium's specific anti-suicide evidence.

## Q7 — Cerebrospinal Fluid Analysis (p. 422)

Which one of the following tests should be performed in all patients with suspected central nervous system infection?

- A. CSF polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing.
- B. Measurement of CSF lactate.
- C. CSF latex agglutination.
- D. CT of the head.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — CSF PCR testing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Per the article, CSF PCR (e.g., meningitis/encephalitis panel) is recommended in all suspected CNS infections for rapid pathogen identification. CT before LP is reserved for those with focal deficits/immunocompromise/papilledema, not universal (D wrong). (Answer confirmed against official key.)

## Q8 — Cerebrospinal Fluid Analysis (p. 422)

Which one of the following organisms is more likely to cause bacterial meningitis in immunocompromised people and older adults than in immunocompetent adults?

- A. Haemophilus influenzae.
- B. Listeria monocytogenes.
- C. Neisseria meningitidis.
- D. Streptococcus pneumoniae.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Listeria monocytogenes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Listeria meningitis disproportionately affects neonates, adults >50, pregnant women, and the immunocompromised — which is why empiric ampicillin is added in these groups.

## Q9 — Cerebrospinal Fluid Analysis (p. 422)

In which one of the following clinical situations should CSF analysis be performed in a patient with suspected subarachnoid hemorrhage?

- A. Presentation <6 hours after onset with positive CT findings.
- B. Presentation <6 hours after onset with negative CT findings.
- C. Presentation >12 hours after onset with positive CT findings.
- D. Presentation >12 hours after onset with equivocal CT findings.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — >12 hours after onset with equivocal CT findings.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Non-contrast CT is highly sensitive for SAH within 6 hours; sensitivity declines thereafter. So LP (looking for xanthochromia) is indicated when presentation is delayed and CT is negative/equivocal. A positive CT already confirms SAH — no LP needed (A, C).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Active vs. Expectant Management in the Third Stage of Labor (p. 404)

Which one of the following statements about active management of the third stage of labor is correct?

- A. It reduces the risk of severe primary postpartum hemorrhage.
- B. It reduces the incidence of postpartum maternal anemia.
- C. It is associated with an increase in postnatal hypertension.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Active management (uterotonic + cord traction) reduces severe PPH and maternal anemia, but the trade-off includes more postnatal hypertension and afterpains (from ergometrine-containing regimens).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Epidural Corticosteroid Injections for Lumbosacral Radicular Pain (p. 405)

Which one of the following statements about epidural corticosteroid injection vs. placebo for acute lumbosacral radicular pain is correct?

- A. Corticosteroid injection relieves pain for 12 months or more vs. placebo.
- B. Corticosteroid injection reduces disability for 12 months or more vs. placebo.
- C. Minor adverse effects are more common with corticosteroid injection than placebo.
- D. The trend is for diminished benefit over time, regardless of the type of injection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Benefit tends to diminish over time, regardless of injection type.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Epidural steroids give only modest, short-term relief; benefit is not maintained at 12 months (A, B wrong), and adverse-effect rates are similar to placebo/control injections (C wrong).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Obstructive Sleep Apnea and Chronic Insomnia Disorder — VA/DoD (p. 442)

According to the VA/DoD guidelines, which one of the following would result in a diagnosis of obstructive sleep apnea?

- A. A STOP score of 1.
- B. Taking opioid medications for the past 10 years.
- C. A respiratory disturbance index of nine events per hour by home sleep apnea test.
- D. An apnea-hypopnea index of 15 events per hour by home sleep apnea test.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — AHI of 15 events/hour by home sleep apnea test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> OSA is diagnosed at AHI ≥15 (or ≥5 with symptoms/comorbidities). An RDI of 9 (C) and a screening STOP score (A) are below diagnostic thresholds; chronic opioid use is a risk factor, not a diagnosis (B).
