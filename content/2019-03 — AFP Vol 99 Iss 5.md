## Q1 — Adhesive Capsulitis: Diagnosis and Management (p. 297)

Which one of the following nonsurgical treatments for adhesive capsulitis carries the highest risk?

- A. Intra-articular corticosteroid injection.
- B. Physiotherapy.
- C. Acupuncture.
- D. Oral corticosteroids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Oral corticosteroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Systemic steroids carry the most systemic adverse effects (hyperglycemia, etc.) for only short-term benefit. **Intra-articular** injection delivers steroid locally with less systemic risk (A); physiotherapy and acupuncture are low-risk (B, C).

## Q2 — Adhesive Capsulitis (p. 297)

Which one of the following tests is required to diagnose adhesive capsulitis?

- A. Magnetic resonance imaging.
- B. A1C level.
- C. Plain radiography.
- D. None of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — None of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Adhesive capsulitis ("frozen shoulder") is a **clinical diagnosis** (pain + global loss of active and passive range of motion). Imaging/labs are used selectively to exclude other causes (e.g., diabetes association), not to make the diagnosis.

## Q3 — Adhesive Capsulitis (p. 297)

Which one of the following is a chief symptom of adhesive capsulitis?

- A. Sudden, sharp pain.
- B. Reproduction of pain with raising arms to the sides.
- C. Pain that radiates to the fingertips.
- D. Pain and a marked decrease in range of motion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Pain with a marked decrease in range of motion.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The hallmark is **progressive pain plus loss of both active and passive ROM** (especially external rotation). Onset is gradual (not sudden, A), and pain doesn't radiate to the fingertips (C).

## Q4 — Gas, Bloating, and Belching: Approach to Evaluation and Management (p. 301)

Which one of the following conditions is most likely to cause a sensation of bloating within 30 minutes after eating or the inability to finish a meal?

- A. Irritable bowel syndrome.
- B. Celiac disease.
- C. Functional dyspepsia.
- D. Slow transit constipation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Functional dyspepsia.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Postprandial fullness and **early satiety** (within ~30 minutes) characterize the postprandial-distress subtype of functional dyspepsia.

## Q5 — Gas, Bloating, and Belching (p. 301)

Which one of the following conditions is most likely to cause abdominal distention more than 30 minutes after eating?

- A. Gastric outlet obstruction.
- B. Irritable bowel syndrome.
- C. Gastroparesis.
- D. Lactose intolerance.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Irritable bowel syndrome.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> IBS-related distention typically develops and worsens through the day (well after eating). Gastric outlet obstruction/gastroparesis cause **early** fullness; lactose intolerance produces gas/diarrhea tied to dairy.

## Q6 — Gas, Bloating, and Belching (p. 301)

Which one of the following would be the most appropriate for initial management of a patient with presumed functional dyspepsia?

- A. Endoscopy.
- B. Urea breath testing.
- C. Trial of proton pump inhibitor therapy.
- D. Gastric emptying study.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Urea breath testing (test for H. pylori).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In patients <60 without alarm features, **test (and treat) for H. pylori** first; a PPI trial follows if symptoms persist. Endoscopy and gastric emptying studies are reserved for alarm features or refractory cases (A, D).

## Q7 — Hepatitis B: Screening, Prevention, Diagnosis, and Treatment (p. 314)

Which one of the following risk factors warrants screening for hepatitis B?

- A. Born in a region with 1% prevalence of chronic hepatitis B.
- B. Men who have sex only with women.
- C. Pregnancy.
- D. Infants born in the United States and parents from a region with 2% prevalence of chronic hepatitis B.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pregnancy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **All pregnant women** are screened (HBsAg). Birthplace screening applies to regions with **≥2%** prevalence (A is below threshold); MSM (not heterosexual men only) are higher risk (B); US-born infants of carrier parents are managed via vaccination/immunoprophylaxis, not the listed screening indication (D).

## Q8 — Hepatitis B (p. 314)

Which one of the following statements about antiviral treatments for chronic hepatitis B is correct?

- A. Combined therapy with pegylated interferon alfa-2a (Pegasys) and nucleoside/nucleotide analogues is not recommended.
- B. Pegylated interferon alfa-2a is usually administered for an indefinite duration.
- C. Entecavir (Baraclude), tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (Viread), and tenofovir alafenamide (Vemlidy) have lower antiviral potency and higher resistance rates than other nucleoside/nucleotide analogues.
- D. Treating pregnant women positive for hepatitis B surface antigen does not reduce perinatal transmission rates.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Combined pegylated interferon + nucleos(t)ide analogue therapy is not recommended.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Peg-interferon is given for a **finite course** (~48 weeks), not indefinitely (B); entecavir/tenofovir are the **high-potency, low-resistance** first-line agents (C reversed); third-trimester antivirals **reduce** perinatal transmission (D wrong).

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Different Durations of Corticosteroid Therapy for COPD Exacerbations (p. 295)

Which one of the following statements about the use of systemic corticosteroids to treat an exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is correct?

- A. More than seven days of treatment is associated with increased adverse effects.
- B. Five days of treatment is likely to be as effective as 10 days.
- C. Five days of treatment is more likely to result in relapse than 10 days.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A five-day course is as effective as a longer (10-day) course.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Short-course (~5 days) systemic steroids are non-inferior for COPD exacerbations **without** more relapse (C wrong) — the basis for the "5-day" standard. The specific 7-day adverse-effect claim (A) isn't established, so "all of the above" is wrong (D).

## Q10 — AHRQ Review: Management of Uterine Fibroids (p. 330)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of uterine fibroids is correct?

- A. High-intensity focused ultrasound improves quality of life.
- B. Compared with hysterectomy, uterine artery embolization is associated with a lower rate of readmissions and unscheduled visits.
- C. Although myomectomy has a low complication rate, it carries a risk of fibroid recurrence.
- D. Selective progesterone receptor modulators may cause bone loss.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Myomectomy has a low complication rate but fibroids can recur.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Myomectomy preserves the uterus but new/regrown fibroids occur over time. UAE has **higher** rates of readmission/reintervention than hysterectomy (B reversed); HIFU evidence is limited (A); **GnRH agonists** (not SPRMs) cause bone loss (D).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Myocardial Infarction — Updated Definition (p. 334)

Which one of the following statements about the definition of myocardial infarction (MI) is correct?

- A. MI related to coronary artery bypass grafting (type 5 MI) is defined as a cardiac troponin level more than three times the 99th percentile in patients with a healthy baseline.
- B. MI with nonobstructive coronary arteries should be used to describe patients with MI but with no angiographic findings of obstructive coronary artery disease.
- C. Presence of coronary thrombus is exclusive to the diagnosis of type 3 MI.
- D. MI related to percutaneous coronary intervention (type 4a MI) is defined as a cardiac troponin level more than two times the 99th percentile in patients with a healthy baseline.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — MINOCA describes MI with no obstructive coronary artery disease on angiography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Fourth Universal Definition sets the CABG (type 5) threshold at **>10×** (A wrong) and PCI (type 4a) at **>5×** the 99th percentile (D wrong); type 3 is sudden cardiac **death** before biomarkers (C wrong).
