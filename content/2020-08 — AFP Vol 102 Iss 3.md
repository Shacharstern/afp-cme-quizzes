## Q1 — Neck Pain: Initial Evaluation and Management (p. 150)

Which one of the following physical examination findings is consistent with myelopathy?

- A. Positive upper limb tension test.
- B. Increased muscle tone and fasciculations.
- C. Absent Babinski reflex.
- D. Absent Hoffmann sign.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Increased muscle tone (with fasciculations).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Myelopathy is an **upper-motor-neuron** process → increased tone/spasticity, hyperreflexia, and **positive** Babinski and Hoffmann signs (so C and D, being *absent*, are normal). A positive upper-limb tension test indicates **radiculopathy**, not myelopathy (A).

## Q2 — Neck Pain (p. 150)

Which one of the following statements about imaging in patients with neck pain is correct?

- A. Radiography should be ordered for every patient with neck pain.
- B. Computed tomography is recommended for patients with progressive neurologic symptoms.
- C. The use of validated clinical tools, such as the Canadian C-Spine Rule, can help guide imaging decisions.
- D. Magnetic resonance imaging rarely detects abnormalities in the cervical spine of asymptomatic patients.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Validated tools (e.g., the Canadian C-Spine Rule) help guide imaging.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most neck pain doesn't need imaging (A wrong). **MRI** (not CT) is preferred for progressive neurologic symptoms (B wrong), and MRI frequently shows **incidental** degenerative findings in asymptomatic people (D wrong).

## Q3 — Neck Pain (p. 150)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of mechanical neck pain is correct?

- A. There is no evidence supporting the use of acupuncture.
- B. Up to 90% of patients will have significant pain relief in four weeks regardless of treatment.
- C. Strong evidence supports early administration of oral steroids to speed recovery.
- D. Narcotics can provide long-term benefits.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Up to ~90% improve within four weeks regardless of treatment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mechanical neck pain is usually **self-limited**. Acupuncture has some supportive evidence (A wrong); oral steroids are not recommended (C wrong); opioids lack long-term benefit and carry harm (D wrong).

## Q4 — Intrapartum Fetal Monitoring (p. 158)

Continuous electronic fetal monitoring has become standard practice for most patients in labor. Which one of the following outcomes has resulted from using continuous electronic fetal monitoring?

- A. Statistical decrease in fetal death.
- B. Reduction in operative vaginal delivery rates.
- C. Increase in cesarean delivery rates.
- D. Increase in neonatal seizures.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Increased cesarean delivery rates.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Continuous EFM **reduces neonatal seizures** (not increases — D wrong) but **increases cesarean and operative vaginal deliveries** (B reversed) without lowering cerebral palsy or perinatal death (A wrong).

## Q5 — Intrapartum Fetal Monitoring (p. 158)

Structured intermittent auscultation is considered an appropriate alternative for certain patients in labor. Which one of the following statements about structured intermittent auscultation is correct?

- A. It increases cesarean delivery rates.
- B. It decreases operative vaginal delivery rates.
- C. It should be considered for high-risk labor.
- D. It increases the chance of switching to continuous electronic fetal monitoring for the duration of labor.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — It decreases operative vaginal delivery rates.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Structured intermittent auscultation is for **low-risk** labor (C wrong) and lowers intervention rates (cesarean and operative vaginal delivery) compared with continuous EFM (A wrong).

## Q6 — Intrapartum Fetal Monitoring (p. 158)

Which one of the following findings on a Category II tracing requires immediate intervention during labor?

- A. Provoked accelerations.
- B. Moderate variability.
- C. Mild variable decelerations.
- D. Recurrent deep decelerations.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Recurrent deep decelerations.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Recurrent deep (late or variable) decelerations signal possible hypoxia and prompt **intrauterine resuscitation**. Accelerations and **moderate variability** are reassuring (A, B); occasional mild variables are benign (C).

## Q7 — Acute Pyelonephritis in Adults: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 173)

Which one of the following oral antibiotics is an appropriate choice for outpatient treatment of acute pyelonephritis when an additional parenteral agent is not indicated?

- A. Nitrofurantoin.
- B. Fosfomycin (Monurol).
- C. Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole.
- D. Ceftriaxone (Rocephin).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Effective pyelonephritis therapy needs an agent that achieves **high renal tissue/serum levels** — fluoroquinolones or TMP-SMX (if susceptible). **Nitrofurantoin and fosfomycin don't reach renal tissue** and are for cystitis only (A, B); ceftriaxone is parenteral (D).

## Q8 — Acute Pyelonephritis in Adults (p. 173)

Which one of the following tests is **not** appropriate for the initial evaluation of uncomplicated acute pyelonephritis?

- A. Computed tomography of the abdomen and pelvis.
- B. Complete blood count.
- C. Basic metabolic panel.
- D. Urine culture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — CT of the abdomen and pelvis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Uncomplicated pyelonephritis is a clinical/lab diagnosis (urinalysis, **urine culture**, CBC, BMP). CT is reserved for **complicated** cases, treatment failure, or suspected obstruction/abscess.

## Q9 — Acute Pyelonephritis in Adults (p. 173)

Which one of the following antibiotics has evidence of safety in utero when treating pregnant patients with acute pyelonephritis?

- A. Imipenem/cilastatin.
- B. Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole.
- C. Plazomicin (Zemdri).
- D. Ceftriaxone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Ceftriaxone.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Beta-lactams/cephalosporins** are the mainstay in pregnancy. TMP-SMX is avoided (1st trimester/near term, B); aminoglycosides/plazomicin carry fetal risk (C); carbapenems are reserved for resistant organisms (A).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Heavy Menstrual Bleeding and the Role of NSAIDs (p. 147)

Your 29-year-old patient reports menorrhagia without dysmenorrhea. Which one of the following statements about treatment is correct?

- A. NSAIDs have been shown to decrease the number of patients who report heavy bleeding.
- B. NSAIDs are more effective at reducing heavy menstrual bleeding than tranexamic acid (Cyklokapron) and the levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system (Mirena).
- C. NICE guidelines suggest that NSAIDs be considered first-line therapy for heavy menstrual bleeding.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — NSAIDs reduce the number of patients reporting heavy bleeding.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs modestly reduce menstrual blood loss but are **less effective** than tranexamic acid or the LNG-IUS (B wrong); the **LNG-IUS** is NICE first-line, not NSAIDs (C wrong).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Smoking Cessation with Text Messaging and App-Based Interventions (p. 148)

A patient asks for help to quit smoking and you consider text-messaging and app-based interventions. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Smartphone interventions increase quit rates vs. minimal smoking cessation support.
- B. In combination with other smoking cessation interventions, smartphone interventions improve quit rates vs. other interventions alone.
- C. Smartphone interventions have not been shown to work better than lower intensity, non–app-supported interventions, such as printed educational materials.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Text-message programs increase quit rates vs. minimal support and add benefit when combined with other methods, but **app-based** tools haven't clearly outperformed lower-intensity options — capturing the mixed but generally positive evidence.

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: VTE in Patients with Cancer — ASCO (p. 188)

A 57-year-old woman who recently completed chemotherapy for breast cancer is diagnosed with a pulmonary embolism. According to ASCO, what is the minimum recommended duration of anticoagulation therapy?

- A. 45 days.
- B. Three months.
- C. Six months.
- D. Indefinitely.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Six months.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ASCO recommends **at least 6 months** of anticoagulation for cancer-associated VTE (LMWH, or a direct oral anticoagulant), with extension considered while active cancer or ongoing risk persists.
