## Q1 — Cluster Headache: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 24)
Which statement about cluster headache is correct?

- A. More common in women than men.
- B. Risk is much higher when a first-degree relative is affected.
- C. They typically affect the second and third branches of the trigeminal nerve.
- D. They rarely occur more than once in the same day.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — First-degree relative greatly raises risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cluster headache is male-predominant, affects the *first* (ophthalmic) trigeminal division, and recurs multiple times daily in clusters.

## Q2 — Cluster Headache (p. 24)
Which statement about diagnosis of cluster headache is correct?

- A. Patients typically want to lie quietly in a dark room.
- B. Neuroimaging is routinely recommended to rule out intracranial pathology.
- C. Episodes are severe, unilateral, and typically last 15 to 180 minutes.
- D. Miosis or ptosis is uncommon and suggests an intracranial lesion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Severe, unilateral, 15–180 minutes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Patients are restless/agitated (unlike migraine); ipsilateral autonomic signs (miosis, ptosis, lacrimation) are characteristic, not alarming. MRI is done at first presentation but not "routinely" thereafter.

## Q3 — Cluster Headache (p. 24)
Which is a first-line abortive therapy for cluster headache?

- A. Triptans.
- B. Nasal lidocaine.
- C. Dihydroergotamine nasal spray.
- D. Oral corticosteroids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Triptans (with high-flow oxygen).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Subcutaneous/intranasal sumatriptan + 100% oxygen are first-line aborts. Steroids are a *bridge/transitional* preventive; verapamil is first-line maintenance.

## Q4 — Adenomyosis: Diagnosis and Management (p. 33)
Which statement about the presentation of adenomyosis is correct?

- A. It is rarely asymptomatic.
- B. It most often occurs in postmenopausal women.
- C. It may present with dyspareunia.
- D. It is commonly associated with amenorrhea.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — May present with dyspareunia.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Classic triad: heavy menstrual bleeding, dysmenorrhea, and an enlarged tender "boggy" uterus; many cases are asymptomatic; typical in reproductive-age multiparous women.

## Q5 — Adenomyosis (p. 33)
Which statement about diagnosis of adenomyosis is correct?

- A. Examination reveals a small, hard uterus.
- B. The presence of uterine fibroids rules out adenomyosis.
- C. There is a pathognomonic ultrasound appearance.
- D. Transvaginal ultrasonography is first-line in suspected adenomyosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — TVUS is the first-line diagnostic test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Uterus is enlarged/boggy; fibroids often coexist; US findings are suggestive but not pathognomonic (MRI/histology confirm).

## Q6 — Adenomyosis (p. 33)
A 33-year-old with dysmenorrhea and a tender uterus, suspected adenomyosis. Which recommendation is correct?

- A. Hysterectomy is the only therapy that improves symptoms.
- B. A trial of NSAIDs or combined oral contraceptives may be started.
- C. The FDA has approved progestin-releasing IUDs for adenomyosis.
- D. Hysteroscopy is not useful in diagnosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Trial of NSAIDs or combined OCPs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Medical management is first-line; hysterectomy is definitive but not "only" option; LNG-IUD is used off-label.

## Q7 — Orthostatic Hypotension: A Practical Approach (p. 39)
Which finding on a simplified Schellong test is diagnostic of orthostatic hypotension?

- A. Diastolic BP drops 5 mm Hg from supine to standing.
- B. HR rises 0.2 bpm per mm Hg SBP drop after standing.
- C. Dizziness/dyspnea after moving from standing to supine.
- D. Systolic BP decreases by 24 mm Hg after standing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — SBP fall of 24 mm Hg (≥20).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Diagnostic thresholds: SBP fall ≥20 mm Hg or DBP fall ≥10 mm Hg within 3 minutes of standing.

## Q8 — Orthostatic Hypotension (p. 39)
Which statement about nonpharmacologic management is correct?

- A. The goal is to normalize blood pressure.
- B. Review current medications for causative agents.
- C. Large infrequent meals are preferred over small frequent meals in postprandial hypotension.
- D. Bed rest is recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Review medications for offending agents.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Goal is symptom relief (not normal BP); small frequent meals help postprandial hypotension; bed rest worsens deconditioning.

## Q9 — Orthostatic Hypotension (p. 39)
Which statement about pharmacologic management is correct?

- A. Fludrocortisone carries long-term risks (LVH, renal failure, hospitalization).
- B. Opioids and anticholinergics can be helpful.
- C. Pyridostigmine and atomoxetine are FDA-approved first-line agents.
- D. Midodrine and droxidopa are best taken at bedtime.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Fludrocortisone has long-term risks.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Midodrine/droxidopa are taken in daytime/upright hours (avoid supine hypertension at night); opioids/anticholinergics worsen OH; pyridostigmine/atomoxetine are off-label.

## Q10 — Transient Global Amnesia (p. 50)
Which patient should be diagnosed with transient global amnesia?

- A. A 27-year-old with amnesia and speech impairment 2 hours after an MVC.
- B. A 52-year-old with amnesia and mild blurry vision after intercourse, resolving in 3 hours.
- C. A 62-year-old with amnesia starting 30 hours ago after diving into cold water.
- D. A 42-year-old after a seizure with amnesia resolving in 12 hours.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Amnesia after intercourse, resolving in 3 hours.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> TGA criteria: witnessed anterograde amnesia, <24 h, no focal neuro deficits, no recent head trauma/seizure, often triggered by Valsalva/exertion/emotion/cold water. A, C (>24h), and D (seizure) are exclusions.

## Q11 — Transient Global Amnesia (p. 50)
Which statement about the diagnostic workup of TGA is correct?

- A. If all TGA criteria are met and symptoms resolved, no workup is required.
- B. TGA cannot be diagnosed without MRI.
- C. Hippocampal change on MRI is permanent.
- D. EEG is a useful adjunct test when TGA is suspected.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — No workup needed if criteria met and resolved.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> TGA is a clinical diagnosis; MRI/EEG aren't required; the small DWI hippocampal lesions are transient.

## Q12 — Transient Global Amnesia (p. 50)
Which statement about TGA is correct?

- A. Incidence decreases with age.
- B. A history of migraine is a risk factor.
- C. The presence of nausea rules out TGA.
- D. Patients commonly forget how to speak or drive.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Migraine history is a risk factor.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Incidence rises with age; nausea/headache can accompany TGA; procedural memory (speaking, driving) is preserved.

## Q13 — Pruritus: Diagnosis and Management (p. 55)
Which presentation suggests allergic contact dermatitis as the cause of pruritus?

- A. A 24-year-old G1 with generalized pruritus at 34 weeks' gestation.
- B. A 25-year-old daycare worker with pruritus mostly on the scalp.
- C. A 15-year-old wrestler with a round ring-like rash on the leg.
- D. A 23-year-old with atopic dermatitis and localized wrist erythema/pruritus.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Localized wrist erythema/pruritus.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Localized rash at a contact site (e.g., watch/nickel) is classic ACD. A = intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy; B = head lice; C = tinea corporis.

## Q14 — Pruritus (p. 55)
After initial labs, what is the next step in an older patient with generalized pruritus plus fever, fatigue, weight loss, and night sweats?

- A. CT of head, chest, abdomen, and pelvis.
- B. Immediate oncology referral.
- C. ESR testing and chest radiography.
- D. Reassurance, topical steroids, follow-up in one month.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — ESR and chest radiography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> B-symptoms raise concern for lymphoma/malignancy; targeted workup (ESR, CXR) precedes whole-body CT or referral.

## Q15 — Pruritus (p. 55)
A 34-year-old with 3 months of generalized pruritus, no primary lesions on exam. Next reasonable step?

- A. Rapidly tapered oral corticosteroids.
- B. Refer to psychiatry for presumed psychiatric cause.
- C. Reassurance and follow-up in one month.
- D. Initial labs: A1C, CBC, CMP, iron studies, TSH.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Obtain initial labs (A1C, CBC, CMP, iron, TSH).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Generalized pruritus without primary lesions → screen for systemic causes (thyroid, renal, hepatic, hematologic, iron, diabetes).

## Q16 — Arterial Atherosclerosis: Vascular Surgery (p. 65)
Which USPSTF AAA screening recommendation is correct?

- A. One-time screening in women 65–75 with significant smoking history.
- B. One-time screening for men 65–75 who have ever smoked.
- C. Evidence insufficient to recommend for/against in men or women 65–75.
- D. Screening not recommended for men or women 65–75.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — One-time screen for men 65–75 who ever smoked.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Grade B for ever-smoking men 65–75; selective (Grade C) for never-smoking men; insufficient (I) for women who ever smoked; recommend against (D) for never-smoking women.

## Q17 — Arterial Atherosclerosis (p. 65)
Which statement about endovascular (EVAR) vs open AAA repair is correct?

- A. Lower short-term (30-day) mortality in all patients.
- B. Lower long-term (>4 years) mortality in all patients.
- C. Lower long-term (>4 years) mortality in patients ≥70.
- D. Reduced need for subsequent procedures.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Lower 30-day mortality in all patients.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> EVAR has a perioperative survival advantage that converges over time; it requires *more* reinterventions long-term.

## Q18 — Arterial Atherosclerosis (p. 65)
A 75-year-old man with stroke and 75% right carotid stenosis. Which procedure is most beneficial?

- A. Carotid artery stenting within two weeks.
- B. Carotid artery stenting more than two weeks later.
- C. Carotid endarterectomy within two weeks.
- D. Carotid endarterectomy more than two weeks later.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Carotid endarterectomy within two weeks.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Symptomatic severe (70–99%) stenosis → CEA, ideally within 2 weeks; CEA outperforms stenting in older patients.

## Q19 — Cochrane: Can Walking Lower Blood Pressure? (p. 22)
Which statement about a prescribed walking regimen is correct?

- A. It lowers diastolic blood pressure and resting heart rate.
- B. Patients should aim for ≥150 minutes/week at moderate intensity.
- C. It may lower systolic blood pressure modestly (<5 mm Hg).
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Walking modestly lowers SBP/DBP and resting HR; target ≥150 min/week.

## Q20 — Cochrane: Compression Stockings for DVT in Air Travel (p. 23)
Which statement about compression stockings to prevent DVT in flight is correct?

- A. Effective at reducing asymptomatic DVT in both high- and low-risk individuals.
- B. It clearly decreases pulmonary embolism risk in both high- and low-risk individuals.
- C. ASH recommends them for flights ≥5 hours in low-risk individuals.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Reduces asymptomatic DVT across risk levels.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> No clear PE benefit; ASH suggests stockings/measures for *high-risk* (not low-risk) long-haul travelers.

## Q21 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: Prediabetes/Diabetes Screening (USPSTF) (p. 73)
Per USPSTF, which statement about prediabetes and diabetes is correct?

- A. More than 50% of U.S. adults meet criteria for prediabetes.
- B. Most people with prediabetes are aware they have it.
- C. Non-Hispanic White people have the highest U.S. diabetes prevalence.
- D. Being overweight or obese is the strongest risk factor for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Overweight/obesity is the strongest risk factor.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ~1/3 (not >50%) of adults have prediabetes and most are *unaware*; prevalence is highest among American Indian/Alaska Native, Hispanic, and non-Hispanic Black populations.

## Q22 — Practice Guideline: Chronic Insomnia (AASM) (p. 97)
Which therapy is effective for chronic insomnia but may be contraindicated in a high-risk occupation (e.g., airline pilot) due to transient daytime somnolence?

- A. Relaxation therapy.
- B. Sleep hygiene education.
- C. Sleep restriction therapy.
- D. Stimulus control.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Sleep restriction therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Restricting time in bed transiently increases daytime sleepiness — risky for pilots/drivers/heavy-machinery operators.

## Q23 — Practice Guideline: Secondary Prevention of Ischemic Stroke (AHA/ASA) (p. 99)
Per 2021 AHA/ASA, if initial evaluation suggests an embolic stroke but a source cannot be found, which is recommended?

- A. A direct-acting oral anticoagulant.
- B. An antiplatelet agent.
- C. Transesophageal echocardiography.
- D. Testing for hypercoagulation and vasculitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Transesophageal echocardiography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> *Answer confirmed by official key; nuance worth noting.* When an embolic source is not found on initial workup, continue searching (TEE, prolonged rhythm monitoring) rather than starting empiric therapy. For confirmed ESUS, DOACs are NOT superior to aspirin → antiplatelet (option B) is used — that's the management once the source remains unfound, so B is a strong distractor.
