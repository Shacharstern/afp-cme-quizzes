## Q1 — Health Maintenance in Postmenopausal Women (p. 407)
According to the ACC/AHA, **which one of the following statements about statins for primary CVD prevention in postmenopausal women is correct?**

- A. Women with a 10-year ASCVD risk >3% should be offered a statin
- B. Absent risk factors/risk-enhancing conditions, women with LDL >130 mg/dL should be offered a statin
- C. Women with diabetes should be offered a statin
- D. Statin therapy should be offered only as secondary prevention

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Women with diabetes should be offered a statin.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Diabetes (age 40–75) is a statin-benefit group regardless of calculated risk. The intermediate-risk threshold for considering statins is ≈7.5% (not 3%, A false); LDL ≥190 mg/dL — not >130 — mandates a statin (B false); statins are used for primary *and* secondary prevention (D false).

## Q3 — Update on Routine Immunizations for Children and Adolescents (p. 419)
A 10-year-old presents for a well-child visit; caregiver is hesitant about COVID-19 vaccine citing myocarditis. **Most appropriate response?**

- A. Explain that myocarditis is far more common after SARS-CoV-2 infection than after vaccination and tends to be mild and self-limited when vaccine related
- B. Recommend delaying vaccination until the child is older
- C. Advise against vaccination, given the caregiver's concerns
- D. Focus instead on other preventive measures

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Vaccine-associated myocarditis is rare, usually mild and self-limited, and far less frequent than myocarditis from infection itself. Accurate counseling addresses the specific concern rather than deferring or capitulating.

## Q4 — Update on Routine Immunizations for Children and Adolescents (p. 419)
A healthy 3-week-old in early RSV season; mother delivered at 36 weeks and did **not** receive the maternal RSV vaccine in pregnancy. **Appropriate next step?**

- A. Administer nirsevimab (Beyfortus) to the infant
- B. Recommend monthly palivizumab throughout the RSV season
- C. Postpone preventive treatment and monitor for symptoms
- D. Vaccinate the infant directly with the Abrysvo RSV vaccine

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Nirsevimab (Beyfortus).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Infants whose mothers did not receive maternal RSV vaccination should get nirsevimab for passive protection. Palivizumab is reserved for select high-risk infants (B); doing nothing leaves the infant unprotected (C); Abrysvo is a maternal/older-adult vaccine, not given to infants (D).

## Q5 — Trigeminal Neuralgia: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 427)
A 53-year-old with intermittent lancinating pain in the right trigeminal distribution. **Recommended initial treatment of trigeminal neuralgia?**

- A. Microvascular decompression surgery
- B. Gabapentin
- C. Lidocaine nasal spray
- D. Carbamazepine

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Carbamazepine.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Carbamazepine (and oxcarbazepine) is first-line for TN. Surgery is reserved for refractory/medication-intolerant cases (A); gabapentin and topical agents are second-line/adjunctive (B, C).

## Q6 — Trigeminal Neuralgia: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 427)
**Which test is recommended during the initial evaluation of suspected TN?**

- A. C-reactive protein or sedimentation rate
- B. Electrophysiologic testing
- C. Brain MRI
- D. Complete blood cell count and comprehensive metabolic panel

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Brain MRI.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High

## Q7 — Female Sexual Dysfunction: Common Questions and Answers (p. 433)
A postmenopausal woman with introital dyspareunia, minimal success with lubricants, exam showing vaginal atrophy. **Appropriate measure?**

- A. OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox)
- B. Flibanserin (Addyi) 100 mg at bedtime
- C. Mindfulness-based therapy
- D. Topical estrogen

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Topical estrogen.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Introital dyspareunia with atrophy = genitourinary syndrome of menopause; local (topical) estrogen is the treatment. Flibanserin targets low desire, not atrophic dyspareunia (B); mindfulness addresses desire/arousal disorders (C); Botox is not indicated here (A).

## Q8 — Female Sexual Dysfunction: Common Questions and Answers (p. 433)

## Q10 — Migraine Headache Prophylaxis (p. 443)
A 28-year-old (G2P1) at 10 weeks' gestation with worsening frequent migraines. **Reasonable prophylactic agent?**

- A. Beta blockers
- B. Divalproex
- C. Topiramate
- D. Feverfew

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Beta blockers.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Beta blockers (e.g., propranolol/metoprolol) are the preferred prophylaxis in pregnancy. Divalproex and topiramate are teratogenic (B, C); feverfew is contraindicated in pregnancy (D).
