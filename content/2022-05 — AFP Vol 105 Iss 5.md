## Q1 — Constipation in Children and Adolescents (p. 469)
You examine a two-year-old child with constipation. Which one of the following should prompt a diagnostic workup beyond the history and physical examination?

- A. The constipation started around the time of transitioning the child to solid foods.
- B. The parent says the child clenches their muscles to avoid having a bowel movement because it hurts.
- C. The child has a family history of cystic fibrosis.
- D. Stool can be felt in the lower left quadrant of the abdomen on examination.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Family history of cystic fibrosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CF is an organic red flag; A, B, D are typical of benign functional constipation. Red flags (failure to thrive, delayed meconium, family hx of CF/Hirschsprung, abnormal neuro exam) prompt workup.

## Q2 — Constipation in Children (p. 469)
A 13-year-old with functional constipation asks about nonmedical therapies. Which recommendation is appropriate?

- A. Eat five servings of fruits and vegetables per day to get the recommended fiber.
- B. One hour or more of moderate-intensity exercise per week will improve symptoms.
- C. Drink two times the normally recommended daily water intake.
- D. Biofeedback has been shown to be effective in treating functional constipation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Five servings of fruits/vegetables per day.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Normal fiber and fluid intake are recommended; excess water and biofeedback are not evidence-supported for routine functional constipation.

## Q3 — Constipation in Children (p. 469)
Which statement about disimpaction as initial therapy for functional constipation in children is correct?

- A. A digital rectal examination should always be performed before disimpaction.
- B. Polyethylene glycol (Miralax) is considered first-line treatment for disimpaction.
- C. Enemas are a reasonable first-line therapy for infants.
- D. Maintenance therapy is not needed if disimpaction is successful.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — PEG (Miralax) is first-line for disimpaction.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Oral PEG preferred over enemas; DRE not mandatory; maintenance therapy is essential after disimpaction.

## Q4 — Tendinopathies of the Foot and Ankle (p. 479)
Which statement about treatment of foot/ankle tendinopathies is correct?

- A. Discussing appropriate footwear and recommending orthotics are reasonable early interventions.
- B. NSAIDs are recommended to reduce inflammation in late-stage degeneration.
- C. Nitroglycerin patches have been associated with tendon rupture.
- D. Prolotherapy is recommended as first-line treatment in late disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Footwear/orthotics are reasonable early interventions.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Tendinopathy is degenerative not inflammatory → NSAIDs have limited late role; nitroglycerin patches may *help* (not cause rupture); prolotherapy is not first-line.

## Q5 — Tendinopathies of the Foot and Ankle (p. 479)
Which condition should be considered when evaluating a lateral ankle sprain?

- A. Posterior tibial tendinopathy.
- B. Peroneal tendinopathy.
- C. Pes planovalgus deformity.
- D. Tibialis anterior tendinopathy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Peroneal tendinopathy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The peroneal tendons run along the lateral ankle and are commonly injured with/mistaken for lateral ankle sprains.

## Q6 — Lung Cancer: Diagnosis, Treatment, Screening (p. 487)
A 52-year-old with 15-year smoking history (miner) presents worried about lung cancer. Which statement about diagnosis is correct?

- A. Cough is the most common presenting symptom.
- B. A negative chest radiograph reliably excludes the diagnosis.
- C. Chronic lung disease and asbestos exposure, but not radon, increase risk.
- D. The patient should be referred for bronchoscopy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Cough is the most common presenting symptom.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CXR does NOT exclude cancer; radon IS a risk factor; no symptoms/findings here warrant bronchoscopy.

## Q7 — Lung Cancer Screening (p. 487)
Which annual CT lung-cancer screening parameter matches the current USPSTF recommendation?

- A. Ages 55–80 with at least a 30 pack-year history.
- B. Ages 50–80 with a 20 pack-year history who currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years.
- C. Ages 50–80 who have ever smoked.
- D. Patients should have a 20-year life expectancy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Ages 50–80, ≥20 pack-years, current or quit <15 years.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> 2021 USPSTF update lowered age to 50 and pack-years to 20 (was 55/30). 🔑 "50–20–15."

## Q8 — Lung Cancer / smoking cessation (p. 487)
Before or after lung cancer screening, which counseling approach is recommended for patients who smoke?

- A. Cessation + CT screening does not decrease lung cancer mortality.
- B. Exercise training negatively affects quality of life in lung cancer.
- C. Screening coupled with cessation support may yield higher quit rates than cessation support alone.
- D. Quitting will not reduce overall cancer risk.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Screening + cessation support → higher quit rates.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Screening visits are a teachable moment; cessation amplifies the mortality benefit of screening.

## Q9 — Evaluation After a First Seizure in Adults (p. 507)
Parents witnessed their 21-year-old lose consciousness. Which finding most likely indicates the event was a seizure?

- A. Head turning or twisting during the event.
- B. Lightheadedness before the event.
- C. Nausea on presentation.
- D. Shortness of breath on presentation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Head turning/twisting during the event.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Lateralizing motor features (head version, automatisms) favor seizure; presyncopal lightheadedness favors syncope.

## Q10 — First Seizure / anti-seizure medication (p. 507)
Which statement about anti-seizure medication after a first seizure is correct?

- A. It is appropriate for all patients after a first seizure.
- B. It reduces seizure recurrence and improves quality of life.
- C. It reduces seizure recurrence during the first two years of use.
- D. Adverse effects are uncommon.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Reduces recurrence during the first two years.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Early treatment lowers short-term recurrence but does NOT improve long-term remission or QoL; not indicated for everyone; AEs are common.

## Q11 — First Seizure / counseling (p. 507)
Which statement about counseling patients after a first seizure is correct?

- A. Federal law bars driving for 12 months after a first seizure.
- B. Physicians are required to report seizures in every state.
- C. Anti-seizure medication does not affect the chance of driving at two years.
- D. A safety companion is recommended when bathing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A safety companion is recommended when bathing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Driving rules are state-specific (not federal); mandatory reporting varies by state. Bathing/swimming safety precautions advised.

## Q12 — STIs: 2021 CDC Guidelines (p. 514)
Which statement about urethritis in women is correct?

- A. Mycoplasma genitalium is the most common causative agent.
- B. Urethral culture is the preferred initial testing method for gonorrhea/chlamydia.
- C. Ceftriaxone monotherapy is the preferred treatment for gonorrhea urethritis.
- D. Test-of-cure should be performed 14 days after treatment of gonorrhea urethritis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ceftriaxone monotherapy is preferred for gonococcal urethritis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> 2021 CDC: ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose; add doxycycline only if chlamydia not excluded. NAAT (not culture) is the preferred test.

## Q13 — STIs / Trichomonas (p. 514)
Which statement about vaginal Trichomonas vaginalis infection is correct?

- A. Women with HIV should be tested for T. vaginalis annually.
- B. A wet mount of vaginal fluid is highly sensitive for T. vaginalis.
- C. First-line treatment is a single 2-g dose of metronidazole.
- D. Partners should not be treated presumptively.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Women with HIV should be tested annually.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Wet mount has poor sensitivity (~50–65%, NAAT preferred); 2021 CDC first-line for women is metronidazole 500 mg BID ×7 days (not single 2-g); partners ARE treated.

## Q14 — STIs / syphilis screening in pregnancy (p. 514)
A 22-year-old, 14 weeks pregnant, currently homeless. Which statement about syphilis screening is correct?

- A. A single test at this visit is sufficient.
- B. Homelessness is associated with higher risk of contracting syphilis.
- C. Treponemal antibody testing is useful in those with a known history of syphilis.
- D. Screening is not necessary because congenital syphilis rates are low.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Homelessness is associated with higher syphilis risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> High-risk pregnant patients are rescreened in the third trimester and at delivery; treponemal tests stay positive for life so they're not useful to track reinfection; congenital syphilis is rising.

## Q15 — Child Abuse: Approach and Management (p. 521)
Which is recommended when evaluating an 11-year-old with depression and substance abuse history for maltreatment?

- A. Both parents should be present for the duration of the interview.
- B. The Pediatric Hurt-Insult-Threaten-Scream-Sex screening tool can help identify child abuse.
- C. Pharmacotherapy should not be used for temporary management.
- D. Parents should be assured CPS will not be contacted.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — The pediatric HITSS screening tool can help identify abuse.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Interview the child separately from parents; never falsely promise no CPS involvement.

## Q16 — Child Abuse (p. 521)
Which finding is highly suggestive of physical child abuse?

- A. Abrasions on both knees.
- B. Localized swelling on the forehead.
- C. Unpatterned ecchymoses on shins.
- D. Scalding immersion burns.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Scalding immersion burns.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Patterned/immersion burns (stocking-glove distribution) are classic for abuse; shin/knee bruises and forehead bumps are common accidental injuries in mobile children.

## Q17 — Cochrane: Reduced Dietary Salt in CKD (p. 466)
A 65-year-old with stage 3 CKD asks about modifying diet. Which statement about adjusting salt intake is correct?

- A. Reducing salt lowers systolic and diastolic blood pressure.
- B. Reducing salt decreases albuminuria.
- C. NIDDK recommends total dietary salt <2,300 mg/day for CKD.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Salt restriction lowers BP and albuminuria; <2,300 mg/day sodium recommended (long-term hard outcomes still uncertain).

## Q18 — Cochrane: Kinesiology Taping for Rotator Cuff Disease (p. 467)
Which statement about kinesiology taping vs sham/other nonsurgical treatments is correct?

- A. Little to no effect on mean overall pain.
- B. Little to no effect on mean active range of motion.
- C. Little to no effect on mean function.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer

> Cochrane found kinesiology taping provides little to no benefit on pain, ROM, or function for rotator cuff disease.

## Q19 — AHRQ: Physical Activity and Health of Wheelchair Users (p. 534)
Which statement about physical activity in wheelchair users/at-risk is correct?

- A. It reduces risk of MI and stroke.
- B. It reduces incidence of hypertension and obesity.
- C. It improves function in patients with cerebral palsy and multiple sclerosis.
- D. It is not beneficial if unable to reach recommended weekly activity levels.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Improves function in cerebral palsy and MS.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Evidence supports functional gains; data insufficient for cardiovascular/metabolic endpoints; any activity (even below targets) is beneficial.

## Q20 — Practice Guideline: STIs (CDC) (p. 553)
A 23-year-old MSM with urethritis and a partner with gonorrhea; NAAT pending; weighs 100 kg. Which antibiotic course does the CDC recommend?

- A. Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM + azithromycin 1 g PO.
- B. Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM + azithromycin 1 g PO.
- C. Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM + doxycycline 100 mg PO BID ×7 days.
- D. Ceftriaxone 1 g IM + doxycycline 100 mg PO BID ×7 days.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM + doxycycline 100 mg BID ×7 days.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> 2021 CDC: ceftriaxone 500 mg IM (≥150 kg → 1 g); azithromycin dropped for chlamydia coverage in favor of doxycycline when chlamydia not excluded.

## Q21 — Practice Guideline: Psoriasis Topical Therapy (AAD) (p. 558)
Moderate plaque psoriasis on extensor elbows and posterior neck at the hairline. Which initial treatment does the AAD recommend?

- A. Clobetasol propionate 0.05% ointment (class 1 corticosteroid).
- B. Triamcinolone acetonide 0.1% cream (class 4 corticosteroid).
- C. Desonide 0.05% gel (class 6 corticosteroid).
- D. Aloe vera ointment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Triamcinolone acetonide 0.1% (class 4, mid-potency).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> *Answer confirmed by official key; nuance only.* Mid-potency balances efficacy with the sensitive posterior-neck/hairline site; super-high-potency clobetasol fits thick plaques but is riskier at thinner skin, and class 6/aloe are too weak for moderate plaques.
