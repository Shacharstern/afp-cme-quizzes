## Q1 — Post-ICU Care in the Outpatient Setting (p. 590)

A 64-year-old patient is having a follow-up visit after ICU discharge for sepsis. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. A home exercise regimen will not be helpful if the patient is deconditioned.
- B. Sepsis-related ICU admissions are associated with a greater risk of critical illness polyneuropathy.
- C. Critical illness myopathy does not respond to physical therapy.
- D. If the patient experiences insomnia, a short-acting benzodiazepine is the best initial treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Sepsis-related ICU admissions carry greater risk of critical illness polyneuropathy.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Sepsis/SIRS, prolonged mechanical ventilation, and hyperglycemia are major risk factors for critical illness polyneuropathy/myopathy. Exercise/PT helps deconditioning and myopathy (A, C wrong); benzodiazepines are avoided for post-ICU insomnia (delirium/dependence risk, D wrong).

## Q2 — Post-ICU Care (p. 590)

Which one of the following statements about psychological complications after ICU discharge is correct?

- A. A depression screening tool designed specifically for ICU-discharge patients should be used.
- B. No interventions have been shown to reduce the risk of developing PTSD.
- C. PTSD is uncommon in family members of patients discharged from an ICU.
- D. A regular exercise regimen may be useful for improving cognitive impairment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A regular exercise regimen may help cognitive impairment.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Post-intensive care syndrome includes cognitive impairment that may improve with physical activity/rehabilitation. ICU diaries reduce PTSD (B wrong); PTSD is common in family members ("PICS-Family", C wrong); standard validated depression screens (e.g., PHQ-9) are used (A wrong).

## Q4 — Dyspareunia in Women (p. 597)

Which one of the following presentations of dyspareunia is most likely due to inadequate vaginal lubrication?

- A. A 27-year-old with vaginal discharge and abnormal odor.
- B. A 32-year-old breastfeeding woman 3 months postpartum with pain mainly at vaginal entry.
- C. A 22-year-old with pain at vaginal entry two months after a sexual assault.
- D. A 47-year-old with 6 months of pain on deep penetration and painful defecation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Breastfeeding woman 3 months postpartum, entry pain.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Lactation suppresses estrogen → vaginal atrophy/dryness → introital (entry) dyspareunia. A suggests infection, C suggests vaginismus/trauma-related, D suggests deep/structural cause (e.g., endometriosis).

## Q5 — Dyspareunia in Women (p. 597)

A 61-year-old postmenopausal patient describes vaginal dryness. Which statement about treatment is correct?

- A. Estrogen improves symptoms in up to 90% of postmenopausal women.
- B. Ospemifene (Osphena) is reasonable in a patient with known DVT history.
- C. Vaginal laser therapy is first-line for postmenopausal vaginal dryness.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Estrogen improves symptoms in up to ~90% of postmenopausal women.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Vaginal estrogen is highly effective for genitourinary syndrome of menopause. Ospemifene carries a thromboembolism warning (contraindicated with prior VTE, B wrong); laser is not first-line (C wrong); lubricants/moisturizers do help (D wrong).

## Q7 — Hypothyroidism: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 605)

In which one of the following situations would thyroid hormone therapy be beneficial for subclinical hypothyroidism?

- A. For any nonpregnant adult.
- B. When thyroid peroxidase antibodies are elevated.
- C. When the TSH level is less than 10 mIU per L.
- D. For patients older than 60 years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — When thyroid peroxidase (TPO) antibodies are elevated.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> In subclinical hypothyroidism, treatment is most justified when TSH ≥10, when symptomatic, or when TPO antibodies are positive (higher progression risk to overt disease). Treatment is *not* indicated for every nonpregnant adult (A), is generally withheld when TSH <10 and asymptomatic (C), and is approached cautiously (not routinely) in those >60 (D). Of the listed options, elevated TPO antibodies is the recognized indication.

## Q8 — Hypothyroidism (p. 605)

Which one of the following statements about patients with hypothyroidism who become pregnant is correct?

- A. There is a pregnancy-associated increased levothyroxine requirement.
- B. TSH levels are increased in the first trimester because of increased hormone levels.
- C. Patients should decrease their levothyroxine dose after learning they are pregnant.
- D. All patients should be screened with a TSH test during pregnancy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Pregnancy increases the levothyroxine requirement.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Known hypothyroid patients typically need a ~25–30% dose increase early in pregnancy. hCG suppresses TSH in the first trimester (B wrong); the dose is increased, not decreased (C wrong); USPSTF/ACOG recommend *targeted*, not universal, thyroid screening in pregnancy (D wrong).

## Q9 — Hypothyroidism (p. 605)

Which one of the following statements about hypothyroidism in older patients is correct?

- A. Levothyroxine should be taken with food.
- B. Even a mildly elevated TSH level is significant in patients older than 60 years.
- C. Subclinical hypothyroidism is associated with ischemic heart disease and heart failure.
- D. Patients older than 60 years should be started on lower dosages of levothyroxine (25 to 50 mcg per day).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Start older patients on lower levothyroxine doses (25–50 mcg/day).** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> In older/cardiac patients, levothyroxine is started low and titrated to avoid precipitating angina/arrhythmia. Levothyroxine is taken on an empty stomach (A wrong); mildly elevated TSH is often an age-related shift and may not be significant (B wrong). Option C states an association sometimes cited for subclinical hypothyroidism — listed in the to-verify line as a plausible alternative.

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Asymptomatic Carotid Artery Stenosis (p. 623)

According to the USPSTF recommendation, which statement about screening for asymptomatic carotid artery stenosis is correct?

- A. The USPSTF found adequate evidence that screening reduces stroke, MI, and death.
- B. The USPSTF found that screening in the general population has no benefit and may be harmful.
- C. The USPSTF found insufficient evidence to assess benefits and harms.
- D. The USPSTF found the magnitude of harms to be substantial.
- E. The USPSTF found convincing evidence that potential benefits outweigh harms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Screening the general (asymptomatic) population has no net benefit and may be harmful.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF gives a **D recommendation** against screening asymptomatic adults for carotid artery stenosis — harms (false positives, confirmatory angiography risk, perioperative stroke/death from intervention) outweigh benefits. This is not an "insufficient evidence/I statement" (C wrong).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Key Potentially Inappropriate Drugs in Pediatrics — The KIDs List (p. 633)

According to the KIDs List, which one of the following medications should always be avoided in neonates?

- A. Antidepressants.
- B. Aspirin.
- C. Fluoroquinolones.
- D. Meperidine (Demerol).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Meperidine (Demerol).** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Meperidine is flagged on the KIDs List as a drug to avoid because the neurotoxic metabolite normeperidine accumulates (immature renal clearance in neonates) → seizure risk, with safer opioid alternatives available. Aspirin (Reye risk) and fluoroquinolones (cartilage) are restricted in children but the "always avoid in neonates" descriptor best fits meperidine.
