## Q1 — Plantar Fasciitis (p. 744)

A patient has sharp pain in the anteromedial aspect of the heel, especially with his first steps of the morning. The pain resolves with activity but returns at the end of the day. Which one of the following findings is most closely associated with this condition?

- A. A pop followed by sudden onset of pain.
- B. Tenderness to palpation on the proximal plantar fascial insertion at the anteromedial calcaneus.
- C. Night pain.
- D. Pain with resistance to foot inversion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Tenderness at the proximal plantar fascia insertion (anteromedial calcaneus).** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Classic plantar fasciitis: **first-step morning pain** with point tenderness at the medial calcaneal tubercle. A "pop"/sudden pain suggests **rupture** (A); **night pain** is a red flag for other pathology (C); inversion-resistance pain points elsewhere (D).

## Q2 — Plantar Fasciitis (p. 744)

A patient with a four-week history of heel pain has ultrasound-confirmed plantar fascia thickness greater than 4.0 mm. Which one of the following accurately represents the most effective treatment for this condition?

- C. Ultrasound-guided corticosteroid injections are superior to ultrasound-guided botulinum toxin injections.

## Q3 — Plantar Fasciitis (p. 744)

Plantar fasciitis is suspected in a patient presenting with heel pain. The pain is reproduced by forced dorsiflexion of the toes at the metatarsophalangeal joints with the ankle stabilized. Which one of the following statements about the suspected diagnosis is correct?

- A. A heel spur on radiography is pathognomonic for plantar fasciitis.
- B. A calcaneal stress fracture on magnetic resonance imaging is highly suggestive of the diagnosis.
- C. The symptoms will likely improve within a year with nonoperative therapy.
- D. The physical examination finding effectively rules out plantar fasciitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Symptoms will most likely improve within a year with nonoperative care.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Plantar fasciitis is **self-limited** (~80–90% resolve within a year). Heel spurs are common and **not** pathognomonic (A); a calcaneal stress fracture is a **different** diagnosis (B); the positive **windlass test supports**, not excludes, plantar fasciitis (D).

## Q4 — Diabetic Kidney Disease: Diagnosis, Treatment, and Prevention (p. 751)

Which one of the following statements about screening for and diagnosing diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is correct?

- A. Physicians should start screening patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus for renal complications five years after the diagnosis is made.
- B. Patients taking glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists can prevent the progression of diabetic retinopathy.
- C. An elevated spot albumin/creatinine ratio should be repeated two or three times to make a diagnosis of microalbuminuria.
- D. A patient's level of albuminuria is not correlated to the risk of developing end stage renal disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Confirm microalbuminuria with 2–3 elevated spot ACR results over 3–6 months.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Type 2 diabetes is screened **at diagnosis** (the 5-year rule is for type 1, A); GLP-1 agonists don't prevent retinopathy (some signal of worsening, B); albuminuria **strongly correlates** with ESRD risk (D wrong).

## Q5 — Diabetic Kidney Disease (p. 751)

Which one of the following statements about the development and progression of DKD in patients with type 2 diabetes is correct?

- A. The development of macroalbuminuria is associated with a higher annual risk of death than progression to end stage renal disease.
- B. After urine tests show more than 300 mg of albumin per g per day, progression of DKD cannot be further delayed.
- C. If a patient taking an angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor demonstrates progression of DKD, the addition of an angiotensin receptor blocker is recommended.
- D. The risk of coronary artery disease is independent of the progression of DKD.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — With macroalbuminuria, the annual risk of death exceeds the risk of reaching ESRD.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Most DKD patients **die (usually cardiovascular) before reaching dialysis**. Progression can still be slowed after macroalbuminuria (B wrong); **don't combine ACE inhibitor + ARB** (C wrong); CAD risk **rises with** DKD progression (D wrong).

## Q6 — Bartholin Duct Cyst and Gland Abscess: Office Management (p. 760)

Which one of the following procedures to treat a Bartholin duct cyst or gland abscess should be avoided because of high rates of lesion recurrence?

- A. Alcohol sclerotherapy.
- B. Simple incision and drainage.
- C. Fistulization using a Word catheter.
- D. Marsupialization.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Simple incision and drainage (high recurrence).** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Simple I&D relieves pain but recurs frequently because no drainage tract is maintained. **Word catheter fistulization** and **marsupialization** create a lasting epithelialized tract → lower recurrence (C, D).
