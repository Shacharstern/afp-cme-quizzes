## Q1 — Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (p. 88)

Which one of the following physical examination tests is used to assess for tight hamstrings associated with patellofemoral pain?

- A. Evaluation for the presence of pain with squatting.
- B. Patellar tilt test.
- C. Passive knee extension test.
- D. Q angle measurement.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The passive knee extension test (for hamstring tightness).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The patellar **tilt test** assesses lateral retinacular tightness, **Q angle** assesses alignment, and squatting reproduces pain — only the passive knee extension test gauges hamstring flexibility.

## Q2 — Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (p. 88)

Which one of the following adjunctive therapies for patellofemoral pain syndrome improved pain when added to exercise?

- A. Knee braces.
- B. Kinesiotaping.
- C. Patellofemoral mobilization.
- D. Therapeutic ultrasound.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Kinesiotaping (added to exercise) improved pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Exercise is the core therapy; **taping** is a useful adjunct. Bracing, joint mobilization, and therapeutic ultrasound lack added benefit (A, C, D).

## Q3 — Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (p. 88)

Which one of the following classes of medications is preferred for short-term pain relief in patients with patellofemoral pain syndrome?

- A. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.
- B. Glucocorticoids.
- C. Glycosaminoglycan polysulphates.
- D. No analgesic is preferred over others.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs provide modest **short-term** pain relief. Glucocorticoids and glycosaminoglycan polysulphates aren't recommended for PFPS (B, C).

## Q4 — Neuropsychological Evaluations in Adults (p. 101)

Which one of the following is an indication for neuropsychological testing?

- A. When a 10- to 15-minute assessment is needed.
- B. To help determine a patient's decision-making capacity in persons with cognitive impairment.
- C. As an alternative to magnetic resonance imaging.
- D. To determine if brain activity is reduced in specific regions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — To help determine decision-making capacity in cognitive impairment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Neuropsychological testing is **detailed/lengthy** (not a 10–15 minute screen, A), assesses **function/cognition** rather than structure or regional activity (C, D — those are imaging roles).

## Q5 — Neuropsychological Evaluations in Adults (p. 101)

Which one of the following statements best describes the role of neuropsychological testing in the diagnosis of traumatic brain injury?

- A. Results are predictive of adaptive functioning.
- B. It cannot distinguish noncognitive factors from cognitive dysfunction.
- C. It cannot detect malingering.
- D. Severe injury reduces the predictive accuracy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Results predict adaptive (real-world) functioning.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Neuropsychological testing **can** separate cognitive from noncognitive contributors (B wrong), includes **validity/effort measures to detect malingering** (C wrong), and remains predictive across injury severity (D wrong).

## Q6 — Neuropsychological Evaluations in Adults (p. 101)

Which one of the following statements about neuropsychological testing is correct?

- A. Most patients report that testing is stressful.
- B. Insurance coverage is available only for patients with mild traumatic brain injury.
- C. It can help to determine driving risk in patients with cognitive impairment.
- D. It cannot distinguish between different types of dementia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — It can help determine driving risk in patients with cognitive impairment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most patients **don't** find it overly stressful (A); coverage isn't limited to mild TBI (B); and the cognitive profile **does** help differentiate dementia subtypes (D wrong).

## Q7 — Primary Care for Persons Who Inject Drugs (p. 109)

Which one of the following statements about naloxone administered for opioid overdose is correct?

- A. It can cause respiratory depression.
- B. Prescriptions should be given to patients at high risk of opioid overdose.
- C. Seeking medical attention after administration is optional.
- D. Observation is not necessary because readministration is rarely required.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Prescribe naloxone to patients at high risk of opioid overdose.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Naloxone **reverses** (doesn't cause) respiratory depression (A); patients still need **emergency care and observation** because its short half-life may require **redosing** as opioids outlast it (C, D wrong).

## Q8 — Primary Care for Persons Who Inject Drugs (p. 109)

Which one of the following is the most common reason for hospitalization in persons who inject drugs?

- A. Opioid-induced pulmonary edema.
- B. Infectious endocarditis.
- C. Bone and skeletal infections.
- D. Skin and soft tissue infections.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Skin and soft tissue infections.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Abscesses/cellulitis from injection are the **most common** infectious hospitalization in PWID. Endocarditis and osteomyelitis are serious but less frequent (B, C).

## Q9 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Risk Assessment for Cardiovascular Disease with Nontraditional Risk Factors (p. 123)

Which one of the following statements accurately summarizes the USPSTF findings about adding nontraditional cardiovascular risk factors to traditional risk assessment for cardiovascular disease (CVD) in asymptomatic adults?

- A. The USPSTF found adequate evidence that adding nontraditional risk factors to existing CVD risk assessment models leads to reduced incidence of CVD events or mortality.
- B. The USPSTF found that adding nontraditional risk factors to existing CVD risk assessment models has no net benefit.
- C. The USPSTF concluded with moderate certainty that the net benefit of adding nontraditional risk factors to existing CVD risk assessment models is small.
- D. The USPSTF concluded that the current evidence is insufficient to assess the balance of benefits and harms of adding nontraditional risk factors to existing CVD risk assessment models.
- E. The USPSTF recommends against adding nontraditional risk factors to existing CVD risk assessment models because the harms outweigh the benefits.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Insufficient evidence (I statement) for adding nontraditional risk factors (ABI, hsCRP, coronary artery calcium).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF could not determine the net benefit of adding ankle-brachial index, high-sensitivity CRP, or coronary artery calcium scoring to traditional risk assessment.

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: Colorectal Cancer Screening — ACS Updated Guideline for Adults with Average Risk (p. 129)

According to the American Cancer Society (ACS), which one of the following statements about screening for colorectal cancer is correct?

- A. Screening with visual examination is preferred over stool testing.
- B. Frail adults at average risk of colorectal cancer with a shorter life expectancy should still be screened through 75 years of age.
- C. Screening should be discouraged in patients older than 85 years.
- D. Screening beginning at 45 years of age is based on a strong recommendation from the ACS.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Screening is discouraged after age 85.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The ACS treats visual and stool-based tests as **acceptable options** (no preference, A); screening should be **individualized by health/life expectancy** (not by a blanket age 75, B); and starting at **45 is a qualified ("opt-in"), not strong, recommendation** (D wrong). After 85, screening is discouraged.
