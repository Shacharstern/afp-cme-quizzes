## Q1 — Nutrition Support Therapy (p. 580)
Which statement about nutrition support therapy in adults is correct?

- A. There is one preferred malnutrition screening tool.
- B. Use a predictive formula (e.g., Mifflin-St Jeor) to calculate resting metabolic rate.
- C. Enteral nutrition should be delayed 48 hours after lower GI surgery.
- D. Use parenteral nutrition in nonsevere acute pancreatitis if support is needed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Use Mifflin-St Jeor to estimate resting metabolic rate.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Several validated screening tools exist (no single preferred one); early enteral feeding is favored; enteral (not parenteral) is preferred in pancreatitis when the gut works.

## Q2 — Nutrition Support Therapy (p. 580)
In which hospitalized patient is enteral nutrition support appropriate?

- A. Small intestinal fistula.
- B. Multisystem organ failure.
- C. Swallowing difficulty.
- D. Not expected to eat for 24 hours.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Swallowing difficulty.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> "If the gut works, use it." Dysphagia with a functional GI tract → enteral feeding. Fistula/MSOF are relative contraindications; 24 h NPO doesn't warrant support.

## Q3 — Nutrition Support Therapy (p. 580)
Appropriate frequency of lab monitoring for a patient on parenteral nutrition?

- A. Daily until PN is discontinued.
- B. Daily until stable, then at least weekly.
- C. Only when dehydrated or volume overloaded.
- D. Only glucose daily; other tests as needed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Daily until stable, then at least weekly.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Frequent early monitoring to catch refeeding/electrolyte shifts, then spaced out once stable.

## Q4 — Septic Arthritis: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 589)
A patient with no systemic symptoms has knee swelling/pain. Which statement about evaluation is correct?

- A. Procalcitonin is diagnostic.
- B. A synovial WBC <50,000/µL excludes septic arthritis.
- C. Blood cultures should be obtained regardless of bacteremia suspicion.
- D. Synovial fluid analysis and culture should be obtained before starting antibiotics.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Aspirate (synovial analysis + culture) before antibiotics.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> No serum marker is diagnostic; septic arthritis can occur with WBC <50,000; blood cultures are guided by suspicion of bacteremia.

## Q5 — Septic Arthritis (p. 589)
After arthrocentesis confirms septic arthritis of the knee, which treatment approach is correct?

- A. Switching IV to oral antibiotics may be appropriate after initial surgery/arthrocentesis.
- B. Empiric therapy should broadly cover gram-negatives.
- C. Surgical management is indicated in most cases.
- D. Debridement is contraindicated in MRSA joint infection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — IV-to-oral switch is reasonable after initial drainage/surgery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Empiric coverage targets gram-positives (incl. MRSA); many cases managed with repeated aspiration; debridement is part of MRSA management, not contraindicated.

## Q6 — Septic Arthritis (p. 589)
Septic arthritis 2 months after knee arthroplasty. Which statement is correct?

- A. A sinus tract to the joint rules out infection.
- B. Arthritis-dermatitis syndrome is a common presentation of prosthetic joint infection.
- C. No further confirmation is needed if two joint cultures grow the same organism.
- D. The patient needs antibiotic prophylaxis before future urologic procedures.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Two cultures with the same organism confirm prosthetic joint infection.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A sinus tract is itself a major diagnostic criterion (doesn't exclude); arthritis-dermatitis is gonococcal; routine prophylaxis for urologic procedures isn't recommended.

## Q7 — Cannabis Essentials (p. 598)
Which condition has evidence of benefit from cannabinoids?

- A. Schizophrenia with psychosis.
- B. Violent behavior.
- C. Neuropathic pain.
- D. Neurodegenerative disorders.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Neuropathic pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Best evidence is for chronic/neuropathic pain, chemo nausea, and spasticity. Cannabis can *worsen* psychosis.

## Q8 — Cannabis Essentials (p. 598)
Which statement about cannabis use in patients on long-term opioid therapy is correct?

- A. Cannabis can be used to wean off opioids.
- B. Concurrent cannabis use should be avoided.
- C. Opioid dosing should be increased when using cannabis.
- D. Double the cannabis dose to overcome opioid tolerance.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Avoid concurrent cannabis with chronic opioids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Combined use increases sedation/respiratory and misuse risk; no good evidence cannabis safely facilitates opioid tapering.

## Q9 — Cannabis Essentials (p. 598)
Frequent cannabis user with anxiety, hunger, tachycardia, reddened eyes. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Cannabis withdrawal syndrome.
- B. Cannabis use disorder.
- C. Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome.
- D. Cannabis intoxication.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cannabis intoxication.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Acute intoxication: tachycardia, conjunctival injection, increased appetite, anxiety/euphoria. Withdrawal causes anorexia/irritability; CHS causes cyclic vomiting.

## Q10 — Thyroiditis: Evaluation and Treatment (p. 609)
Which statement about Hashimoto thyroiditis is correct?

- A. Exam typically reveals a large isolated nodule.
- B. About 95% have anti-thyroid peroxidase antibodies.
- C. Older patients should start higher-dose levothyroxine (~100 mcg/day).
- D. Recheck TSH every 12 weeks during levothyroxine initiation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — ~95% have anti-TPO antibodies.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Gland is diffusely enlarged (not a solitary nodule); start low-dose levothyroxine in older/cardiac patients; recheck TSH ~6 weeks after dose changes.

## Q11 — Thyroiditis (p. 609)
A patient 2 months postpartum with hyperthyroidism diagnosed as postpartum thyroiditis. Which statement is correct?

- A. ~70% recurrence with future pregnancies.
- B. Likely hyperthyroid phase for two years postpartum.
- C. May develop exophthalmos.
- D. Doesn't change lifetime risk of permanent hypothyroidism.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — ~70% recur in subsequent pregnancies.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Hyperthyroid phase is transient (weeks); no eye disease (that's Graves'); increases lifetime risk of permanent hypothyroidism.

## Q12 — Thyroiditis (p. 609)
A 42-year-old, SARS-CoV-2 one month earlier, anterior neck pain, fatigue, tender swollen thyroid. Which statement is correct?

- A. Caused by autoantibodies to TSH receptors.
- B. May develop difficulty swallowing.
- C. >50% chance of permanent hypothyroidism after resolution.
- D. Corticosteroids are not effective for the pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — May develop dysphagia from a swollen, tender gland.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Subacute (granulomatous/de Quervain) thyroiditis is post-viral, painful; most recover euthyroid; NSAIDs/corticosteroids relieve pain. (TSH-receptor antibodies = Graves'.)

## Q13 — Community-Acquired Pneumonia in Children (p. 618)
Which statement about clinical diagnosis of pediatric CAP is correct?

- A. Grunting and retractions are uncommon but strongly suggest pneumonia.
- B. Presence/absence of fever is not associated with CAP.
- C. Assessment of retractions and respiratory rate has poor interrater reliability.
- D. Overall clinical impression has poor accuracy for CAP.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Grunting/retractions are uncommon but highly suggestive.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Fever is associated with CAP; respiratory rate/retraction assessment has reasonable reliability; gestalt clinical impression performs fairly well.

## Q14 — Community-Acquired Pneumonia in Children (p. 618)
Which is NOT an important factor in initial antibiotic selection for pediatric CAP?

- A. Age.
- B. Treatment setting.
- C. Vaccination status.
- D. Respiratory rate.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Respiratory rate.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Age, setting (inpatient/outpatient), and vaccination status drive pathogen likelihood and drug choice; RR gauges severity but not the specific antibiotic.

## Q15 — Hepatitis C: Diagnosis and Management (p. 626)
Which statement about current HCV screening guidelines is correct?

- A. Patients <18 should not be screened.
- B. Pregnant patients screened only if not previously tested.
- C. Anti-HCV antibody testing is the preferred initial screening test.
- D. Point-of-care tests shouldn't be used (PPV <50%).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Anti-HCV antibody is the preferred initial screen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Screen all adults ≥18 once and every pregnancy; reflex to HCV RNA if antibody-positive. POC antibody tests are acceptable.

## Q16 — Hepatitis C (p. 626)
Which statement about acute HCV infection is correct?

- A. HCV RNA isn't detectable until two months after exposure.
- B. Fewer than half of patients clear the virus spontaneously.
- C. Hepatitis B surface antigen is associated with lower clearance.
- D. Male sex increases chances of clearing the virus.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — <50% clear HCV spontaneously.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most progress to chronic infection. HCV RNA is detectable within 1–2 weeks; female sex and favorable IL28B genotype increase clearance.

## Q17 — Hepatitis C (p. 626)
Which statement about chronic HCV infection is correct?

- A. Fulminant hepatitis occurs in 10%.
- B. Up to 30% develop cirrhosis over 25–30 years.
- C. Female sex is a risk factor for progression to cirrhosis.
- D. Patients should not receive hepatitis A/B vaccines.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Up to ~30% develop cirrhosis over 25–30 years.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Fulminant hepatitis is rare; male sex/alcohol accelerate fibrosis; HAV and HBV vaccination ARE recommended in chronic HCV.

## Q18 — Cochrane: NSAIDs/Acetaminophen for Postpartum Perineal Pain (p. 575)
Which statement about treating postpartum perineal pain is correct?

- A. A single oral NSAID dose reduces the need for further analgesia.
- B. A single oral acetaminophen dose reduces the need for further analgesia.
- C. ACOG recommends opioids only for breakthrough pain.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Single-dose NSAID and acetaminophen both help; opioids reserved for breakthrough pain (stepwise analgesia).

## Q19 — Cochrane: Trigger Finger — NSAID vs Steroid Injection (p. 577)
Which statement about NSAID vs steroid injection for trigger finger is correct?

- A. No difference in reducing symptoms over 12–24 weeks.
- B. No difference in improving active motion over 12–24 weeks.
- C. No difference in patient satisfaction over 12–24 weeks.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The single trial found no significant difference between NSAID and steroid injection across symptoms, motion, and satisfaction.

## Q21 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Gestational Diabetes (p. 641)
A 28-year-old at 30 weeks, no prior GDM screening, first pregnancy, no comorbidities. Per USPSTF, most appropriate approach?

- A. Screen for gestational diabetes at this visit.
- B. Don't screen — presents after 28 weeks.
- C. Don't screen — no risk factors.
- D. Don't screen — harms outweigh benefits.
- E. Refer to a diabetes educator for home glucose monitoring.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Screen at this visit.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF: screen all asymptomatic pregnant patients at ≥24 weeks (Grade B). Never previously screened and now at 30 weeks → screen now.

## Q22 — Lown Right Care: Prediabetes Diagnosis (p. 649)
Which statement about prediabetes and diabetes in adults is correct?

- A. Most with fasting glucose 126–140 mg/dL develop clinical diabetes within 10 years.
- B. Older adults with prediabetes are more likely to progress than younger adults.
- C. About 7 people need 3 years of intensive lifestyle/weight coaching to prevent one progression to diabetes.
- D. Treating prediabetes with metformin reduces vascular complications.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — NNT ≈ 7 over 3 years of lifestyle intervention.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Older adults often *regress* to normoglycemia or die of other causes before progressing; metformin hasn't shown vascular outcome benefit in prediabetes.

## Q23 — Practice Guideline: Lyme Disease (IDSA/AAN/ACR) (p. 652)
A 51-year-old found an attached Ixodes tick. In which situation is single-dose doxycycline prophylaxis appropriate?

- A. Tick found and removed on the first day of the trip.
- B. Patient presents one week after returning home.
- C. Tick removed two days after return; patient contacted you the day of removal.
- D. The patient has a rash consistent with erythema migrans.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Removed within ~72 h and presenting within 72 h of removal.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Prophylaxis requires: identified *Ixodes*, attached ≥36 h, started within 72 h of removal, endemic area. Brief attachment (A) doesn't qualify; >72 h delay (B) is too late; EM (D) means treat for disease, not prophylaxis.

## Q24 — Practice Guideline: Microscopic Hematuria (AUA) (p. 655)
A 51-year-old woman, 5 RBC/hpf, 22 pack-year history (quit 10 years ago), no symptoms or family history. Per AUA, most appropriate evaluation?

- A. Repeat urinalysis in six months.
- B. Cystoscopy and CT urography.
- C. Cystoscopy and MR urography.
- D. Cystoscopy and renal ultrasonography.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cystoscopy and renal ultrasonography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> AUA 2020 risk strata: age 50–59, 10–30 pack-years, and 3–25 RBC/hpf = **intermediate risk** → cystoscopy + renal ultrasound. CT urography is reserved for *high*-risk (≥60, >30 pack-years, >25 RBC/hpf, or gross hematuria).
