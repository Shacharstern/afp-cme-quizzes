## Q1 — Treatment of the Common Cold (p. 281)

Which one of the following effects is typically achieved with the use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs in adults with the common cold?

- A. Reduced cough.
- B. Shorter duration of symptoms.
- C. Reduced pain and sneezing.
- D. Reduced total symptom score.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Reduced pain and sneezing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs ease the **analgesic/discomfort** symptoms (headache, myalgia, sneezing) but do **not** shorten illness duration, reduce cough, or improve the overall symptom score (A, B, D wrong).

## Q2 — Treatment of the Common Cold (p. 281)

Which one of the following statements about zinc for the common cold in adults is correct?

- A. There is consistent evidence of a reduction in symptom duration when treatment begins within 24 hours of symptom onset.
- B. There is consistent evidence of a reduction in symptom severity when treatment begins within 24 hours of symptom onset.
- C. There is consistent evidence of a reduction in both symptom duration and severity when treatment begins within 24 hours of symptom onset.
- D. There is consistent evidence of no therapeutic effect.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Consistent evidence of reduced symptom duration when started within 24 hours.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Zinc, begun **within 24 hours**, shortens cold **duration**; the evidence for reducing **severity** is inconsistent (B, C overstate), and there is a real (if modest) effect (D wrong). Counsel on GI side effects/anosmia risk with intranasal forms.

## Q3 — Treatment of the Common Cold (p. 281)

Which one of the following interventions is effective for children with the common cold?

- A. Antihistamines.
- B. Antitussives.
- C. Intranasal corticosteroids.
- D. Acetylcysteine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Acetylcysteine (mucolytic).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mucolytics (acetylcysteine/carbocysteine) have some benefit in children >2 years. **Antihistamines, antitussives, and intranasal steroids are not effective** for the pediatric common cold (A, B, C).

## Q4 — Top POEMs of 2018 (Choosing Wisely) (p. 290)

Which one of the following statements about musculoskeletal conditions is correct?

- A. Arthroscopic subacromial decompression surgery is rarely performed.
- B. Two recent randomized controlled trials identified a subgroup of patients with subacromial pain for whom the benefits of decompression surgery outweigh the harms.
- C. Compared with a benztropine placebo, amitriptyline at a dosage of 25 mg per day reduced disability from chronic lower back pain at three months.
- D. Compared with a benztropine placebo, amitriptyline at a dosage of 25 mg per day improved chronic lower back pain at six months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Low-dose amitriptyline reduced chronic low back pain disability at three months.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The POEM showed amitriptyline 25 mg improved **disability at 3 months** (benefit not clearly sustained to pain scores at 6 months, D). Subacromial decompression is **common but not better than placebo/no benefit subgroup** (A, B wrong).

## Q5 — Top POEMs of 2018 (p. 290)

Which one of the following statements about infections is correct?

- A. Broad-spectrum antibiotics are associated with more adverse events than narrow-spectrum antibiotics in children with acute respiratory tract infections.
- B. Narrow-spectrum antibiotics are associated with higher rates of treatment failure than broad-spectrum antibiotics in children with acute respiratory tract infections.
- C. Nasal saline irrigation alone is statistically inferior to irrigation plus intranasal steroids for chronic sinusitis.
- D. Intranasal steroids are unlikely to have clinically important benefits over nasal saline irrigation alone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Broad-spectrum antibiotics cause more adverse events than narrow-spectrum in pediatric ARTIs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Narrow-spectrum antibiotics work **as well** (no higher failure, B wrong) with **fewer adverse effects** — supporting Choosing Wisely. The sinusitis options misstate the saline/steroid comparison (C, D).

## Q6 — Top POEMs of 2018 (p. 290)

Which one of the following statements about hypertension definition and treatment thresholds to reduce cardiovascular events is correct?

- A. Using the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association's lower hypertension definition and treatment threshold provides benefits similar to higher targets regardless of baseline cardiovascular risk.
- B. Patients at low risk of cardiovascular disease are most likely to benefit from a lower treatment threshold.
- C. Patients at high risk of cardiovascular disease are most likely to benefit from a lower treatment threshold.
- D. The patient's personal values and preferences are not relevant to blood pressure treatment goals.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — High-cardiovascular-risk patients benefit most from a lower treatment threshold.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Benefit tracks **baseline absolute risk** — treating lower-risk patients to lower targets yields little benefit with more harm (A, B wrong). Shared decision-making **does** matter (D wrong).

## Q7 — Venous Ulcers: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 298)

Which one of the following statements about venous ulcers is correct?

- A. Symptoms are at their worst first thing in the morning.
- B. Pruritus is not a symptom and should prompt an alternate diagnosis.
- C. Recurrence of venous leg ulcers is uncommon.
- D. They tend to appear on bony prominences.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — They tend to appear near bony prominences (the medial malleolus/gaiter area).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Venous ulcers classically sit in the **"gaiter" area around the medial malleolus**. Symptoms worsen **at the end of the day** with dependency (A reversed); **pruritus is common** (B wrong); **recurrence is frequent** (C wrong). *(Teaching nuance: venous ulcers are distinguished from pressure ulcers, which occur over sacrum/heels; answer confirmed by key.)*

## Q8 — Venous Ulcers (p. 298)

A patient presents with a venous ulcer and you are weighing which compression modalities to use for treatment. Which one of the following is correct?

- A. Unna boots are best used in nonambulatory patients.
- B. Leg elevation can prevent ulcer recurrence when used with compression.
- C. Unna boots should be reserved for highly exudative ulcers.
- D. Elastic compression bandages require frequent changes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Leg elevation, combined with compression, helps prevent ulcer recurrence.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Compression plus elevation is the cornerstone. Unna boots (inelastic) work best in **ambulatory** patients (calf-pump action) and aren't for highly exudative wounds (A, C wrong); elastic bandages need **less frequent** changes (D wrong).

## Q9 — Venous Ulcers (p. 298)

Which one of the following treatments is recommended for improved healing of venous ulcers?

- A. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy.
- B. Negative pressure wound therapy.
- C. Pentoxifylline.
- D. Phlebotonics.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pentoxifylline.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Pentoxifylline** (with compression) improves venous ulcer healing. Hyperbaric oxygen, negative-pressure therapy, and phlebotonics lack strong evidence for venous ulcers.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Use of Amphetamines for Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder in Adults (p. 278)

Which one of the following statements about the use of amphetamines for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder in adults is correct?

- A. They have been shown to reduce clinician-rated symptoms.
- B. They have been shown to reduce depression symptoms.
- C. They have not been shown to increase the risk of adverse effects, leading to discontinuation of treatment.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Amphetamines reduce clinician-rated ADHD symptoms.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> They improve core ADHD symptoms but do **not** treat depression (B wrong) and **do increase adverse effects** that prompt discontinuation (C wrong) — so "all of the above" is incorrect (D).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Treatment of Threatened Miscarriage with Progestogens (p. 279)

Which one of the following statements about the use of progestogens for treating threatened miscarriage is correct?

- A. When considered together, all oral preparations of progestogen decrease the risk of miscarriage.
- B. Vaginal preparations and oral preparations are equally effective.
- C. All preparations increase the risk of antepartum hemorrhage and hypertension.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Pooled oral progestogen preparations decrease the risk of miscarriage.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Oral progestogens reduced miscarriage in threatened miscarriage. Oral and vaginal routes were **not shown equivalent** (B), and progestogens did **not** increase antepartum hemorrhage/hypertension (C wrong).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Tonsillectomy in Children — AAO–HNS (p. 316)

According to the AAO–HNS, which one of the following patients is the best candidate for tonsillectomy?

- A. A child with a history of five recurrent throat infections in the past year with none in the past two years.
- B. A child with four recurrent throat infections in the past year and three in the year before, but with no throat infections three years ago.
- C. A child with obesity but no obstructive sleep-disordered breathing or tonsillar hypertrophy.
- D. A child with obstructive sleep-disordered breathing who also has tonsillar hypertrophy, poor school performance, and behavioral issues.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — The child with sleep-disordered breathing, tonsillar hypertrophy, and resulting school/behavior problems.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Obstructive sleep-disordered breathing with tonsillar hypertrophy and comorbidities (growth, school, behavior) is a strong indication. The recurrent-infection threshold (Paradise criteria: ~7 in 1 year, 5/yr × 2, or 3/yr × 3) isn't met by the patterns in A and B.
