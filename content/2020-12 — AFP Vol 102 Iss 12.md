## Q1 — Acute Chest Pain in Adults: Outpatient Evaluation (p. 721)

Which one of the following statements about acute coronary syndrome is correct?

- A. Typical chest pain is chest or arm discomfort that is not affected by exertion or rest.
- B. Unstable angina does not occur at rest.
- C. Acute myocardial infarction (MI) requires ST segment elevations on electrocardiography.
- D. It includes unstable angina, ST segment elevation MI, and non–ST segment elevation MI.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — ACS encompasses unstable angina, STEMI, and NSTEMI.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ACS is the umbrella term for these three. Typical angina **is** provoked by exertion and relieved by rest (A wrong); unstable angina **can** occur at rest (B wrong); NSTEMI is an MI without ST elevation (C wrong).

## Q2 — Acute Chest Pain in Adults (p. 721)

Which one of the following is the least common cause of chest pain in the primary care setting?

- A. Reflux esophagitis.
- B. Chest wall pain.
- C. Pneumonia.
- D. Costochondritis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pneumonia.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Chest wall/musculoskeletal pain** is the single most common cause in primary care; GI causes are also frequent. Pneumonia is comparatively uncommon among undifferentiated outpatient chest pain.

## Q3 — Acute Chest Pain in Adults (p. 721)

A 60-year-old man presents with recurrent episodes of substernal chest pain relieved by rest. ECG shows a new T-wave inversion in the anterior leads. Which one of the following steps is most appropriate?

- A. Schedule an exercise stress test with echocardiography.
- B. Schedule an exercise stress test with myocardial perfusion.
- C. Schedule coronary computed tomography angiography.
- D. Transport the patient immediately to the emergency department for further evaluation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Transport immediately to the emergency department.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Exertional/rest-relieved pain **plus new ischemic ECG changes** = unstable angina/ACS → emergent evaluation, not outpatient stress testing. Elective stress/CTA is for **stable, low-risk** patients (A–C wrong here).

## Q4 — Peripheral Neuropathy: Evaluation and Differential Diagnosis (p. 732)

A 57-year-old patient has had pain and weakness in the hands and feet for five months. Exam confirms bilateral hand and foot sensory deficits on monofilament testing. Which one of the following studies is indicated for further evaluation?

- A. No additional studies are indicated.
- B. Magnetic resonance imaging.
- C. Nerve conduction tests.
- D. Complete blood count; comprehensive metabolic profile; TSH, vitamin B12, and fasting glucose levels; and serum protein electrophoresis with immunofixation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Broad first-line laboratory panel (CBC, CMP, TSH, B12, fasting glucose, SPEP/immunofixation).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Initial workup of distal symmetric polyneuropathy targets the common reversible causes — **diabetes, B12 deficiency, thyroid, and paraproteinemia**. Nerve conduction/MRI come later for atypical or unexplained cases.

## Q5 — Peripheral Neuropathy (p. 732)

An older adult presents with numbness suspected to be peripheral neuropathy. Which one of the following findings would prompt referral for electrodiagnostic testing?

- A. Slow progressive onset.
- B. "Stocking and glove" distribution.
- C. Rapidly progressive clinical course.
- D. Elevated fasting blood glucose level.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Rapidly progressive clinical course.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Red-flag features — **rapid progression**, asymmetry, motor-predominant, or proximal involvement — warrant EMG/NCS to exclude treatable/dangerous causes (e.g., GBS, vasculitis). A slow, symmetric, stocking-glove pattern with diabetes is the typical, expected picture.

## Q6 — Peripheral Neuropathy (p. 732)

A patient presents with a symmetrical distal sensorimotor neuropathy and orthostatic intolerance. Initial labs, including blood glucose, are normal, but symptoms are worsening. Which one of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Testing for a hereditary neuropathy.
- B. Electrodiagnostic testing.
- C. Magnetic resonance imaging.
- D. Treatment with capsaicin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Electrodiagnostic testing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Worsening neuropathy with **autonomic features** and unrevealing labs → EMG/NCS to characterize the lesion and guide further targeted testing. Capsaicin treats symptoms without diagnosing (D); hereditary/MRI workup follows electrodiagnostics if needed.

## Q7 — Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy: Recognition and Management (p. 740)

Which one of the following history and physical examination findings suggests cervical myelopathy?

- A. Symptoms that tend to fluctuate.
- B. Loss of the triceps reflex in the presence of brisk biceps and brachioradialis reflexes.
- C. Deep tendon hyperreflexia.
- D. A negative Lhermitte sign.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Deep tendon hyperreflexia.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Myelopathy is an **upper-motor-neuron** process → hyperreflexia, clonus, Hoffmann/Babinski signs, and a **positive** Lhermitte sign. Symptoms are typically progressive, not fluctuating (A), and a focal reflex loss alone points to radiculopathy (B).

## Q8 — Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (p. 740)

You suspect your 55-year-old male patient has progressive degenerative cervical myelopathy. Which one of the following would be the first-choice imaging modality?

- A. Plain radiography.
- B. Computed tomography myelography.
- C. Magnetic resonance imaging with and without contrast.
- D. Imaging is not indicated.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — MRI with and without contrast.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> MRI best visualizes cord compression, signal change, and soft tissue. CT myelography is reserved for patients who **can't** have MRI (B); plain films don't show the cord (A).

## Q9 — Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (p. 740)

Which one of the following patients with degenerative cervical myelopathy should be referred for surgical consultation?

- A. Asymptomatic patients with an incidental finding of cord compression.
- B. Patients with stable, mild disease.
- C. Patients with moderate disease responding to physical therapy.
- D. Patients with mild, progressive disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Patients with mild, progressive disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Moderate/severe** or **progressive** myelopathy warrants surgical referral (decompression). Asymptomatic incidental compression and stable mild disease can be observed (A, B); responding-to-PT moderate disease is being managed conservatively (C).

## Q10 — Lown Right Care: Preoperative Evaluation and Frailty Assessment in Older Patients (p. 753)

Which one of the following statements about preoperative evaluation of older patients is correct?

- A. Preoperative health and functional status are not important considerations for operative risk assessment.
- B. Depression does not affect postoperative risk.
- C. All surgical procedures are considered high risk for patients who are frail or very frail.
- D. Preoperative risk reduction interventions have been extensively tested in surgical patients who are frail.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — In frail/very frail patients, all surgical procedures are considered high risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Frailty amplifies operative risk regardless of the nominal procedure risk. Functional status **is** central to risk (A wrong); depression **worsens** outcomes (B wrong); risk-reduction interventions are **not** well-tested in frail surgical patients (D wrong).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Off-Label Use and Misuse of Hormone Supplements — AACE/ACE (p. 759)

A 60-year-old patient has fatigue, low energy, low libido, and a testosterone level of 290 ng/mL. According to AACE/ACE, which one of the following treatments is most likely to improve symptoms with limited adverse effects?

- A. Exercise, weight loss, and improved sleep.
- B. Weekly testosterone injections.
- C. Thyroid support supplements.
- D. Adrenal support supplements.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Exercise, weight loss, and improved sleep.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For borderline/low-normal testosterone with nonspecific symptoms, **lifestyle measures** are first-line and lowest-risk. Empiric testosterone is not warranted here (B); "thyroid/adrenal support" supplements are unproven and discouraged (C, D).
