## Q1 — ADHD in Children: Common Questions and Answers (p. 592)

Which one of the following statements about ADHD for preschool children is correct?

- A. Administration of diagnostic tools should be delayed until the child is six years of age.
- B. Medication is preferred over psychosocial intervention.
- C. Parent training in behavior management is a first-line therapy.
- D. Electrocardiography is required if methylphenidate (Ritalin) is being considered.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Parent training in behavior management is first-line for preschoolers.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For ages 4–5, **behavioral parent training comes before medication** (methylphenidate only if behavioral therapy fails and impairment is moderate-severe). Diagnosis can be made before age 6 (A wrong); routine ECG isn't required without cardiac history (D wrong).

## Q2 — ADHD in Children (p. 592)

A parent is concerned about emotional lability in their middle school child with ADHD and wants to switch from stimulants to a "natural" treatment. Which one of the following is correct?

- A. Elimination diets are recommended.
- B. Omega-3 fatty acid supplementation has been shown to be effective.
- C. Implementing a sleep management plan improves symptoms and health-related quality of life.
- D. Acupuncture and mindfulness training are recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A sleep management plan improves symptoms and quality of life.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Optimizing sleep meaningfully helps ADHD symptoms. Omega-3 has only marginal effect (B); elimination diets, acupuncture, and mindfulness lack sufficient evidence to recommend (A, D wrong).

## Q3 — ADHD in Children (p. 592)

Which one of the following statements about treatment for ADHD is correct?

- A. Alpha-2 receptor agonists are more effective than stimulants for reducing symptoms of ADHD in adolescents.
- B. Atomoxetine (Strattera) is considered the initial treatment of choice.
- C. Stimulants should be started at the highest effective dose and titrated down.
- D. Gastrointestinal symptoms are among the most common adverse effects of stimulant and nonstimulant medications.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — GI symptoms are among the most common adverse effects of stimulant and nonstimulant medications.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Stimulants are first-line and most effective** (A, B wrong) and are started **low and titrated up** (C wrong). Appetite suppression and GI upset are common across both drug classes.

## Q4 — Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease: Diagnosis and Management (p. 603)

Which one of the following factors indicates the highest risk of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)?

- A. Dyslipidemia.
- B. Genetic factors.
- C. Obesity.
- D. Type 2 diabetes mellitus.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Obesity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Obesity carries the strongest association — NAFLD prevalence reaches ~75–90% in severe obesity. Type 2 diabetes and dyslipidemia are major comorbid risk factors but obesity is the dominant driver in this article.

## Q5 — Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (p. 603)

Which one of the following statements is consistent with current NAFLD screening recommendations?

- A. All adults with obesity should be screened using the NAFLD Fibrosis Score or the Fibrosis-4 Score.
- B. All adults with obesity should be screened with ultrasonography.
- C. Patients with diabetes who are also overweight or obese should be screened with the NAFLD Fibrosis Score or the Fibrosis-4 Score.
- D. Routine screening is not recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Routine screening is not recommended.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Major societies (AASLD) and USPSTF do **not** recommend routine NAFLD screening — even in high-risk groups — because of uncertain test performance and treatment benefit. NAFLD is usually found incidentally.

## Q6 — Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (p. 603)

A patient with obesity was recently diagnosed with NAFLD and hypercholesterolemia, on moderate-intensity statin therapy for four months. Liver enzymes are normal. Which one of the following statements about her statin therapy is correct now that she has known NAFLD?

- A. She should be switched to a low-intensity statin.
- B. Statin therapy should be continued.
- C. The risk of cardiovascular disease is now decreased so the dose should be reduced.
- D. Statin therapy should be discontinued.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Statin therapy should be continued.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Statins are **safe in NAFLD/NASH** and these patients are high cardiovascular risk — continue therapy and monitor. NAFLD is not a reason to lower the dose or stop (A, C, D wrong).

## Q7 — Oral Manifestations of Commonly Prescribed Drugs (p. 613)

Which one of the following statements about medication-induced gingival enlargement is correct?

- A. The patient's gums will eventually adapt to the medication.
- B. Stopping the causative medication does not reverse changes.
- C. Oral hygiene measures can help prevent this condition.
- D. Topical folic acid is often implicated in the development of this condition.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Good oral hygiene helps prevent gingival enlargement.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Meticulous plaque control reduces drug-induced gingival overgrowth (classic culprits: phenytoin, cyclosporine, calcium channel blockers). Changes often **regress** after stopping the drug (B wrong); folic acid may actually *help*, not cause it (D wrong).

## Q8 — Oral Manifestations of Commonly Prescribed Drugs (p. 613)

A patient presents with discoloration of the gums; the cause appears to be pigmentation of the underlying bone. Which one of the following is the most likely cause?

- A. Minocycline (Minocin).
- B. Inhaled corticosteroids.
- C. Losartan (Cozaar).
- D. Bisphosphonates.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Minocycline.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Minocycline causes blue-gray **bone/mucosal and tooth pigmentation**. Inhaled steroids → oral candidiasis; bisphosphonates → osteonecrosis of the jaw; losartan isn't a pigmentation cause.

## Q9 — Oral Manifestations of Commonly Prescribed Drugs (p. 613)

A patient on multiple medications presents with ropey saliva, lack of saliva pooling, atrophy of the dorsal-tongue filiform papillae, fissured tongue, and dental caries. After judicious medication review, which one of the following treatments would be appropriate?

- A. Topical folic acid.
- B. Chlorhexidine gluconate (Peridex).
- C. Sucking on sugary, hard candies.
- D. Cevimeline.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cevimeline.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The picture is **xerostomia** (often anticholinergic burden). A muscarinic agonist (**cevimeline** or pilocarpine) stimulates saliva. Sugary candies worsen caries (C wrong); chlorhexidine and folic acid don't treat dry mouth (A, B wrong).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Behavioral Counseling to Prevent STIs (p. 623)

A 24-year-old cisgender woman presents for a routine exam. She uses an etonogestrel implant, does not drink or inject drugs, has had three male partners in the past year with inconsistent condom use, had chlamydia at 17, and had normal cervical cytology at 21. According to the USPSTF recommendation on preventing STIs, which one of the following factors indicates she is at increased risk?

- A. She is younger than 25 years.
- B. She uses long-acting, reversible contraception.
- C. She was screened for cervical cancer at 21 years of age.
- D. She has multiple sex partners.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Multiple sex partners (with inconsistent condom use).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Although age <25 is a risk factor for *chlamydia/gonorrhea screening*, the USPSTF STI **behavioral-counseling** risk markers are behavioral — new/multiple partners, inconsistent condom use, a partner with an STI. LARC use and prior cervical screening don't raise STI risk (B, C wrong).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Autism Spectrum Disorder — AAP (p. 629)

The parents of a five-year-old with autism (already receiving ABA, speech, and occupational therapy) ask about additional therapies. Which one of the following may be considered according to AAP guidelines?

- A. Chelation.
- B. Hyperbaric oxygen.
- C. Antidepressant medication.
- D. Melatonin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Melatonin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Melatonin is reasonable for the **sleep problems** common in ASD. Chelation and hyperbaric oxygen are **harmful/ineffective** and recommended against (A, B); antidepressants aren't core ASD therapy and carry pediatric cautions (C).
