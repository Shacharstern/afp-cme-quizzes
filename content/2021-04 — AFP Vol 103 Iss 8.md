## Q1 — Interpreting SARS-CoV-2 Diagnostic Tests (p. 465)

Which one of the following statements about SARS-CoV-2 antigen tests is correct?

- A. Antigen tests have uniformly high sensitivity.
- B. The accuracy of antigen tests is similar for symptomatic and asymptomatic patients.
- C. Antigen tests are used primarily for testing children.
- D. Antigen tests have uniformly high specificity.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Antigen tests have uniformly high specificity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Rapid antigen tests are highly *specific* (few false positives) but variably *sensitive* (A wrong). Sensitivity is higher in symptomatic than asymptomatic people (B wrong).

## Q2 — Interpreting SARS-CoV-2 Diagnostic Tests (p. 465)

Which one of the following statements about when SARS-CoV-2 antigen tests are most sensitive is correct?

- A. At the time of initial exposure to a symptomatic person.
- B. Around the time of symptom onset.
- C. About five to seven days after symptom onset.
- D. Accuracy does not vary with timing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Around the time of symptom onset.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Antigen sensitivity peaks when viral load is highest — the first ~5–7 days of symptoms, around onset. It is poor at the moment of exposure (incubation) and declines later in the course.

## Q3 — Interpreting SARS-CoV-2 Diagnostic Tests (p. 465)

Which one of the following statements about false-positive and false-negative test results is correct?

- A. False-positive results are more likely when disease prevalence is low.
- B. False-negative results are more likely when disease prevalence is low.
- C. Prevalence does not significantly impact false-positive rates.
- D. Prevalence does not significantly impact false-negative rates.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — False positives are more likely when prevalence is low.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Positive predictive value falls as prevalence drops, so a larger share of positives are false. (Bayes: low pre-test probability → more false positives among all positives.)

## Q4 — Mifepristone and Misoprostol for Early Pregnancy Loss and Medication Abortion (p. 473)

A healthy patient with reliable menstrual history presents with a 7-week pregnancy she wants to terminate, choosing mifepristone + misoprostol. Which statement is correct?

- A. Serial beta-hCG must confirm viable pregnancy before proceeding.
- B. Ultrasonography must be performed to confirm gestational age.
- C. At greater than 6 weeks, the patient is no longer eligible for this regimen.
- D. If the patient has an IUD, ultrasonography should be performed because of higher ectopic risk.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — With an IUD in place, perform ultrasonography (higher proportion of ectopics).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> With a reliable LMP and no risk factors, routine ultrasound/serial hCG is not required before medication abortion (A, B wrong), and the regimen is used up to 10–11 weeks (C wrong). An IUD raises the relative likelihood that a pregnancy is ectopic → image first.

## Q5 — Mifepristone and Misoprostol (p. 473)

Which one of the following is the most effective use of mifepristone and misoprostol for early pregnancy loss?

- A. Misoprostol-alone regimens.
- B. Misoprostol 800 mcg vaginally, repeated 72 hours later if no bleeding.
- C. Mifepristone 200 mg orally, followed by another dose in 48 hours.
- D. Mifepristone 200 mg orally, followed by 800 mcg misoprostol vaginally 24 to 48 hours later.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Mifepristone 200 mg PO, then misoprostol 800 mcg vaginally 24–48 h later.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The sequential mifepristone-then-misoprostol regimen is more effective than misoprostol alone (the NEJM Schreiber trial showed higher completion). Repeating mifepristone (C) is not the regimen.

## Q6 — Mifepristone and Misoprostol (p. 473)

Which one of the following statements about safety, tolerability, and effective use of mifepristone and misoprostol is correct?

- A. Nausea should be treated with antiemetics.
- B. NSAIDs are contraindicated during the first 3 to 5 hours of bleeding and cramping.
- C. Prophylactic antibiotics are recommended.
- D. Same-day insertion of an etonogestrel implant (Nexplanon) is contraindicated.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Treat nausea with antiemetics.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Antiemetics are appropriate supportive care. NSAIDs are *not* contraindicated and may be used for cramping (B wrong); routine prophylactic antibiotics are not recommended (C wrong); same-day implant placement is acceptable (D wrong).

## Q7 — Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome and Hypermobility Spectrum Disorders (p. 481)

Which one of the following statements about EDS classification is correct?

- A. Generalized joint hypermobility is required to diagnose hypermobile EDS.
- B. Hypermobility spectrum disorders occur only with extra-articular involvement.
- C. Patients with hypermobile EDS are most likely to have a diagnosable genetic syndrome.
- D. Patients with hypermobile EDS are commonly asymptomatic.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Generalized joint hypermobility is required to diagnose hypermobile EDS.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Hypermobile EDS (hEDS) requires generalized joint hypermobility plus systemic/family criteria. Uniquely among EDS subtypes, hEDS has **no** identified genetic test (C wrong); patients are typically symptomatic (D wrong).

## Q8 — Hypermobile EDS (p. 481)

Which one of the following is an appropriate diagnostic approach in patients with suspected hypermobile EDS?

- A. A positive Beighton score effectively rules out the diagnosis.
- B. Radiography can distinguish Marfan syndrome from hypermobile EDS.
- C. Genetic testing is confirmatory.
- D. Echocardiography should be used to look for aortic root dilation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Use echocardiography to look for aortic root dilation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Echocardiography screens for aortic root involvement (overlap with Marfan/vascular EDS). A positive Beighton score *supports* hypermobility (A wrong); no confirmatory genetic test exists for hEDS (C wrong); radiography does not differentiate Marfan (B wrong).

## Q9 — Hypermobile EDS (p. 481)

Which one of the following statements about management of hypermobile EDS and hypermobility spectrum disorders is correct?

- A. Tai chi is an effective therapy studied in patients with hypermobility spectrum disorders.
- B. Perioperative management is the same in patients with and without EDS.
- C. Cognitive behavior therapy was shown in small studies to be beneficial for anxiety and physical function.
- D. Splints and orthotics are contraindicated in hypermobile EDS.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — CBT showed benefit for anxiety and physical function in small studies.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Management is multidisciplinary; small studies support CBT. EDS patients need modified perioperative care (fragile tissues, B wrong); splints/orthotics can help joint stability (D wrong); tai chi has no specific evidence here (A wrong).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Hepatitis B Virus Infection in Adolescents and Adults (p. 493)

A 15-year-old born in Central America (immigrated 8 years ago) whose live-in sibling was just diagnosed with HBV asks about testing. They are not sexually active and have no drug use. According to the USPSTF, which is an appropriate next step?

- A. Check immunization records and screen only if not vaccinated as a child.
- B. Do not screen because the patient is not at high risk.
- C. Do not screen because the patient is younger than 18 years.
- D. Screen because all adolescents and adults should be screened periodically.
- E. Screen because the patient is at high risk.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **E — Screen because the patient is at high risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF (B recommendation) advises screening adolescents/adults at increased risk — including those born in regions of high/intermediate HBV endemicity and household contacts of HBV-infected persons. Both apply here. Prior vaccination does not remove the indication (A wrong).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Dyslipidemia Management for CVD Prevention — VA/DoD (p. 507)

A 56-year-old with diabetes and hypertension has an 8% 10-year estimated CVD risk and no known CVD. Which treatment do the VA/DoD guidelines recommend for primary prevention?

- A. No treatment.
- B. Omega-3 supplement.
- C. Moderate-dose statin.
- D. High-dose statin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Moderate-dose statin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> VA/DoD recommend a moderate-dose statin for primary prevention in patients with diabetes or 10-year risk ≥6–12%. High-dose statins are reserved for established ASCVD/secondary prevention (D); omega-3 is not recommended for prevention (B).
