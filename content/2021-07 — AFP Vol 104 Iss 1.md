## Q1 — Unintentional Weight Loss in Older Adults (p. 34)
Appropriate first step in evaluating unexplained, unintentional weight loss in older adults?

- A. History and physical examination.
- B. Colonoscopy.
- C. CT of the chest.
- D. Swallowing study.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — History and physical examination.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A thorough H&P (plus basic labs) directs targeted testing; broad imaging/endoscopy without clinical leads is low-yield.

## Q2 — Unintentional Weight Loss (p. 34)
Which is part of the "Meals on Wheels" mnemonic for unintentional weight loss?

- A. Satiety.
- B. Wasting syndrome.
- C. Religious dietary practices.
- D. Eating problems.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Eating problems.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> *Meals on Wheels* = Medications, Emotional (depression), Anorexia/Alcoholism, Late-life paranoia, Swallowing disorders, Oral problems, No money, Wandering (dementia), Hyperthyroidism, Enteric problems, **Eating problems**, Low-salt/low-cholesterol diets, Social/Shopping issues.

## Q3 — Unintentional Weight Loss (p. 34)
Which patient is at highest risk of unintentional weight loss?

- A. An 80-year-old healthy patient in assisted living with a spouse.
- B. A 70-year-old who lives alone with mild paranoia and alcoholism.
- C. A 75-year-old nonsmoker with mild dementia living with their daughter.
- D. An 85-year-old with prostate cancer living independently.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Lives alone, mild paranoia, and alcoholism.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Social isolation, psychiatric symptoms, and alcohol use cluster multiple Meals-on-Wheels risk factors, conferring the highest risk here.

## Q4 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2020 (p. 41)
Which statement about screening and prevention is correct?

- A. Daily step counts have no relationship to mortality.
- B. Bone mineral density testing should generally be repeated every three years.
- C. Aspirin is no longer recommended for primary prevention.
- D. Women vaccinated against HPV before age 17 have a *smaller* reduction in invasive cervical cancer than those vaccinated later.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Aspirin is no longer recommended for primary prevention.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Higher step counts *lower* mortality; HPV vaccination before 17 gives the *largest* cervical cancer reduction; aspirin's bleeding risk now outweighs primary-prevention benefit for most adults.

## Q5 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2020 (p. 41)
Which statement about treatment for musculoskeletal disorders is correct?

- A. For knee OA, corticosteroid injections are superior to physical therapy.
- B. In acute low back pain, adding a muscle relaxant to ibuprofen does not improve function or pain.
- C. Acetaminophen provides pain relief in acute low back pain.
- D. Acetaminophen is not better than placebo for migraine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Adding a muscle relaxant to ibuprofen doesn't improve acute LBP outcomes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PT is at least as good as steroid injection for knee OA; acetaminophen is *ineffective* for acute LBP but *does* help migraine — so C and D are reversed.

## Q6 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2020 (p. 41)
Which statement about respiratory disorders is correct?

- A. Combining a fear-based message with empowering information does not affect antibiotic-seeking behavior.
- B. Antibiotics, but not corticosteroids, improve COPD exacerbations.
- C. Antibiotics help inpatient severe exacerbations but not outpatient ones.
- D. The optimal dose or duration of corticosteroid treatment for COPD exacerbations is not known.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Optimal corticosteroid dose/duration for COPD exacerbations is unknown.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Both antibiotics and corticosteroids benefit exacerbations across settings; the precise steroid regimen remains uncertain (short courses, e.g. 5 days, are commonly used).

## Q7 — Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (p. 49)
Which correctly describes CRPS?

- A. Most cases involve the proximal part of an extremity.
- B. Nerve involvement is usually identified on nerve conduction testing.
- C. Trophic changes almost always suggest an alternative diagnosis.
- D. Injury or surgery is often the inciting event.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Injury or surgery is often the inciting event.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CRPS usually affects a *distal* extremity, nerve conduction is typically normal in CRPS type I, and trophic/skin changes are *characteristic* features, not exclusions.

## Q8 — Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (p. 49)
A patient with a well-healed lower-extremity fracture has burning pain and skin color changes. Which is correct when CRPS is suspected?

- A. Ultrasonography usually confirms CRPS.
- B. Cellulitis is a common ancillary finding in CRPS.
- C. Pain out of proportion to the injury is a defining characteristic.
- D. Weakness and dystonia rule out CRPS.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pain out of proportion to the inciting injury.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CRPS is a clinical diagnosis (Budapest criteria); no imaging confirms it. Motor findings (weakness, dystonia) can occur and don't exclude it.

## Q9 — Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (p. 49)
Which reflects an appropriate treatment approach for CRPS?

- A. The affected extremity should be immobilized.
- B. Complementary therapies generally shorten symptom duration.
- C. Osteopathic manipulation is best for hyperalgesia.
- D. Physical and occupational therapy are recommended to encourage use of the affected limb.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Physical/occupational therapy to maintain use of the limb.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Early mobilization and graded use (not immobilization) are central; immobilization worsens disuse and pain.

## Q10 — Management of Infants Born to Mothers with HIV (p. 58)
Which statement about perinatal HIV management is correct?

- A. Only high-risk pregnant women should be screened for HIV.
- B. Only pregnant women with viral loads >400 should start antiretroviral therapy.
- C. Cesarean delivery is not indicated for women with high viral loads.
- D. IV zidovudine (Retrovir) should be given only to women with HIV RNA >1,000 copies/mL.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Intrapartum IV zidovudine for maternal HIV RNA >1,000 copies/mL.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> All pregnant women are screened and all with HIV start ART; scheduled cesarean is recommended when viral load is high (>1,000) near delivery.

## Q11 — Management of Infants Born to Mothers with HIV (p. 58)
Appropriate initial HIV test for an infant born to a mother with HIV?

- A. HIV nucleic acid test.
- B. HIV antibody test.
- C. Combined HIV antigen/antibody assay.
- D. Testing is not required if the mother received intrapartum IV zidovudine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — HIV nucleic acid (virologic) testing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Maternal antibodies cross the placenta and persist for months, so antibody/combo assays can't diagnose infant infection — virologic (NAT/PCR) testing is required.

## Q12 — Management of Infants Born to Mothers with HIV (p. 58)
Regarding antiretroviral post-exposure prophylaxis within 6 hours of delivery for an infant born to a mother with HIV:

- A. Give only if maternal HIV RNA >1,000 copies/mL.
- B. Give only if the mother did not receive intrapartum IV zidovudine.
- C. Not necessary if the mother achieved viral suppression (<50 copies/mL).
- D. Administer to all infants born to mothers with HIV.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Give prophylaxis to all HIV-exposed infants.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Every exposed neonate receives prophylaxis promptly (regimen intensity depends on maternal viral control), regardless of maternal viral load or intrapartum zidovudine.

## Q13 — Pharmacologic Therapy for Acute Pain (p. 63)
A 60-year-old has acute wrist pain. Which statement about pain management is correct?

- A. Combining acetaminophen and NSAIDs is contraindicated.
- B. Acetaminophen's anti-inflammatory effects make it ideal.
- C. Relatively selective COX-2 inhibitors should be prescribed at the highest possible dose to increase selectivity.
- D. Topical NSAIDs are an effective option.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Topical NSAIDs are effective.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Topical NSAIDs work well for localized musculoskeletal pain with minimal systemic exposure. Acetaminophen + NSAID combination is safe and synergistic; acetaminophen has *no* anti-inflammatory effect; pushing COX-2 dose only raises cardiovascular/GI risk.

## Q14 — Pharmacologic Therapy for Acute Pain (p. 63)
A 35-year-old has neck pain after a soccer "header." Which statement is correct?

- A. Cardiovascular adverse effects are common with topical NSAIDs.
- B. Oxycodone 5 mg is the initial treatment of choice.
- C. A nonbenzodiazepine muscle relaxant may be beneficial.
- D. NSAID therapy should be escalated to the highest allowable dose to maximize anti-inflammatory effects.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A nonbenzodiazepine muscle relaxant may help.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For acute musculoskeletal neck/back pain, NSAIDs ± a short course of a nonbenzodiazepine muscle relaxant are reasonable; opioids are not first-line, and topical NSAIDs carry little systemic risk.

## Q15 — Pharmacologic Therapy for Acute Pain (p. 63)
A patient has a humeral fracture from a fall. Which statement about pain management is correct?

- A. Capsaicin and lidocaine augment pain relief.
- B. Dual-action medications (e.g., tramadol) are first-line for acute pain.
- C. Dual-action medications carry fewer risks than full-agonist opioids.
- D. NSAIDs may be considered for short-term relief when bleeding risk is low.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Short-term NSAIDs when bleeding risk is low.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs are effective short-term analgesics for fractures (mind bleeding/renal risk). Tramadol is not first-line and still carries opioid/serotonergic risks; topical agents aren't established for acute fracture pain.

## Q16 — Costochondritis: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 73)
Which statement about costochondritis and musculoskeletal chest pain is correct?

- A. Most adolescent chest pain is due to costochondritis.
- B. Costochondritis is most common in adolescents.
- C. Up to 45% of emergency department visits for chest pain are musculoskeletal.
- D. It is less common in women than in men.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Up to ~45% of ED chest-pain visits are musculoskeletal.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Musculoskeletal causes account for a large share of chest pain. Costochondritis is more common in adults and in women; most pediatric chest pain is idiopathic, not specifically costochondritis.

## Q17 — Costochondritis (p. 73)
Which statement about diagnosing costochondritis is correct?

- A. It typically presents as constant, unilateral pain.
- B. It typically presents as bilateral chest pain exacerbated by coughing, stretching, or deep breaths.
- C. Imaging tests should be used to diagnose it.
- D. There are no validated clinical prediction rules for diagnosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Bilateral pain reproduced by coughing, stretching, or deep breathing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Costochondritis is a clinical diagnosis: reproducible, often multilevel/bilateral chest-wall tenderness worsened by movement/breathing. Imaging only excludes other causes.

## Q18 — Costochondritis (p. 73)
Which statement about treatment and prognosis of costochondritis is correct?

- A. Pain resolves within three weeks in about 90% of patients.
- B. NSAIDs are contraindicated in most cases.
- C. Recurrence rates are high, with 20% recurring within one year.
- D. RCTs have demonstrated the effectiveness of acupuncture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — ~90% resolve within about three weeks.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Costochondritis is self-limited and managed with NSAIDs/reassurance. *Nuance:* a minority have more prolonged or recurrent symptoms, so the "90% in 3 weeks" figure is a generalization. Acupuncture lacks RCT support. Answer confirmed per key.

## Q19 — Cochrane: Acromioclavicular Joint Dislocation — Surgical vs Conservative (p. 28)
A 32-year-old has a grade IV AC joint dislocation after a bicycling accident. Which statement about management is correct?

- A. Refer for emergency surgical management.
- B. For shoulder function, conservative and surgical management are equally successful after one year.
- C. Conservative management is more likely than surgery to cause adverse effects.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Equivalent shoulder function at one year.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Per Cochrane, surgical and conservative management yield similar 1-year function, and *surgery* carries more adverse effects/complications. (Note: clinically, high-grade IV–VI injuries are still often referred for surgical consideration.)

## Q20 — Cochrane: Rapid Point-of-Care Tests for SARS-CoV-2 (p. 29)
Which statement about rapid point-of-care antigen and molecular tests is correct?

- A. Rapid antigen tests average ~68.9% sensitivity and perform best in symptomatic patients.
- B. Rapid molecular tests average ~95.1% sensitivity and perform best in symptomatic patients.
- C. The traditional test for SARS-CoV-2 is laboratory PCR.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Antigen tests are less sensitive (~69%) and best when viral loads are high (symptomatic); molecular POC tests are more sensitive (~95%); lab RT-PCR remains the reference standard.

## Q21 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Lung Cancer (p. 79)
A 68-year-old Black man, 39 pack-year smoking history, quit 6 years ago. Per the USPSTF, how should you counsel him about lung cancer screening?

- A. Screen with chest radiography.
- B. Do not screen because he quit more than five years ago.
- C. Screen with sputum cytology and biomarkers because he is younger than 70.
- D. Screen with low-dose CT.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Low-dose CT.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> 2021 USPSTF: annual low-dose CT for adults 50–80 with a ≥20 pack-year history who currently smoke or quit within 15 years. He qualifies (age 68, 39 pack-years, quit 6 years ago). Chest x-ray/sputum cytology are not recommended.

## Q22 — Practice Guidelines: COPD Treatment (VA/DoD) (p. 98)
Per the VA/DoD, which therapy is most effective for stable COPD?

- A. Monotherapy with a LABA.
- B. Monotherapy with a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA).
- C. Monotherapy with an inhaled corticosteroid (ICS).
- D. Combination ICS + LABA.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — LAMA monotherapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The VA/DoD favors a LAMA as the most effective single initial bronchodilator for stable COPD (reduces exacerbations more than LABA); ICS-containing regimens are reserved for specific phenotypes.

## Q23 — Practice Guidelines: COPD Exacerbations (AAFP) (p. 100)
Per the AAFP, which regimen is recommended for an acute COPD exacerbation?

- A. Short-duration oral corticosteroids and an antibiotic.
- B. Long-duration oral corticosteroids and magnesium sulfate.
- C. Short-duration oral corticosteroids and theophylline.
- D. Long-duration oral corticosteroids and a mucolytic.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Short-course oral corticosteroids plus an antibiotic.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A short course (≈5 days) of oral corticosteroids plus an antibiotic is recommended; magnesium, theophylline, and mucolytics are not standard exacerbation therapy.

## Q24 — Practice Guidelines: COPD Treatment (American Thoracic Society) (p. 102)
Per the ATS, which therapy is recommended for baseline treatment of symptomatic COPD?

- A. Monotherapy with a LABA.
- B. Monotherapy with an ICS.
- C. Combination LAMA + LABA.
- D. Combination ICS + LABA + LAMA.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — LAMA + LABA combination.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The ATS recommends dual long-acting bronchodilator therapy (LAMA + LABA) over monotherapy for symptomatic COPD; triple therapy is added for those with continued exacerbations (esp. with eosinophilia).
