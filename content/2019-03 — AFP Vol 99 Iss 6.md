## Q1 — Lower Extremity Peripheral Artery Disease: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 362)

Which one of the following statements about symptoms in patients known to have lower extremity peripheral artery disease (PAD) is correct?

- A. One-half of patients have leg symptoms, but the symptoms are different from classic claudication.
- B. Most patients have no leg symptoms at all.
- C. Dependent rubor is present in early disease.
- D. Most patients with symptoms experience intermittent claudication.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — About half have leg symptoms that are atypical (not classic claudication).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most symptomatic PAD is **atypical**, not classic intermittent claudication (D); a substantial proportion are asymptomatic but "most have none" overstates it (B); dependent rubor is a sign of **advanced** ischemia (C wrong).

## Q2 — Lower Extremity Peripheral Artery Disease (p. 362)

A resting ankle-brachial index (ABI) is performed on a patient. The results are normal, but PAD is still suspected because of the patient's symptoms. Which one of the following tests should be performed as the next step?

- A. Angiography.
- B. Cardiac exercise treadmill test.
- C. Exercise ABI test.
- D. Ultrasonography for abdominal aortic aneurysm.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Exercise (post-exercise) ABI test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A normal **resting** ABI doesn't exclude PAD when symptoms suggest it — an exercise ABI unmasks a pressure drop. Angiography is reserved for revascularization planning (A).

## Q3 — Lower Extremity Peripheral Artery Disease (p. 362)

Your patient with PAD takes aspirin and statins long-term. He now wakes at night with burning pain in his left forefoot for the past few days; sitting up and hanging the leg over the bed relieves it but the foot becomes red. Which one of the following is the correct response?

- A. Add clopidogrel (Plavix) to aspirin.
- B. Start a supervised exercise program.
- C. Start therapy with cilostazol (Pletal).
- D. Urgent referral to a vascular surgeon.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Urgent referral to a vascular surgeon.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Nocturnal/rest pain relieved by dependency (with dependent rubor) is **critical limb ischemia** — a limb-threatening emergency needing urgent revascularization. Exercise programs and cilostazol are for **claudication**, not critical ischemia (B, C).

## Q4 — Primary Care of Breast Cancer Survivors (p. 370)

A 62-year-old woman completed breast cancer treatment one year ago and is doing well. How often should she undergo mammography screening of the remaining breast tissue to detect recurrence?

- A. Every six months until year 5, then every year.
- B. Every year until year 5, then no further mammograms are needed.
- C. Every year.
- D. Every two years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Annual mammography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Breast cancer survivors get **annual** mammography of remaining breast tissue indefinitely to detect recurrence/new primaries — not stopping at 5 years (B) or every 2 years (D).

## Q5 — Primary Care of Breast Cancer Survivors (p. 370)

Which one of the following medications is **not** recommended for improving bone density in patients who have been treated for breast cancer?

- A. Denosumab (Prolia).
- B. Teriparatide (Forteo).
- C. Vitamin D.
- D. Zoledronic acid (Reclast).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Teriparatide (Forteo).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Teriparatide (an anabolic PTH analog) is **avoided in patients with prior malignancy/skeletal radiation** (osteosarcoma concern). Antiresorptives (bisphosphonates, denosumab) plus vitamin D are appropriate for treatment-related bone loss.

## Q6 — Right Care for Children: Top Five Do's and Don'ts (p. 376)

Which one of the following statements about the use of long-acting reversible contraception in adolescents is correct?

- A. It is less effective than oral contraception.
- B. It is more likely to be discontinued after two years.
- C. 2% to 5% of young women use it.
- D. It increases overall health care costs.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Only about 2% to 5% of young women use LARC.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> LARC is underused despite being **first-line** for adolescents — it is **more** effective than OCPs (A), has **higher** continuation (B), and is **cost-saving** (D wrong).

## Q7 — Right Care for Children (p. 376)

Compared with expectant observation, immediate antibiotic treatment of acute otitis media reduces the risk of which one of the following outcomes?

- A. Abnormal tympanometry findings.
- B. Tympanic membrane perforation.
- C. Recurrence of acute otitis media.
- D. None of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — None of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Immediate antibiotics don't reduce abnormal tympanometry, perforation, or recurrence versus watchful waiting — supporting a **watchful-waiting** option in appropriate children.

## Q8 — Right Care for Children (p. 376)

Which one of the following statements about using preparticipation sports evaluations to identify athletes at risk for sudden cardiac death is correct?

- A. They often do not identify athletes at highest risk.
- B. They have low false-positive rates.
- C. They have low false-negative rates.
- D. The annual occurrence of sudden cardiac death in the United States is 15 per 150,000 athletes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — They often fail to identify the athletes at highest risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Preparticipation screening (including ECG-based) has **high false-positive and false-negative rates** (B, C wrong) and frequently misses at-risk athletes — its yield for preventing sudden cardiac death is limited.

## Q9 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Atrial Fibrillation with Electrocardiography (p. 383)

A 67-year-old woman with controlled hypertension and treated hypothyroidism, no symptoms, requests ECG to diagnose atrial fibrillation. According to the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF), should she be screened?

- A. No; the USPSTF recommends against screening for atrial fibrillation with ECG because it has no net benefit.
- B. Yes; the USPSTF found that the net benefit of screening for atrial fibrillation with ECG is substantial in patients 65 years or older.
- C. Yes; the USPSTF found that the net benefit of screening for atrial fibrillation with ECG is moderate, regardless of patient age.
- D. Uncertain; the USPSTF concludes that the current evidence is insufficient to assess the balance of benefits and harms of screening for atrial fibrillation with ECG in asymptomatic adults 65 years or older.
- E. Yes; the USPSTF found that the net benefit of screening for atrial fibrillation with ECG is moderate in adults without contraindications to anticoagulation therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Insufficient evidence (I statement) to assess benefits/harms of ECG screening for AF in asymptomatic adults ≥65.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The 2018 USPSTF gave an **I statement** for ECG screening for atrial fibrillation — neither recommending for nor against it.

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: HIV Prevention and Treatment with ART — International Antiviral Society (p. 395)

According to the International Antiviral Society, which one of the following statements about initiating antiretroviral therapy (ART) in patients with HIV is correct?

- A. ART is contraindicated in pregnancy.
- B. ART should not be started without the results of routine laboratory testing, including HIV-1 RNA levels, CD4 cell count, HIV genotyping, viral hepatitis, and blood chemistry panel.
- C. Most persons with opportunistic infections should receive ART within two weeks of diagnosis.
- D. Prophylaxis for Pneumocystis pneumonia is warranted for all patients with a CD4 count lower than 400 cells per µL.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Most patients with opportunistic infections should start ART within two weeks of diagnosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Early ART is favored (notable exceptions: cryptococcal/TB meningitis, where it's briefly delayed). ART is **essential in pregnancy** (A wrong); **don't delay** initiation awaiting labs (B wrong); PCP prophylaxis is for CD4 **<200** (D wrong).
