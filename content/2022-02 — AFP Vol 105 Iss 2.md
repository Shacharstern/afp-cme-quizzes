## Q1 — Fever of Unknown Origin in Adults (p. 137)
Which statement about the diagnosis of FUO is correct?

- A. Fever must be present for at least three weeks to meet the current definition.
- B. Most cases in developed countries are eventually found to be occult infections.
- C. Among diagnosed cases, infections, malignancies, or inflammatory diseases are leading causes.
- D. Discernible patterns are unlikely to suggest a specific cause.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Infections, malignancies, and inflammatory diseases are leading causes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Classic FUO triad of causes. Current definitions vary (3 weeks is the *classic* outpatient criterion but not absolute); fever patterns can give clues; a large share remain undiagnosed.

## Q2 — Fever of Unknown Origin (p. 137)
A 27-year-old back from rural South America, fevers to 102°F for 3 weeks. Which statement is correct?

- A. Malignancies are the most likely cause diagnosed early in FUO.
- B. A negative whole-blood IGRA rules out active pulmonary TB.
- C. Travel history should be disregarded to avoid unnecessary exotic testing.
- D. Recent travel means VTE should be considered, as it is a recognized cause of FUO.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — VTE is a recognized cause of FUO and travel raises that risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> *Answer confirmed by official key; nuance only.* Venous thromboembolism/PE is on the FUO differential and long travel is a risk factor. IGRA does NOT rule out active TB; travel history is essential, not disregarded.

## Q3 — Fever of Unknown Origin (p. 137)
Which statement about evaluation and treatment of FUO is correct?

- A. Neutropenic patients with FUO should be treated empirically with broad-spectrum antibiotics.
- B. Nuclear imaging has no role in FUO.
- C. Bone marrow biopsy is not useful for FUO in HIV patients.
- D. Few patients have symptom resolution without identifying a specific etiology.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Neutropenic FUO → empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Neutropenic fever is an emergency. Nuclear imaging (PET) does help; bone marrow biopsy is useful in HIV; many FUOs resolve spontaneously without a diagnosis.

## Q4 — Hemoptysis: Evaluation and Management (p. 144)
Which statement about massive hemoptysis is correct?

- A. Mortality is less than 20% if untreated.
- B. Clinical factors such as vital signs and ability to maintain an airway should define it.
- C. Exsanguination is the most threatening complication.
- D. It rarely requires ICU admission.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Define it by clinical/functional factors (airway, vitals).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Massive hemoptysis is defined functionally rather than by a strict volume cutoff. 🔑 Death is from **asphyxiation** (drowning in blood), NOT exsanguination — option C is the classic trap.

## Q5 — Hemoptysis (p. 144)
Which statement about evaluation of hemoptysis is correct?

- A. It is rare to be unable to determine the cause.
- B. Chest radiography has high sensitivity for site and etiology.
- C. CXR, CT, and CT angiography can all be appropriate imaging studies.
- D. Bronchoscopy should not be performed until a definitive cause is known.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — CXR, CT, and CTA can all be appropriate.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cause is often unknown (cryptogenic); CXR has limited sensitivity; bronchoscopy is itself diagnostic/therapeutic.

## Q6 — Hemoptysis (p. 144)
Which statement about management of hemoptysis is correct?

- A. Tranexamic acid is contraindicated.
- B. All patients with hemoptysis should be admitted.
- C. Surgery is first-line in most cases.
- D. Bronchial arterial embolization stops bleeding in at least 70% of cases.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Bronchial artery embolization succeeds in ≥70%.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Embolization is the preferred intervention for massive hemoptysis. TXA can help; not all hemoptysis needs admission; surgery is reserved for refractory cases.

## Q7 — Borderline Personality Disorder (p. 156)
Which clinical feature is a hallmark of borderline personality disorder?

- A. Flat affect.
- B. Denial of physical ailments.
- C. Over- and undervaluing other people.
- D. Excessively positive self-image.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Over-/undervaluing others (splitting).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Splitting (idealization/devaluation) plus unstable relationships, identity, and affect are core BPD features.

## Q8 — Borderline Personality Disorder (p. 156)
Which action should physicians take when caring for patients with BPD?

- A. Deflect threats of self-harm.
- B. Refer to another physician when they become difficult.
- C. Engage in intimate and familiar conversation.
- D. Set firm limits on manipulative behaviors.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Set firm, consistent limits.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Clear boundaries and continuity of care; never ignore self-harm threats or abandon the patient.

## Q9 — Borderline Personality Disorder (p. 156)
Which is first-line treatment for BPD?

- A. Psychotherapy.
- B. Quetiapine (Seroquel).
- C. SSRIs.
- D. Buprenorphine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Psychotherapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Evidence-based psychotherapy (DBT, MBT, etc.) is first-line; medications are adjunctive for specific symptoms only.

## Q10 — Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young (p. 162)
Which statement about the cause/pathophysiology of MODY subtypes 1–3 is correct?

- A. Impaired insulin secretion inherited autosomal dominant.
- B. Beta-cell damage usually causes complete insulin deficiency.
- C. Insulin resistance is the primary pathology.
- D. Islet cell autoantibodies found in >95%.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Autosomal dominant impaired insulin secretion.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> MODY is monogenic, autosomal dominant, antibody-negative, with preserved (not absent) insulin and no insulin resistance.

## Q11 — MODY (p. 162)
Recommended initial pharmacologic treatment for MODY2?

- A. A sulfonylurea.
- B. Insulin.
- C. A GLP-1 agonist.
- D. Pharmacologic treatment is not required.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — No pharmacologic treatment required.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> MODY2 (glucokinase mutation) causes mild, stable, nonprogressive hyperglycemia that needs no treatment outside pregnancy.

## Q12 — MODY (p. 162)
Recommended initial treatment for nonpregnant patients with MODY1 or MODY3 uncontrolled by lifestyle?

- A. A sulfonylurea.
- B. Insulin.
- C. DPP-4 inhibitor.
- D. Metformin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Sulfonylurea.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> MODY1 (HNF4A) and MODY3 (HNF1A) are exquisitely sensitive to low-dose sulfonylureas.

## Q13 — Common Sleep Disorders in Children (p. 168)
Which should be an integral part of sleep hygiene for children?

- A. Exercise before bedtime.
- B. Turning on an overhead light.
- C. Learning to fall asleep alone.
- D. Avoiding snacks at bedtime.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Learning to fall asleep alone.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Self-soothing/independent sleep onset reduces night wakings. Avoid pre-bed exercise and bright light; a light snack is fine.

## Q14 — Common Sleep Disorders in Children (p. 168)
Most appropriate method to diagnose OSA in a 4-year-old who snores with apneic episodes?

- A. Physical examination findings.
- B. Polysomnography.
- C. Home sleep apnea testing.
- D. Iron studies.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Polysomnography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In-lab PSG is the gold standard for pediatric OSA; home testing is not validated in young children.

## Q15 — Common Sleep Disorders in Children (p. 168)
Iron deficiency testing is warranted for which childhood sleep disorder?

- A. Insomnia.
- B. Delayed sleep phase syndrome.
- C. Parasomnias.
- D. Restless legs syndrome.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Restless legs syndrome.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> RLS (and periodic limb movements) is linked to iron deficiency; check ferritin and treat if low.

## Q16 — Cervical Ripening and Induction of Labor (p. 177)
A term patient asks what increases the chance of spontaneous vaginal delivery. Most likely beneficial?

- A. Acupuncture.
- B. A single dose of castor oil.
- C. Nipple stimulation.
- D. Sexual intercourse.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Nipple stimulation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Nipple stimulation releases endogenous oxytocin and has the best evidence among nonpharmacologic methods. Castor oil/intercourse/acupuncture have weak/unclear evidence.

## Q17 — Cervical Ripening and Induction of Labor (p. 177)
In which situation should prostaglandins be avoided during induction?

- A. If a balloon catheter is also being used.
- B. If any contractions are present.
- C. If the patient is nulliparous.
- D. If the patient has had a prior cesarean delivery.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Prior cesarean delivery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Prostaglandins raise uterine rupture risk after prior cesarean → avoided; mechanical methods (balloon) preferred in TOLAC.

## Q18 — Cervical Ripening and Induction of Labor (p. 177)
Which statement about oxytocin use during induction is correct?

- A. Low-dose protocols give shorter time to delivery than high-dose.
- B. Oxytocin is more effective with greater cervical dilation.
- C. Oxytocin is more effective at earlier gestational age.
- D. Oxytocin is more effective in higher BMI.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — More effective with greater cervical dilation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A more favorable (dilated) cervix responds better; high-dose protocols shorten time to delivery; higher BMI needs more oxytocin.

## Q19 — Cochrane: Behavioral Interventions for Smoking Cessation (p. 133)
Which statement about behavioral interventions (counseling, financial incentives, texting) is correct?

- A. Behavioral interventions are effective compared with no intervention.
- B. They add no benefit when pharmacotherapy is prescribed.
- C. They are associated with significant risks.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Effective vs no intervention.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Behavioral support works alone AND augments pharmacotherapy, with minimal risk.

## Q20 — Cochrane: Virtual vs In-Person Pulmonary Rehab (p. 135)
Which statement about virtual vs in-person pulmonary rehabilitation is correct?

- A. Virtual is as effective at increasing six-minute walking distance.
- B. Virtual is as effective at improving quality-of-life scores.
- C. Patients receiving virtual rehab are more likely to complete the program.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Virtual pulmonary rehab matches in-person on 6MWD and QoL and improves completion/adherence.

## Q21 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: Healthy Weight in Pregnancy (USPSTF) (p. 187)
Per USPSTF, to which group should physicians offer behavioral interventions to promote healthy gestational weight gain?

- A. Only patients with prior overweight/obesity.
- B. Only patients with a history of preeclampsia.
- C. All pregnant patients.
- D. Only patients with prior gestational diabetes.
- E. All patients planning or capable of pregnancy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — All pregnant patients.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF (B recommendation): offer behavioral counseling for healthy weight gain to *all* pregnant individuals, not a risk-selected subset.

## Q22 — AHRQ: Cervical Ripening in the Outpatient Setting (p. 193)
Which statement about cervical ripening is correct?

- A. Outpatient prostaglandin ripening causes more cesareans than inpatient.
- B. Outpatient mechanical ripening causes more uterine infections than inpatient.
- C. The most common reason for cervical ripening is postterm pregnancy.
- D. Mechanical methods are superior to prostaglandins.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Postterm pregnancy is the most common indication.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Outpatient vs inpatient outcomes are comparable; neither route nor method is clearly superior overall.

## Q23 — Practice Guideline: Antibiotic Courses (ACP) (p. 205)
A healthy 41-year-old with CAP on CXR, no recent hospitalization. Per ACP, which course is recommended?

- A. Doxycycline for five days.
- B. Doxycycline for 10 days.
- C. Levofloxacin for five days.
- D. Levofloxacin for 10 days.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Doxycycline for five days.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ACP "shorter is better": treat uncomplicated CAP for a minimum of 5 days (and until clinically stable/afebrile 48–72 h).

## Q24 — Practice Guideline: Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (ACC/AHA) (p. 207)
Per ACC/AHA, which activity is recommended for an asymptomatic 18-year-old with HCM (cousin died of SCD)?

- A. Avoiding commercial motor vehicle driving.
- B. Continuing moderate-intensity exercise.
- C. Only low-intensity exercise (golf, bowling).
- D. Moderate/vigorous exercise only after ICD placement.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Continue moderate-intensity exercise.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> 2020 ACC/AHA guideline liberalized recreational exercise in HCM — moderate-intensity activity is reasonable; decisions are individualized via shared decision-making (not blanket disqualification).
