# AFP CME Quiz — May 1, 2021 (Vol 103, Issue 9)

> [!info] Source & answer provenance

## Q1 — The Preparticipation Physical Evaluation (p. 539)

Which one of the following statements about preparticipation physical evaluations (PPEs) for student athletes is correct?

- A. History alone can detect most general medical conditions pertinent to sports participation.
- B. PPEs are routinely fully covered by health insurance.
- C. Strong evidence supports the effectiveness of PPEs in preventing sudden cardiac death.
- D. The American Heart Association recommends a one-time screening ECG for athletes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — History alone detects most relevant general medical conditions.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A focused history identifies ~75% of problems affecting athletes. Evidence that PPEs prevent sudden cardiac death is weak (C wrong); the AHA does **not** endorse routine screening ECG (D wrong); coverage is inconsistent (B wrong).

## Q2 — The Preparticipation Physical Evaluation (p. 539)

Which one of the following statements about mental health conditions in student athletes is correct?

- A. Routine screening for anxiety disorders is not currently recommended as part of a PPE.
- B. Students taking ADHD medications are not permitted to participate in college sports.
- C. Suicide is more common among college student athletes than all college students.
- D. The PHQ-9 is an appropriate screening instrument for use in school-aged athletes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — PHQ-9 is an appropriate screening instrument for school-aged athletes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Validated tools (e.g., PHQ-9) can be incorporated into the PPE for depression screening. Athletes on ADHD medication can compete with documentation/TUE (B wrong); athletes have *lower* suicide rates than non-athletes (C wrong).

## Q3 — The Preparticipation Physical Evaluation (p. 539)

Which one of the following statements about relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S) syndrome is correct?

- A. Athletes with RED-S tend to have higher than average bone density.
- B. RED-S is more common in male athletes than female athletes.
- C. Menstrual dysfunction is a common symptom of RED-S in female athletes.
- D. Oral contraceptive use is contraindicated in patients with RED-S.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Menstrual dysfunction is a common symptom in female athletes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> RED-S (formerly female athlete triad) features low energy availability → menstrual dysfunction → **low** bone density (A wrong). It is more common in females (B wrong); OCPs are not contraindicated (D wrong).

## Q4 — In-flight Medical Emergencies (p. 547)

Which one of the following is the most common category of in-flight medical emergencies in the U.S.?

- A. Stroke or transient ischemic attack.
- B. Allergic reaction.
- C. Obstetric emergencies.
- D. Syncope or near-syncope.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Syncope or near-syncope.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Syncope/presyncope is the single most common in-flight emergency (~30%), driven by cabin hypoxia, dehydration, and prolonged sitting. GI and respiratory complaints follow.

## Q5 — In-flight Medical Emergencies (p. 547)

Which one of the following is the most common medical reason for aircraft diversion in the U.S.?

- A. Obstetric emergencies.
- B. Possible stroke or TIA.
- C. Cardiac arrest.
- D. Allergic reactions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Cardiac arrest.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Although uncommon, cardiac arrest is the leading reason aircraft are diverted, given its lethality and the inadequacy of in-flight resuscitation. Most syncope events resolve without diversion.

## Q6 — In-flight Medical Emergencies (p. 547)

Which one of the following makes the final decision to divert an aircraft for an in-flight medical emergency?

- A. Medical volunteer responding to the emergency.
- B. Ground-based medical support.
- C. Pilot.
- D. Flight dispatch.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The pilot (captain).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The pilot in command holds final authority to divert, weighing medical advice (from responding volunteers and ground-based support) against operational/safety factors.

## Q7 — Urethritis: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 553)

Which one of the following regimens is recommended for empiric treatment of urethritis?

- A. A single 1-g dose of oral azithromycin.
- B. 100 mg of oral doxycycline twice per day for seven days.
- C. A single 500-mg dose of IM ceftriaxone plus 100 mg of oral doxycycline twice per day for seven days.
- D. A single 250-mg dose of IM ceftriaxone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — IM ceftriaxone 500 mg once + doxycycline 100 mg BID × 7 days.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Empiric therapy covers both gonorrhea and chlamydia. The 2020 CDC update raised ceftriaxone to 500 mg and replaced azithromycin with doxycycline as the chlamydia partner agent.

## Q8 — Urethritis (p. 553)

Which one of the following is the most common symptom in men with urethritis?

- A. Fever.
- B. Rectal pain or bleeding.
- C. Urethral pruritus.
- D. Mucopurulent urethral discharge.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Mucopurulent urethral discharge.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Discharge (often with dysuria) is the cardinal symptom of urethritis in men. Fever is uncommon; pruritus and rectal symptoms are not the predominant complaints.

## Q9 — Urethritis (p. 553)

Which one of the following is the most common cause of nongonococcal urethritis?

- A. Chlamydia trachomatis.
- B. Mycoplasma genitalium.
- C. Ureaplasma urealyticum.
- D. Trichomonas vaginalis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Chlamydia trachomatis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Chlamydia is the leading identifiable cause of NGU. M. genitalium is the next most common; Ureaplasma and Trichomonas are less frequent contributors.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Ketogenic Diets for Drug-Resistant Epilepsy (p. 524)

Which one of the following statements about dietary restrictions to treat drug-resistant epilepsy is correct?

- A. Ketogenic diets have been shown to decrease the risk of seizures in children.
- B. Ketogenic diets increase the risk of GI adverse effects in children.
- C. A modified Atkins diet has been proven to improve rates of seizure freedom in adults.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Ketogenic diets decrease seizure risk in children.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found ketogenic diets increase seizure freedom/reduction in children with drug-resistant epilepsy. Adult data (including modified Atkins) are low-certainty/not proven (C wrong); GI effects occur but that wasn't the supported conclusion making "all of the above" correct (B/D wrong).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Exercise for Dysmenorrhea (p. 525)

A 23-year-old with 4 years of menstrual cramps asks about low-risk treatment options. Which statement about treatment of dysmenorrhea is correct?

- A. Naproxen works better than other NSAIDs.
- B. Exercise therapy works alone and adds to the benefit of NSAID therapy.
- C. Aerobic exercise is more effective than other exercise for dysmenorrhea.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Exercise works alone and adds to NSAID benefit.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review supports exercise as effective monotherapy and as an adjunct to NSAIDs. No NSAID is clearly superior (A wrong); no specific exercise modality proved superior (C wrong).

## Q12 — Implementing AHRQ Reviews: Nonopioid Pharmacologic Treatments for Chronic Pain (p. 561)

Which one of the following nonopioid medications improves short-term pain in patients with chronic neuropathic pain?

- A. Tricyclic antidepressants.
- B. Gabapentin.
- C. Capsaicin patch.
- D. Medical cannabis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Gabapentin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The AHRQ review found gabapentin improves short-term neuropathic pain. (SNRIs such as duloxetine also have evidence; among the listed options gabapentin is the supported choice. TCA, capsaicin, and cannabis evidence was insufficient/weaker in this review.)

## Q13 — Practice Guidelines: Eosinophilic Esophagitis — AGA/JTF Guidelines (p. 573)

According to the AGA and JTF guidelines, which one of the following therapies is recommended for treatment of eosinophilic esophagitis?

- A. Infliximab (Remicade).
- B. Montelukast (Singulair).
- C. Oral steroids.
- D. Topical steroids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Topical (swallowed) steroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> AGA/JTF recommend swallowed topical corticosteroids (e.g., fluticasone, budesonide) over systemic/oral steroids for EoE. PPIs and dietary elimination are also first-line; infliximab and montelukast are not recommended.
