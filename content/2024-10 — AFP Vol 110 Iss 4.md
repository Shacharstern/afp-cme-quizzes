## Q1 — Diagnosing Common Benign Skin Tumors (p. 353)
A rapidly growing lesion with a central keratin-filled crater develops on the arm of an older patient. Which one of the following diagnostic or therapeutic concerns is specific to the investigation of this lesion?

- A. Dermoscopy cannot be used to differentiate this condition from squamous cell carcinoma.
- B. It warrants considering an underlying diagnosis of systemic lupus erythematosus or immunodeficiency.
- C. Rapid proliferation of additional lesions should prompt evaluation for an associated systemic malignancy.
- D. Referral is warranted due to risk of local bony extension.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Dermoscopy cannot reliably differentiate it from SCC.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The lesion is a keratoacanthoma. Its key clinical problem is that it cannot be reliably distinguished from well-differentiated SCC clinically, dermoscopically, or even histologically — so it is generally managed as an SCC (excision). It does not signal SLE/immunodeficiency or bony extension. (Multiple eruptive KAs can rarely signal Muir-Torre, but that's *additional* lesions, not this solitary one.) **⚠️ Verify** wording.

## Q2 — Diagnosing Common Benign Skin Tumors (p. 353)
A 68-year-old has three dome-shaped, small, red to deep purple, soft, compressible papules on his trunk, present a few years and asymptomatic. Most appropriate treatment approach?

- A. Immediate referral for dermoscopy and excision.
- B. A trial of beta blockers.
- C. A trial of isotretinoin.
- D. No treatment is required.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — No treatment is required.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> These are cherry angiomas — benign vascular papules. They need no treatment; removal is purely cosmetic. Beta blockers (for infantile hemangiomas) and isotretinoin are irrelevant here.

## Q3 — Melanoma: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 367)
Which one of the following does the USPSTF most strongly recommend to improve prevention and diagnosis of skin cancer?

- A. Routine counseling for sun protection in patients 6 months to 24 years of age with fair skin.
- B. Routine counseling for sun protection in patients older than 24 years with average risk.
- C. Counseling asymptomatic adults to perform a regular skin self-examination.
- D. Performing a complete visual skin examination in asymptomatic adolescents and adults.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Sun-protection counseling for fair-skinned patients aged 6 months–24 years.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> This is the only Grade **B** recommendation. For adults >24 it's a selective "C" (offer/provide selectively). Counseling adults on skin self-exam and routine whole-body visual screening of asymptomatic adults are both rated "I" (insufficient evidence).

## Q4 — Melanoma (p. 367)
A 65-year-old man with fair skin has a 2-cm pigmented lesion on his back. Which one of the following diagnostic considerations is correct?

- A. A superficial shave biopsy is acceptable due to the lesion's size and location.
- B. Itch or inflammation suggests a benign etiology.
- C. Mnemonics have limited use in patients with Fitzpatrick skin types I and II.
- D. Dermoscopy reduces the number of melanomas missed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Dermoscopy reduces missed melanomas.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Full-thickness excisional (not superficial shave) biopsy is preferred so Breslow depth can be measured (A wrong). Itch/inflammation can be a melanoma feature, not reassurance (B wrong). ABCDE-type mnemonics remain useful, including in fair skin (C wrong). Trained dermoscopy improves diagnostic accuracy.

## Q5 — Management of Late-Term and Postterm Pregnancy (p. 378)
Per ACOG, the earliest gestational age an elective induction can be performed at the patient's request (uncomplicated nulliparous) is which one of the following?

- A. 37 weeks.
- B. 39 weeks.
- C. 40 weeks.
- D. 41 weeks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — 39 weeks.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Elective induction before 39 weeks is not appropriate without medical indication; 39 weeks is the earliest acceptable for an elective/by-request induction (ARRIVE-informed). Inductions at 37–38 weeks require an indication.

## Q6 — Management of Late-Term and Postterm Pregnancy (p. 378)
At 38 weeks, an uncomplicated nulliparous patient wishes to avoid induction. Which one of the following methods is recommended to increase her chances of spontaneous labor?

- A. Acupuncture.
- B. Hot bath.
- C. Nipple stimulation.
- D. Sexual intercourse.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Nipple stimulation.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Breast/nipple stimulation promotes endogenous oxytocin release and has the best (Cochrane-supported) evidence among these for promoting spontaneous labor at term. Acupuncture, hot baths, and intercourse lack convincing evidence.

## Q7 — Diagnosis and Management of OCD in Primary Care (p. 385)
A 19-year-old with anxiety screens positive for intrusive thoughts. Which one of the following symptoms most strongly supports a diagnosis of OCD?

- A. Inattention and lack of focus.
- B. Performing compulsive behaviors to suppress urges.
- C. Involuntary motor or vocal tics.
- D. Behaviors focused on perfectionism and sense of control.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Compulsive behaviors performed to suppress obsessions/urges.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> OCD is defined by obsessions plus compulsions done to neutralize the distress of those obsessions. Inattention suggests ADHD, involuntary tics suggest a tic disorder, and rigid perfectionism/control suggests obsessive-compulsive *personality* disorder (ego-syntonic) rather than OCD.

## Q8 — Diagnosis and Management of OCD (p. 385)
A patient with hand-washing compulsions wants medication but worries about lifelong therapy. Which one of the following statements about treatment is correct?

- A. Tricyclic antidepressants are first-line, short-term treatments for OCD.
- B. Tapering of pharmacologic agents can begin 6 weeks after a positive response to medication.
- C. If relapse occurs despite a gradual taper, the medication should be continued indefinitely.
- D. There is no added benefit to combining an SSRI with psychotherapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — If relapse occurs despite a gradual taper, continue the medication indefinitely.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> SSRIs (not TCAs) are first-line; clomipramine is a second-line TCA (A wrong). Medication is typically continued 1–2 years before attempting a slow taper, not 6 weeks. Shorter periods results in relapse rates of up to 80% (B wrong). Combining an SSRI with CBT/ERP adds benefit (D wrong). Patients who relapse on tapering generally need long-term therapy.

## Q9 — Injections of the Hand and Wrist, Part I (p. 395)
A 75-year-old has thumb-base pain when turning a key and gripping. Which one of the following details is most helpful when addressing this condition?

- A. Irregular motion or clicking at the proximal interphalangeal joint is a diagnostic hallmark.
- B. Radiographic imaging is required to confirm the diagnosis.
- C. Use of a thumb spica splint is not helpful when treating this condition.
- D. Corticosteroid injections for this condition should aim for the basal joint space in the anatomic snuff-box.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Inject the basal (first CMC) joint space via the anatomic snuff-box.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> This is first carpometacarpal (basal joint) thumb OA. Diagnosis is clinical (positive grind test); radiographs are supportive but not required (B wrong). A thumb spica splint *does* help (C wrong). PIP clicking describes trigger finger, not basal joint OA (A wrong).

## Q10 — Injections of the Hand and Wrist, Part I (p. 395)
A 60-year-old with RA reports her index finger gets stuck/catches with pain on opening. Best first-line treatment for this condition?

- A. Ultrasonography for diagnosis, then ultrasound-guided corticosteroid injection.
- B. Splinting and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.
- C. Palpation-guided corticosteroid injection.
- D. Referral to a hand surgeon.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Splinting and NSAIDs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This is trigger finger (stenosing flexor tenosynovitis). Per this AFP article, conservative measures — splinting (MCP in slight flexion) plus NSAIDs — are the **first-line** treatment; corticosteroid injection is the next step if conservative therapy fails, and surgery is reserved for refractory cases.

## Q11 — Injections of the Hand and Wrist, Part II (p. 402)
A 40-year-old with prior de Quervain dx unresponsive to injection now has dorsal-radial wrist/distal-forearm pain, swelling, and a squeaking sound with extension. She probably has which one of the following?

- A. Carpal tunnel syndrome.
- B. Intersection syndrome.
- C. Triangular fibrocartilage complex injury.
- D. Ganglion cyst.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Intersection syndrome.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Pain/swelling several centimeters proximal to the wrist on the dorsal-radial forearm, with crepitus or a "squeak" on wrist/thumb movement, is classic intersection syndrome (where the first and second dorsal compartments cross). CTS is volar/neurologic; TFCC injury is ulnar-sided; a ganglion is a discrete cyst.

## Q12 — Injections of the Hand and Wrist, Part II (p. 402)
A patient has a slow-growing dorsal wrist outpouching near the scapholunate joint; ultrasound shows a homogeneous hypoechoic fluid collection. Best guidance?

- A. Surgical biopsy is needed to confirm the diagnosis.
- B. Aspiration is appropriate in patients with significant symptoms.
- C. Corticosteroid injection offers the most definitive treatment approach.
- D. Aspiration and fenestration in combination increase the risk of fat atrophy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Aspiration is appropriate for significantly symptomatic patients.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> This is a dorsal ganglion cyst — diagnosis is clinical/ultrasound, not biopsy (A wrong). Many are observed; aspiration is offered for bothersome symptoms (though recurrence is common). Corticosteroid injection is not definitive (C wrong), and the fat-atrophy claim (D) is incorrect.

## Q13 — Cochrane: Adding a LABA or LAMA to ICS for Uncontrolled Asthma (p. 346)
A 44-year-old has had several oral-steroid-requiring exacerbations despite a medium-dose ICS. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Switching to a high-dose ICS alone does not reduce moderate-to-severe exacerbations compared with a medium-dose ICS alone.
- B. Adding a LABA to a medium- or high-dose ICS likely reduces moderate-to-severe exacerbations compared with continuing medium-dose ICS alone.
- C. Adding a LAMA to a medium-dose ICS possibly reduces moderate-to-severe exacerbations compared with continuing medium-dose ICS alone.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found that simply increasing to high-dose ICS alone gave little benefit, while adding a LABA (moderate-certainty) or a LAMA (lower-certainty) to ICS reduced exacerbations — supporting step-up to combination therapy over ICS dose escalation alone. 

## Q14 — Cochrane: Disulfiram for the Treatment of Cocaine Dependence (p. 347)
A 35-year-old has worsening cocaine cravings affecting work. Which one of the following statements about disulfiram for cocaine use disorder is correct?

- A. It improves frequency and amount of use.
- B. It improves continuous abstinence.
- C. It improves point abstinence (number abstinent at end of treatment).
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — It improves point abstinence.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Low ⚠️⚠️
> The Cochrane evidence for disulfiram in cocaine use disorder is weak and inconsistent: it did **not** show clear benefit on frequency/amount of use or continuous abstinence, with at most a possible signal for point abstinence. **⚠️⚠️ Verify against print key** — the review's bottom line is "little to no benefit," so the keyed positive answer is uncertain (B is a plausible alternative).

## Q15 — Practice Guidelines: Concussion in Sport (6th International Conference) (p. 435)
A 19-year-old, 4 days after a head-to-head football collision, has mild headaches and difficulty focusing and has had two prior concussions. Per the 6th Conference, which one of the following is most appropriate?

- A. Prescribe strict rest and abstinence from activity until symptoms resolve.
- B. Clear him to participate in the next game.
- C. Advise him to retire from contact sports.
- D. Recommend attempting light-intensity activity, increasing intensity as tolerated.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Light-intensity activity, increasing intensity as tolerated.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The 2023 (6th) consensus abandoned prolonged strict rest; after 24–48 h of relative rest, sub-symptom-threshold aerobic activity that is gradually advanced speeds recovery. He is still symptomatic so cannot be cleared (B); retirement isn't dictated by three concussions alone (C); strict rest is outdated (A).

## Q16 — Practice Guidelines: Atopic Dermatitis (AAAAI/ACAAI Joint Task Force) (p. 437)
A 30-year-old with severe atopic dermatitis currently in remission. Which one of the following maintenance therapies does the AAAAI/ACAAI recommend?

- A. Crisaborole 2% ointment (Eucrisa), a topical PDE-4 inhibitor.
- B. Tacrolimus 0.1% ointment, a topical calcineurin inhibitor.
- C. Ruxolitinib 1.5% cream (Opzelura), a topical Janus kinase inhibitor.
- D. Oral prednisone, a systemic corticosteroid.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Tacrolimus 0.1% ointment (topical calcineurin inhibitor).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Proactive intermittent maintenance with a topical calcineurin inhibitor (or topical corticosteroid) to previously affected skin is the established strategy to prevent flares in well-controlled atopic dermatitis. Systemic prednisone is discouraged for chronic AD (D wrong); crisaborole and ruxolitinib are alternatives but tacrolimus is the recommended proactive-maintenance agent.
