## Q1 — Gout: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 533)

Which one of the following statements about diet and gout is correct?

- A. Red wine and liquor are greater risk factors than beer.
- B. Consumption of seafood, especially oily fish such as sardines and anchovies, reduces gout risk.
- C. Consumption of two or more sugary drinks per day, including fruit juices, increases gout risk in men.
- D. Meat is no longer thought to be a risk factor for gout.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — ≥2 sugary drinks/day (including fruit juice) raises gout risk in men.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Fructose raises urate. **Beer** is the highest-risk alcohol (purines + alcohol), more than wine/liquor (A wrong); purine-rich oily fish **increase** risk (B reversed); red/organ meat remains a risk factor (D wrong).

## Q2 — Gout (p. 533)

Which one of the following statements about the diagnosis of gout is correct?

- A. Synovial fluid aspiration is routinely recommended to confirm the diagnosis.
- B. Serum uric acid levels may be low or normal during an acute attack.
- C. Radiography is recommended to detect crystals in the affected joint.
- D. Fever and elevated white blood cell count are common in acute gout.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Serum uric acid can be low or normal during an acute flare.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Urate often drops acutely, so a normal level doesn't exclude gout — recheck after the flare. Aspiration is the gold standard but not *routine* in classic podagra (A); radiographs don't show crystals (C); systemic fever/leukocytosis should prompt concern for septic joint, not typical gout (D).

## Q3 — Gout (p. 533)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of gout is correct?

- A. Allopurinol is the preferred agent if urate-lowering therapy is indicated.
- B. Febuxostat (Uloric) is associated with lower cardiovascular mortality than allopurinol.
- C. For an acute episode, high-dose colchicine is more effective than and as well tolerated as low-dose colchicine.
- D. For an acute episode, corticosteroids should be given as a joint injection rather than orally.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Allopurinol is the preferred urate-lowering agent.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Allopurinol is first-line. Febuxostat carries a **higher** cardiovascular mortality signal (CARES, B wrong); **low-dose** colchicine is as effective and better tolerated than high-dose (C wrong); oral and intra-articular steroids are both acceptable (D wrong).

## Q4 — Eye Emergencies (p. 539)

Which one of the following is indicated for a suspected mechanical globe injury?

- A. Immediate removal of a protruding object from the affected eye.
- B. Measurement of eye pressure using tonometry.
- C. Application of fluorescein dye to help confirm the diagnosis.
- D. Application of a pressure dressing to the eye.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Apply fluorescein dye (Seidel test) to help confirm the injury.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A streaming "waterfall" of aqueous diluting fluorescein (positive Seidel sign) confirms globe rupture. **Never** remove an impaled object, apply tonometry, or place a pressure patch — all can extrude intraocular contents (A, B, D wrong). Shield the eye and refer.

## Q5 — Eye Emergencies (p. 539)

Which one of the following is consistent with rapid, painless loss of vision; afferent pupillary defect; and retinal whitening with a foveal cherry-red spot?

- A. Chemical burn.
- B. Mechanical globe injury.
- C. Retinal tear.
- D. Central retinal artery occlusion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Central retinal artery occlusion.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The classic triad — sudden painless monocular vision loss, RAPD, and a **cherry-red spot** with retinal pallor — is CRAO ("stroke of the eye"); treat emergently and evaluate for embolic source/GCA.

## Q6 — Eye Emergencies (p. 539)

A 70-year-old patient presents with sudden vision loss, jaw pain, and an elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate. Which one of the following treatments is the most appropriate?

- A. Systemic steroids.
- B. Antibiotics.
- C. Pentoxifylline (Trental).
- D. Autologous serum drops.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Systemic (high-dose) corticosteroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Vision loss + jaw claudication + high ESR in an older adult = **giant cell arteritis** → start steroids **immediately** (don't wait for biopsy) to protect the other eye.

## Q7 — Common Benign Chronic Vulvar Disorders (p. 550)

A patient with genitourinary syndrome of menopause (vulvar dryness, burning, itching) has not improved with vaginal lubricants or moisturizers. Which one of the following is the preferred next step in treatment?

- A. Intravaginal prasterone (Intrarosa).
- B. Low-dose intravaginal estrogen.
- C. Fractional carbon-dioxide vaginal laser therapy.
- D. Low-potency vulvar topical steroids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Low-dose intravaginal estrogen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> When nonhormonal measures fail in GSM, **local low-dose vaginal estrogen** is the preferred, evidence-based therapy. Prasterone is an alternative; CO2 laser lacks robust evidence (FDA-cautioned); topical steroids treat dermatoses, not GSM.

## Q8 — Common Benign Chronic Vulvar Disorders (p. 550)

Which one of the following conditions is most associated with an increased risk of vulvar squamous cell carcinoma?

- A. Lichen planus.
- B. Lichen sclerosus.
- C. Lichen simplex chronicus.
- D. Vulvodynia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Lichen sclerosus.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Vulvar **lichen sclerosus** carries a ~4–5% lifetime risk of vulvar SCC — treat with potent topical steroids and monitor. Lichen simplex chronicus and vulvodynia don't carry this malignant potential.

## Q9 — Common Benign Chronic Vulvar Disorders (p. 550)

Which one of the following symptoms is the hallmark of lichen simplex chronicus?

- A. Dyspareunia.
- B. Intense vulvar pruritus that may disturb sleep.
- C. Vulvar dryness.
- D. Vulvar pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Intense vulvar pruritus that may disturb sleep.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> LSC is the end result of a chronic **itch–scratch cycle** → lichenified, thickened skin; the dominant symptom is relentless itching, often worse at night. Pain/dryness/dyspareunia point to other diagnoses.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Mechanical Methods of Induction of Labor (p. 530)

Which one of the following statements about the use of balloon catheters for induction of labor is correct?

- A. They reduce the risk of uterine tachysystole compared with low-dose vaginal misoprostol (Cytotec).
- B. They are less likely to result in a vaginal delivery within 24 hours compared with low-dose vaginal misoprostol.
- C. They increase the risk of cesarean delivery when compared with oxytocin (Pitocin).
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Balloon catheters reduce uterine tachysystole vs. vaginal misoprostol.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mechanical ripening avoids prostaglandin-driven hyperstimulation (fewer FHR changes). Balloons are **similar** for vaginal delivery within 24 h (B wrong) and do **not** raise cesarean risk vs. oxytocin (C wrong).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Metformin to Prevent Diabetes in Patients at Increased Risk (p. 531)

In a patient with an A1C of 6.0%, which one of the following statements about the risk of developing type 2 diabetes is correct?

- A. Metformin decreases the risk in the next one to five years compared with standard diet and exercise recommendations.
- B. Metformin is more effective than intensive diet and exercise recommendations at reducing the risk.
- C. Metformin combined with intensive diet and exercise recommendations is more effective than intensive diet and exercise recommendations alone.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Metformin lowers 1–5-year diabetes risk vs. standard advice.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Per the DPP, metformin beats placebo/standard care, but **intensive lifestyle is more effective than metformin** (B wrong), and adding metformin to intensive lifestyle gives **no extra benefit** over lifestyle alone (C wrong) — so "all of the above" is wrong (D).

## Q12 — AHRQ Review: Treatment of Depression in Children and Adolescents (p. 558)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of depression in children and adolescents is correct?

- A. Family therapy causes more harms than benefits.
- B. Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors improve symptoms.
- C. Guidelines recommend that physicians consider six to eight weeks of active support and monitoring before beginning treatment.
- D. Paroxetine therapy decreases suicidal ideation compared with placebo.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Consider 6–8 weeks of active support and monitoring before starting treatment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For mild pediatric depression, watchful waiting with active support precedes pharmacotherapy. **Fluoxetine** (an SSRI) is best-supported, not SNRIs (B wrong); paroxetine is avoided due to suicidality signal (D wrong); family therapy is not net-harmful (A wrong).

## Q13 — Practice Guidelines: Accidental Hypothermia — Wilderness Medical Society (p. 571)

An experienced hiker is observed stumbling and vigorously shivering but is mentally alert. According to the Wilderness Medical Society, how should the hiker be treated?

- A. They should exercise immediately to build up warmth.
- B. They should sit down, insulated from the ground and elements, for 30 minutes.
- C. They should be kept horizontal and not be allowed to stand or walk until warmed.
- D. They should not be allowed to eat or drink until warmed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Sit down, insulated from the ground and elements, for ~30 minutes (mild hypothermia).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Alert + vigorous shivering = **mild** hypothermia: stop heat loss, insulate, add calories/warm fluids, rewarm ~30 min before resuming activity. Strict horizontal/no-movement precautions and afterdrop concerns apply to **severe** hypothermia, not mild (C wrong); food/fluids are encouraged (D wrong).
