## Q1 — Toilet Training: Common Questions and Answers (p. 468)

Which one of the following is associated with earlier readiness for toilet training?

- A. Presence of autism spectrum disorder.
- B. Presence of Down syndrome.
- C. Female sex.
- D. Male sex.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Female sex.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Girls tend to reach readiness/complete training earlier than boys. Developmental conditions (autism, Down syndrome) are associated with **later** readiness (A, B).

## Q2 — Toilet Training (p. 468)

A 27-month-old boy who has been toilet training for two months now refuses to sit on the toilet. Which one of the following is the most appropriate advice to give his parents?

- A. This is happening because toilet training was started too early.
- B. Taking a break from toilet training for one to three months may be helpful.
- C. They should take his favorite toy away for an hour when he refuses to sit on the toilet.
- D. Use of advanced toilet training for a few weeks has been shown to help in this situation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Take a break from toilet training for one to three months.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Resistance is best handled by **pausing** and restarting later without pressure. Avoid punitive approaches (C) and power struggles; a 27-month start isn't "too early" (A).

## Q3 — Toilet Training (p. 468)

Which one of the following is an indication of readiness to initiate toilet training?

- A. Lack of nighttime bowel control.
- B. The child stays dry for one hour at a time.
- C. The child gets on the toilet, but immediately gets off before urinating or defecating.
- D. The child shows discomfort when wearing a soiled diaper.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — The child shows discomfort when wearing a soiled diaper.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Awareness of/discomfort with soiling signals readiness. Children should stay dry for **~2 hours** (not 1, B), and the other behaviors don't indicate readiness.

## Q4 — Acne Vulgaris: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 475)

Which one of the following statements about treatments for acne is correct?

- A. Topical antibiotics should be used in combination with benzoyl peroxide to reduce the risk of resistance.
- B. Topical dapsone (Aczone) is more effective in adolescent females than adult women.
- C. Treatments for acne should have a minimum duration of four weeks to assess effectiveness.
- D. Topical erythromycin is favored over topical clindamycin for mild to moderate acne because of the declining effectiveness of clindamycin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Pair topical antibiotics with benzoyl peroxide to reduce resistance.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Benzoyl peroxide curbs antibiotic resistance. Topical dapsone works better in **adult women** (B reversed); allow **~8–12 weeks** to judge response (C wrong); **clindamycin is preferred over erythromycin** (less resistance, D reversed).

## Q5 — Acne Vulgaris (p. 475)

Which one of the following scenarios is a recommended use of topical retinoid monotherapy for acne?

- A. Tretinoin (Retin-A) to treat an 18-year-old with mild comedonal acne.
- B. Adapalene (Differin) to treat a 10-year-old with moderate inflammatory acne.
- C. Tazarotene (Tazorac) to treat a 26-year-old pregnant woman with mild comedonal acne.
- D. Tretinoin to treat a 30-year-old with moderate inflammatory acne.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Tretinoin monotherapy for an 18-year-old with mild comedonal acne.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Retinoid monotherapy suits **mild comedonal** acne. **Moderate inflammatory** acne needs combination therapy (B, D); **tazarotene is teratogenic** — contraindicated in pregnancy (C).

## Q6 — Postpartum Care: An Approach to the Fourth Trimester (p. 485)

Which one of the following statements about screening for gestational diabetes mellitus in women during the postpartum period is correct?

- A. Gestational diabetes in isolation should not prompt additional diabetes screening.
- B. The recommended screening test is an A1C level at four weeks postpartum.
- C. Screening is recommended between four and 12 weeks postpartum.
- D. Women should continue to be screened for diabetes every six months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Screen between four and 12 weeks postpartum.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Women with GDM get a **75-g 2-hour OGTT at 4–12 weeks** postpartum (not A1C), then screening **every 1–3 years** thereafter (D wrong). GDM **does** mandate follow-up screening (A wrong).

## Q7 — Postpartum Care (p. 485)

Which one of the following statements about a 22-year-old woman who developed hypertension during her pregnancy is correct?

- A. Blood pressure and body weight should be monitored every three months.
- B. A blood pressure check should be performed within the first seven days after delivery.
- C. There is no long-term risk of heart disease in women with hypertension during pregnancy.
- D. Prescribe opioids instead of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs for postpartum pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Check blood pressure within the first seven days after delivery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Postpartum hypertension can peak/worsen in the first week → early BP check (~3–10 days). Hypertensive pregnancy **raises lifetime cardiovascular risk** (C wrong); **NSAIDs are preferred** over opioids for postpartum pain (D reversed).

## Q8 — Postpartum Care (p. 485)

Which one of the following statements about postpartum thyroid disorders is correct?

- A. Hyperthyroidism is more common than hypothyroidism.
- B. Postpartum hyperthyroidism typically becomes a chronic illness.
- C. Infants of women who are breastfeeding and being treated for thyroid disorders should have their thyroid-stimulating hormone, total serum free thyroxine, and total triiodothyronine levels checked periodically.
- D. Women with a history of postpartum thyroiditis should have annual thyroid function screening performed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — History of postpartum thyroiditis → annual thyroid function screening.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> These women have a high rate of progressing to permanent hypothyroidism, so screen yearly. Postpartum thyroiditis classically passes through a **transient** hyperthyroid then hypothyroid phase (B wrong); breastfed infants don't need routine thyroid testing (C wrong).

## Q9 — Point-of-Care Guides: Predicting the Risk of Postoperative Pulmonary Complications (p. 499)

Which one of the following is an important factor when predicting the risk of postoperative pulmonary complications?

- A. Whether the surgery is an emergency.
- B. The type of surgery.
- C. The presence of sepsis.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Risk prediction (e.g., ARISCAT) combines **emergency status, surgical site/type, and acute conditions like sepsis** along with age, anemia, SpO₂, and respiratory infection.

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: Influenza Vaccination — Updated ACIP Recommendations (p. 505)

Which one of the following statements is correct regarding a healthy 12-month-old who has not received the influenza vaccine previously?

- A. Live attenuated influenza vaccine should be administered before October.
- B. A quadrivalent cell culture–based vaccine (Flucelvax) should be administered before October.
- C. Fluzone Quadrivalent vaccine can only be administered in a dose of 0.25 mL for this age group.
- D. Fluarix Quadrivalent vaccine should be administered in two doses, ideally by the end of October.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Give Fluarix Quadrivalent in two doses, ideally by the end of October.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> First-time vaccinees aged 6 months–8 years need **two doses** ≥4 weeks apart. **LAIV is not used under age 2** (A); Flucelvax isn't licensed for this age (B); Fluzone dosing options aren't limited to 0.25 mL (C).
