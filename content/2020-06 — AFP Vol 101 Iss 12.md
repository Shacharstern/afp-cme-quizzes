## Q1 — Management of Fever in Infants and Young Children (p. 721)

Which one of the following statements about the use of lumbar puncture for the evaluation of febrile infants is correct?

- A. It is recommended for all infants one month and younger with fever.
- B. It is recommended for all infants three months and older with fever.
- C. It should be used selectively in febrile infants regardless of age.
- D. Low protein and elevated glucose levels in the cerebrospinal fluid are associated with bacterial meningitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — LP is recommended for all febrile infants one month of age and younger.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Neonates (≤28 days) with fever get a **full sepsis workup including LP**. Bacterial meningitis shows **high protein and low glucose** (D reversed). Older infants are risk-stratified, not universally tapped (B, C).

## Q2 — Management of Fever in Infants and Young Children (p. 721)

Which one of the following statements about antibiotic therapy for febrile infants is correct?

- A. Ampicillin is recommended for listeriosis infection in infants one to three months of age.
- B. If pneumonia is suspected, amoxicillin/clavulanate (Augmentin) is the recommended empiric antibiotic therapy.
- C. Children three to 36 months of age with normal urinalysis findings and no localizing signs should not receive antibiotics.
- D. Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole is recommended for children one to three months of age with urinary findings.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Well-appearing children 3–36 months with normal urinalysis and no localizing signs don't need antibiotics.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In the vaccinated era, occult serious bacterial infection is rare; observe rather than treat empirically. Listeria is uncommon beyond the neonatal period (A overgeneralizes); amoxicillin (not Augmentin) is first-line for CAP (B); TMP-SMX is not first-line for young-infant UTI (D).

## Q3 — Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (p. 730)

Which one of the following statements about the development of acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is correct?

- A. Most cases in adults are secondary to cardiogenic shock.
- B. Older age is a risk factor.
- C. Inflammation is not a significant factor in the development of ARDS.
- D. Children can develop ARDS only if they have underlying pulmonary disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Older age is a risk factor.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ARDS is **noncardiogenic** pulmonary edema — sepsis/pneumonia/aspiration, not cardiogenic shock (A wrong); **inflammation/diffuse alveolar damage is central** (C wrong); children develop ARDS without pre-existing lung disease (D wrong).

## Q4 — Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (p. 730)

A 63-year-old man admitted three days ago with pneumonia has developed ARDS. Which one of the following statements about therapy is correct?

- A. Oxygen therapy via face mask is likely to correct hypoxia.
- B. The target oxygenation level after intubation is 97%.
- C. After intubation, initial tidal volumes should be 6 mL per kg of predicted body weight.
- D. Intubated patients with ARDS should not receive anticoagulants because of the risk of pulmonary hemorrhage.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Use lung-protective ventilation: ~6 mL/kg of predicted body weight.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Low-tidal-volume ventilation reduces mortality. Refractory hypoxemia usually needs intubation, not a face mask (A); a SpO₂ target of ~88–95% (not 97%) avoids hyperoxia (B); VTE prophylaxis **is** indicated (D wrong).

## Q5 — Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (p. 730)

Which one of the following statements about the long-term outcomes of patients with ARDS is correct?

- A. Higher Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE II) scores are associated with poorer long-term physical and psychiatric outcomes.
- B. Mobilization therapy may reduce total days on a ventilator but has no effect on physical functioning after extubation.
- C. Almost all patients regain their previous level of functioning within five years after hospital discharge.
- D. Lung function is diminished by up to 25% for as long as five years after hospital discharge.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Higher APACHE II scores predict poorer long-term physical and psychiatric outcomes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ARDS survivors have prolonged physical, cognitive, and psychiatric morbidity. Early mobilization **improves** post-extubation function (B wrong); many do **not** fully recover (C wrong); lung function largely recovers, not down 25% at 5 years (D wrong).

## Q6 — Foreign Bodies in the Skin (p. 740)

Which one of the following situations best supports a foreign body being left in place?

- A. When it is in close proximity to an open joint.
- B. If it is small, inert, and deeply embedded and located away from vital structures.
- C. When persistent inflammation is present.
- D. When the object is associated with a cosmetic deformity.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Small, inert, deeply embedded objects away from vital structures may be left in place.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Removal risk may exceed benefit for deep inert objects. **Remove** objects near joints/vessels/nerves (A), those causing inflammation/infection (C), and those causing cosmetic or functional problems (D).

## Q7 — Foreign Bodies in the Skin (p. 740)

Which one of the following recommendations about removing a foreign body is correct?

- A. Antiseptic solution should be used to irrigate the wound.
- B. Saline irrigation is better than tap water at preventing infection.
- C. Ischemic tourniquets may remain secured for up to 30 minutes.
- D. Aggressive irrigation should be used to help dislodge objects.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ischemic tourniquets may remain in place for up to 30 minutes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Tap water and saline are **equivalent** for irrigation (A, B wrong); **antiseptics are tissue-toxic** and not used to irrigate open wounds (A); high-pressure irrigation can drive debris deeper (D wrong).

## Q8 — Foreign Bodies in the Skin (p. 740)

Which one of the following imaging modalities best detects vegetative (radiolucent) material?

- A. Radiography.
- B. Ultrasonography.
- C. Computed tomography.
- D. Fluoroscopy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Ultrasonography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Radiolucent** materials (wood, thorns, plastic) are poorly seen on x-ray/fluoroscopy; **ultrasound** is the best bedside modality for these (CT is an alternative but with radiation/cost).

## Q9 — Lown Right Care: Anticoagulation in Older Adults (p. 748)

Which one of the following statements about anticoagulation therapy to prevent strokes in older adults with atrial fibrillation is correct?

- A. Randomized controlled trials of anticoagulation include many patients older than 90 years.
- B. The CHA₂DS₂-VASc score has a very good predictive ability for ischemic stroke.
- C. Patients taking warfarin (Coumadin) experience more severe bleeds and hemorrhagic strokes than patients taking placebo.
- D. Direct oral anticoagulants are less effective than warfarin at preventing strokes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Warfarin causes more severe bleeds and hemorrhagic strokes than placebo.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Anticoagulation trades ischemic-stroke prevention for bleeding risk. The very old (>90) are **underrepresented** in trials (A wrong); CHA₂DS₂-VASc has only **modest** predictive ability (B wrong); DOACs are **at least as effective** as warfarin with less intracranial bleeding (D wrong).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Cognitive Impairment in Older Adults (p. 753)

Based on the USPSTF recommendation statement, which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Mild cognitive impairment almost always progresses to dementia.
- B. Increasing age is the highest known risk factor for cognitive impairment.
- C. Cardiovascular risk factors (e.g., hypertension, diabetes mellitus) are not associated with risk of dementia.
- D. Mild cognitive impairment and dementia interfere with independent daily functioning.
- E. Dementia currently affects an estimated 8 million to 9 million people in the United States.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Increasing age is the highest known risk factor for cognitive impairment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> MCI does **not** always progress to dementia (A wrong); cardiovascular risk factors **are** linked to dementia (C wrong); MCI by definition does **not** impair independent function (only dementia does — D wrong); US dementia prevalence is ~**5 million** in this statement (E wrong).

## Q11 — Point-of-Care Guides: Clinical Diagnosis of Acute Bacterial Rhinosinusitis (p. 758)

Which one of the following statements about diagnosing acute bacterial rhinosinusitis is correct?

- A. Maxillary toothache and purulent nasal discharge are associated with a bacterial cause of sinusitis.
- B. Previous diagnoses of sinusitis increase the likelihood that the current episode is sinusitis.
- C. The presence of cold symptoms before the development of sinus symptoms decreases the likelihood of a bacterial cause of sinusitis.
- D. About two-thirds of patients with acute rhinosinusitis have a bacterial cause.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Maxillary toothache and purulent discharge point to a bacterial cause.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Useful predictors include maxillary toothache, purulent discharge, "double sickening," and poor response to decongestants. Prior sinusitis history doesn't help (B); most acute rhinosinusitis is **viral** — only ~0.5–2% is bacterial (D wrong).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Asthma — GINA Updated Recommendations (p. 762)

According to GINA, which one of the following therapies is recommended for a 13-year-old boy with asthma who has symptoms only twice a month, no exacerbation history, and no other risk factors?

- A. Albuterol inhaler as needed for symptoms.
- B. Daily moderate-dose inhaled beclomethasone.
- C. Daily low-dose inhaled fluticasone (Flovent).
- D. Low-dose budesonide/formoterol (Symbicort) as needed for symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — As-needed low-dose budesonide/formoterol.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> GINA no longer recommends **SABA-only** treatment (A) — even mild/intermittent asthma should receive ICS-containing therapy. As-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol (Step 1/2) is preferred over daily ICS for this infrequent-symptom pattern (B, C).
