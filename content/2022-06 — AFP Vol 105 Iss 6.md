## Q1 — Metabolic Surgery for Adult Obesity (p. 593)
Which patient is a good candidate for metabolic surgery per current guidelines?

- A. BMI 33 with newly diagnosed diabetes.
- B. BMI 31 with poorly controlled diabetes despite optimal management.
- C. BMI 37 with no other conditions.
- D. BMI 34 with statin-controlled hyperlipidemia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — BMI 31 with poorly controlled diabetes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> 2022 ASMBS/IFSO criteria: BMI ≥35 regardless of comorbidity, OR BMI 30–34.9 with metabolic disease (especially uncontrolled type 2 diabetes despite optimal medical therapy). BMI 37 alone also qualifies (≥35), but the question's intent is the lowered threshold for refractory diabetes.

## Q2 — Metabolic Surgery (p. 593)
Correct statement about patients after metabolic surgery?

- A. They rarely need vitamin supplementation.
- B. They should eat a low-protein diet for at least two years.
- C. They should avoid all NSAIDs.
- D. They should drink extra fluids with meals.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Avoid all NSAIDs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs raise marginal-ulcer/anastomotic risk. Patients need lifelong vitamin/mineral supplementation, a *high-protein* diet, and should avoid drinking with meals (dumping/early satiety).

## Q3 — Metabolic Surgery (p. 593)
Correct statement about long-term outcomes of metabolic surgery?

- A. On average it adds more than two years to life expectancy.
- B. Most patients reach a BMI ≤25.
- C. Most patients regain 90% of lost weight by 10 years.
- D. It does not reduce the chance of developing type 2 diabetes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Adds >2 years of life expectancy on average.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most patients do not reach normal BMI (B false), regain only a fraction of lost weight (C false), and surgery markedly lowers incident type 2 diabetes (D false).

## Q4 — Soft Tissue Masses (p. 602)
Which finding suggests a soft tissue mass is more likely malignant?

- A. Diameter ≥5 cm.
- B. Lack of pain or tenderness.
- C. Mass that fluctuates in size.
- D. Soft consistency.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Diameter ≥5 cm.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Size ≥5 cm, firm/hard consistency, deep location, and rapid growth are "red flags." Painlessness alone is nonspecific; fluctuation and soft consistency favor benign lesions.

## Q5 — Soft Tissue Masses (p. 602)
A mass present 4 months without change. Which warrants malignancy consideration?

- A. A 3-cm apparent lipoma on the upper arm.
- B. A 3-cm firm subcutaneous chest mass with a central punctum.
- C. An irregular nodular mass over the plantar fascia.
- D. An apparent hematoma on the upper arm.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A persistent "hematoma" without clear trauma.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> A non-resolving hematoma without a trauma history can be a sarcoma masquerading as a bruise. The lipoma, the punctum-bearing epidermal cyst, and the plantar fibroma are characteristically benign. Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q6 — Soft Tissue Masses (p. 602)
Correct statement about aspiration of a ganglion cyst?

- A. A small needle is preferred to minimize pain.
- B. Aspiration is recommended for all ganglion cysts.
- C. Cysts on the radial volar wrist can be aspirated without increased risk.
- D. Recurrence is common.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Recurrence is common.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Ganglion cysts frequently recur after aspiration. Many are observed (not all aspirated); volar radial cysts sit near the radial artery, so aspiration there carries *increased* risk.

## Q7 — Acute Stroke Diagnosis (p. 616)
Correct statement about evaluating sudden-onset stroke symptoms?

- A. rtPA is contraindicated until stroke mimics are excluded.
- B. A standardized stroke scale isn't needed if stroke is obvious.
- C. Noncontrast brain CT should be interpreted within 45 minutes of presentation.
- D. A HINTS exam should be done on all stroke patients.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Noncontrast CT interpreted within 45 minutes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Door-to-CT-interpretation target is ≤45 min. rtPA is not withheld pending mimic exclusion; a standardized scale (NIHSS) is always used; HINTS is for selected acute vestibular syndrome, not every patient.

## Q8 — Acute Stroke Diagnosis (p. 616)
A 67-year-old, 2–3 hours of mild unilateral weakness and facial droop, no pain. Initial imaging to determine thrombolytic candidacy?

- A. Noncontrast CT.
- B. CT perfusion.
- C. Lumbar puncture.
- D. CT angiography.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Noncontrast CT.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The first step is noncontrast CT to exclude hemorrhage before thrombolysis. Perfusion/angiography guide later thrombectomy decisions.

## Q9 — Acute Stroke Diagnosis (p. 616)
A patient with ischemic stroke treated with rtPA. Correct statement about poststroke evaluation?

- A. MRA is preferred over CT arteriography for carotid stenosis.
- B. Carotid Doppler ultrasonography is the diagnostic standard for carotid stenosis.
- C. Long-term monitoring for paroxysmal AF is warranted in stroke not otherwise specified.
- D. Transthoracic echocardiography should be done to find PFO in all ischemic strokes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Long-term rhythm monitoring for paroxysmal AF in cryptogenic stroke.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cryptogenic/embolic stroke of undetermined source warrants prolonged AF monitoring. Carotid Doppler is a screening test (CTA/MRA confirm); echocardiography is not routine for all strokes.

## Q10 — Community-Acquired Pneumonia (p. 625)
Correct statement about the causes of CAP?

- A. Legionella is seen only in localized outbreaks.
- B. Mycoplasma incidence is consistent year to year.
- C. Influenza virus is the most common cause of CAP.
- D. H. influenzae and S. pneumoniae are the most common bacterial pathogens.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — H. influenzae and S. pneumoniae are the most common bacterial pathogens.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> S. pneumoniae remains the leading bacterial cause, with H. influenzae also frequent. Legionella occurs sporadically (A false); Mycoplasma is epidemic/cyclic (B false); a single virus isn't the overall most common cause (C false). Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q11 — Community-Acquired Pneumonia (p. 625)
Correct statement about the clinical diagnosis of CAP?

- A. Egophony is not predictive of CAP.
- B. Normal vital signs and normal lung exam essentially rule out CAP.
- C. The physician's overall clinical impression is unreliable.
- D. Measured fever is the most accurate single finding.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Normal vitals + normal lung exam essentially rule out CAP.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The absence of any vital-sign or auscultatory abnormality makes pneumonia very unlikely. Egophony *does* increase likelihood; gestalt clinical impression is useful; no single sign (including fever) is definitive.

## Q12 — Community-Acquired Pneumonia (p. 625)
Recommended initial outpatient antibiotic for CAP with comorbid diabetes?

- A. Amoxicillin/clavulanate monotherapy 2,000/125 mg BID.
- B. Levofloxacin 750 mg daily plus a macrolide or doxycycline.
- C. Amoxicillin/clavulanate 2,000/125 mg BID plus a macrolide or doxycycline.
- D. Clarithromycin 500 mg BID plus doxycycline 100 mg BID.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Augmentin plus a macrolide or doxycycline.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> With comorbidities, use a beta-lactam + macrolide/doxycycline OR respiratory fluoroquinolone *monotherapy*. A fluoroquinolone is given alone, so option B (levofloxacin + macrolide) is wrong; beta-lactam monotherapy is inadequate coverage.

## Q13 — Common Finger Fractures and Dislocations (p. 631)
Distal phalanx injury after hammering; cannot flex the DIP; x-ray shows a displaced proximal fragment with ~50% joint-surface involvement. Correct management?

- A. Buddy taping in mild flexion for four weeks.
- B. Referral to an orthopedic specialist.
- C. Splint the joint in mild (10°) flexion for four weeks.
- D. Splint the joint in full extension for four weeks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Orthopedic referral.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Loss of active DIP flexion = FDP avulsion ("jersey finger"), and a displaced intra-articular fragment involving ~50% of the surface, both need surgical referral.

## Q14 — Common Finger Fractures and Dislocations (p. 631)
Middle phalanx injury; on making a fist the third and fourth fingers cross (malrotation); small nondisplaced extra-articular fracture. Correct management?

- A. Buddy taping to the adjacent finger.
- B. Dorsal splinting in full extension.
- C. Referral for surgery.
- D. Ulnar gutter splinting.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Referral for surgery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Finger crossing/overlap on flexion indicates a **rotational deformity**, which must be corrected operatively even if the fracture looks minor on x-ray.

## Q15 — Common Finger Fractures and Dislocations (p. 631)
Most common type of finger dislocation?

- A. Dorsal dislocation of the MCP joint.
- B. Volar dislocation of the PIP joint.
- C. Dorsal dislocation of the PIP joint.
- D. Hyperextension dislocation of the DIP joint.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Dorsal dislocation of the PIP joint.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Dorsal PIP dislocation (middle phalanx displaced dorsally) is the most common finger dislocation, typically from hyperextension/axial load.

## Q16 — Preoperative Evaluation in Children (p. 640)
A healthy 17-year-old female having an orthopedic procedure under general anesthesia. Recommended preoperative test?

- A. Basic hemogram and metabolic panel.
- B. Electrocardiography.
- C. Pregnancy test.
- D. Urine drug screen.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pregnancy test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Routine labs/ECG/drug screens are not indicated in a healthy child. A pregnancy test is recommended for those of childbearing potential before general anesthesia.

## Q17 — Preoperative Evaluation in Children (p. 640)
What is the purpose of the ADVANCE program in preoperative care?

- A. Ensure advance directives are completed and filed.
- B. Categorize patients on the ASA risk scale.
- C. Provide easy-to-understand patient education on fasting, fluids, and testing.
- D. Reduce parental and child anxiety about surgery.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Reduce parental and child anxiety about surgery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ADVANCE is a family-centered behavioral preparation program shown to lower perioperative anxiety (an alternative to sedative premedication).

## Q18 — Preoperative Evaluation in Children (p. 640)
A 14-year-old, overweight, with well-controlled mild persistent asthma and no other problems. Appropriate ASA category?

- A. ASA I.
- B. ASA II.
- C. ASA III.
- D. ASA IV.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — ASA II.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mild, well-controlled systemic disease (mild asthma, being overweight) = ASA II. ASA III would be substantive functional limitation/poorly controlled disease.

## Q19 — Cochrane: Mini-Cog, IQCODE, MoCA, MMSE for Predicting Dementia (p. 590)
Correct statement about cognitive testing to predict dementia in *asymptomatic* primary care patients?

- A. The Mini-Cog has reliably high sensitivity and specificity.
- B. The MoCA has reliably high sensitivity and specificity.
- C. The MMSE has reliably high sensitivity and specificity.
- D. Evidence is lacking; the balance of benefits and harms of screening cannot be determined.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Evidence is lacking; benefit/harm balance can't be determined.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In asymptomatic patients these tools lack reliable predictive accuracy, consistent with USPSTF's "insufficient evidence" position on screening.

## Q20 — Cochrane: SSRIs/SNRIs for Premature Ejaculation (p. 591)
Correct statement about SSRI/SNRI treatment for lifelong premature ejaculation?

- A. Treatment does not increase ejaculatory latency time.
- B. Treatment does not improve satisfaction.
- C. Adverse effects are common, forcing many patients to stop SSRIs.
- D. The ISSM does not support off-label SSRI/SNRI use for this.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Adverse effects are common and lead many to discontinue.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SSRIs/SNRIs do prolong intravaginal ejaculatory latency time and improve satisfaction (A, B false), and the ISSM supports their off-label use (D false) — but adverse effects limit adherence.

## Q21 — PPIP: USPSTF Screening for Atrial Fibrillation (p. 659)
Which statement is correct per the USPSTF recommendation on AF screening?

- A. Stroke is the first sign of AF in ~20% of patients with AF-associated stroke.
- B. Screening once, intermittently, or continuously detects all episodes equally.
- C. A large percentage of the U.S. population is screened regularly for AF.
- D. Only electrocardiography is appropriate for screening.
- E. Any identified AF episode should be treated with anticoagulation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Stroke is the first manifestation of AF in ~20% of AF-related strokes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Detection rates vary by screening intensity (B false); routine population screening is uncommon (C false); multiple modalities exist beyond ECG (D false); anticoagulation depends on stroke risk, not every detected episode (E false).

## Q22 — Point-of-Care Guides: Bruising to Predict Child Abuse (p. 661)
Which is NOT a risk factor for child abuse in a child with a bruise?

- A. Age younger than four months.
- B. Bruising on the fleshy part of the cheek.
- C. Bruising on the angle of the jaw.
- D. Bruising on the knees.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Bruising on the knees.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Knees/shins are common *accidental* bruise sites in mobile children. Bruising in a pre-mobile infant (<4 months) or over the cheek/jaw/torso/ear is concerning for abuse ("TEN-4" pattern).

## Q23 — Lown Right Care: Overuse of Interventional Procedures for Low Back Pain (p. 667)
Correct statement about surgery for low back pain?

- A. Surgery generally gives better results than medical management.
- B. Spinal fusion improves more than 50% of patients.
- C. Failed back surgery syndrome causes pain but not functional disability.
- D. Acute surgical indications include infection and progressive neurologic symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Acute indications include infection and progressive neurologic deficits.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For most mechanical LBP, surgery is not superior to conservative care (A false); fusion outcomes are modest (B false); failed back surgery syndrome causes both pain *and* disability (C false). Emergent surgery is for cauda equina, infection, instability, or progressive deficit.

## Q24 — Practice Guidelines: SGLT-2 Inhibitors and GLP-1 RAs in Type 2 Diabetes (BMJ Rapid Rec) (p. 675)
A 61-year-old with T2DM, A1C 8.2%, hypertension, total cholesterol 290, no CV or renal disease. Per BMJ/MAGIC, what to add to metformin to best reduce overall mortality?

- A. Canagliflozin (an SGLT-2 inhibitor).
- B. Dulaglutide (a GLP-1 receptor agonist).
- C. Insulin.
- D. None — just increase the metformin dose.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — An SGLT-2 inhibitor (canagliflozin).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> With multiple cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, dyslipidemia), the BMJ/MAGIC recommendation favors adding an SGLT-2 inhibitor (or GLP-1 RA) for mortality/morbidity benefit. The guideline's risk-stratified wording is why this is flagged; answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q25 — Practice Guidelines: C. difficile Infection (IDSA Focused Update) (p. 678)
A 78-year-old with an *initial* C. difficile infection. Which treatment does the IDSA recommend to decrease recurrence?

- A. Bezlotoxumab (Zinplava).
- B. Fecal microbiota transplantation.
- C. Fidaxomicin (Dificid).
- D. Vancomycin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Fidaxomicin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The 2021 IDSA/SHEA update prefers fidaxomicin over vancomycin for initial CDI to reduce recurrence. Bezlotoxumab is an adjunct for recurrent/high-risk disease; FMT is for multiply recurrent CDI.
