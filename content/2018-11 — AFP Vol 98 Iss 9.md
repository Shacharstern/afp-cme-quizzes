# AFP CME Quiz — November 1, 2018 (Vol 98, Issue 9)

## Q1 — Knee Pain in Adults and Adolescents: The Initial Evaluation (p. 576)

A patient reports several weeks of dull, aching anterior knee pain that is exacerbated by prolonged sitting and climbing stairs. Which one of the following diagnoses is most likely?

- A. Pes anserine bursitis.
- B. Slipped capital femoral epiphysis.
- C. Patellofemoral pain syndrome.
- D. Osteoarthritis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Patellofemoral pain syndrome.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Anterior knee pain worse with **stairs and prolonged sitting ("theater sign")** is classic PFPS. Pes anserine bursitis is medial; SCFE presents as hip/knee pain in younger children; OA is older with crepitus/stiffness.

## Q2 — Knee Pain in Adults and Adolescents (p. 576)

Which one of the following tests is most commonly used to assess for anterior cruciate ligament injury?

- A. Anterior drawer.
- B. Lachman.
- C. Pivot shift.
- D. McMurray.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — The Lachman test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The **Lachman** test is the most sensitive/commonly used ACL test. The anterior drawer is less sensitive, pivot shift is specific but harder to perform, and **McMurray tests the meniscus** (D).

## Q3 — Knee Pain in Adults and Adolescents (p. 576)

Which one of the following is the most appropriate indication for ordering radiography in a patient with knee pain?

- A. Patient request.
- B. Pain lasting more than four weeks.
- C. Acute nontraumatic knee pain.
- D. Inability to bear weight for four steps immediately after injury.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Inability to bear weight for four steps immediately and in the office.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This is an **Ottawa Knee Rule** criterion (also: age ≥55, isolated patellar tenderness, fibular head tenderness, can't flex to 90°). Patient request and chronic/atraumatic pain aren't indications by themselves (A, B, C).

## Q4 — Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus in Children (p. 590)

Which one of the following statements about the diagnosis of diabetes mellitus in children and adolescents is correct?

- A. Most children are asymptomatic at the time of diagnosis.
- B. Autoantibodies to insulin, islet cell cytoplasm, and glutamic acid decarboxylase are present in the majority of children with type 1 diabetes.
- C. Diagnostic criteria are based on risk factors rather than blood glucose or A1C levels in children with type 2 diabetes.
- D. The presence of ketoacidosis essentially rules out type 2 diabetes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Autoantibodies (insulin, islet cell, GAD) are present in most children with type 1 diabetes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Autoantibodies help distinguish type 1 from type 2. Many children are **symptomatic** at diagnosis (A); diagnosis uses **glucose/A1C** criteria (C wrong); **DKA can occur in type 2** ("ketosis-prone"), so it doesn't exclude it (D wrong).

## Q5 — Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus in Children (p. 590)

A 15-year-old obese patient has not responded to lifestyle interventions for the treatment of type 2 diabetes. Which one of the following statements about pharmacologic interventions is correct?

- A. Metformin should be initiated at 500 mg per day, regardless of the patient's weight.
- B. Metformin is contraindicated in patients younger than 16 years.
- C. Thiazolidinediones and incretins are the only medications approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of type 2 diabetes in children and adolescents.
- D. Insulin therapy should be limited to patients with signs of ketosis or ketoacidosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Start metformin at 500 mg/day (then titrate).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Metformin is FDA-approved from **age 10** (B wrong); **metformin and insulin** are the approved agents (not TZDs/incretins, C wrong); insulin is used for ketosis **and** for marked hyperglycemia at presentation (D too narrow).

## Q6 — Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus in Children (p. 590)

An asymptomatic 12-year-old obese patient is diagnosed with type 2 diabetes (A1C 7.5%, systolic BP in the 92nd percentile). Which one of the following is an appropriate next step?

- A. He should be started on an angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor.
- B. He should be started on insulin.
- C. A1C should be monitored every three months.
- D. He should self-monitor his blood glucose levels three times per day until blood glucose targets are reached.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Monitor A1C every three months.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A1C is tracked **quarterly**. A BP in the 92nd percentile isn't hypertensive (≥95th), so no ACE inhibitor yet (A); an asymptomatic A1C of 7.5% starts with **metformin**, not insulin (B); and intensive thrice-daily glucose self-monitoring isn't required for non-insulin-treated, asymptomatic patients (D).

## Q7 — The Pregnant Patient: Managing Common Acute Medical Problems (p. 595)

A pregnant patient presents with nausea and vomiting at 10 weeks estimated gestational age. Which one of the following statements about this patient is correct?

- A. At this gestational age, her symptoms are unlikely to be benign.
- B. It is important for her to keep her stomach as full as possible at all times.
- C. P6 acupressure should be considered as a first-line treatment.
- D. Promethazine and ondansetron (Zofran) tend to aggravate refractory vomiting.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — P6 (wrist) acupressure is a reasonable first-line, nonpharmacologic option.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Nausea/vomiting at 10 weeks is usually **benign** (A wrong); **small, frequent** meals (not a constantly full stomach) help (B wrong); promethazine and ondansetron **treat**, not worsen, vomiting (D wrong).

## Q8 — The Pregnant Patient (p. 595)

Which one of the following conditions can be treated safely without further evaluation during pregnancy?

- A. A new-onset headache.
- B. Vomiting after 20 weeks estimated gestational age.
- C. Heartburn with a positive Murphy sign.
- D. Nasal congestion and rhinorrhea.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Nasal congestion and rhinorrhea (rhinitis of pregnancy / common cold).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The others are **red flags**: new headache (preeclampsia), **late** vomiting (preeclampsia/HELLP, GI pathology), and a positive **Murphy sign** (cholecystitis) all warrant evaluation (A, B, C).

## Q9 — The Pregnant Patient (p. 595)

A pregnant woman at 38 weeks estimated gestational age has severe itching but no rash. Which one of the following is correct?

- A. She should be given the varicella vaccine.
- B. Liver function tests and serum bile acid levels should be obtained.
- C. She should be treated with topical steroids.
- D. She should be given a course of nitrofurantoin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Obtain liver function tests and serum bile acid levels.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Severe third-trimester pruritus **without a rash** suggests **intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy** (palms/soles) — check bile acids/LFTs (it raises stillbirth risk). Varicella vaccine is contraindicated in pregnancy (A).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Culture-Specific Asthma Education Programs for Minority Groups (p. 572)

Which one of the following statements about culture-specific asthma education programs is correct?

- A. They improve asthma control in children.
- B. They reduce the number of severe asthma exacerbations requiring hospitalization in children.
- C. They have no effect on overall asthma exacerbations in children.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Culturally tailored asthma education improves control and reduces hospitalizations for exacerbations, though the effect on **overall** exacerbation counts was not clearly demonstrated — capturing the mixed findings.

## Q11 — Implementing AHRQ Effective Health Care Review: Treatment of Knee Osteoarthritis (p. 603)

Which one of the following treatments for knee osteoarthritis improves long-term (more than 26 weeks) pain outcomes?

- A. Platelet-rich plasma injections.
- B. Glucosamine and chondroitin.
- C. Combined exercise programs.
- D. Tai chi.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Combined (multimodal) exercise programs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Exercise has the best evidence for **durable** knee OA pain relief. PRP, glucosamine/chondroitin, and tai chi lack strong long-term (>26-week) evidence (A, B, D).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus — ACP Updated Guidance on A1C Targets (p. 613)

Which one of the following statements about the American College of Physicians' (ACP) guidelines on A1C targets in type 2 diabetes mellitus is correct?

- A. The target A1C should be between 6% and 7% in most individuals with diabetes.
- B. A stricter A1C target is preferred in patients with other chronic conditions.
- C. The target A1C for older patients is 7%.
- D. When the A1C level is below 6.5%, medication doses can be decreased.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — If A1C falls below 6.5%, consider de-intensifying (decreasing) medications.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The ACP recommended a target of **7%–8%** for most (not 6–7%, A), **less strict** goals with limited life expectancy/comorbidity (B reversed), and **de-intensification** for older/frail patients rather than a fixed 7% (C). De-escalating at A1C <6.5% avoids hypoglycemia.
