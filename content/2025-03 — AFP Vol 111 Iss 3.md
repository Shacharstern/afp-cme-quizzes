## Q1 — Acute Bronchitis: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 214)
Patients should be counseled that the typical duration of an episode of acute bronchitis is which one of the following?

- A. 7 days.
- B. 1 to 2 weeks.
- C. 10 to 14 days.
- D. 2 to 3 weeks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — 2 to 3 weeks.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The cough of acute bronchitis typically lasts up to ~3 weeks (mean ~18 days). Counseling patients on this expected duration reduces inappropriate antibiotic requests.

## Q2 — Acute Bronchitis (p. 214)
Which one of the following patient characteristics is associated with a very low likelihood of community-acquired pneumonia in healthy patients younger than 70 years?

- A. Normal vital signs and chest examination.
- B. Absence of sputum production.
- C. Absence of wheezing.
- D. Absence of coryza.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Normal vital signs and chest examination.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Absence of vital-sign abnormalities (fever, tachycardia, tachypnea, hypoxia) plus a normal lung exam makes CAP very unlikely and argues against chest radiography in otherwise healthy younger adults.

## Q3 — Chronic Pelvic Pain in Women (p. 218)
A 50-year-old postmenopausal woman has long experienced pelvic pain associated with childhood sexual abuse. Which one of the following physical findings is most clearly associated with central sensitization?

- A. Lack of Carnett sign.
- B. Cutaneous allodynia and hyperalgesia.
- C. Atrophy of the external genitalia.
- D. Postcoital bleeding.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Cutaneous allodynia and hyperalgesia.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Allodynia (pain to non-painful stimulus) and hyperalgesia are hallmarks of central sensitization. A relates to abdominal-wall vs visceral pain; C and D point to local mucosal/atrophic causes.

## Q4 — Chronic Pelvic Pain in Women (p. 218)
A 28-year-old woman with worsening noncyclic pelvic pain for 5 years, unresponsive to OTC analgesics, has an unremarkable exam and negative pregnancy, STI, and pelvic ultrasound workup. Which one of the following treatments should be recommended?

- A. Sertraline (SSRI).
- B. Duloxetine (SNRI).
- C. Tizanidine (skeletal muscle relaxant).
- D. Surgery referral for uterosacral ligament or nerve ablation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Duloxetine (SNRI).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> SNRIs (duloxetine) have the best evidence for chronic/centralized pain — they target both pain and mood. SSRIs are weaker for pain. Tizanidine and ablative surgery (LUNA) are not recommended (LUNA shown ineffective). **⚠️ Verify** SNRI vs SSRI wording.

## Q5 — Pacifiers: Common Questions and Answers (p. 230)
A 4-day-old infant has lost 11% of her birth weight, and the mother reports breastfeeding latch issues. Which one of the following actions would be most appropriate regarding a pacifier?

- A. Ask to see the pacifier to ensure it is an orthodontic type.
- B. Avoid pacifier use until 6 months of age.
- C. Delay offering a pacifier until breastfeeding has been well established.
- D. Recommend offering a pacifier when putting the infant to bed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Delay until breastfeeding is well established.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> With ongoing latch problems and excess weight loss, introducing a pacifier risks worsening breastfeeding; delay until feeding is established (typically 3–4 weeks). Orthodontic type confers no proven benefit (A).

## Q6 — Pacifiers (p. 230)
Which one of the following is a benefit of weaning pacifier use by 12 months of age?

- A. Increased breastfeeding duration.
- B. Reduced rate of dental malocclusions.
- C. Reduced rate of sudden infant death syndrome.
- D. Reduced rate of acute otitis media.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Reduced rate of acute otitis media.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Pacifier use increases AOM risk, so weaning by ~12 months lowers AOM incidence. SIDS protection (C) is a benefit of *use* in the first 6 months, not of weaning; malocclusion is driven by prolonged use beyond toddlerhood. **⚠️ Verify** D vs B.

## Q7 — Care of Infants and Children With Down Syndrome (p. 236)
A 2-year-old with Down syndrome has a normal well-child exam and no parental concerns. Which one of the following tests is recommended for this child?

- A. Flexion/extension cervical spine radiography.
- B. Testicular examination.
- C. Sleep study.
- D. Measurement of tissue transglutaminase IgA.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Sleep study (polysomnography).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> OSA is highly prevalent and often silent in Down syndrome; AAP recommends polysomnography for ALL children with DS by age 4 regardless of symptoms — parental report is unreliable. Routine flexion/extension C-spine films are no longer recommended (A); celiac testing (D) is symptom-driven. **⚠️ Verify** C vs D given the child is only 2 (PSG guideline is "by age 4").

## Q8 — Down Syndrome (p. 236)
Which one of the following statements about antepartum screening tests for Down syndrome is correct?

- A. A double-bubble sign on prenatal ultrasonography is diagnostic for Down syndrome.
- B. Screening should be offered only to high-risk pregnant patients.
- C. Second trimester screening is more sensitive than integrated first and second trimester screening.
- D. Cell-free DNA has the lowest false-positive rate of all the screening options.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cell-free DNA has the lowest false-positive rate.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> cfDNA (NIPT) has the highest sensitivity and lowest false-positive rate. A wrong — double-bubble suggests duodenal atresia (associated, not diagnostic). B wrong — screening offered to all. C wrong — integrated screening outperforms second-trimester alone.

## Q10 — The Pretravel Consultation (p. 245)
A 22-year-old develops acute non-bloody diarrhea while traveling and is unable to leave his hotel room. In addition to oral hydration, which one of the following is advised?

- A. One 4-mg dose of loperamide.
- B. Single-dose antibiotic self-treatment.
- C. An antibiotic continued until symptoms subside.
- D. A fluoroquinolone if in an area with resistant enterotoxigenic E. coli.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Single-dose antibiotic self-treatment.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Moderate–severe traveler's diarrhea (functional impairment, can't leave room) warrants single-dose antibiotic self-therapy (azithromycin preferred; fluoroquinolone where appropriate), usually with loperamide. A (loperamide alone) is for mild disease; C is unnecessarily prolonged; D is backwards (fluoroquinolones avoided where resistance is high). **⚠️ Verify** B vs A.

## Q15 — Practice Guidelines: Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease (ACG) (p. 284)
A 32-year-old with 6 months of heavy alcohol use presents with acute jaundice, elevated transaminases, AST:ALT 1.5, and MELD 21. Per ACG, which one of the following is most likely to reduce mortality risk?

- A. A granulocyte colony–stimulating factor.
- B. Pentoxifylline (hemorheologic agent).
- C. Prednisolone (corticosteroid).
- D. Rifaximin (antibiotic).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Prednisolone (corticosteroid).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> This is severe alcohol-associated hepatitis (MELD >20). ACG recommends corticosteroids (prednisolone) to improve short-term (28-day) survival. Pentoxifylline is no longer recommended; G-CSF and rifaximin are not first-line for reducing mortality here.
