## Q1 — Evaluation of Jaundice in Adults (p. 25)
A patient presents for a wellness visit with apparent jaundice. Which one of the following imaging modalities is a first-line option for diagnosis?

- A. MRI of the chest and abdomen.
- B. CT without contrast media.
- C. Ultrasonography of the abdomen.
- D. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Abdominal ultrasonography.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> US is first-line — it distinguishes obstructive (dilated ducts, stones) from hepatocellular causes, is cheap, and avoids radiation/contrast. ERCP is therapeutic/invasive; CT and MRI are second-line.

## Q2 — Evaluation of Jaundice in Adults (p. 25)
Which one of the following conditions is a common extrahepatic cause of conjugated hyperbilirubinemia?

- A. Obstruction from choledocholithiasis (common bile duct stone).
- B. Hepatocellular damage from viral hepatitis.
- C. Sarcoidosis.
- D. Gilbert syndrome.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Choledocholithiasis (biliary obstruction).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia from an *extrahepatic* cause = obstruction of the biliary tree (CBD stone). Viral hepatitis is hepatocellular (intrahepatic); Gilbert causes *unconjugated* hyperbilirubinemia.

## Q3 — Early Childhood Concerns (p. 31)
Parents of a 6-month-old report he cries for several hours after being put in his crib; no medical problems found. Which one of the following is recommended for addressing early childhood sleep problems?

- A. Treat the child with low-dose melatonin.
- B. Allow the child to cry until he falls asleep.
- C. Move the nighttime bath to early morning.
- D. Refer the child for a sleep medicine evaluation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Behavioral extinction (allowing the child to self-soothe to sleep).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Behavioral interventions (graduated/standard extinction plus consistent bedtime routines) are first-line for early childhood sleep problems. Melatonin isn't recommended for routine infant sleep, and sleep-medicine referral isn't needed without red flags. **⚠️ Verify** wording (graduated vs full extinction).

## Q4 — Early Childhood Concerns (p. 31)
Parents are concerned about their 5-year-old's picky eating. Which one of the following is recommended?

- A. Don't allow the child to leave the table until all plated foods are eaten.
- B. Reassure the parents that picky eating is temporary for most children.
- C. Encourage ≥36 oz/day of milk supplementation.
- D. Introduce new foods five times before removing them from the diet.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Reassure that picky eating is usually temporary.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Most picky eating is developmentally normal and self-limited. Coercive feeding (A) backfires; excessive milk (C) displaces iron-rich foods; new foods often need 8–15 exposures, not 5 (D).

## Q5 — Dysuria: Evaluation and Differential Diagnosis in Adults (p. 37)
A 21-year-old woman has 3 days of dysuria with mild frequency, urgency, and new vaginal discharge, and prior UTIs. Which one of the following findings decreases the likelihood of UTI?

- A. Urinary frequency.
- B. Urinary urgency.
- C. Presence of vaginal discharge.
- D. History of UTI.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Vaginal discharge.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Vaginal discharge/irritation lowers the probability of UTI and points toward vaginitis or cervicitis/STI. Frequency, urgency, and prior UTI all *raise* the likelihood of UTI.

## Q6 — Dysuria (p. 37)
A 35-year-old man has recurrent dysuria despite urethritis treatment; NAAT negative for gonorrhea and chlamydia. Which one of the following is the next step?

- A. CT of abdomen and pelvis without contrast.
- B. Testing for Mycoplasma genitalium.
- C. Treatment for prostatitis.
- D. Testing for Trichomonas vaginalis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Test for Mycoplasma genitalium.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Persistent/recurrent non-gonococcal, non-chlamydial urethritis is most often due to *M. genitalium*. Trichomonas is a less common cause; CT and empiric prostatitis treatment aren't the next step. **⚠️ Verify** B vs D.

## Q7 — Acute Rhinosinusitis: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 47)
Which one of the following statements about diagnosing acute bacterial rhinosinusitis is correct?

- A. The physician's overall clinical impression is not helpful.
- B. Cacosmia, purulent nasal discharge, and maxillary tooth pain make bacterial rhinosinusitis more likely.
- C. Radiography or CT is recommended to confirm the diagnosis.
- D. Antral puncture for fluid collection is recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Cacosmia, purulent discharge, and maxillary tooth pain raise the likelihood.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> ABRS is a clinical diagnosis. Overall clinical impression IS useful (A wrong); imaging is not routinely recommended (C wrong); antral puncture is invasive and not used clinically (D wrong).

## Q8 — Acute Rhinosinusitis (p. 47)
Per IDSA, which one of the following is an indication for prescribing antibiotics for acute bacterial rhinosinusitis?

- A. Change in color of nasal discharge.
- B. Fever greater than 100.4°F (38.0°C).
- C. Significant worsening after 3 to 5 days (double-sickening).
- D. Moderate symptoms lasting longer than 3 days.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Double-sickening (worsening after initial improvement).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> IDSA antibiotic criteria: severe onset (≥3–4 days of high fever + purulence), persistent symptoms ≥10 days, OR worsening/"double-sickening." Discharge color change alone (A) doesn't indicate bacterial infection.

## Q9 — Sudden Vision Loss: A Diagnostic Approach (p. 54)
An 80-year-old woman has sudden painless right-eye vision loss and a new right temporal headache. Which one of the following should be initiated immediately?

- A. Temporal artery biopsy.
- B. MR angiography of the right internal carotid artery.
- C. Initiation of intravenous steroids.
- D. Consultation with ophthalmology.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Immediate high-dose (IV) corticosteroids.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> This is giant cell arteritis threatening the contralateral eye. Start corticosteroids *immediately* — do NOT delay for biopsy (biopsy remains positive for ~1–2 weeks after steroids are begun).

## Q10 — Sudden Vision Loss (p. 54)
A 70-year-old with hypertension and hyperlipidemia has painless right-eye vision loss (light perception only) starting 1 hour ago, preceded by transient monocular vision loss 2 weeks earlier. Which one of the following is the most likely cause?

- A. Central retinal artery occlusion.
- B. Optic neuritis.
- C. Temporal arteritis.
- D. Acute angle-closure glaucoma.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Central retinal artery occlusion.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Sudden painless monocular vision loss with vascular risk factors and preceding amaurosis fugax (transient monocular blindness) is classic embolic CRAO. Optic neuritis is painful and younger; angle-closure is painful with a red eye; GCA usually has systemic/temporal symptoms.

## Q11 — GER in Infants and Children (p. 62)
A term (39-week) 10-month-old, exclusively breast-milk fed, has regurgitation and irritability after feeds. Both parents have GERD; mother has mild asthma. Which one of the following is a risk factor for developing GERD in this infant?

- A. Prematurity.
- B. Exclusively feeding with breast milk.
- C. Parental history of GERD.
- D. Asthma in his mother.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Parental history of GERD.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Family history confers increased GERD risk. Prematurity is a risk factor generally but this infant is term (so A doesn't apply here); breastfeeding is protective (B); the mother's asthma isn't his risk factor (D). **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q12 — GER in Infants and Children (p. 62)
A 6-month-old has worsening inconsolable crying, feeding refusal, coughing/choking with feeds, and recurrent otitis media — likely progression to GERD. Which one of the following complications is she at risk of developing?

- A. Esophageal stricture.
- B. Pyloric stenosis.
- C. Malrotation.
- D. Intestinal atresia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Esophageal stricture.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Chronic acid exposure in GERD can cause esophagitis and stricture. Pyloric stenosis, malrotation, and intestinal atresia are congenital/anatomic conditions unrelated to reflux.

## Q14 — ACE Inhibitor/ARB for Diabetic Kidney Disease (p. 24)
A patient with type 2 diabetes has microalbuminuria on two occasions ≥3 months apart. Which one of the following statements about ACE inhibitors or ARBs in diabetic nephropathy is correct?

- A. They reduce progression to end-stage kidney disease.
- B. They reduce all-cause mortality.
- C. They reduce cardiovascular mortality.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — They reduce progression to end-stage kidney disease.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The renoprotective benefit (slowing progression to ESKD) is the best-established effect. Mortality and CV-mortality benefits are not consistently demonstrated, so "all of the above" overstates the evidence. **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q15 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: Screening for Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy (p. 81)
A 25-year-old in her first pregnancy presents at ~20 weeks for routine care; uncomplicated, BP 118/58. Per the USPSTF, when should she be screened for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy?

- A. Once, at 20 weeks (this visit).
- B. Once, at 28 weeks.
- C. Once, at 36 weeks.
- D. During each prenatal visit, including this one.
- E. Once per trimester (before 12 weeks, 13–27 weeks, and 28–40 weeks).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Blood pressure measurement at every prenatal visit.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends screening for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy with blood-pressure measurements throughout pregnancy at *each* prenatal visit (Grade B) — not a single time point or once per trimester.

Per AAOS, which one of the following conservative treatments has the best evidence for improving pain and function in hip osteoarthritis?

## Q17 — Practice Guidelines: Rapid Sequence Intubation (SCCM) (p. 92)
A 74-year-old with sepsis (confusion, fever, hypotension) has SpO₂ 86% on nasal cannula and needs intubation. Per SCCM, which one of the following would most likely be beneficial?

- A. Head-down (Trendelenburg) position.
- B. Supine position.
- C. Preoxygenate using high-flow nasal oxygen.
- D. Preoxygenate using oxygen by face mask.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Preoxygenate with high-flow nasal oxygen.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> In hypoxemic critically ill patients, preoxygenation with high-flow nasal oxygen (or NIV) outperforms a simple face mask and reduces peri-intubation desaturation. Head-up (not Trendelenburg/supine) positioning is preferred. **⚠️ Verify** C vs NIV framing.
