## Q1 — Amblyopia: Detection and Treatment (p. 745)

Which one of the following statements about testing for amblyopia is correct?

- A. An occluder is preferable to an eye patch when performing visual acuity tests.
- B. Vision screening should be done once between four and six years of age.
- C. Visual acuity of less than 20/40 in either eye of a child three to five years of age is abnormal.
- D. Photoscreening devices are limited by their inability to capture the red reflex.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Acuity worse than 20/40 in a 3–5-year-old is abnormal.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Age-based thresholds (20/40 at 3–5 years, 20/30 by ~6) trigger referral. Vision screening is recommended **at least once at 3–5 years** (and ideally repeated, B too limited); photoscreeners **do** detect amblyogenic risk factors (D wrong).

## Q2 — Amblyopia (p. 745)

A five-year-old child with eye deviation is found to have moderate amblyopia. Which one of the following statements about the treatment of this patient is correct?

- A. Because of the child's age, treatment will likely be ineffective.
- B. Patching the eye is superior to other treatments.
- C. If treatment is successful, monitoring is no longer required.
- D. Atropine 1% ophthalmic drops can be effective when given on two consecutive days per week.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Atropine 1% drops two consecutive days per week can be effective.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Atropine penalization rivals patching (B wrong). Treatment at age 5 is **effective** (A wrong), and **ongoing monitoring** is needed because amblyopia can recur after success (C wrong).

## Q3 — Influenza: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 751)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of influenza is correct?

- A. Treatment within 24 hours of symptom onset results in a reduction in symptom duration of approximately one day.
- B. Treatment within 48 hours of symptom onset results in a reduction in symptom duration of approximately one day.
- C. Treatment within 72 hours of symptom onset results in a reduction in symptom duration of approximately one day.
- D. Treatment within 96 hours of symptom onset results in a reduction in symptom duration of approximately one day.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Treatment within 24 hours shortens symptoms by about a day.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Antivirals work best the earlier they're started; **starting within 24 hours** gives the greatest benefit (~1 day). Benefit declines after 48 hours, the usual outer window for starting therapy.

## Q4 — Influenza (p. 751)

For which one of the following age ranges is an annual influenza vaccination recommended?

- A. Adults 18 to 65 years.
- B. Children younger than five years and adults 65 years and older.
- C. All people six months and older who do not have contraindications.
- D. All people two years and older.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — All people six months and older without contraindications.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ACIP recommends universal annual influenza vaccination for everyone **≥6 months** of age (barring contraindications) — not just selected age bands.

## Q5 — Cirrhosis: Diagnosis and Management (p. 759)

Which one of the following statements about the progression of chronic liver disease to cirrhosis is correct?

- A. All patients with chronic liver disease will progress to cirrhosis within 20 years.
- B. Men with chronic liver disease are more likely to progress to cirrhosis than women, regardless of the underlying etiology.
- C. Younger patients with chronic liver disease have a greater risk of progression to cirrhosis.
- D. Coinfection with HIV and hepatitis C virus significantly increases the risk of progression to cirrhosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — HIV/HCV coinfection significantly accelerates progression to cirrhosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Not all chronic liver disease progresses (A wrong); progression risk is etiology-dependent, not uniformly male (B); and **older** age increases progression risk (C reversed).

## Q6 — Cirrhosis (p. 759)

A 54-year-old woman has cirrhosis secondary to chronic hepatitis B. Which one of the following recommendations is correct?

- A. She should stop taking her metformin and begin taking insulin, regardless of her A1C level.
- B. Statins are contraindicated.
- C. She should have right upper quadrant ultrasonography every six months.
- D. She should get a 23-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (Pneumovax 23) once she turns 65.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Right upper quadrant ultrasonography every six months (HCC surveillance).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cirrhosis (and chronic HBV) warrants **6-monthly ultrasound** for hepatocellular carcinoma. Metformin can usually continue (A wrong); statins are **safe** in compensated liver disease (B wrong); pneumococcal vaccination is recommended **now**, not deferred to 65 (D wrong).

## Q7 — Cirrhosis (p. 759)

Which one of the following statements about transient elastography is correct?

- A. It is more accurate than biomarker-based scoring tools at ruling out cirrhosis.
- B. One drawback is that it takes 90 minutes to perform.
- C. It is more accurate in patients with obesity.
- D. It is more accurate if performed during an episode of acute hepatic inflammation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — It outperforms biomarker scores for ruling out cirrhosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Transient elastography (FibroScan) is quick (minutes, not 90, B wrong), but **less reliable in obesity** (C reversed) and **falsely elevated during acute inflammation/cholestasis** (D wrong).

## Q8 — Lown Right Care: Overuse of Statins in Older Adults (p. 742)

Which one of the following statements about statins is correct?

- A. The most commonly reported adverse effect is fatigue.
- B. Statins slightly decrease the risk of developing diabetes mellitus.
- C. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force states that current evidence is insufficient to assess the balance of benefits and harms of statins for primary prevention for persons older than 75 years.
- D. There has been a decrease in people older than 65 years taking statins for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease since 2012.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — USPSTF finds insufficient evidence for statin primary prevention in adults >75.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The most common adverse effect is **myalgia**, not fatigue (A); statins **increase** diabetes risk slightly (B reversed); and statin use for primary prevention has **risen**, not fallen (D wrong).

## Q9 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Pancreatic Cancer (p. 771)

Which one of the following statements represents the USPSTF findings regarding the potential benefits and harms of screening for pancreatic cancer?

- A. The USPSTF found adequate evidence that screening for pancreatic cancer improves disease-specific morbidity but not all-cause mortality.
- B. The USPSTF found adequate evidence that the harms of treating pancreatic cancer are small.
- C. The USPSTF found insufficient evidence to assess the benefits and harms of screening for pancreatic cancer.
- D. The USPSTF found no evidence that screening for pancreatic cancer improves disease-specific morbidity or mortality.
- E. Based on the increasing incidence of pancreatic cancer, the USPSTF found convincing evidence that the potential benefits of screening outweigh the harms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — No evidence that screening improves pancreatic cancer morbidity or mortality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends **against** screening asymptomatic adults (a **D** recommendation): no benefit demonstrated, and harms (false positives, surgical morbidity) are at least moderate. This is a recommendation against, not an "insufficient evidence" I statement (C wrong).

## Q10 — Point-of-Care Guides: Diagnosing DVT in Nonpregnant Adults in the Primary Care Setting (p. 778)

Which one of the following statements about the Wells score for evaluating the risk of deep venous thrombosis (DVT) is correct?

- A. It uses a subjective judgment question, which is a potential limitation.
- B. It is more accurate in older patients than in younger patients.
- C. It is more accurate for recurrent than for initial DVT.
- D. It is more accurate than the Dutch Primary Care Rule for ruling out DVT.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — It includes a subjective "alternative diagnosis" judgment — a limitation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Wells score's "an alternative diagnosis is at least as likely" item introduces **subjectivity/inter-rater variability**. It is **less** reliable in recurrent DVT and not clearly superior to the Dutch Primary Care Rule (C, D wrong).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Managing Chronic Hypertension in Pregnant Women — ACOG (p. 782)

According to ACOG, which one of the following is the recommended blood pressure treatment goal for pregnant patients with chronic hypertension?

- A. 110 to 120 mm Hg systolic and 75 to 84 mm Hg diastolic.
- B. 120 to 130 mm Hg systolic and 80 to 89 mm Hg diastolic.
- C. 120 to 139 mm Hg systolic and 80 to 99 mm Hg diastolic.
- D. 120 to 159 mm Hg systolic and 80 to 109 mm Hg diastolic.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — 120–159 mm Hg systolic and 80–109 mm Hg diastolic.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This 2019 ACOG bulletin used a **permissive** target to avoid over-treatment and placental hypoperfusion, treating only above ~160/110. (Note: the later CHAP trial shifted practice toward a <140/90 goal — a useful contrast point.)
