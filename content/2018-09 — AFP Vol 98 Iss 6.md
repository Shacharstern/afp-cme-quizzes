## Q1 — Well-Child Visits for Infants and Young Children (p. 347)

Which one of the following anticipatory guidance statements for children is correct?

- A. Infant walkers should be used to reduce the risk of falls.
- B. Solid foods should be introduced before six months of age to reduce the risk of atopic disease.
- C. Window locks and furniture anchors should be used to reduce the risk of accidents.
- D. Bed sharing with a parent should be encouraged to reduce the risk of sudden infant death syndrome.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Window locks and furniture anchors reduce injury risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Infant **walkers increase** fall/injury risk (A); solids start **around 6 months** (B); **bed-sharing increases SIDS** risk — room-sharing without bed-sharing is advised (D wrong).

## Q2 — Well-Child Visits (p. 347)

Which one of the following dietary recommendations for infants is correct?

- A. Introduction of peanut-based foods and eggs before one year of age decreases the risk of developing food allergies.
- B. Vitamin D supplementation is not needed for exclusively breastfeeding infants because they receive an adequate amount in breast milk.
- C. Breastfeeding is recommended for at least 18 months and ideally for two years.
- D. Cow's milk should be introduced at six months to reduce the risk of iron deficiency.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Early introduction of peanut and egg reduces food-allergy risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Breastfed infants **need vitamin D** supplementation (B wrong); exclusive breastfeeding is recommended ~6 months with continuation to ~12 months+ (C overstates the minimum); **cow's milk is delayed until 12 months** (causes iron deficiency, D wrong).

## Q3 — Hemolytic Anemia: Evaluation and Differential Diagnosis (p. 354)

In a patient with macrocytic anemia and suspected hemolysis, which one of the following levels should be decreased?

- A. Lactate dehydrogenase.
- B. Unconjugated bilirubin.
- C. Haptoglobin.
- D. Reticulocytes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Haptoglobin (decreased).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Hemolysis **consumes haptoglobin** (low), while **LDH, unconjugated bilirubin, and reticulocytes rise** (A, B, D).

## Q4 — Hemolytic Anemia (p. 354)

Which one of the following characteristics increases the PLASMIC score for ADAMTS13 enzyme activity in a patient with suspected thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura?

- A. Platelet count of 60 × 10³ per μL.
- B. Creatinine level of 1.5 mg per dL.
- C. History of cancer.
- D. History of kidney transplantation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A creatinine <2.0 mg/dL (here 1.5) scores a point.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PLASMIC points include **platelets <30** (60 doesn't qualify, A), **creatinine <2.0**, MCV <90, INR <1.5, hemolysis, **no active cancer, and no solid-organ/stem-cell transplant** — so cancer and transplant **lower** the score (C, D).

## Q5 — Hemolytic Anemia (p. 354)

Which one of the following historical or physical findings suggests hemolytic uremic syndrome as the etiology of hemolytic anemia?

- A. Diarrhea.
- B. Hematuria.
- C. Family history of hemolytic anemia.
- D. Recent blood transfusion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Diarrhea (often bloody, preceding illness).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Typical HUS follows **Shiga toxin–producing E. coli** diarrheal illness, presenting with the triad of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury.

## Q6 — Intestinal Obstruction: Evaluation and Management (p. 362)

In a patient with suspected acute intestinal obstruction, which one of the following laboratory findings suggests dehydration?

- A. Elevated serum lactate level.
- B. Decreased hematocrit level.
- C. Elevated blood urea nitrogen level.
- D. Leukocytosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Elevated blood urea nitrogen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A high BUN (with hemoconcentration → **increased** hematocrit, B reversed) reflects volume depletion. Elevated lactate suggests **ischemia/strangulation** and leukocytosis suggests inflammation/infection (A, D).

## Q7 — Intestinal Obstruction (p. 362)

Which one of the following imaging studies is recommended by the American College of Radiology as the initial test in patients with suspected acute intestinal obstruction?

- A. Computed tomography.
- B. Contrast fluoroscopy.
- C. Ultrasonography.
- D. Magnetic resonance imaging.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Computed tomography (with contrast).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CT is the ACR-preferred initial study — it confirms obstruction, localizes the transition point, and identifies the cause and complications (ischemia, perforation).

## Q8 — Breastfeeding: Common Questions and Answers (p. 368)

Which one of the following statements about supporting continuation of breastfeeding in a new mother is correct?

- A. Telephone support is equal to face-to-face support strategies in preventing breastfeeding cessation.
- B. Skin-to-skin contact at birth has no effect on the likelihood that an infant will still be breastfeeding at one to four months.
- C. The Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative leads to sustained breastfeeding for up to six months after leaving the hospital.
- D. Professional support, such as from physicians and nurses trained in lactation support, is beneficial in prolonging breastfeeding.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Professional (trained) lactation support prolongs breastfeeding.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Face-to-face support outperforms telephone-only (A); **skin-to-skin contact improves** continuation (B reversed); the Baby-Friendly Initiative helps initiation but doesn't reliably sustain to 6 months (C overstated).

## Q9 — Breastfeeding (p. 368)

Which one of the following statements about the infant's latch during breastfeeding is correct?

- A. The infant's neck should be flexed.
- B. The nipple should be guided downward into the infant's mouth.
- C. The LATCH (latch, audible swallowing, type of nipple, comfort of mother, help required) scoring system can be used to assess for an adequate latch and determine the need for intervention.
- D. Frenotomy to treat ankyloglossia reduces latch effectiveness in the initial period.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The LATCH scoring system assesses latch adequacy and need for intervention.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The neck should be slightly **extended** (A wrong), the nipple aimed toward the **palate/upward** (B wrong), and **frenotomy improves** latch in tongue-tie (D reversed).

## Q10 — Breastfeeding (p. 368)

Which one of the following is the most common cause of nipple pain?

- A. Dermatologic conditions.
- B. Strong infant suck.
- C. Milk blisters.
- D. Improper infant positioning.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Improper infant positioning/latch.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most early nipple pain results from **poor positioning and latch** — correct this first before considering dermatologic causes, blebs, or infection.

## Q11 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Cardiovascular Disease Risk with Electrocardiography (p. 375)

A 55-year-old man at low 10-year CVD risk, asymptomatic, with no risk factors, presents for an annual visit. Based on the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommendation on screening for CVD risk with electrocardiography, which one of the following statements about screening this patient is correct?

- A. The patient should be screened because male sex is a risk factor for CVD.
- B. The patient should be screened because older age is a risk factor for CVD.
- C. The patient should be considered for screening after a discussion of the benefits and harms.
- D. The patient should not be screened because he has no family history of CVD.
- E. The patient should not be screened because the evidence suggests there is no net benefit of screening in asymptomatic, low-risk patients.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **E — Don't screen — there's no net benefit of ECG screening in asymptomatic, low-risk adults.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends **against** resting/exercise ECG in **low-risk** asymptomatic adults (a **D** recommendation; for intermediate/high risk it's an I statement). The reason is lack of net benefit, not specifically family history (D).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Deprescribing Antipsychotics for Behavioral and Psychological Symptoms of Dementia and Insomnia (p. 394)

Which one of the following statements about deprescribing antipsychotics is correct?

- A. Antipsychotics can be stopped immediately in older patients taking them for insomnia.
- B. Antipsychotics should be stopped in an older patient with dementia and aggressive behavior who has not improved with therapy after two weeks.
- C. Antipsychotics should not be deprescribed in a patient with dementia who is being treated for psychosis without treatment response.
- D. At least four attempts at deprescribing antipsychotic therapy should be made before resuming low-dose therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Antipsychotics used for insomnia can be stopped immediately (no taper needed).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Antipsychotics aren't appropriate for insomnia and can simply be discontinued. For dementia behavioral symptoms, attempt deprescribing after **stabilization (~3 months)** rather than a fixed 2-week rule (B), and **deprescribe even** when there's no treatment response (C reversed).
