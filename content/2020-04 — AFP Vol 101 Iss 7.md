## Q1 — Cutaneous Cryosurgery for Common Skin Conditions (p. 399)

Which one of the following statements about cryotherapy for actinic keratoses is correct?

- A. Increasing freeze time does not increase the success rate.
- B. Treatment requires two to three freeze cycles.
- C. The success rate is 69% for a freeze time greater than five seconds.
- D. At least 30 seconds of freeze time is recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The success rate is ~69% for a freeze time greater than five seconds.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Longer freeze times **increase** clearance (A wrong). A **single** freeze cycle is typically used for actinic keratoses (B wrong), and 30 seconds isn't required for these superficial lesions (D wrong).

## Q2 — Cutaneous Cryosurgery (p. 399)

Which one of the following statements about cryotherapy for malignant lesions is correct?

- A. It is an option for any basal cell carcinoma.
- B. It is an option for low-risk basal cell and squamous cell carcinoma lesions.
- C. Biopsy is not required for typically appearing basal cell carcinoma lesions before cryotherapy.
- D. It is not recommended for treatment of any malignant skin lesions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — It is an option for low-risk basal cell and squamous cell carcinomas.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cryotherapy can treat **low-risk** BCC/SCC — not high-risk/recurrent or facial high-risk lesions (A too broad, D too restrictive). **Biopsy confirmation is required before** treating any malignancy (C wrong).

## Q3 — Cutaneous Cryosurgery (p. 399)

Which one of the following statements about cryotherapy for viral skin lesions is correct?

- A. For keratinized plantar warts, a single treatment cycle is usually adequate.
- B. For plantar warts, at least four weeks should be allowed between treatment cycles.
- C. Keratolytics or shaving the wart should be avoided before cryotherapy.
- D. Cryotherapy is similarly effective to daily salicylic acid for warts.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cryotherapy is similarly effective to daily salicylic acid for warts.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Thick plantar warts usually need **multiple** cycles (A wrong) repeated every **2–3 weeks** (B wrong), and **paring/keratolytics improve** results (C wrong). Overall efficacy is comparable to topical salicylic acid.

## Q4 — Sepsis: Diagnosis and Management (p. 409)

Which one of the following infections is the most common cause of sepsis overall?

- A. Genitourinary tract infection.
- B. Infection related to an indwelling device.
- C. Pneumonia.
- D. Skin and soft tissue infection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pneumonia.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Respiratory infection (pneumonia) is the **most common** source of sepsis, followed by genitourinary and intra-abdominal infections.

## Q5 — Sepsis (p. 409)

Based on current guidelines, which one of the following statements about intravenous fluid administration in patients with sepsis-related hypotension is correct?

- A. Albumin is the fluid of choice, if available.
- B. 30 mL per kg of an intravenous crystalloid, such as saline, should be administered within the first three hours of presentation.
- C. A 1-L bolus of saline should be administered every hour until blood pressure normalizes.
- D. Administration of vasopressor therapy via peripheral administration is preferred over a central venous catheter.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Give 30 mL/kg of IV crystalloid within the first three hours.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Surviving Sepsis bundle calls for **30 mL/kg crystalloid** early. **Crystalloid** (not albumin) is first-line (A); fixed hourly boluses regardless of response aren't recommended (C); central access is preferred for sustained vasopressors (D).

## Q6 — Sepsis (p. 409)

Following adequate fluids, the patient's MAP remains below 65 mm Hg. Which one of the following vasopressor therapies should be used first?

- A. Epinephrine.
- B. Norepinephrine.
- C. Vasopressin.
- D. Vasopressin plus epinephrine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Norepinephrine.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor** in septic shock. Vasopressin or epinephrine are added as second agents if the MAP target isn't met.

## Q7 — Frequent Headaches: Evaluation and Management (p. 419)

A 50-year-old man presents with suspected trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia. He is otherwise healthy with normal neurologic findings. Which one of the following is the most appropriate diagnostic approach?

- A. Magnetic resonance imaging with and without contrast.
- B. Positron emission tomography.
- C. Transcranial Doppler ultrasonography.
- D. Imaging is not warranted.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — MRI with and without contrast.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias (e.g., cluster headache) warrant **brain MRI with and without contrast** to exclude secondary/pituitary causes, even with a normal exam. PET and transcranial Doppler aren't indicated (B, C).

## Q8 — Frequent Headaches (p. 419)

Which one of the following is considered a red flag finding that warrants further investigation in a patient with frequent headaches?

- A. Headache onset is slow over time.
- B. Position change does not affect headache.
- C. Sleep and emotional disturbances.
- D. Headaches associated with exercise or the Valsalva maneuver.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Headache triggered by exercise or the Valsalva maneuver.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Exertional/Valsalva-triggered headache (raising suspicion for posterior fossa/structural lesions) is a red flag — as are **thunderclap** onset, neurologic deficits, age >50 new onset, and systemic symptoms. Gradual onset and stable benign features are reassuring (A, B, C).

## Q9 — Frequent Headaches (p. 419)

A patient with frequent migraine takes sumatriptan (Imitrex) every other day, and the headache has escalated to chronic migraine. Which one of the following statements about treatment is correct?

- A. He will need inpatient treatment for gradual tapering.
- B. Some studies indicate that topiramate (Topamax) may be effective.
- C. The patient is a candidate for small-molecule calcitonin gene-related peptide receptor antagonists.
- D. Nonpharmacologic approaches would most likely be ineffective for this patient.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Some studies indicate topiramate may be effective.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This is **medication-overuse headache** (frequent triptan use). Topiramate has evidence for chronic migraine/MOH and can be used during withdrawal. Most patients are managed **outpatient** (A); nonpharmacologic measures **do** help (D wrong).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Pelvic Floor Muscle Training vs. Control for Urinary Incontinence in Women (p. 393)

Which one of the following statements about pelvic floor muscle training for urinary incontinence in women is correct?

- A. Pelvic floor muscle training has been shown to reduce the average number of leakage episodes per day in patients with stress urinary incontinence vs. those receiving control treatments.
- B. Pelvic floor muscle training does not reduce the volume of leakage in all subtypes of urinary incontinence (i.e., stress, urge, and mixed) compared with control treatments.
- C. Pelvic floor muscle training does not increase the likelihood of improvement or cure in all subtypes of urinary incontinence.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — PFMT reduces daily leakage episodes in stress urinary incontinence vs. control.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PFMT is first-line for stress (and mixed) incontinence — it increases cure/improvement and reduces leakage frequency and volume, making B and C (and therefore D) incorrect.

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Interventions for Reducing Childhood Obesity (p. 394)

Which one of the following statements about the use of dietary and physical activity interventions in patients at risk of childhood obesity is correct?

- A. Guidelines from the Department of Health and Human Services recommend at least an hour a day of moderate-intensity aerobic activity.
- B. Successful interventions include a physical activity component.
- C. No studies of these interventions have reported an increased risk of adverse effects.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> HHS recommends ≥60 minutes/day of moderate-vigorous activity for children; effective obesity-prevention programs incorporate physical activity (often with diet), and no significant harms were reported.

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Frostbite — Wilderness Medical Society (p. 440)

According to the Wilderness Medical Society, which one of the following statements about the management of frostbite is correct?

- A. Walking on a frostbitten foot can limit tissue injury.
- B. The extremity should be warmed only if there is no risk of refreezing.
- C. All patients with frostbite should receive supplemental oxygen to reduce tissue injury.
- D. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs should be avoided.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Rewarm only if there is no risk of refreezing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Refreezing after thawing is devastating** — keep frozen until definitive warm rewarming is possible. Walking on a thawed foot worsens injury (A); oxygen helps only if hypoxic/high altitude (C); **NSAIDs are recommended** (anti-thromboxane, D reversed).
