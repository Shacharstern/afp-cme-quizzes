## Q1 — Smell and Taste Disorders in Primary Care (p. 240)
Which one of the following statements about the evaluation for smell and taste disorders is correct?

- A. Differentiating a smell disorder from a taste disorder is usually easy.
- B. Patients often underestimate their level of olfactory dysfunction.
- C. Testing for a taste disorder is easily performed in any outpatient setting.
- D. All patients with olfactory dysfunction should undergo MRI of the head.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Patients often underestimate their level of olfactory dysfunction.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Self-reported smell loss correlates poorly with objective testing; patients commonly underestimate deficits, so formal psychophysical testing is needed. Smell vs taste is often hard to distinguish clinically (most "taste" complaints are actually olfactory), taste testing is not routinely available, and MRI is reserved for selected cases.

## Q2 — Smell and Taste Disorders (p. 240)
A 34-year-old patient with a 20-pack-year smoking history reports loss of smell. Which statement applies?

- A. Even if they quit, their sense of smell will not return to normal.
- B. Vascular injury has been identified as the major mechanism of smell loss in smokers.
- C. Some evidence suggests the patient's spouse may lose their sense of smell from secondhand smoke.
- D. The effects of secondhand smoke on children's sense of smell have not been studied.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Some evidence suggests the spouse may lose their sense of smell from secondhand smoke.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Smoking cessation can improve olfaction over time (so A is false). The damage is direct injury to olfactory epithelium, not primarily vascular (B false). Secondhand smoke effects on children have been studied (D false).

## Q3 — Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (p. 249)
A 27-year-old (G2P1) with PCOS, BMI 27, prior pregnancy-induced HTN at 34 wk and a 3,830-g infant by cesarean at 38 wk. Which is a risk factor suggesting **early** GDM screening?

- A. Delivery of an infant weighing more than 3,800 g.
- B. Cesarean delivery.
- C. Preterm delivery at 38 weeks.
- D. Polycystic ovary syndrome.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Polycystic ovary syndrome.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PCOS is an established risk factor warranting early (first-trimester) GDM screening. Macrosomia is defined as >4,000 g (3,830 g doesn't qualify), 38 weeks is term (not preterm), and cesarean delivery is not itself a GDM risk factor.

## Q4 — Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (p. 249)
A 32-year-old (G1) at 30 wk: 50-g screen 156 mg/dL; on 100-g OGTT, fasting and 3-hour values slightly **above** NDDG thresholds but 1-hour and 2-hour values **below** thresholds. Most appropriate action?

- A. Perform no further evaluation or treatment.
- B. Repeat a 100-g OGTT in four weeks.
- C. Monitor fasting and two-hour postprandial glucose levels.
- D. Start metformin at 500 mg daily.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Monitor fasting and two-hour postprandial glucose levels.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NDDG criteria require **two or more** abnormal values on the 100-g OGTT to diagnose GDM. With only two elevated values here... (fasting + 3-hour = two abnormal) borderline — the article advises monitoring glucose rather than no action or immediate pharmacotherapy. Reasonable surveillance is the keyed answer.

## Q7 — Peripartum Depression (p. 267)
Which statement about screening for peripartum depression is correct?

- A. TSH and prolactin should be measured in all peripartum patients with depressive symptoms.
- B. Peripartum depression should be considered in all pregnant/postpartum patients with symptoms for 3–4 days.
- C. Patients who screen positive should be assessed for suicidal ideation and comorbid anxiety disorder.
- D. Initial screening is recommended using the PHQ-2 tool.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Assess positive screens for suicidal ideation and comorbid anxiety.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Positive screens require evaluation for suicidality and anxiety comorbidity. Routine TSH/prolactin in everyone is not indicated, the symptom duration threshold is ~2 weeks (not 3–4 days), and the EPDS / PHQ-9 (not PHQ-2 alone) are the validated tools.

## Q8 — Peripartum Depression (p. 267)
Which statement about peripartum depression and medications is correct?

- A. SSRI use has not been associated with poor pregnancy outcomes.
- B. Breastfeeding is an absolute contraindication to pharmacotherapy.
- C. Omega-3 fatty acids are recommended for prevention in the second and third trimesters.
- D. If benefit outweighs risk, sertraline, citalopram, and escitalopram may be considered during pregnancy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Sertraline, citalopram, escitalopram may be considered if benefit outweighs risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SSRIs are used in pregnancy when benefits outweigh risks; sertraline is often preferred. Breastfeeding is not an absolute contraindication, omega-3s aren't a recommended preventive, and SSRIs do carry some (small) outcome associations (so A is overstated).

## Q9 — Primary Aldosteronism (p. 273)
Which statement about primary aldosteronism is correct?

- A. A normal potassium level excludes the diagnosis.
- B. It can lead to hyperkalemia.
- C. It is the cause of hypertension in more than 1 in 20 patients in primary care settings.
- D. Well-controlled hypertension excludes the diagnosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — It causes hypertension in more than 1 in 20 primary care patients.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Primary aldosteronism is far more common than historically thought (~5–10% of hypertension, higher in resistant HTN). Most patients are **normokalemic** (A false); it causes **hypo**kalemia, not hyper (B false); and it can occur even when BP appears controlled (D false).

## Q10 — Primary Aldosteronism (p. 273)
Hypertensive patient with risk factors for PA. Appropriate step for initial case detection?

- A. Measure aldosterone 2 hours after oral ACE inhibitor.
- B. Simultaneously measure plasma aldosterone concentration and plasma renin activity.
- C. Measure 24-hour urine aldosterone after three days of a high-salt diet.
- D. Order CT of the adrenal glands.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Simultaneously measure plasma aldosterone and plasma renin (the aldosterone-renin ratio).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The **aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR)** is the recommended screening test. Confirmatory salt-loading and adrenal CT (with possible adrenal vein sampling) come **after** a positive screen, not as initial case detection.

## Q11 — Delirium in Older Persons (p. 278)
Most effective treatment for **prevention** of delirium in hospitalized patients?

- A. Nonpharmacologic interventions.
- B. Haloperidol, 1 mg IV.
- C. Aripiprazole, 5 mg orally.
- D. Olanzapine (Zyprexa), 10 mg orally.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Nonpharmacologic interventions.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Multicomponent nonpharmacologic bundles (reorientation, sleep hygiene, early mobilization, hydration, sensory aids — e.g., HELP) are the only interventions proven to prevent delirium. Antipsychotics do **not** prevent it.

## Q12 — Delirium in Older Persons (p. 278)
Which strategy is a useful intervention to prevent delirium?

- A. Sufficient hydration and nutrition.
- B. Bed rest.
- C. Opioids to eliminate pain.
- D. Physical restraints.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Sufficient hydration and nutrition.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Adequate hydration/nutrition is a core preventive measure. Bed rest, routine opioids, and restraints all **increase** delirium risk.

## Q13 — Cochrane: Dual vs Triple Inhaler Therapy in Asthma (p. 236)
37-year-old with asthma, two oral-steroid-requiring exacerbations in 12 months despite maintenance therapy. Which statement about ICS/LABA/LAMA triple therapy is correct?

- A. Medium-dose triple therapy reduces steroid-requiring exacerbations vs medium-dose dual (ICS/LABA) therapy.
- B. Triple therapy reduces asthma-related hospitalizations vs medium-dose dual therapy.
- C. Triple therapy increases serious adverse events vs dual therapy.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Triple therapy reduces steroid-requiring exacerbations vs medium-dose dual therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found triple therapy modestly **reduces exacerbations** but did **not** significantly reduce hospitalizations and did **not** increase serious adverse events — so B, C, and "all of the above" are incorrect.

## Q14 — Cochrane: Antibiotics for Acute Uncomplicated Diverticulitis (p. 237)
52-year-old with diverticulosis, left-sided cramping pain, diarrhea, nausea; no sepsis; imaging shows uncomplicated diverticulitis. Which statement about antibiotics is correct?

- A. It decreases the risk of emergency surgery within 30 days.
- B. It lowers the risk of surgery after 30 days.
- C. It lowers the risk of all-cause mortality.
- D. It does not lower the risk of emergency surgery or mortality.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Antibiotics do not lower the risk of emergency surgery or mortality.** 🧠 Generated (content-trusted) · Confidence: High
> ⚠️ **Option-order note:** the embedded print key reads **"A,"** but the on-screen option order here puts the correct conclusion at **D**. RCTs (AVOD, DIABOLO) and the Cochrane review consistently show antibiotics do **not** reduce complications, emergency surgery, recurrence, or mortality in *uncomplicated* diverticulitis. Trust the clinical content (D); cross-reference the print PDF (key letter A) to confirm the print option lettering.

## Q15 — Lown Right Care: Diagnostic Overshadowing (p. 292)
Which statement about diagnostic overshadowing and disabilities is correct?

- A. Overshadowing may occur in patients with mental, but not physical, disabilities.
- B. People with mental illness have a higher incidence of underlying physical illnesses than the general population.
- C. People with disabilities have a lower risk of cancer than the general population.
- D. Only physicians with low (not high) experience are vulnerable to overshadowing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — People with mental illness have a higher incidence of underlying physical illness.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Diagnostic overshadowing — attributing new physical symptoms to a pre-existing mental or physical disability — harms a population that actually carries a **higher** physical-illness burden. It occurs with both mental and physical disabilities and affects clinicians at all experience levels.

## Q16 — Point-of-Care Guides: Suicidal Ideation Screening (p. 311)
Which statement about the Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ) tool is correct?

- A. It has not been validated for use in the primary care setting.
- B. It has not been validated for use in the emergency department.
- C. It takes 20 minutes to administer.
- D. The negative predictive value was 99.5% to 100% in the study populations.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — NPV was 99.5%–100% in the study populations.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The ASQ is a brief (~20-second) tool with a very high negative predictive value, validated in both pediatric primary care and emergency settings — making it excellent for **ruling out** acute suicide risk.

## Q17 — Practice Guidelines: Inpatient Hyperglycemia (Endocrine Society) (p. 313)
53-year-old **without diabetes**, admitted for skin/soft-tissue infection; day-2 point-of-care glucose 220 mg/dL. Per the Endocrine Society, most appropriate treatment?

- A. Start basal-bolus (basal + mealtime + corrective) insulin.
- B. Corrective insulin dosing for elevated glucose.
- C. Daily DPP-4 inhibitor.
- D. Insulin pump therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Corrective ("correction-scale") insulin for the elevated glucose.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For a patient without known diabetes and a single/early hyperglycemic reading, start with corrective insulin and monitor; escalate to scheduled basal-bolus only if hyperglycemia persists. The Endocrine Society also endorses DPP-4 inhibitors as an adjunct in selected non-ICU patients, but corrective insulin is the most appropriate initial step here.

## Q18 — Practice Guidelines: Heart Failure (AHA/ACC) (p. 315)
55-year-old admitted for HF exacerbation; echo shows LVEF 38%. Already on lisinopril, atorvastatin, carvedilol, and spironolactone. Per AHA/ACC, which medication should also be offered?

- A. Empagliflozin (Jardiance), an SGLT-2 inhibitor.
- B. Hydralazine, a vasodilator.
- C. Isosorbide dinitrate, a nitrate.
- D. Verapamil, a nondihydropyridine calcium channel blocker.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Empagliflozin (an SGLT-2 inhibitor).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> He is already on three of the four GDMT pillars (ACE inhibitor, beta-blocker, MRA); the missing pillar is an **SGLT-2 inhibitor**, now recommended for HFrEF regardless of diabetes. Nondihydropyridine CCBs (verapamil) are **harmful** in HFrEF.
