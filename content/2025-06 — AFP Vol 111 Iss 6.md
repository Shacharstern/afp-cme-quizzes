## Q1 — Acute Monoarthritis (p. 497)
A patient presents with knee swelling and pain. There is edema over the knee with erythema and warmth. The patient has decreased passive and active range of motion due to pain. **Which one of the following is most suggestive that the underlying diagnosis affects the joint itself?**

- A. Decreased active range of motion
- B. Decreased passive range of motion
- C. Edema over the joint
- D. Erythema over the joint

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Decreased passive range of motion.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Limited **passive** ROM points to intra-articular pathology (true arthritis). Pain limiting only **active** ROM can arise from periarticular structures (tendon, bursa, muscle). Swelling, warmth, and erythema are nonspecific and occur in both articular and periarticular processes.

## Q2 — Acute Monoarthritis (p. 497)
Arthrocentesis was performed on a patient with suspected septic arthritis to obtain blood cell count and bacterial culture. **Which one of the following synovial fluid results is most suggestive of septic arthritis?**

- A. No bacteria on Gram stain
- B. Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio of 25 or more
- C. Neutrophil level of 90% or more
- D. White blood cell count of 50,000/μL (50 × 10⁹/L) or more

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — WBC count of 50,000/μL or more.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Synovial WBC ≥50,000/μL is the classic threshold raising the likelihood of septic arthritis (≥100,000/μL more so). A negative Gram stain does **not** exclude infection. PMN ≥90% also supports septic arthritis but the WBC count is the single result AFP emphasizes. 
> For septic arthritis:
> 	- a WBC count of more than 50,000/μL has a +LR of 4.7; 
> 	- a WBC count of more than 100,000/μL has a +LR of 13.2; 
> 	- a neutrophil level of more than 90% in the synovial fluid has a +LR of 2.7

## Q3 — Long-Term Opioid Therapy for Nonterminal Pain (p. 508)
**Which one of the following statements about opioid use in the setting of nonterminal chronic pain is correct?**

- A. Opioids are superior to NSAIDs for physical functioning
- B. Buprenorphine is not an appropriate therapy for chronic nonterminal pain
- C. Immediate-release opioids are preferable to extended-release opioids
- D. Opioids should never be discontinued once started because of the risk of morbidity caused by the discontinuation itself

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Immediate-release opioids are preferable to extended-release.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Per CDC 2022 guidance, when opioids are used, **immediate-release** formulations are preferred over extended-release/long-acting, which carry higher overdose risk. Opioids are *not* superior to NSAIDs for function (A false); buprenorphine *is* appropriate (B false); opioids can and sometimes should be tapered (D false).

## Q4 — Long-Term Opioid Therapy for Nonterminal Pain (p. 508)
A 55-year-old patient with severe chronic musculoskeletal pain after a motor vehicle crash 6 months ago has been on long-term opioid therapy (70 MME/day) without significant relief and would like to stop. **Which one of the following statements about this patient is correct?**

- A. The patient is at very low risk of morbidity from opioid discontinuation
- B. The speed of the taper will have no impact on withdrawal symptoms
- C. The patient can expect worse functioning after discontinuation
- D. The patient should be counseled that the tapering process could take months to years to complete

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Tapering could take months to years.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Tapering long-term opioids is slow — often months to years for patients on stable long-term therapy. Abrupt discontinuation carries real morbidity (A false); taper speed strongly affects withdrawal (B false); many patients maintain or improve function after a careful taper (C false).

## Q5 — Nocturia: Evaluation and Management (p. 515)
A 58-year-old man with well-controlled hypertension wakes ≥2 times nightly to urinate for the past few months. No daytime urinary symptoms, feels he empties completely, denies hesitancy or frequency. **Which one of the following would be most appropriate to help diagnose the source of his nocturia?**

- A. Postvoid residual
- B. Ultrasonography of the kidneys and bladder
- C. Comprehensive metabolic panel and complete blood cell count
- D. Assessment of sleep habits and risk of sleep apnea

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Assessment of sleep habits and risk of sleep apnea.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Isolated nighttime voiding **without** daytime LUTS points away from bladder outlet/storage pathology and toward a sleep disorder (e.g., OSA) or nocturnal polyuria. Assess sleep first. PVR and imaging are lower yield given complete emptying and no obstructive symptoms.

## Q6 — Nocturia: Evaluation and Management (p. 515)
A 72-year-old woman reports nocturia at her annual wellness visit. **Which one of the following statements about treating her nocturia is correct?**

- A. Desmopressin should be used cautiously to treat nocturnal polyuria, given the risk for hypernatremia
- B. Antimuscarinic agents, such as oxybutynin, are the preferred initial treatment option for overactive bladder
- C. Local estrogen therapy can be used to treat lower urinary tract symptoms of menopause
- D. Restricting daily fluid intake to between 72 and 80 oz is recommended as a first-line treatment

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Local estrogen therapy for menopausal LUTS.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Vaginal/local estrogen treats genitourinary syndrome of menopause and its LUTS. Desmopressin risk is **hyponatremia**, not hypernatremia (A false). Behavioral therapy — not antimuscarinics — is first-line for OAB (B false). Blanket high fluid restriction is not first-line (D false).

## Q7 — Endometrial Cancer: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 526)
A 42-year-old premenopausal woman has had intermenstrual bleeding for 4 months. Endometrial biopsy 2 months ago was negative, but bleeding persists. Her mother had endometrial carcinoma. **Which one of the following is the most appropriate next step?**

- A. Transvaginal ultrasonography
- B. Repeat endometrial biopsy
- C. Referral for dilation and curettage
- D. Reassurance that the cause is likely benign with follow-up in 6 months

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Referral for dilation and curettage.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Persistent abnormal bleeding **despite a negative office biopsy** warrants hysteroscopy with D&C, because blind office sampling can miss focal lesions — especially with a family history concerning for Lynch-associated cancer. Reassurance (D) is inappropriate. 
> 
> **Dilation and curettage** is recommended if:
> 	- an adequate sample cannot be obtained
> 	- the diagnosis is unclear
> 	- a focal lesion such as an endometrial polyp or mass is present



## Q8 — Endometrial Cancer: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 526)
**Which one of the following procedures is typically recommended for initial treatment of low-risk, stage I endometrial cancer in postmenopausal women?**

- A. Total abdominal hysterectomy without oophorectomy
- B. Minimally invasive total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
- C. Total abdominal hysterectomy plus chemotherapy
- D. Total abdominal hysterectomy plus chemotherapy and radiation

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Minimally invasive total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Standard initial treatment for low-risk, stage I disease is minimally invasive total hysterectomy with BSO. Adjuvant chemo/radiation is reserved for higher-risk/advanced disease; oophorectomy is included in postmenopausal women.

## Q9 — Evaluation of Short and Tall Stature in Children (p. 532)
A 4-year-old boy was >2 SD below normal height until 18 months, now grows normally but is consistently short. Parents average height; father was a "late bloomer." Radiography shows delayed bone age. **Which one of the following outcomes is most likely?**

- A. His adult height will be less than the expected midparental height
- B. He will have catch-up growth at puberty and achieve a normal height
- C. He will require growth hormone to achieve a minimum average height
- D. Accurate prognosis requires endocrine, genetic, and gastrointestinal evaluation

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Catch-up growth at puberty, normal adult height.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Classic **constitutional growth delay** ("late bloomer"): delayed bone age, normal growth velocity, family history. These children have delayed puberty with later catch-up growth and reach a normal adult height near their midparental target. No GH or extensive workup needed.

## Q10 — Evaluation of Short and Tall Stature in Children (p. 532)
A 9-year-old girl with short stature is diagnosed with Turner syndrome. **Which one of the following statements about treatment is correct?**

- A. She should be treated with growth hormone at diagnosis
- B. Growth hormone will need to be continued for the rest of her life
- C. Low-dose androgen therapy is an option for this patient
- D. Pharmacologic treatment is effective only in patients with idiopathic short stature

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — She should be treated with growth hormone at diagnosis.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High ⚠️
>  "The FDA has approved recombinant GH therapy for GH deficiency or insufficiency, Turner syndrome, Prader-Willi syndrome, Noonan syndrome, chronic kidney failure, children born SGA without catch-up growth, those with short stature homeobox-containing (SHOX) gene deficiencies (eg, SHOX gene deficiency or haploinsufficiency), and idiopathic short stature.
>  Individualized therapy should be started as soon as possible after diagnosis to achieve optimal height potential"
>  
>  GH is not lifelong (stopped near final height, B false); pharmacologic treatment helps several conditions, not only idiopathic short stature (D false).

## Q11 — Cochrane: BP Targets in CKD (p. 495)
In patients with CKD, treatment for hypertension is recommended. **Which one of the following statements about lower BP targets (130/80) vs higher targets (160/90) is correct?**

- A. They are associated with decreased total mortality
- B. They are associated with decreased risk of total cardiovascular mortality
- C. They are associated with decreased progression to end-stage renal disease
- D. They do not increase serious adverse events

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — They do not increase serious adverse events.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High ⚠️⚠️
> The Cochrane review found little to no difference between lower and higher targets for total mortality, CV mortality, or progression to ESRD (so A, B, C are not supported), with lower targets not clearly increasing serious adverse events.

## Q15 — Practice Guidelines: Diabetes-Related Foot Infections (IWGDF/IDSA) (p. 562)
A 54-year-old man with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes presents with a painful foot ulcer. Local inflammation (erythema, warmth) but no systemic infection or osteomyelitis. Probe-to-bone test negative. He recently received antibiotics for pneumonia. **According to IWGDF/IDSA guidelines, which one of the following is recommended?**

- A. Culture of a bone sample
- B. Culture of a soft tissue sample
- C. Culture of a wound swab of the ulcer base
- D. Empiric antibiotic treatment

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Culture of a soft tissue sample.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> IWGDF/IDSA recommend a **deep soft-tissue specimen** (curettage or biopsy) rather than a superficial swab (C discouraged). Probe-to-bone negative with no osteomyelitis argues against bone culture (A). Recent antibiotic exposure makes culture-guided therapy preferable to blind empiric treatment (D).
