# AFP CME Quiz — November 15, 2019 (Vol 100, Issue 10)

## Q1 — Depression in Children and Adolescents (p. 609)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of major depressive disorder in children and adolescents is correct?

- A. Most antidepressants are approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for use in adolescents 12 years and older.
- B. Benefit of monotherapy with an antidepressant or psychotherapy is high.
- C. Tricyclic antidepressants are the first-line medication recommended for children and adolescents with depression.
- D. The combination of cognitive behavior therapy and an antidepressant is more effective than either treatment alone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — CBT plus an antidepressant is more effective than either alone.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Combination therapy (per TADS) outperforms monotherapy, whose benefit is only **modest** (B). Only a **few** SSRIs are FDA-approved in youth (A); **TCAs are not effective/recommended** in children (C wrong).

## Q2 — Depression in Children and Adolescents (p. 609)

Which one of the following statements about screening for depressive disorder in children and adolescents is correct?

- A. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force found insufficient evidence to recommend for or against screening for major depressive disorder.
- B. The American Academy of Family Physicians recommends screening up to 19 years of age.
- C. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends annual screening of adolescents with a self-report tool.
- D. The Guidelines for Adolescent Depression in Primary Care recommends one-time screening at 15 years of age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The AAP recommends annual adolescent screening with a self-report tool.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The **USPSTF recommends** screening adolescents 12–18 (a B recommendation, so A is wrong); AAFP follows for ages 12–18 (B wrong); GLAD-PC advises **annual**, not one-time, screening (D wrong).

## Q3 — Depression in Children and Adolescents (p. 609)

Which one of the following medications is U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved for depression in children and adolescents eight years and older?

- A. Fluoxetine (Prozac).
- B. Sertraline (Zoloft).
- C. Paroxetine (Paxil).
- D. Escitalopram (Lexapro).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Fluoxetine (Prozac).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Fluoxetine is FDA-approved from age 8** for depression (escitalopram is approved from 12). **Paroxetine is avoided** in youth (suicidality signal); sertraline is approved for pediatric OCD, not depression.

## Q4 — The Changing Climate: Managing Health Impacts (p. 618)

Which one of the following is the greatest risk factor for heat injury?

- A. Lack of social support.
- B. Previous acute kidney injury.
- C. Female sex.
- D. Age older than 50 years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Lack of social support (social isolation).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Social isolation is the strongest risk factor for heat-related death — isolated, often elderly people lack help to cool/hydrate during heat waves.

## Q5 — The Changing Climate (p. 618)

Which one of the following is most likely to become more of a health threat in the United States because of climate change?

- A. Tetanus.
- B. Chikungunya virus.
- C. Histoplasmosis.
- D. Respiratory syncytial virus.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Chikungunya virus.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Warming expands the range of **Aedes mosquitoes**, increasing the threat of vector-borne arboviruses (chikungunya, dengue, Zika). The other infections aren't primarily climate/vector-driven.

## Q6 — The Changing Climate (p. 618)

Which one of the following should family physicians consider recommending to their patients to improve health and reduce greenhouse gas emissions?

- A. Moving to an inland state to avoid coastal changes.
- B. Installing solar panels on the roof of their house.
- C. Transitioning to active transport (e.g., bicycling, jogging).
- D. Avoiding outdoor exercise.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Transitioning to active transport (bicycling, walking).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Active transport is a "**co-benefit**" — it improves cardiovascular/metabolic health while lowering emissions. It's the clinically actionable counseling point.

## Q7 — Specific Learning Disabilities: The Family Physician's Role (p. 628)

Which one of the following statements about a school-based eligibility evaluation of a child with academic underachievement is correct?

- A. A visual impairment corrected by glasses is considered to be a combined school-based and medical eligibility category.
- B. Intellectual disability and specific learning disability are mutually exclusive eligibility categories.
- C. Response-to-intervention protocols help determine if the child's individualized education program (IEP) is successful.
- D. The cognitive adverse effects of antiepileptic drugs are excluded from IEP eligibility criteria.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Intellectual disability and specific learning disability are mutually exclusive categories.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A specific learning disability requires achievement **below** what cognitive ability predicts — by definition distinct from global intellectual disability. RTI protocols **identify/qualify** children (not measure IEP success, C).

## Q8 — Specific Learning Disabilities (p. 628)

Which one of the following statements about the IEP is correct?

- A. It is transferred automatically from the school to the medical setting.
- B. It is difficult for a physician to make ongoing changes to an IEP.
- C. Children who do not qualify for an IEP may be eligible for a 504 plan.
- D. It is ineffective for children with specific learning disabilities.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Children who don't qualify for an IEP may be eligible for a 504 plan.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A **504 plan** provides accommodations for those who don't meet IDEA/IEP criteria. IEPs require parental consent to share (not automatic, A), can be updated, and **are** effective for learning disabilities (B, D wrong).

## Q9 — Specific Learning Disabilities (p. 628)

Which one of the following statements about the medical evaluation of a child with academic underachievement is correct?

- A. The physician provides a summary of identified medical needs or medical evaluation steps.
- B. The physician determines whether a child has a specific learning disability.
- C. A subspecialty referral for neurodevelopmental disorders is managed through the child's school.
- D. School evaluations and services for conditions such as asthma or epilepsy, which can result in absenteeism, can proceed without medical evaluation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — The physician summarizes identified medical needs/evaluation steps.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The physician's role is **medical** — identify/treat conditions and communicate needs to the school. The **school**, not the physician, formally determines a specific learning disability (B); subspecialty referrals are medical (C); medical conditions affecting school need medical input (D).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for HIV Infection and Preexposure Prophylaxis (p. 637)

A 29-year-old transgender woman sexually active with men (oral and anal intercourse, condoms ~two-thirds of the time) tests negative for HIV/STIs. According to the USPSTF, which one of the following behaviors or factors makes her a candidate for preexposure prophylaxis?

- A. She regularly receives STI screenings by visiting urgent care centers.
- B. She is a transgender woman.
- C. She has never tested positive for STIs.
- D. She occasionally does not use condoms when having sex with partners who are at high risk of HIV infection.
- E. She has never engaged in transactional sex work.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Inconsistent condom use with partners at high risk of HIV.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF (grade **A**) recommends PrEP for people at increased risk — defined by **behaviors** like inconsistent condom use with high-risk partners. Transgender identity alone, negative STI history, or screening location aren't the qualifying criteria (B, C, A, E).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Gestational Hypertension and Preeclampsia — ACOG (p. 649)

According to ACOG, which one of the following patients should receive low-dose aspirin prophylaxis starting between 12 and 28 weeks' gestation?

- A. A 28-year-old in her first pregnancy.
- B. A 23-year-old with two previous pregnancies without preeclampsia, but whose mother had preeclampsia.
- C. A 24-year-old in her first pregnancy who has taken medication for chronic hypertension for three years.
- D. A 21-year-old female with obesity and one previous pregnancy without preeclampsia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The patient with chronic hypertension.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A **high-risk** factor (chronic hypertension, prior preeclampsia, diabetes, renal/autoimmune disease, multifetal gestation) warrants low-dose aspirin. Nulliparity, family history, and obesity are **moderate** factors that require **two** to trigger prophylaxis (A, B, D).
