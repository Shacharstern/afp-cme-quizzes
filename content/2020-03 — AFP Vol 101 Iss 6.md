## Q1 — Managing Hypertension Using Combination Therapy (p. 341)

Which one of the following combinations of antihypertensive agents is inappropriate for a 44-year-old white man with no comorbidities whose blood pressure is 162/91 mm Hg?

- A. A calcium channel blocker (CCB) and thiazide diuretic.
- B. An angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor (ACEI) and CCB.
- C. An ACEI and angiotensin receptor blocker.
- D. A thiazide diuretic and ACEI.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — An ACEI plus an ARB.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Never combine an ACEI and an ARB** (dual RAAS blockade → hyperkalemia, AKI, no added benefit). The other pairings (CCB + thiazide, ACEI + CCB, thiazide + ACEI) are all recommended combinations.

## Q2 — Managing Hypertension Using Combination Therapy (p. 341)

A 72-year-old black woman with type 2 diabetes and hypertension takes only hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg daily. She now has microalbuminuria. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Her hydrochlorothiazide should be switched to a CCB.
- B. An angiotensin receptor blocker could be added to her regimen.
- C. Eighth Joint National Committee guidelines do not apply because of her age.
- D. Increasing her hydrochlorothiazide dosage should be the first step to intensify therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — An ARB could be added to her regimen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Microalbuminuria/diabetes** is a compelling indication for a renoprotective ACEI **or** ARB, regardless of race. Adding (not just up-titrating the diuretic) is appropriate; JNC 8 guidance still applies (C, D wrong).

## Q3 — Genital Ulcers: Differential Diagnosis and Management (p. 355)

Which one of the following is the most common cause of genital ulcers in the United States?

- A. Herpes simplex virus.
- B. Chancroid.
- C. Granuloma inguinale.
- D. Lymphogranuloma venereum.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Herpes simplex virus.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **HSV** is the most common cause of genital ulcers in the US (syphilis is second). Chancroid, granuloma inguinale, and LGV are rare in the US.

## Q4 — Genital Ulcers (p. 355)

A 25-year-old man presents for evaluation of an ulcer on his penis. Which one of the following questions should the physician routinely include in the history?

- A. Does he have sex with men?
- B. Does he have a history of urethral discharge?
- C. Does he have a history of recently starting antiretroviral therapy?
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A thorough sexual/medical history — partners and sexual practices, associated urethral symptoms, and medications (some, e.g., during immune reconstitution, alter presentation) — guides the differential and testing.

## Q5 — Genital Ulcers (p. 355)

Which one of the following laboratory tests should be routinely performed in patients with genital ulcer disease?

- A. Immunoglobulin M testing for herpes simplex virus types 1 and 2.
- B. Polymerase chain reaction testing for herpes simplex virus DNA.
- C. Polymerase chain reaction testing for Haemophilus ducreyi.
- D. Direct fluorescent antibody tests for HIV.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — PCR testing for herpes simplex virus DNA.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **HSV PCR** (NAAT) is the most sensitive routine test for genital ulcers, plus syphilis testing. IgM serology is unreliable (A); H. ducreyi PCR isn't routine (C); HIV is tested serologically, not by DFA (D).

## Q6 — Office Spirometry: Indications and Interpretation (p. 362)

Which one of the following is acceptable to use any time within the 12 hours before pulmonary function testing?

- A. Up to 16 oz of caffeinated coffee.
- B. Cigarettes.
- C. Long-acting muscarinic antagonist.
- D. Long-acting beta agonist.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Up to 16 oz of caffeinated coffee.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Bronchodilators must be **held** before testing — LABAs ~12 h and LAMAs ~24 h (C, D not acceptable within 12 h) — and **smoking** is avoided around testing (B). By the article's hold list, moderate caffeine is the only acceptable option here. *(Teaching nuance: many labs also restrict caffeine same-day; answer confirmed by official key.)*

## Q7 — Office Spirometry (p. 362)

Which one of the following spirometry results indicates that full pulmonary function testing should be performed?

- A. Normal forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV₁)/forced vital capacity (FVC) ratio and decreased FVC.
- B. Decreased FEV₁/FVC ratio and decreased FVC with full improvement after administration of a bronchodilator.
- C. FEV₁/FEV₆ ratio of less than 0.73.
- D. FEV₁/FVC ratio less than the lower limit of normal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Normal FEV₁/FVC ratio with a decreased FVC.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This pattern suggests a possible **restrictive** defect, which spirometry alone can't confirm → full PFTs with lung volumes/DLCO. A low FEV₁/FVC ratio (B, C, D) indicates **obstruction**, diagnosable by spirometry.

## Q8 — Office Spirometry (p. 362)

Which one of the following statements about spirometry is correct?

- A. Pulmonary function testing performed in a laboratory is more accurate than high-quality pulmonary function testing performed in a family physician's office.
- B. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening asymptomatic adults for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with spirometry.
- C. The combination of a decreased FEV₁/FVC ratio and decreased FVC is classified as a mixed pattern.
- D. A 5% increase in FEV₁ after albuterol inhalation confirms full reversibility of an obstructive defect.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A decreased FEV₁/FVC ratio with a decreased FVC is a mixed pattern.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> High-quality office spirometry is comparable to lab testing (A wrong); the USPSTF recommends **against** screening asymptomatic adults for COPD (B wrong); reversibility requires a **≥12% and ≥200 mL** increase in FEV₁, not 5% (D wrong).

## Q9 — Lown Right Care: Fracture Prevention in Older Adults (p. 370)

Which one of the following statements about fracture prevention in older adults is correct?

- A. Most hip fractures are unrelated to falls.
- B. Antiresorptive therapies reduce nonvertebral fracture risk during the first year of therapy.
- C. Calcium carbonate should not be taken within two hours of bisphosphonates.
- D. Bisphosphonate therapy should be continued for life.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Calcium carbonate should not be taken within two hours of a bisphosphonate.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Calcium impairs bisphosphonate absorption — separate the doses. Most hip fractures **result from falls** (A wrong); nonvertebral fracture reduction takes **longer than 1 year** to appear (B wrong); bisphosphonates need a **drug holiday** after ~3–5 years, not lifelong use (D wrong).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Medication Use to Reduce Risk of Breast Cancer (p. 373)

According to the USPSTF recommendation, who should be offered risk-reducing medications?

- A. A 40-year-old woman with a first-degree relative with breast cancer.
- B. A 50-year-old woman with a first-degree relative who developed breast cancer at 55 years of age.
- C. A 40-year-old woman with a 5% risk of developing breast cancer in the next five years.
- D. A 50-year-old woman with a second-degree relative with bilateral breast cancer.
- E. A 60-year-old woman with a 1% risk of developing breast cancer in the next five years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The 40-year-old woman with a 5% five-year breast cancer risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends offering risk-reducing medication (tamoxifen, raloxifene, aromatase inhibitors) to women at **increased risk** and low risk of harm — generally a **5-year risk ≥3%**. Family history alone without a calculated elevated risk doesn't meet the threshold (A, B, D, E).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Group B Streptococcus Disease — AAP (p. 378)

A well-appearing infant is born at 38 weeks to a mother who tested positive for GBS at 36 weeks. Intrapartum antibiotics were not given and there was no maternal fever. According to the AAP, which one of the following is the best initial strategy?

- A. Administer empiric antibiotics with ampicillin and ceftazidime (Fortaz).
- B. Administer empiric antibiotics with ampicillin and ceftriaxone (Rocephin).
- C. Obtain blood cultures.
- D. Perform clinical monitoring for 36 to 48 hours.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Clinical monitoring for 36 to 48 hours.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A **well-appearing** term infant whose only risk factor is inadequate GBS prophylaxis is observed (≥36–48 h), not reflexively cultured or treated. Empiric antibiotics/cultures are reserved for ill-appearing infants or chorioamnionitis (A, B, C).
