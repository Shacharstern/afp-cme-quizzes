## Q1 — Interventional Radiology: Indications and Best Practices (p. 547)

Which one of the following statements about central venous catheters is correct?

- A. Placing ports surgically is not as expensive as using interventional radiology.
- B. Nontunneled catheters have a lower infection rate per catheter-day than tunneled catheters.
- C. Infections occur in about one-fifth of patients with tunneled catheters.
- D. Subcutaneous ports cannot be placed using interventional radiology.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Infections occur in roughly one-fifth of patients with tunneled catheters.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Nontunneled** catheters have **higher** infection rates per catheter-day (B reversed); IR placement is generally **less** costly than surgical (A wrong); ports **can** be placed by IR (D wrong).

## Q2 — Interventional Radiology (p. 547)

A 29-year-old patient with long-standing pelvic pain is diagnosed with a 5-cm uterine fibroid. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. About one-third of patients receiving uterine artery embolization for fibroids require surgical treatment within two years.
- B. More than one-half of all women will have symptomatic fibroids in their lifetime.
- C. Patients have higher satisfaction with uterine artery embolization than with myomectomy.
- D. Uterine artery embolization should not be used in women who wish to retain fertility.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — About one-third need surgery within two years after uterine artery embolization.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> UAE is effective but has notable reintervention rates. Fibroids are common but most are **asymptomatic** (B overstated); satisfaction is comparable, not clearly higher, vs. myomectomy (C); UAE is **relatively** cautioned (not absolutely contraindicated) with future fertility (D too absolute).

## Q3 — Clinically Relevant Drug-Drug Interactions in Primary Care (p. 558)

Which one of the following statements about the use of antibiotics in patients receiving warfarin (Coumadin) is correct?

- A. The empiric warfarin dosage should be reduced 25% to 50% upon initiation of rifampin.
- B. The precise effect on international normalized ratio (INR) from any antibiotic is predictable.
- C. The patient's INR should be checked soon after initiating antibiotics and soon after finishing them.
- D. Metronidazole (Flagyl) has minimal influence on INR.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Check the INR shortly after starting and after stopping the antibiotic.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Rifampin induces** metabolism (would require an **increase**, not reduction, A reversed); antibiotic effects on INR are **unpredictable** (B wrong); **metronidazole strongly raises** INR (D wrong) — so close monitoring is key.

## Q4 — Clinically Relevant Drug-Drug Interactions (p. 558)

Which one of the following patients warrants an empiric dosage adjustment?

- A. A patient receiving apixaban (Eliquis) being started on ketoconazole.
- B. A patient receiving warfarin being started on penicillin G benzathine.
- C. A patient receiving warfarin being started on atorvastatin (Lipitor).
- D. A patient receiving pravastatin (Pravachol) being started on an azole antifungal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Apixaban + ketoconazole (a strong CYP3A4/P-glycoprotein inhibitor) needs dose adjustment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Ketoconazole markedly raises apixaban levels → bleeding risk (reduce dose or avoid). Penicillin minimally affects warfarin (B); **pravastatin isn't CYP3A4-metabolized**, so azoles don't significantly raise it (D); atorvastatin/warfarin needs monitoring, not empiric adjustment (C).

## Q5 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2018 for Primary Care Physicians (p. 565)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of musculoskeletal pain is correct?

- A. Opioid and acetaminophen combination analgesics relieve acute extremity pain better than ibuprofen and acetaminophen combinations.
- B. Anticonvulsants are effective for the treatment of low back pain with radiculopathy.
- C. Nonopioid medications are at least as effective as opioid medications for improving pain-related function in persons with chronic back, knee, or hip pain.
- D. Anticonvulsants do not increase the risk of adverse events.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Nonopioids are at least as effective as opioids for chronic back/knee/hip pain-related function.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The SPACE trial found no advantage for opioids. Ibuprofen/acetaminophen matches opioid combos for acute pain (A wrong); **anticonvulsants don't help** radicular back pain and **do increase** adverse events (B, D wrong).

## Q6 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2018 (p. 565)

Which one of the following statements about behavioral medicine is correct?

- A. Two-thirds of patients with anxiety disorders will relapse after discontinuation of antidepressant therapy.
- B. Persons who participate in more than 150 minutes of at least moderate-intensity physical activity per week have a lower risk of depression.
- C. There is no relationship between increased physical activity and a lower risk of depression.
- D. Nonbenzodiazepine hypnotics decrease the risk of falls or fractures.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — ≥150 minutes/week of moderate activity is associated with lower depression risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Physical activity protects against depression (so C is wrong). Anxiety relapse after stopping antidepressants is **lower** than two-thirds (A overstated); nonbenzodiazepine hypnotics (e.g., zolpidem) **increase** fall/fracture risk (D reversed).

## Q7 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2018 (p. 565)

Which one of the following statements about screening and prevention is correct?

- A. The fecal immunochemical test is less sensitive and specific than guaiac-based fecal occult blood tests for colorectal cancer screening.
- B. The initiation of statin therapy in patients 75 years or older without preexisting cardiovascular disease or diabetes mellitus clearly reduces the likelihood of developing cardiovascular disease.
- C. The benefits of aspirin for primary prevention in patients with moderate risk of cardiovascular disease clearly outweigh the harms.
- D. Exercise, with or without vision assessment/treatment and environmental assessment/modification, reduces the risk of injurious falls in older adults.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Exercise (± vision and home-hazard interventions) reduces injurious falls in older adults.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> FIT is **more** accurate than guaiac FOBT (A reversed); statin benefit >75 in primary prevention is **uncertain** (B); aspirin primary prevention benefits **don't clearly outweigh** bleeding harms (C wrong).

## Q8 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Saline Irrigation for Allergic Rhinitis (p. 544)

Which one of the following statements about using nasal saline irrigation to treat the symptoms of allergic rhinitis is correct?

- A. When used alone, it reduces the severity of allergy symptoms vs. no treatment.
- B. When used alone, it does not yield better outcomes than intranasal corticosteroids.
- C. When added to pharmacologic treatments, it does not further improve symptoms.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Saline irrigation helps versus no treatment, is **not superior to intranasal corticosteroids**, and adds limited benefit when combined with pharmacotherapy — overall a low-risk adjunct.

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Aromatase Inhibitors (Letrozole) vs. Clomiphene for Subfertile Women with PCOS (p. 545)

When comparing letrozole with clomiphene for the treatment of subfertility in women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Letrozole results in a higher incidence of live birth.
- B. Letrozole results in a higher incidence of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome.
- C. Letrozole results in a higher risk of miscarriage.
- D. Letrozole results in a higher risk of a multiple pregnancy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Letrozole achieves a higher live-birth rate.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Letrozole is now **first-line ovulation induction in PCOS** — higher live-birth and ovulation rates than clomiphene, **without** more OHSS, miscarriage, or multiple pregnancy (B, C, D wrong).

## Q10 — AHRQ Review: Psychological and Pharmacologic Treatments for Adults with PTSD (p. 577)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is correct?

- A. Pharmacotherapy is superior to psychotherapy.
- B. Male veterans have lower rates of PTSD than the general population.
- C. Fluoxetine, paroxetine, and venlafaxine reduce PTSD symptoms.
- D. Individual trauma-focused psychotherapy is not recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Fluoxetine, paroxetine, and venlafaxine reduce PTSD symptoms.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SSRIs/SNRIs have good evidence. **Trauma-focused psychotherapy is first-line** and at least as effective as medication (A, D wrong); veterans have **higher** PTSD rates (B wrong).

## Q11 — Point-of-Care Guides: Applying the INTERCHEST Clinical Decision Rule for CAD (p. 584)

Which one of the following is a positive clinical predictor of a cardiovascular cause of chest pain based on the INTERCHEST chest pain rule for coronary artery disease (CAD)?

- A. Pain reproduced by palpating the chest wall.
- B. Age older than 50 years.
- C. Chest pain occurs at rest.
- D. Chest discomfort feels like pressure.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Chest discomfort that feels like pressure.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> INTERCHEST positive predictors include **pressure-type discomfort, pain worse with exertion, prior CAD, and a clinician feeling the pain is cardiac**. Chest-wall reproducibility **lowers** the score (A); pain *at rest* and age alone aren't positive items as listed (B, C).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Cholesterol Management — ACC/AHA Updated Guideline (p. 589)

According to the ACC/AHA guideline, which of the following statements about statin therapy is correct?

- A. Patients with diabetes who have multiple atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk factors should be treated with high-intensity statin therapy with a goal of reducing low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels by 50% or more.
- B. Patients 40 to 75 years without diabetes who are at intermediate risk with LDL-C levels of 70 to 189 mg per dL should be treated with a high-intensity statin to lower LDL-C levels by 50% or more.
- C. A nonfasting lipid measurement should be repeated three to four weeks after starting a statin.
- D. LDL-C levels should initially be reduced with moderate-intensity statin therapy in patients up to 75 years with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and a goal of lowering LDL-C levels by 30%.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Diabetes plus multiple ASCVD risk factors → high-intensity statin (≥50% LDL-C reduction).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Intermediate-risk patients get a **moderate**-intensity statin (~30–49% reduction), not high (B wrong); a follow-up lipid panel is checked at **4–12 weeks** (C); established ASCVD warrants **high**-intensity statin (≥50% reduction), not moderate/30% (D wrong).
