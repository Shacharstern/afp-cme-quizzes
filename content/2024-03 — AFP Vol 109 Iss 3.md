## Q1 — Newborn Skin Part I: Common Rashes and Skin Changes (p. 212)
A well-appearing, full-term newborn has scattered small pustular lesions on the face, trunk, and extremities on day 3 of life. Lesions have a red base, are not grouped, and spare the palms and soles. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Congenital syphilis.
- B. Erythema toxicum neonatorum.
- C. Herpes simplex virus infection.
- D. Transient neonatal pustular melanosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Erythema toxicum neonatorum.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> ETN is benign, appears in the first few days in a well infant as erythematous macules/papules/pustules ("flea-bitten") sparing palms/soles, with eosinophils on smear. HSV is grouped vesicles in an ill infant; congenital syphilis often involves palms/soles; transient neonatal pustular melanosis is present *at birth* and leaves hyperpigmented macules.

## Q2 — Newborn Skin Part II: Birthmarks (p. 217)
A newborn has a nevus flammeus (port-wine stain). Which one of the following locations would require further evaluation and possibly treatment?

- A. Forehead.
- B. Hand.
- C. Neck.
- D. Chest.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Forehead.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> A port-wine stain in the V1 (forehead/upper-eyelid) trigeminal distribution raises concern for Sturge-Weber syndrome (leptomeningeal angiomatosis, seizures, glaucoma), warranting ophthalmology and neuroimaging evaluation. Truncal/extremity lesions carry no such association.

## Q3 — Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy: Prevention and Treatment (p. 226)
Which one of the following statements about diabetic peripheral neuropathy is correct?

- A. Glycemic control does not influence the risk of diabetic peripheral neuropathy in patients with type 1 diabetes.
- B. Patients taking metformin should be checked for vitamin B₁₂ deficiency.
- C. Monofilament testing is more than 95% sensitive for diabetic peripheral neuropathy.
- D. After diabetic peripheral neuropathy begins, tobacco cessation has no effect on the clinical course.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Patients taking metformin should be checked for vitamin B₁₂ deficiency.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Long-term metformin lowers B₁₂ and can mimic/worsen neuropathy. Tight glycemic control strongly reduces neuropathy risk in type 1 diabetes (A wrong); the 10-g monofilament is specific but only moderately sensitive (C wrong); smoking cessation does improve outcomes (D wrong).

## Q4 — Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy (p. 226)
A 61-year-old with long-standing, controlled type 2 diabetes is diagnosed with diabetic peripheral neuropathy. Which one of the following statements about treatment is correct?

- A. Gabapentin, 300 mg daily, is likely to reduce her pain by 50%.
- B. Valproic acid is likely to be as effective as gabapentin for pain.
- C. The effectiveness of duloxetine (Cymbalta) for diabetic peripheral neuropathy plateaus at 40 to 60 mg daily.
- D. Exercise has been shown to worsen diabetic peripheral neuropathy over time.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Duloxetine's benefit plateaus around 40–60 mg/day.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Higher duloxetine doses add little analgesia but more adverse effects. Gabapentin at only 300 mg/day is sub-therapeutic for a 50% response (A wrong); valproic acid has weak/inconsistent evidence (B wrong); and exercise *helps* (D wrong). **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q5 — Chronic Low Back Pain in Adults (p. 233)
A 47-year-old man has had low back pain for 13 weeks that improves when lying down. No red flags and a normal neuromuscular exam. Appropriate first step?

- A. Obtain plain film radiography of the lumbar spine.
- B. Begin a trial of ibuprofen.
- C. Recommend intermittent periods of bed rest.
- D. Recommend a home exercise program and physical therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Home exercise program and physical therapy.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> For chronic low back pain without red flags, nonpharmacologic active therapy (exercise, PT) is first-line. Imaging isn't indicated without red flags; bed rest is harmful and discouraged; NSAIDs are an adjunct, not the preferred first step.

## Q6 — Chronic Low Back Pain (p. 233)
A 68-year-old postmenopausal, sedentary woman on osteoporosis treatment has several weeks of low back pain; imaging shows no significant abnormality. Which one of the following nonpharmacologic treatments would be contraindicated?

- A. Acupuncture.
- B. High-velocity low-amplitude manipulative techniques.
- C. Pilates.
- D. Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — High-velocity, low-amplitude spinal manipulation.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Forceful HVLA manipulation risks vertebral compression fracture in osteoporotic bone and is contraindicated. Acupuncture, Pilates/gentle exercise, and TENS are safe options.

## Q7 — Pancreatic Cancer: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 245)
Which one of the following is the recommended first-line imaging test for patients with symptoms highly suggestive of pancreatic cancer without evidence of biliary obstruction?

- A. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography.
- B. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).
- C. Pancreas-protocol, contrast-enhanced abdominal computed tomography (CT).
- D. Abdominal plain film radiography.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pancreas-protocol, contrast-enhanced abdominal CT.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Dedicated pancreatic-protocol CT is first-line for detection and staging/resectability assessment. ERCP is therapeutic (biliary obstruction); MRI/MRCP is a problem-solving adjunct; plain films have no role.

## Q8 — Pancreatic Cancer (p. 245)
Which one of the following treatments is potentially useful in all stages of pancreatic cancer?

- A. Systemic chemotherapy.
- B. Surgical resection.
- C. Radiation therapy.
- D. Antioxidants, such as vitamin E.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Systemic chemotherapy.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Chemotherapy has a role across resectable (neoadjuvant/adjuvant), locally advanced, and metastatic disease. Surgery is only for the minority with resectable tumors; radiation is selective; antioxidants are not a treatment.

## Q9 — Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy (p. 251)
A 33-year-old at 32 weeks has a new BP of 153/90 mm Hg with no prior elevations, no headache/vision changes/RUQ pain, and normal labs. Most appropriate medication?

- A. Hydrochlorothiazide.
- B. Labetalol.
- C. Nifedipine.
- D. No medication.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — No medication.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> This is non-severe gestational hypertension (BP < 160/110, no severe features). Antihypertensive therapy in pregnancy is reserved for severe-range pressures (≥160/110); non-severe gestational hypertension is managed expectantly with close monitoring. **⚠️ Verify** (the CHAP-trial trend toward treating ≥140/90 mainly addresses *chronic* hypertension).

## Q10 — Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy (p. 251)
A 36-year-old with preeclampsia without severe features presents at 35 weeks with RUQ pain, malaise, BP 149/104, Hgb 8.6, platelets 92k, ALT 110, AST 96, LDH 650. Most appropriate treatment?

- A. Intravenous betamethasone.
- B. Intravenous labetalol.
- C. Intravenous magnesium sulfate.
- D. Oral nifedipine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Intravenous magnesium sulfate.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Hemolysis + elevated liver enzymes + low platelets defines HELLP syndrome (a severe-feature preeclampsia variant). Magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis plus delivery is the cornerstone. Betamethasone is for fetal lung maturity < 34 weeks (she's 35); her BP isn't in the severe systolic range requiring acute labetalol/nifedipine.

## Q11 — Cochrane: Thiazide Diuretics vs. Other Antihypertensive Drug Classes (p. 209)
A patient with a strong family history of stroke and congestive heart failure is diagnosed with hypertension. Which one of the following statements about thiazide and thiazide-like diuretics is correct?

- A. They decrease the risk of cardiovascular events compared with calcium channel blockers.
- B. They increase the risk of stroke compared with ACE inhibitors.
- C. They are associated with more withdrawals/discontinuations due to adverse effects compared with beta blockers, calcium channel blockers, ACE inhibitors, and alpha blockers.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Thiazides decrease cardiovascular events compared with calcium channel blockers.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The Cochrane evidence favors first-line thiazides, including a reduction in heart failure versus CCBs (relevant to this patient). They do not increase stroke versus ACE inhibitors (B wrong) and are generally well tolerated with *fewer*, not more, withdrawals (C wrong), so D fails. **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q12 — Cochrane: Direct Oral Anticoagulants vs. Conventional Anticoagulants for VTE (p. 210)

- B. Conventional anticoagulation decreases the risk of bleeding compared with DOACs.

## Q13 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: Screening/Preventive Interventions for Oral Health (p. 261)
A 27-year-old man presents with his 6-year-old daughter; both lost dental insurance and haven't had dental exams in 12 months, with a recent shift to fast/snack foods. Both are asymptomatic. - B. Screen for dental caries only in the 27-year-old.
- C. Screen for dental caries and periodontal disease in both patients.
- D. Screen for dental caries and periodontal disease in the 27-year-old; screen for dental caries only in the six-year-old.
- E. Use clinical judgment to decide whether to screen either the parent or child for oral health conditions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **E — Use clinical judgment.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The USPSTF concludes current evidence is *insufficient* (I statement) to assess the balance of benefits and harms of routine oral-health screening by primary care clinicians in asymptomatic children ≥5 years and in adults — so management defaults to clinical judgment (and referral to a dental home). **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q14 — Lown Right Care: Avoiding Delays in Diagnosing Endometriosis (p. 268)
Which one of the following statements about endometriosis is correct?

- A. Fatigue is the most commonly reported symptom.
- B. A menstrual cycle of less than 24 days is a risk factor for endometriosis.
- C. Endometriosis has little effect on home or work productivity and absenteeism.
- D. Endometriosis is associated with a lower risk of ovarian cancer.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A menstrual cycle shorter than 24 days is a risk factor.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Shorter cycles (and early menarche) mean more frequent retrograde menstruation, raising endometriosis risk. The most common symptom is cyclic pelvic pain/dysmenorrhea, not fatigue (A wrong); the disease markedly reduces productivity (C wrong); and it is associated with a *higher* risk of certain ovarian cancers (endometrioid/clear cell) (D wrong). **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q15 — Point-of-Care Guides: ASCCP Management Guidelines for Abnormal Cervical Cancer Screening (p. 275)
Per the ASCCP guidelines, which one of the following statements about management of abnormal cervical cancer screening is correct?

- A. Colposcopy is recommended following any abnormal cervical cancer screening.
- B. Repeat testing with HPV and cervical cytology is recommended following any abnormal cervical cytology.
- C. Recommendations for colposcopy and treatment are based on a patient's immediate risk of CIN3+.
- D. Long-term surveillance is based on a patient's immediate risk of CIN3+.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Colposcopy/treatment decisions are based on the immediate risk of CIN3+.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The 2019 ASCCP guidelines use risk-based management: immediate CIN3+ risk drives whether to do colposcopy or treat now, while *future* (5-year) CIN3+ risk drives the surveillance interval (so D is mismatched). Not every abnormality goes straight to colposcopy or repeat co-testing (A, B too absolute).

## Q16 — Practice Guidelines: Management of Acute Lower GI Bleeding — ACG (p. 284)
A 61-year-old healthy patient with persistent hematochezia is admitted; BP 125/85, HR 90 (hemodynamically stable). Per the ACG, which one of the following evaluation plans is most appropriate?

- A. Immediate computed tomography (CT) angiography.
- B. CT angiography, with colonoscopy in 24 hours.
- C. Colonoscopy in 14 days.
- D. Colonoscopy or CT is not needed if the patient has had a colonoscopy in the past five years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Non-emergent colonoscopy.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Colonoscopy is the recommended initial diagnostic procedure for hemodynamically stable lower GI bleeding (after adequate bowel prep), performed non-urgently. CT angiography is reserved for hemodynamically unstable patients with ongoing bleeding (A, B). A prior colonoscopy doesn't obviate evaluation of new bleeding (D). **⚠️ Verify** the stated timing/interval.
