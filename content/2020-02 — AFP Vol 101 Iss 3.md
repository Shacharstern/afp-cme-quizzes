## Q1 — Screening and Counseling Adolescents and Young Adults (p. 147)

Which one of the following accurately reflects the mnemonic to facilitate collection of a psychosocial history?

- A. Sexuality, school, homework, activities, depression, emotional support.
- B. Strengths, school, home, activities, drugs, emotions/eating, sexuality, safety.
- C. Strengths, sexuality, healthy behavior, active lifestyle, depression, eating disorders.
- D. Socialization, sexuality, homework, alcohol, drugs, e-cigarettes, safety.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Strengths, School, Home, Activities, Drugs, Emotions/eating, Sexuality, Safety (SSHADESS).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The **SSHADESS** interview is the strengths-based adolescent psychosocial framework (an update of the HEEADSSS mnemonic). The other strings are distractors.

## Q2 — Screening and Counseling Adolescents and Young Adults (p. 147)

Social media use in adolescents may have a negative health effect on which one of the following?

- A. Driving safety.
- B. Social support.
- C. Family communication.
- D. Access to information.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Driving safety.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Texting/social-media use while driving raises crash risk. Social media can otherwise **enhance** social support, communication, and information access (B, C, D are potential benefits).

## Q3 — Chronic Wounds: Evaluation and Management (p. 159)

Which one of the following statements about debridement of chronic wounds is correct?

- A. Surgical debridement methods should leave rolled edges of the wound intact.
- B. Aggressive debridement doubles the likelihood of healing.
- C. Debridement should be avoided in patients with diabetic ulcers.
- D. Debridement should be performed no more than once every two weeks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Aggressive (frequent) debridement roughly doubles the likelihood of healing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Removing nonviable tissue and **rolled (epibole) edges** promotes healing (A wrong); debridement is **central** to diabetic ulcer care (C wrong) and is done **as often as needed**, not biweekly (D wrong).

## Q4 — Chronic Wounds (p. 159)

A patient with diabetes was unaware of a shallow ulcer on the plantar aspect of his metatarsal heads. Which one of the following treatments is specific to this kind of ulcer?

- A. Debridement.
- B. Infection control.
- C. Moisture balance.
- D. Total contact cast.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Total contact cast (offloading).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Plantar (neuropathic) diabetic foot ulcers heal only when pressure is **offloaded** — the total contact cast is the gold standard. Debridement, infection control, and moisture balance apply to chronic wounds generally.

## Q5 — Chronic Wounds (p. 159)

Which one of the following statements about chronic wounds is correct?

- A. Wounds with visible biofilm should not be debrided.
- B. The toe-brachial index is less reliable than the ankle-brachial index in detecting arterial compression in patients with diabetes.
- C. A donut-style cushion should be used to offload pressure.
- D. Wounds should be kept moist to promote faster healing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Keeping wounds moist promotes faster healing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Moist wound healing is a core principle. Biofilm **should** be debrided (A wrong); in diabetes (calcified vessels) the **toe-brachial index is more reliable** than ABI (B reversed); **donut cushions are harmful** and concentrate pressure (C wrong).

## Q6 — Clostridioides difficile Infection: Update on Management (p. 168)

Which one of the following statements about diagnostic testing in a hospitalized patient with suspected Clostridioides difficile infection is correct?

- A. A cell cytotoxicity neutralization assay should be performed.
- B. A negative enzyme immunoassay for glutamate dehydrogenase essentially rules out infection.
- C. Enzyme immunoassay alone is best in a low-prevalence setting.
- D. Nucleic acid amplification testing (NAAT) is the best test to distinguish C. difficile colonization from active infection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A negative glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH) EIA essentially rules out infection.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> GDH is highly sensitive → a negative result effectively excludes C. difficile (used in multistep algorithms with toxin EIA). NAAT detects the organism but **cannot distinguish colonization from active infection** (D wrong); cell cytotoxicity assays are impractical (A).

## Q7 — Clostridioides difficile Infection (p. 168)

A patient being treated for cellulitis develops C. difficile infection confirmed by NAAT. This is his first recurrence. Which one of the following treatment approaches is most appropriate?

- A. He should be treated with oral vancomycin.
- B. He should be treated with metronidazole (Flagyl).
- C. He should undergo fecal microbiota transplantation.
- D. A test of cure is recommended after treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Oral vancomycin (often a tapered/pulsed course) or fidaxomicin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For a first recurrence, use **oral vancomycin** (or fidaxomicin); **metronidazole is no longer preferred** (B). FMT is reserved for **multiple** recurrences (C); **no test of cure** — toxin assays stay positive after clinical resolution (D wrong).

## Q8 — Clostridioides difficile Infection (p. 168)

Which one of the following strategies for prevention of C. difficile infection is recommended?

- A. Contact precautions should continue for up to 12 hours after resolution of the patient's diarrhea.
- B. Probiotics are a first-line preventive measure.
- C. During outbreaks of C. difficile infection or when direct contact with feces is likely, the use of soap and water is preferred over alcohol-based hand hygiene products.
- D. Bed bathing is as effective as showering for reducing positive C. difficile skin cultures.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — During outbreaks/likely fecal contact, soap and water is preferred over alcohol-based hand rubs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Spores resist alcohol** — mechanical washing is needed. Contact precautions continue **≥48 hours** after diarrhea resolves (A wrong); probiotics aren't first-line prevention (B); **showering** beats bed bathing for removing spores (D wrong).

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: OnabotulinumtoxinA for the Prevention of Chronic Migraine in Adults (p. 144)

Which one of the following statements about the use of onabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) for chronic migraine in adults is correct?

- A. It decreases the number of migraine days per month.
- B. It is associated with an increase in nonserious adverse effects vs. placebo.
- C. It is recommended as an option for prophylaxis by the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence and the Institute for Clinical Systems Improvement.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> OnabotulinumtoxinA modestly reduces migraine days in **chronic** migraine, causes more minor adverse effects (neck pain, ptosis) than placebo, and is endorsed by NICE/ICSI for prophylaxis.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Accuracy of Dermoscopy vs. Visual Inspection for Diagnosing Melanoma in Adults (p. 145)

Which one of the following statements about the use of dermoscopy to aid in the detection of melanoma is correct?

- A. Image-based assessment is more accurate than in-person assessment.
- B. Dermoscopic training improves diagnostic accuracy.
- C. Family physicians are more accurate than dermatologists at diagnosing melanoma.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Dermoscopic training improves diagnostic accuracy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Dermoscopy outperforms naked-eye inspection **when the clinician is trained**. **In-person** assessment is more accurate than image-based (A reversed), and dermatologists outperform generalists (C wrong).

## Q11 — AHRQ Review: Adverse Effects of Pharmacologic Treatments of Major Depression in Older Adults (p. 179)

Which one of the following statements about adverse events associated with treatment of major depression in adults 65 years and older is correct?

- A. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) have higher rates of adverse events during the acute phase of treatment compared with placebo.
- B. SSRIs and tricyclic antidepressants have similar rates of discontinuation due to adverse events.
- C. Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors have higher rates of adverse events and discontinuation of therapy during the acute phase of treatment compared with placebo.
- D. Duloxetine is associated with decreased fall rates.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — SNRIs have higher adverse-event and discontinuation rates vs. placebo in the acute phase.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SNRIs were notably less tolerated than placebo. The evidence for SSRIs vs. placebo on acute adverse events was **insufficient/not clearly higher** (A); TCAs cause **more** discontinuation than SSRIs (B wrong); duloxetine **increases**, not decreases, fall risk (D reversed).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Breast Cancer Screening — ACP Guidance Statements (p. 184)

According to the ACP, which one of the following statements about screening for breast cancer in average-risk women at age 40 is correct?

- A. There is a significant increase in false-positive results.
- B. Screening should be performed annually until age 50.
- C. There is no effect on breast cancer mortality.
- D. The benefits outweigh harms for most women.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — There is a significant increase in false-positive results.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For average-risk women 40–49, ACP advises **individualized** decisions because the small mortality benefit is offset by **more false positives**, biopsies, and overdiagnosis — so benefits don't clearly outweigh harms (D wrong), screening isn't routinely annual (B), and there is **some** mortality effect (C wrong).
