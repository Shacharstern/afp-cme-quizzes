## Q1 — Health Maintenance for Women of Reproductive Age (p. 209)

Which one of the following screening recommendations for women of reproductive age is correct?

- A. Pelvic examinations should be performed to screen for ovarian cancer.
- B. Breast cancer screening should be done biennially with no upper age limit.
- C. Because they are at higher risk than men, women should be screened more often for colorectal cancer.
- D. Hepatitis C virus screening should be performed routinely in people 18 to 79 years of age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Routine HCV screening for people 18 to 79 years of age.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF expanded HCV screening to all adults 18–79 once. Pelvic exams don't screen for ovarian cancer (A wrong); mammography has an upper-age caveat/insufficient evidence ≥75 (B wrong); CRC risk is not higher in women warranting extra screening (C wrong).

## Q2 — Health Maintenance for Women of Reproductive Age (p. 209)

Which one of the following preventive health measures is correct?

- A. Patient self-collected HPV samples appear less accurate than physician-collected samples.
- B. Folic acid should be administered to a transgender man who intends to become pregnant.
- C. The CDC routinely recommends catch-up HPV vaccination for adults 27 to 45.
- D. Women should be advised the safe upper limit is 14 alcoholic drinks per week.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Give folic acid to a transgender man who intends to become pregnant.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Anyone who can become pregnant should take preconception folic acid for neural-tube-defect prevention. Self-collected HPV is comparably accurate (A wrong); HPV vaccination 27–45 is shared decision-making, not routine (C wrong); the limit for women is ≤7 drinks/week (D wrong).

## Q3 — Health Maintenance for Women of Reproductive Age (p. 209)

According to USPSTF recommendations, which one of the following statements about health screening is correct?

- A. Screening for intimate partner violence includes significant harms.
- B. Women 24 and younger should be tested routinely for herpes simplex virus.
- C. All persons 15 to 65 years of age should be screened routinely for HIV infection.
- D. There is insufficient evidence to recommend screening for unhealthy alcohol and drug use.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Screen all persons 15 to 65 for HIV.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF gives HIV screening (ages 15–65) an A. IPV screening is recommended with minimal harms (A wrong); routine serologic HSV screening is recommended *against* (B wrong); screening for unhealthy alcohol use is recommended (B-grade, D wrong).

## Q4 — Frailty: Evaluation and Management (p. 219)

Which one of the following presentations is most closely associated with late-stage frailty?

- A. Generalized weakness.
- B. Exhaustion.
- C. Inappropriate medication use.
- D. Weight loss.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Weight loss.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In the frailty progression, weakness/slowness/exhaustion appear earlier; unintentional weight loss (sarcopenia, anorexia of aging) marks more advanced, late-stage frailty.

## Q5 — Frailty: Evaluation and Management (p. 219)

Which one of the following statements about the evaluation of an older adult for frailty is correct?

- A. Screening for frailty is best accomplished through a comprehensive geriatric assessment.
- B. Laboratory tests and imaging studies are commonly diagnostic.
- C. Low energy expenditure helps exclude a diagnosis of frailty.
- D. The most practical and reliable assessment tool is the Fried frailty phenotype.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Frailty evaluation is best accomplished through comprehensive geriatric assessment (CGA).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The CGA is the most thorough approach to characterizing frailty and guiding management. Frailty is a clinical syndrome, not a lab/imaging diagnosis (B wrong); low energy expenditure is a *criterion for*, not against, frailty (C wrong). (Option D — the Fried phenotype as "most practical/reliable" — is a defensible alternative; the official key selects A.)

## Q6 — Frailty: Evaluation and Management (p. 219)

Which one of the following statements about implementation of treatment for frailty in older adults is correct?

- A. Prefrail older adults have a better chance than frail cohorts to improve their frailty diagnosis.
- B. On average, the harms of physical activity exceed the benefits in the prefrail population.
- C. A combination of medications targeting frailty symptoms improves function.
- D. Nutritional education alone has been shown to prevent the progression of frailty.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Prefrail patients are more likely to improve than frail patients.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Frailty exists on a continuum; intervening earlier (prefrail) gives a better chance of reversal. Exercise benefits outweigh harms (B wrong); no medication combination is proven (C wrong); nutrition works best combined with exercise, not alone (D wrong).

## Q7 — Bipolar Disorders: Evaluation and Treatment (p. 227)

Which one of the following statements about the presentation of bipolar disorders is correct?

- A. Patients often present during a hypomanic episode.
- B. The presentation of depression in bipolar disorders is often indistinguishable from unipolar depression.
- C. Children of parents with bipolar disorder are no more likely than others to develop it.
- D. Bipolar disorders typically do not present with comorbid psychiatric conditions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Bipolar depression is often indistinguishable from unipolar depression.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Patients usually present in a depressive episode that looks like unipolar depression — hence frequent misdiagnosis; screen for past (hypo)mania before prescribing antidepressants. Patients rarely present during hypomania (A wrong); bipolar is highly heritable (C wrong) and very comorbid (D wrong).

## Q8 — Bipolar Disorders (p. 227)

A 34-year-old recently diagnosed with bipolar I disorder after a manic episode is now taking a mood stabilizer. Which one of the following is the most appropriate recommendation?

- A. Discontinue oral contraceptives.
- B. Discontinue the mood stabilizer after six months.
- C. Be aware of early signs of relapse, such as sleep disturbance, increased goal orientation, or increased agitation.
- D. Avoid psychotherapy because it has no effect on long-term outcomes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Recognize early relapse signs (sleep change, increased goal orientation, agitation).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Psychoeducation about prodromal/relapse signs is core to bipolar management. Maintenance pharmacotherapy is long-term, not stopped at 6 months (B wrong); psychotherapy improves outcomes (D wrong); OCPs aren't routinely stopped (A wrong, though valproate teratogenicity warrants counseling).

## Q9 — Bipolar Disorders (p. 227)

Which one of the following statements about patients who have bipolar disorder and a comorbidity is correct?

- A. Patients with newly diagnosed bipolar disorder should have baseline lipid and A1C measurements.
- B. Patients with bipolar disorder are less likely to have cardiovascular disease than the general population.
- C. Metformin is contraindicated in patients with bipolar disorder.
- D. Patients with bipolar disorder have similar rates of anxiety disorders as the general population.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Obtain baseline lipids and A1C.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mood stabilizers/antipsychotics cause metabolic effects, and bipolar patients have higher cardiovascular and anxiety-disorder burden — so baseline metabolic monitoring is needed (B, D wrong). Metformin is not contraindicated and may offset weight gain (C wrong).

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: ACIP Approves 2021 Adult and Child/Adolescent Immunization Schedules (p. 250)

Which one of the following statements about SARS-CoV-2 vaccination is correct?

- A. The Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna vaccines are contraindicated in people with high-risk medical conditions.
- B. In the U.S., the second vaccine dose can be from a different manufacturer.
- C. The second vaccine dose can be given 14 days after the first.
- D. The CDC implemented additional measures to monitor vaccine safety.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — The CDC implemented additional safety-monitoring measures (e.g., v-safe).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Enhanced surveillance (v-safe, VAERS) was deployed. The mRNA vaccines are *recommended* in high-risk conditions (A wrong); the series should use the same manufacturer (B wrong, per 2021 guidance); minimum intervals were 21/28 days, not 14 (C wrong).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Exercise-Associated Hyponatremia — Wilderness Medical Society (p. 252)

A 31-year-old runner presents with confusion and vomiting at a marathon finish line; rectal temperature 101.2°F (38.4°C). Which one of the following does the Wilderness Medical Society recommend as immediate treatment?

- A. Aggressive immersive cooling.
- B. Feeding with high-sodium foods.
- C. Fluid restriction until diuresis.
- D. Intravenous hypertonic saline bolus.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — IV hypertonic saline bolus.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Confusion/vomiting in an endurance athlete suggests exercise-associated hyponatremic encephalopathy; the WMS recommends a bolus of IV 3% hypertonic saline. The mildly elevated temperature is not heat stroke (A wrong); oral sodium/fluid restriction is too slow for encephalopathy (B, C wrong).
