## Q1 — Migraine Headache Prophylaxis (p. 17)

Which one of the following should be a consideration for beginning prophylactic medications?

- A. Having three migraines per month.
- B. Having migraines from consuming too much caffeine.
- C. Having eight or more migraine days each month.
- D. Having migraines that respond to acute treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Eight or more migraine days per month.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Prophylaxis is considered with **frequent/disabling** attacks (often ≥4–6, clearly by ~8 migraine days/month), poor response to acute therapy, or overuse of acute medications. Caffeine-triggered headache and treatment-responsive migraines aren't indications themselves (B, D).

## Q2 — Migraine Headache Prophylaxis (p. 17)

Which one of the following medications has the strongest evidence for use in the prevention of episodic migraine headaches?

- A. Gabapentin (Neurontin).
- B. Propranolol.
- C. Fluoxetine (Prozac).
- D. Nifedipine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Propranolol.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Beta blockers (propranolol, metoprolol), topiramate, and valproate have the strongest prophylaxis evidence. Gabapentin, SSRIs, and nifedipine are not first-line (A, C, D).

## Q3 — Migraine Headache Prophylaxis (p. 17)

Which one of the following is a potentially serious adverse effect of petasites, an extract of the butterbur plant?

- A. Hepatotoxicity.
- B. Gastrointestinal upset.
- C. Palpitations.
- D. Increased belching.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Hepatotoxicity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Butterbur (petasites) may prevent migraine but carries a risk of **liver toxicity** (and pyrrolizidine-alkaloid concerns), limiting its recommendation. Belching/GI upset are minor effects (B, D).

## Q4 — Inborn Errors of Metabolism: From Preconception to Adulthood (p. 25)

Which one of the following best represents the current recommendation for preconception carrier screening?

- A. It does not influence the incidence of inborn errors of metabolism in infants.
- B. It is an acceptable option for all patients.
- C. It is appropriate only for women of Ashkenazi Jewish descent.
- D. It has a high sensitivity for detecting all variants of inborn errors of metabolism by using new DNA analysis techniques.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Carrier screening is an acceptable option for all patients.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Expanded/pan-ethnic carrier screening is offered to **all** (not only Ashkenazi Jewish, C) and can inform reproductive choices (A wrong). No test detects **all** variants (D overstated).

## Q5 — Inborn Errors of Metabolism (p. 25)

You receive a report that a healthy-appearing infant in the newborn nursery tested positive for an inborn error of metabolism. Which one of the following is an appropriate next step?

- A. Assume that the test result is a false positive.
- B. Immediately consult with a metabolic subspecialist.
- C. Repeat the test in one month.
- D. Repeat the test immediately.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Immediately consult a metabolic subspecialist.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A positive newborn screen requires **prompt specialist involvement** and confirmatory testing — even if the infant looks well — because some IEMs decompensate rapidly. Don't dismiss it as false positive or delay (A, C).

## Q6 — Inborn Errors of Metabolism (p. 25)

A two-day-old infant with a positive newborn screen is now lethargic and feeding poorly while awaiting air transport (up to two hours). While waiting, which one of the following is the next appropriate step?

- A. Bottle feeding with formula if the infant is not breastfeeding successfully.
- B. Bottle feeding with pumped breast milk if the infant is not breastfeeding successfully.
- C. Discontinuing oral feeding and administering a high-rate glucose infusion.
- D. Maintaining hydration with standard saline infusion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Stop oral feeds and give a high-rate glucose (dextrose) infusion.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A decompensating infant with a suspected IEM needs to **stop protein/feeds and reverse catabolism** with IV dextrose (often D10 at high rates) — continued feeding (A, B) can worsen a metabolic crisis; plain saline doesn't address catabolism (D).

## Q7 — Urine Drug Tests: Ordering and Interpretation (p. 33)

Which one of the following statements about urine drug testing is correct?

- A. Eating poppy seeds can cause a positive test result.
- B. Passive inhalation of marijuana smoke is likely to produce a positive result.
- C. Patients who are rapid metabolizers may have a true-negative result.
- D. Most commercial urine drug tests can detect synthetic cathinones (bath salts).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Rapid metabolizers can produce a true-negative result.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Poppy seeds cause a **true** positive (opiates are present) rather than a false one as commonly assumed (A is a classic trap — the metabolite is genuinely there); passive marijuana inhalation rarely tests positive (B); standard panels **don't** detect synthetic cathinones (D).

## Q8 — Urine Drug Tests (p. 33)

Which one of the following statements about urine drug testing is correct?

- A. Only positive immunoassay results need to be confirmed.
- B. Immunoassays are best for detecting synthetic or semisynthetic opioids.
- C. Immunoassays provide quantitative measures of major classes of misused drugs.
- D. Gas or liquid chromatography can accurately distinguish individual substances.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Gas/liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry accurately identifies individual substances.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Confirmatory chromatography is the gold standard. Immunoassays are **qualitative** screens (C wrong) that often **miss synthetic/semisynthetic opioids** (B wrong); **unexpected results** (positive or negative) may need confirmation (A too narrow).

## Q9 — Urine Drug Tests (p. 33)

A patient who is not taking opioids as prescribed attempts to provide a positive urine specimen by adding the drug directly to his urine. Which one of the following findings confirms this?

- A. Low concentration of the drug in the urine.
- B. Low urine creatinine level and specific gravity.
- C. Drug metabolites are absent in the urine.
- D. Presence of 6-monoacetylmorphine in the urine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Absence of expected drug metabolites (parent drug present without metabolites).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Spiking ("pill in the cup") shows the **parent drug but no metabolites** that would form with actual ingestion. Low creatinine/specific gravity indicates **dilution** (B); 6-monoacetylmorphine indicates **heroin** use (D).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Pharmacologic Interventions for Apathy in Patients with Alzheimer Disease (p. 14)

The family of a patient with Alzheimer disease asks about methylphenidate (Ritalin) as adjunctive therapy. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. It has been shown to improve apathy scores in select patients when compared with placebo.
- B. It has not been shown to improve cognition based on Mini-Mental State Examination scores when compared with placebo.
- C. It has been shown to increase the risk of serious adverse effects when compared with placebo.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Methylphenidate improves apathy scores vs. placebo in selected patients.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Methylphenidate modestly helps apathy. It **did** show some cognitive (MMSE) benefit (B wrong) and **did not** significantly increase serious adverse effects (C wrong), so "all of the above" is incorrect (D).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Prophylactic Vaccination Against Human Papillomavirus (p. 15)

A 27-year-old woman and her 16-year-old sister present for wellness visits. Which one of the following statements about the use of prophylactic human papillomavirus vaccine in these individuals is correct?

- A. When given at 16 years of age, it prevents the development of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) 2 or CIN 3.
- B. When given at 27 years of age, it prevents the development of CIN 2 or CIN 3.
- C. When given at any age, it is associated with an increase in serious adverse effects, including miscarriage in pregnant women.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Given at 16, HPV vaccine prevents CIN 2/CIN 3.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Efficacy is greatest in **HPV-naïve adolescents** before exposure. Benefit is much smaller in already-exposed older women (B overstated); the vaccine has a **strong safety record** with no increase in serious harms/miscarriage (C wrong).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Deprescribing Benzodiazepine Receptor Agonists for Insomnia in Adults (p. 57)

Which one of the following statements about deprescribing benzodiazepine receptor agonists (BZRAs) for insomnia is correct?

- A. A BZRA taper should be recommended in a 40-year-old woman who has used one for sleep at least two times a week for three weeks.
- B. Cognitive behavior therapy in addition to tapering has not been shown to improve cessation of BZRA use.
- C. A slow taper requires reducing medication dosing by 10% weekly for at least six weeks.
- D. Patients 65 years and older should be advised to slowly taper off of BZRAs regardless of the duration of use.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Adults ≥65 should be advised to taper off BZRAs regardless of duration of use.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Deprescribing targets adults **≥65 (any duration)** and those >18 using BZRAs **≥4 weeks** (so a 3-week user isn't yet targeted, A); **CBT improves** cessation success (B wrong); taper schedules are individualized, not a fixed 10%/week rule (C).
