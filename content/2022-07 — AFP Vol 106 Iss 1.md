## Q1 — Office-Based Urinalysis (p. 27)
Which statement about urine collection is correct?

- A. Genital cleansing is proven to decrease contamination.
- B. In stable febrile infants, a urine collection bag is a reasonable initial sample.
- C. Midstream collection is proven to reduce contamination.
- D. Midstream collection is not recommended for detecting microhematuria.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A bag specimen is a reasonable initial sample in stable febrile infants.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> A negative bag specimen helps rule out UTI in low-risk infants (a positive needs catheter confirmation). Evidence does not clearly show genital cleansing or midstream collection reduces contamination. Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q2 — Office-Based Urinalysis (p. 27)
A trace level of which is *normal* on urine dipstick?

- A. Bilirubin.
- B. Glucose.
- C. Ketones.
- D. Urobilinogen.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Urobilinogen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Small amounts of urobilinogen are normal. Bilirubin, glucose, and ketones should be absent — their presence is pathologic.

## Q3 — Office-Based Urinalysis (p. 27)
Which positive finding is most predictive of UTI?

- A. Bacteria on microscopy.
- B. Epithelial cells.
- C. Leukocyte esterase.
- D. Nitrites.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Nitrites.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Nitrites are highly *specific* (gram-negative bacteria reduce nitrate→nitrite), so a positive result strongly predicts UTI. Leukocyte esterase is sensitive but less specific; epithelial cells suggest contamination.

## Q4 — Celiac Disease (p. 36)
Which statement about prevalence/risk factors for celiac disease is correct?

- A. Celiac does not present after age 50.
- B. A second-degree relative with celiac does not increase risk.
- C. Breastfeeding reduces the risk in infants.
- D. Children may present with short stature and failure to thrive.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Children may present with short stature and failure to thrive.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Celiac can present at any age (A false); first- and second-degree relatives are at increased risk (B false); breastfeeding has not been shown to prevent it (C false).

## Q5 — Celiac Disease (p. 36)
A 32-year-old with diarrhea/bloating, recently eating more whole grains. Recommended first step in testing?

- A. Stop gluten, then test in 2–6 weeks.
- B. IgA tissue transglutaminase (tTG-IgA) serology.
- C. HLA DQ2/DQ8 typing.
- D. EGD with small-bowel biopsy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — tTG-IgA serology (while still eating gluten).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> tTG-IgA is first-line and requires the patient to *continue* gluten (so A is wrong). HLA typing only excludes disease; biopsy confirms positive serology.

## Q6 — Celiac Disease (p. 36)
Correct treatment/surveillance statement for diagnosed celiac disease?

- A. Switch from wheat/barley to rye products.
- B. Six-month gluten-free diet, then reintroduce gluten to check for symptoms.
- C. No routine follow-up labs needed after diagnosis.
- D. Persistent symptoms should prompt considering continued gluten ingestion or coexisting conditions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Persistent symptoms → look for hidden gluten or coexisting conditions.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Rye also contains gluten (A false); the diet is lifelong, not a 6-month trial (B false); follow-up serology/labs are recommended (C false).

## Q7 — Acute Pancreatitis (p. 44)
Best initial lab test for suspected acute pancreatitis?

- A. Amylase (elevated longer than lipase).
- B. Lipase (elevated longer than amylase).
- C. Serial lipase measurements to improve sensitivity.
- D. Alkaline phosphatase (elevated in all forms).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Serum lipase.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Lipase is more sensitive/specific and stays elevated longer than amylase. Serial measurements aren't needed for diagnosis.

## Q8 — Acute Pancreatitis (p. 44)
Correct statement about mild acute *biliary* pancreatitis?

- A. Cholecystectomy during the initial admission decreases length of stay and readmissions.
- B. Cholecystectomy should be delayed due to increased complications.
- C. ERCP is indicated within 24 hours.
- D. ERCP is contraindicated in cholangitis or biliary obstruction.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Same-admission cholecystectomy lowers LOS and readmissions.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Early (same-admission) cholecystectomy is recommended for mild biliary pancreatitis. ERCP is reserved for cholangitis/obstruction (so D is backwards).

## Q9 — Acute Pancreatitis (p. 44)
Which should be part of routine initial management of acute pancreatitis?

- A. Pain control with opioids.
- B. Strict nonfeeding for the first 48 hours.
- C. Goal-directed fluid resuscitation with lactated Ringer solution.
- D. Prophylactic antibiotics.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Goal-directed fluid resuscitation with lactated Ringer.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Early goal-directed LR is the cornerstone. Early (not delayed) feeding is favored; prophylactic antibiotics are not recommended in the absence of infection.

## Q10 — Care of the Active Female (p. 52)
A female runner with anterior knee pain worsened by squatting/prolonged sitting (PFPS). Treatment most likely to give long-term improvement?

- A. Quadriceps strengthening alone.
- B. Exercises focused on posterior hip and quadriceps.
- C. Foot orthoses to correct overpronation.
- D. Regular patellar taping.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Combined posterior-hip and quadriceps exercises.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For patellofemoral pain, hip- (gluteal) plus knee-focused exercise outperforms knee-only programs for durable improvement. Orthoses/taping give only short-term relief.

## Q11 — Care of the Active Female (p. 52)
Which high school athlete has the highest risk of concussion and postconcussive symptoms?

- A. Female soccer player.
- B. Male soccer player.
- C. Female gymnast.
- D. Male gymnast.

> [!success]- Reveal answer

> In sex-comparable sports (e.g., soccer), females have higher concussion rates and more prolonged postconcussive symptoms than males.

## Q12 — Care of the Active Female (p. 52)
Which preparticipation exam question screens for relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S)?

- A. Family history of sudden death?
- B. Previous concussion?
- C. More shortness of breath during exercise than anticipated?
- D. When was your most recent menstrual period?

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Date of most recent menstrual period.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Menstrual dysfunction/amenorrhea is a core marker of RED-S (low energy availability). The other questions screen for cardiac, neurologic, or pulmonary issues.

## Q13 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2021 (p. 61)
Which statement about diabetes is correct?

- A. Most adults in their 70s with prediabetes will develop diabetes.
- B. Sulfonylureas reduce hospitalizations in older patients.
- C. SGLT-2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce all-cause mortality.
- D. Benefits of SGLT-2 inhibitors are limited to people with diabetes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — SGLT-2 inhibitors and GLP-1 RAs reduce all-cause mortality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most older adults with prediabetes regress or die of other causes rather than progress (A false); SGLT-2 inhibitors benefit patients with HF/CKD even without diabetes (D false).

## Q14 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2021 (p. 61)
Which statement about musculoskeletal conditions is correct?

- A. For nonspecific LBP, muscle relaxants are superior to NSAIDs.
- B. Exercise produces sustained relief of chronic low back pain.
- C. Opioids relieve acute muscle pain better than acetaminophen + ibuprofen.
- D. How MRI results are communicated has no effect on LBP treatment response.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Exercise gives sustained relief of chronic low back pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Muscle relaxants aren't clearly superior to NSAIDs (A false); opioids are not superior to acetaminophen+ibuprofen for acute MSK pain (C false); MRI result framing does influence outcomes (D false). Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q15 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2021 (p. 61)
Which statement about cardiovascular disease is correct?

- A. Optimal aspirin dose for secondary prevention is 325 mg/day.
- B. Framingham overestimates 5-year risk but the Pooled Cohort Equations do not.
- C. Office BP readings match research-protocol readings.
- D. Egg consumption does not increase the risk of cardiovascular events.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Egg consumption does not increase cardiovascular risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Low-dose (81 mg) aspirin is as effective as 325 mg (A false); both risk calculators can overestimate (B false); routine office BP typically reads higher than standardized research measurement (C false).

## Q16 — Acute Diarrhea in Adults (p. 72)
Which presentation is most consistent with *noninflammatory* acute diarrhea?

- A. 24-year-old, two days of watery diarrhea, no fever.
- B. 34-year-old, bloody diarrhea and severe abdominal pain.
- C. 24-year-old, seven days of diarrhea and fever.
- D. 34-year-old, diarrhea with positive Shiga toxin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Brief watery diarrhea without fever.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Noninflammatory = watery, afebrile, self-limited. Blood, fever, prolonged course, or Shiga toxin indicate inflammatory diarrhea.

## Q17 — Acute Diarrhea in Adults (p. 72)
A 44-year-old, four days of nonbloody diarrhea, no travel/unusual food, normal exam/vitals. Best approach?

- A. Stool Shiga toxin test.
- B. Chemistry panel for electrolytes.
- C. Encourage early return to a normal diet.
- D. Test stool for occult blood.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Encourage early return to a normal diet.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Uncomplicated, nonbloody, afebrile acute diarrhea needs supportive care and early refeeding — no routine stool testing or labs.

## Q18 — Acute Diarrhea in Adults (p. 72)
A 67-year-old back from travel, two days of bloody diarrhea + fevers, well-controlled diabetes, mildly tachycardic, normal BP, mild abdominal tenderness. Most appropriate treatment statement?

- A. Loperamide should not be prescribed.
- B. A three-day course of empiric antibiotics is reasonable.
- C. Probiotics are contraindicated in travelers' diarrhea.
- D. Oral rehydration solutions increase the number of diarrhea episodes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A three-day empiric antibiotic course is reasonable.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Moderate-to-severe travelers' diarrhea with fever/blood in a comorbid patient warrants empiric antibiotics. Loperamide may be used *adjunctively* with antibiotics; ORS reduces, not increases, episodes. Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q19 — Cochrane: DPP-4 / GLP-1 / SGLT-2 in Cardiovascular Disease (p. 24)
Which statement about diabetes medications in patients with CVD is correct?

- A. DPP-4 inhibitors reduce cardiovascular and all-cause mortality.
- B. GLP-1 receptor agonists are ineffective at reducing CV and all-cause mortality.
- C. SGLT-2 inhibitors reduce cardiovascular and all-cause mortality.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — SGLT-2 inhibitors reduce CV and all-cause mortality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> DPP-4 inhibitors are mortality-neutral (A false); GLP-1 RAs *do* reduce CV events/mortality (B false).

## Q20 — Cochrane: Palliative Care Interventions in Advanced Dementia (p. 25)
After a new Alzheimer diagnosis, which is likely to increase with advance care planning?

- A. Documentation of advance directives.

## Q21 — PPIP: Screening for Chlamydia and Gonorrhea (p. 81)
A sexually active 20-year-old woman, monogamous 6-month relationship, no condoms, no STI history. Per USPSTF, appropriate screening?

- A. Screen only for chlamydia (more common in women).
- B. Screen only for gonorrhea (PID risk).
- C. Do not screen — monogamous and no risk factors.
- D. Screen for chlamydia and gonorrhea — often asymptomatic and common in young adults.
- E. Screen only if symptomatic.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Screen for both chlamydia and gonorrhea.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF recommends screening all sexually active women ≤24 years (and older women at risk), regardless of symptoms or reported monogamy, because infections are common and frequently asymptomatic.

## Q22 — Practice Guidelines: High-Flow Nasal Oxygen for Acute Respiratory Failure (ACP) (p. 100)
A 46-year-old with dyspnea and SpO₂ 83%. Which would the ACP recommend initially to reduce intubation and death?

- A. Bilevel positive airway pressure.
- B. Continuous positive airway pressure.
- C. High-flow oxygen by nasal cannula (e.g., 20 L/min).
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — High-flow nasal oxygen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The ACP recommends high-flow nasal oxygen over conventional oxygen and over noninvasive ventilation for acute *hypoxemic* respiratory failure, reducing intubation and mortality. Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q23 — Practice Guidelines: Palliative Care of Patients With Cancer (NCCN) (p. 102)
A 57-year-old with advanced metastatic cancer, weight loss, severe chemo side effects, poor prognosis. Recommended NCCN palliative intervention?

- A. Assess patient distress on a 10-point scale.
- B. Insist the patient continue chemotherapy.
- C. Encourage appetite-stimulant therapy to prevent weight loss.
- D. Titrate pain medication until pain level is zero.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Assess distress on a 10-point scale (distress thermometer).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NCCN emphasizes routine distress screening. Goals-of-care should be respected (not coerced chemo), appetite stimulants have limited benefit in cancer cachexia, and the pain goal is comfort/function, not necessarily zero.
