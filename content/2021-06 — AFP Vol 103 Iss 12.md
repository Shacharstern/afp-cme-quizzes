## Q1 — Irritable Bowel Syndrome: Questions and Answers for Effective Care (p. 727)

A 48-year-old patient reports bloating and abdominal pain. Which one of the following is considered an alarm feature that warrants further testing?

- A. Symptoms have been occurring at least one day per week for the past three months.
- B. Symptoms are accompanied by unintended weight loss.
- C. The patient has both constipation and diarrhea.
- D. Stooling is exclusive to waking hours.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Symptoms accompanied by unintended weight loss.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Unintentional weight loss is a classic IBS "alarm/red-flag" feature (along with rectal bleeding, anemia, nocturnal symptoms, age >50 at onset, family history of GI cancer/IBD) prompting workup for organic disease. Option A describes the Rome IV symptom-frequency criterion (supports IBS, not an alarm); mixed bowel habits (C) is just IBS-M.

## Q2 — Irritable Bowel Syndrome (p. 727)

A 26-year-old patient with chronic abdominal pain, alleviated slightly by defecation, presents for an initial visit. The patient's stools have recently become mushy and more frequent. Which one of the following approaches should be used first?

- A. Prescribe insoluble fiber to decrease pain and bloating.
- B. Refer the patient to a gastroenterologist for evaluation and medication management.
- C. Focus on the most troublesome symptoms or triggers and on improving quality of life.
- D. Prescribe a gluten-free diet as a general first-line strategy to improve symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Focus on the most troublesome symptoms/triggers and on improving quality of life.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> IBS management is symptom-targeted and patient-centered. Insoluble fiber can worsen bloating (soluble fiber preferred); routine GI referral is unnecessary without alarm features; gluten-free diets are not a blanket first-line recommendation.

## Q3 — Mobility Assistive Device Use in Older Adults (p. 737)

Which one of the following statements about Medicare coverage of mobility assistive devices is correct?

- A. They are considered medically necessary if the patient needs it to leave their home.
- B. If the physician deems it medically necessary, it is automatically approved by Medicare.
- C. They are covered under Medicare as durable medical equipment.
- D. The patient is not required to pay a deductible.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Covered under Medicare as durable medical equipment (DME).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Medicare Part B covers mobility assistive devices as DME. Medical necessity is judged by need *within the home* (not to leave it — A is wrong); physician certification does not equal automatic approval (B); standard Part B deductible/coinsurance applies (D).

## Q4 — Mobility Assistive Device Use (p. 737)

When fitting a patient for a standard cane, the top of the handle should be in which one of the following positions?

- A. 1.5 to 2 inches above the wrist of an arm hanging by the patient's side.
- B. At the level of the midpalm of an arm hanging by the patient's side.
- C. At the level of the midpalm with the elbow flexed to 45 degrees.
- D. At the level of the wrist of an arm hanging by the patient's side.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — At the level of the wrist of an arm hanging by the side.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Proper cane height: with the arm relaxed at the side, the cane handle should reach the wrist crease, producing ~15–30° elbow flexion when gripped.

## Q5 — Mobility Assistive Device Use (p. 737)

A patient with heart failure becomes tired while ambulating but has no difficulties with balance or gait. Which one of the following would be the most appropriate assistive device for this patient?

- A. Standard walker.
- B. Four-wheel rolling walker.
- C. Platform crutches.
- D. Manual wheelchair.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Four-wheel rolling walker (rollator).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A rollator with a seat suits patients who fatigue (e.g., HF, COPD) but have intact balance — it permits rest breaks and smooth ambulation. A standard walker (must be lifted with each step) is for those needing more stability/weight-bearing; not indicated here.

## Q6 — Obstetric Lacerations: Prevention and Repair (p. 745)

An infant weighing 3,680 g is delivered at term via uncomplicated spontaneous vaginal delivery. After delivery, a superficial laceration on the left labia minora continues to ooze blood despite holding pressure. Which one of the following statements about this laceration is correct?

- A. Failure to suture this laceration will increase the patient's risk of pain at eight weeks postpartum.
- B. A simple, interrupted suture to achieve hemostasis is appropriate.
- C. There is no need to assess for patient comfort prior to repair.
- D. Using surgical glue for such lacerations increases long-term complications.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A simple, interrupted suture to achieve hemostasis is appropriate.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> First-degree/superficial lacerations are often left unrepaired, but **active bleeding** is an indication to suture for hemostasis. Leaving small lacerations unsutured does not increase later pain (A wrong); comfort/anesthesia should always be assessed (C wrong); tissue adhesive is a reasonable low-complication option (D wrong).

## Q7 — Obstetric Lacerations (p. 745)

An infant is born via vacuum-assisted delivery. Close inspection reveals that the anal sphincter complex is torn but the rectal mucosa is intact. Which one of the following types of obstetric laceration is this?

- A. First degree.
- B. Second degree.
- C. Third degree.
- D. Fourth degree.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Third degree.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Degrees: 1st = skin/mucosa only; 2nd = into perineal muscles; **3rd = involves the anal sphincter complex** (rectal mucosa intact); 4th = extends through the anal/rectal epithelium. Sphincter torn + mucosa intact = third degree.

## Q8 — Obstetric Lacerations (p. 745)

Which one of the following statements about third-degree obstetric lacerations is correct?

- A. These are the least commonly seen obstetric lacerations.
- B. There is no evidence that perineal massage reduces these types of lacerations.
- C. Routine episiotomy decreases the incidence of third-degree lacerations.
- D. The anal sphincter should be repaired using an absorbable synthetic suture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — The anal sphincter should be repaired using an absorbable synthetic suture.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> OASIS repair uses absorbable synthetic suture (e.g., polyglactin/PDS). Antenatal perineal massage *does* reduce severe lacerations (B wrong); **routine** episiotomy *increases* OASIS risk (C wrong); 4th-degree are the least common (A wrong).

## Q9 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Interventions for Tobacco Smoking Cessation in Adults, Including Pregnant Persons (p. 753)

According to the USPSTF recommendation statement, which one of the following statements about the use of e-cigarettes for tobacco cessation in adults, including pregnant people, is correct?

- A. e-Cigarettes have been definitively shown to increase tobacco cessation in adults.
- B. e-Cigarettes are typically used only in the short term when used for tobacco cessation.
- C. The harms associated with e-cigarette use are well known and minor because nicotine is the only toxic substance emitted.
- D. The current evidence is insufficient to assess the balance of benefits and harms of e-cigarettes for tobacco cessation in adults, including pregnant people.
- E. There is convincing evidence that e-cigarettes for tobacco cessation are most effective when paired with behavioral counseling interventions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Current evidence is insufficient (I statement) to assess benefits/harms of e-cigarettes for cessation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF gives behavioral interventions + FDA-approved pharmacotherapy an **A** in non-pregnant adults, behavioral interventions an **A** in pregnancy, and an **I statement** for e-cigarettes (and for pharmacotherapy in pregnancy). Harms are not fully characterized (C wrong).

## Q10 — Lown Right Care: Poor Physician-Patient Communication and Medical Error (p. 757)

Which one of the following statements about poor communication and medical error is correct?

- A. Patient adherence to care plans is unrelated to the quality of physician-patient communication.
- B. Visit times increase when patients are allowed to tell their story and physicians explain information in a way that patients understand.
- C. Physicians often overestimate their communication skills.
- D. Communication skills tend to improve during a physician's career.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Physicians often overestimate their communication skills.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Self-assessment of communication is poorly calibrated — clinicians rate themselves higher than patients do. Good communication *improves* adherence (A wrong), does **not** lengthen visits (B wrong), and skills tend to *erode* without deliberate training (D wrong).

## Q11 — Point-of-Care Guides: Predicting True Penicillin Allergy in Adults (p. 760)

Which one of the following is an independent predictor of a true penicillin allergy?

- A. Allergic event occurring more than five years ago.
- B. History of three or more mild cutaneous reactions.
- C. History of anaphylaxis or angioedema.
- D. Previous allergic episode that resolved without treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — History of anaphylaxis or angioedema.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Features predicting a *true* IgE-mediated allergy: anaphylaxis/angioedema, recent reaction, and need for treatment of the reaction. Remote reactions (>5 yr, ~80% lose sensitivity), self-resolving episodes, and vague mild rashes argue *against* true allergy.

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Hypertension — New Guidelines from the International Society of Hypertension (p. 763)

According to the new ISH guidelines, which one of the following BP measurements would be diagnostic for hypertension?

- A. A single measurement of 136/89 mm Hg in the office.
- B. Two or more measurements between 135/85 and 140/90 mm Hg in the office.
- C. Home BP monitoring log with an average of 132/82 mm Hg.
- D. Ambulatory BP monitoring with a 24-hour average of 132/82 mm Hg.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — ABPM 24-hour average of 132/82 mm Hg.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ISH out-of-office thresholds: **24-hour ABPM average ≥130/80** = hypertension → 132/82 qualifies. Home/daytime threshold is ≥135/85 (so 132/82 home does *not* qualify — C wrong). Office diagnosis needs ≥140/90 on ≥2 visits (A and B below threshold).
