## Q1 — Telemedicine Management of Musculoskeletal Issues (p. 147)

Pain that localizes to the medial epicondyle with wrist flexion and pronation while holding a weighted object suggests which one of the following diagnoses?

- A. Lateral epicondylitis.
- B. Medial epicondylitis.
- C. Biceps tendon rupture.
- D. Triceps tendon rupture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Medial epicondylitis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Medial epicondyle pain provoked by resisted **wrist flexion and pronation** ("golfer's elbow") localizes to the flexor-pronator origin. Lateral epicondylitis ("tennis elbow") is provoked by resisted wrist *extension/supination*.

## Q2 — Telemedicine Management of Musculoskeletal Issues (p. 147)

Which one of the following household items, which can be used for telemedicine strength testing, weighs approximately 5 lb (2.27 kg)?

- A. 2-L bottle of soda.
- B. 1-L bottle of soda.
- C. Quart of milk.
- D. Large bag of potatoes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — 2-L bottle of soda.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A 2-L bottle weighs ≈ 4.4–5 lb, a handy household proxy for graded resistance during a virtual strength exam. A 1-L bottle/quart of milk ≈ 2 lb; a bag of potatoes is heavier and variable.

## Q3 — Telemedicine Management of Musculoskeletal Issues (p. 147)

Which one of the following findings from the virtual history and physical examination indicates that a patient with musculoskeletal issues needs an in-office or emergency department assessment?

- A. Lack of deformity.
- B. Presence of a clear diagnosis.
- C. Presence of anticipated response to home rehabilitation.
- D. Concern for neurovascular compromise.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Concern for neurovascular compromise.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Suspected neurovascular compromise (or fracture/deformity, infection, unremitting pain) is a red flag requiring in-person evaluation. The other options describe reassuring features that support continued virtual/home management.

## Q4 — Targeted Cancer Therapies (p. 155)

Which one of the following statements about targeted cancer therapy is correct?

- A. Monoclonal antibodies work by upregulating oncogene signaling.
- B. Small molecule inhibitors target only cancer cell proteins and do not have systemic adverse effects.
- C. Antibody-drug conjugates allow delivery of chemotherapy agents inside tumor cells.
- D. Immunotherapy refers to monoclonal antibodies that directly trigger apoptosis of cancer cells.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Antibody-drug conjugates deliver chemotherapy inside tumor cells.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ADCs link a cytotoxic payload to a targeting antibody → selective intracellular delivery. Monoclonal antibodies *block* (not upregulate) signaling (A wrong); small-molecule inhibitors hit shared pathways → systemic effects (B wrong); immunotherapy unleashes the *immune system* rather than directly triggering apoptosis (D wrong).

## Q5 — Targeted Cancer Therapies (p. 155)

Which one of the following statements about options for targeted cancer therapy in a 63-year-old patient diagnosed with metastatic colorectal cancer is correct?

- A. Patients with RAS mutations are more likely to benefit from epidermal growth factor receptor monoclonal antibodies.
- B. Testing for genetic mutations should be performed only if initial chemotherapy proves ineffective.
- C. Patients with BRAF-mutated colorectal cancer are candidates for a BRAF small molecule inhibitor.
- D. Targeted cancer therapy is not indicated because the cancer has metastasized.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — BRAF-mutated colorectal cancer → BRAF small molecule inhibitor.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> *RAS* mutations predict **resistance** to anti-EGFR antibodies (A reversed); molecular testing is done **up front** in metastatic disease, not after failure (B wrong); targeting is *especially* relevant in metastatic disease (D wrong).

## Q6 — Targeted Cancer Therapies (p. 155)

Which one of the following statements about adverse effects of targeted cancer therapy is correct?

- A. Patients with preexisting autoimmune conditions are at higher risk of toxicity with immunotherapy-based cancer treatments.
- B. Targeted cancer therapy drugs are not known to interact with warfarin (Coumadin).
- C. If patients do not develop adverse effects within the first month of targeted cancer therapy then there is little risk of toxicity.
- D. It is easy to predict which patients will develop adverse effects from targeted cancer therapies.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Preexisting autoimmune disease raises immunotherapy toxicity risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Checkpoint inhibitors can flare autoimmune disease (immune-related adverse events). Drug interactions (including warfarin) do occur (B wrong); irAEs can appear **late**, even months in (C wrong); toxicity is **not** reliably predictable (D wrong).

## Q7 — Sexual Assault of Women (p. 168)

Which one of the following statements about screening for sexual assault is correct?

- A. No validated screening tools for sexual assault are currently available.
- B. Most patients are willing to disclose exposure to sexual violence whether or not they are asked.
- C. Minimizing the impact of sexual assault is the best initial response to disclosure.
- D. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends universal intimate partner violence screening in women of reproductive age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — USPSTF recommends universal IPV screening in reproductive-age women.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF gives a **B** recommendation for IPV screening in women of reproductive age. Validated tools exist (A wrong); patients often **won't** disclose unless asked (B wrong); minimizing harms rapport — validate instead (C wrong).

## Q8 — Sexual Assault of Women (p. 168)

Which one of the following statements about evaluating a patient for sexual assault in the acute setting is correct?

- A. Testing for HIV should be delayed until six weeks after the assault.
- B. Informed consent is not needed before collecting evidence during the examination.
- C. A urine specimen should be obtained before initial physical examination and evidence collection.
- D. Empiric treatment against chlamydia, gonorrhea, and trichomoniasis is recommended for all survivors.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Empiric treatment for chlamydia, gonorrhea, and trichomoniasis for all survivors.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Presumptive STI prophylaxis is offered to all adult survivors. Baseline HIV testing is done at presentation (A wrong); **informed consent is mandatory** before evidence collection (B wrong); urine is collected as part of, not before, the structured exam/evidence kit (C wrong).

## Q9 — Sexual Assault of Women (p. 168)

A patient who has been sexually assaulted presents to the emergency department six hours after the attack. Health records show that the patient received the hepatitis B vaccination series as a child, but the vaccination status of the assailant is unknown. Which one of the following statements about this patient receiving the hepatitis B vaccination is correct?

- A. No treatment is recommended.
- B. The patient should get a single vaccination booster.
- C. Hepatitis B immunoglobulin should be administered.
- D. Hepatitis B vaccination series should be initiated.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Give a single hepatitis B vaccine booster.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A previously fully vaccinated survivor with unknown-source exposure needs only a **booster dose** — not HBIG and not a full new series. HBIG ± full series is reserved for unvaccinated/incompletely vaccinated survivors.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Exercise vs. No Exercise for Acute Respiratory Tract Infections (p. 144)

Your patient runs several days a week and asks whether they should stop running when they get a mild upper respiratory tract infection. Which one of the following statements about acute respiratory tract infections is correct?

- A. Regular exercise increases their severity.
- B. Regular exercise reduces their duration.
- C. Regular exercise reduces their overall occurrence.
- D. Regular exercise may reduce their overall severity and the number of days with symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Exercise may reduce overall severity and number of symptomatic days.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found low-certainty evidence that regular exercise may reduce ARTI **severity and symptom-days**, but did **not** show a reduction in occurrence or duration alone (B, C wrong) and did not increase severity (A wrong).

## Q11 — Topical and Device-Based Treatment of Toenail Onychomycosis (p. 145)

Your patient prefers a topical medication for onychomycosis even though oral agents are the recommended first-line treatment. Which one of the following statements about topical treatment of onychomycosis is correct?

- A. Efinaconazole 10% solution (Jublia) is less effective than placebo at achieving clinical cure.
- B. Efinaconazole 10% solution is effective at achieving mycologic cure.
- C. Topical treatments are not associated with any adverse effects.
- D. Topical treatments are always less expensive than oral medications.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Efinaconazole 10% is effective at achieving mycologic cure.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Efinaconazole outperforms placebo for mycologic (and modest clinical) cure (A reversed); topicals can cause local irritation (C wrong); they are often **more** expensive and require prolonged daily use (D wrong).

## Q12 — AHRQ Review: Brief Cognitive Testing in Alzheimer-Type Dementia (p. 183)

Which one of the following statements about brief cognitive tests for detecting cognitive impairment in older adults is correct?

- A. They are better at distinguishing clinical Alzheimer-type dementia (ATD) from mild cognitive impairment than distinguishing clinical ATD from normal cognitive function.
- B. Most have low sensitivity and specificity.
- C. The Montreal Cognitive Assessment is the only stand-alone test with sensitivity and specificity greater than 0.90 for distinguishing clinical ATD from normal cognition.
- D. They are all proprietary.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — MoCA is the only stand-alone brief test with sensitivity AND specificity >0.90 (ATD vs. normal).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Brief tests are better at separating dementia from **normal** cognition than from MCI (A reversed); most have **reasonable** accuracy (B wrong); several are free/public domain (D wrong).

## Q13 — Practice Guidelines: Chronic Pelvic Pain in Women — ACOG (p. 186)

According to recommendations from the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), which one of the following treatments is most likely to benefit a 36-year-old patient with a two-year history of pelvic pain that is not related to menstrual cycles and gets worse during intercourse?

- A. Weekly massage therapy.
- B. Laparoscopic adhesiolysis.
- C. Opioids.
- D. Pelvic floor physical therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Pelvic floor physical therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Non-cyclic pain worsened by intercourse points to a **myofascial/pelvic floor** component; ACOG endorses pelvic floor PT. Adhesiolysis doesn't reliably relieve pain (B wrong); opioids are discouraged for chronic pelvic pain (C wrong); generic massage lacks evidence (A wrong).
