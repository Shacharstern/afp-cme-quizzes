## Q1 — Type 1 Diabetes: Management Strategies (p. 154)

Which one of the following statements about the benefits of intensive insulin therapy in patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus is correct?

- A. Intensive therapy prevents the progression of retinopathy.
- B. Intensive therapy decreases rates of hypoglycemia.
- C. All-cause mortality is not affected.
- D. Treatment goals are less stringent for pregnant women.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Intensive therapy slows the progression of retinopathy (and other microvascular complications).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Intensive control (DCCT) reduces microvascular disease and improves mortality (C wrong) but **increases hypoglycemia** (B reversed); pregnancy requires **tighter**, not looser, targets (D wrong).

## Q2 — Type 1 Diabetes (p. 154)

Which one of the following statements about glucose self-monitoring in patients with type 1 diabetes is correct?

- A. Continuous self-monitoring eliminates the need for capillary testing.
- B. Self-monitoring contributes to tight control.
- C. Weekly monitoring of fasting glucose usually suffices to achieve A1C goals.
- D. The main advantage of continuous self-monitoring is prevention of hypoglycemic events.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Frequent self-monitoring contributes to tight glycemic control.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CGM still requires periodic capillary confirmation (A); weekly fasting checks are inadequate for type 1 (C); CGM's benefits are broad (improved control/time-in-range), not solely hypoglycemia prevention (D).

## Q3 — Type 1 Diabetes (p. 154)

Which one of the following statements about dosing of insulin in patients with type 1 diabetes is correct?

- A. Optimal dosing consists of mixed short- and intermediate-acting insulins twice daily.
- B. Multiple injections result in better glucose control than continuous infusions.
- C. Long-acting insulins are best for use in continuous pump infusions.
- D. Frequent administration of rapid-acting insulin every two to four hours may be needed when the patient is ill.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — During illness, rapid-acting insulin may be needed every two to four hours.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Basal-bolus (or pump) regimens are preferred over twice-daily mixed insulin (A); pumps and MDI give **comparable** control (B); pumps use **rapid-acting** insulin, not long-acting (C wrong).

## Q4 — Rheumatologic Tests: A Primer for Family Physicians (p. 164)

Which one of the following test results has the greatest specificity for systemic lupus erythematosus?

- A. Positive anti-double-stranded DNA antibody titer.
- B. Positive antinuclear antibody titer with nucleolar pattern.
- C. Positive antinuclear antibody titer with speckled pattern.
- D. Positive anti-Smith antibody titer.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Anti-Smith antibody (most specific for SLE).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Anti-Sm** is the most specific (though less sensitive) SLE marker; anti-dsDNA is also specific and tracks activity (A). A positive ANA is sensitive but **nonspecific** (B, C).

## Q5 — Rheumatologic Tests (p. 164)

Anticyclic citrullinated peptide antibody testing is useful in the diagnosis of which one of the following connective tissue disorders?

- A. Sjogren syndrome.
- B. Dermatomyositis.
- C. Rheumatoid arthritis.
- D. Scleroderma.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Rheumatoid arthritis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Anti-CCP** is highly specific for RA and predicts erosive disease.

## Q6 — Chronic Nonallergic Rhinitis (p. 171)

Which one of the following is considered a first-line treatment for chronic nonallergic rhinitis?

- A. Intranasal corticosteroids.
- B. Oral decongestants.
- C. Combination decongestants and antihistamines.
- D. Oral antihistamines.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Intranasal corticosteroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Intranasal corticosteroids (± intranasal antihistamine) are first-line. **Oral** antihistamines are largely ineffective in nonallergic rhinitis (D), and oral decongestants are adjuncts with adverse effects (B, C).

## Q7 — Chronic Nonallergic Rhinitis (p. 171)

Which one of the following statements about antihistamine therapy for chronic nonallergic rhinitis is correct?

- A. Oral antihistamines are as effective as inhaled or intranasal antihistamines.
- B. First- and second-generation antihistamines are equally effective.
- C. The intranasal antihistamine azelastine has been shown to improve nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, postnasal drip, and sneezing.
- D. Oral antihistamines are more effective for nonallergic rhinitis than for allergic rhinitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Intranasal azelastine improves congestion, rhinorrhea, postnasal drip, and sneezing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Intranasal** antihistamines outperform oral ones for nonallergic rhinitis (A, D wrong); the generations aren't equivalent (B).

## Q8 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Interventions for Treatment of Overweight and Obese Children (p. 151)

Which one of the following statements about childhood obesity is correct?

- A. Childhood obesity (i.e., an age- and sex-specific body mass index in the 95th percentile or greater) affects 17% of children two to 19 years of age in the United States.
- B. Childhood obesity leads to adult obesity and is associated with respiratory, metabolic, and psychosocial disorders throughout the life span.
- C. Behavioral interventions, including advice on diet and physical activity, can reduce body weight, and the effects are maintained up to six months after the intervention.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> US childhood obesity prevalence is ~17%, it tracks into adulthood with multisystem complications, and behavioral (diet + activity) interventions produce weight reductions sustained at least ~6 months.

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: The Role of Proton Pump Inhibitor Therapy in Patients with Functional Dyspepsia (p. 152)

Which one of the following statements about the use of proton pump inhibitor (PPI) therapy for functional dyspepsia is correct?

- A. Standard-dose PPI therapy is better at reducing symptoms than low-dose PPI therapy.
- B. Adding a prokinetic agent increases the effectiveness of PPI therapy.
- C. PPIs and histamine H₂ antagonists are similarly effective in reducing symptoms.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — PPIs and H₂ antagonists are similarly effective for functional dyspepsia.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Low-dose PPI works as well as standard-dose** (A wrong), and adding a prokinetic didn't clearly improve PPI efficacy in this review (B wrong) — so "all of the above" is incorrect (D).

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: Diabetes — ADA Revised Position Statement on Standards of Medical Care (p. 187)

Which one of the following statements about the American Diabetes Association (ADA) guidelines on managing diabetes mellitus is correct?

- A. Patients with type 2 diabetes and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease should be managed initially with dual therapy.
- B. Continuous glucose monitoring is recommended starting at 15 years of age in patients with type 1 diabetes.
- C. In patients with prediabetes, referral to a behavioral lifestyle intervention program modeling the Diabetes Prevention Program should be made to encourage weight loss and physical activity.
- D. Testing for diabetes should be performed every six months in persons who already have prediabetes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Refer prediabetes patients to a Diabetes Prevention Program–modeled lifestyle intervention.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Type 2 diabetes still **starts with metformin monotherapy** (plus lifestyle), even with ASCVD (A overstated for the era); CGM benefits **all ages** with type 1 (B); prediabetes is rescreened **annually**, not every 6 months (D wrong).
