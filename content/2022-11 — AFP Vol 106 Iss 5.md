## Q1 — Smoking Cessation (p. 513)
Most effective treatment for a nonpregnant adult ready to quit smoking?

- A. Starting an exercise program.
- B. Nicotine replacement therapy started on the quit date.
- C. Motivational interviewing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Pharmacotherapy + behavioral intervention.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Combining medication with counseling is more effective than either alone — the consistent finding across cessation evidence.

## Q3 — Smoking Cessation (p. 513)
Most accurate statement about e-cigarettes and smoking?

- A. The USPSTF finds insufficient evidence to recommend e-cigarettes for cessation.
- B. Adolescents who use e-cigarettes are less likely to smoke.
- C. Consistent evidence shows e-cigarette use reduces smoking.
- D. Adults switching to e-cigarettes are at low risk of dependency.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — USPSTF: insufficient evidence for e-cigarettes as a cessation aid.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Adolescent e-cigarette use *increases* later smoking; evidence on cessation is inconsistent; dependency risk is real. The USPSTF gives an I statement.

## Q4 — Long COVID (p. 523)
Recommended initial laboratory evaluation for a patient with long COVID?

- A. C-reactive protein and erythrocyte sedimentation rate.
- B. EBV viral capsid antigen testing.
- C. Urine culture.
- D. IgG and IgM antibodies to SARS-CoV-2.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — CRP and ESR.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Initial workup is a focused, symptom-guided panel (CBC, metabolic panel, inflammatory markers like CRP/ESR, TSH). Serology doesn't confirm long COVID and isn't part of the standard evaluation.

## Q5 — Long COVID (p. 523)
Which is a common symptom of long COVID?

- A. Fatigue.
- B. Cognitive impairment (brain fog).
- C. Anosmia.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Fatigue, brain fog, and persistent anosmia/dysgeusia are all hallmark post-acute COVID symptoms, alongside dyspnea and exertional intolerance.

## Q6 — Long COVID (p. 523)
Correct statement about treatment of long COVID?

- A. The goal is return to normal activity within six months.
- B. Treatment should be individualized to the patient.
- C. Randomized trials have identified several effective medical therapies.
- D. Polypharmacy is an unlikely cause of cognitive impairment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Individualize treatment.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Management is symptom-directed and individualized (pacing, rehab, treating comorbidities). No proven disease-specific drug therapy exists; polypharmacy *should* be reviewed as a reversible contributor to brain fog.

## Q10 — Head and Neck Injuries / Sideline (p. 543)
An athlete had transient burning shoulder pain that resolved in minutes (a "stinger"). Which statement is correct?

- A. Should not return to play the same day.
- B. Immediate ED transport is needed.
- C. The C7 nerve root is most commonly involved.
- D. Persistent, bilateral extremity paresthesia warrants cervical spine stabilization.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Bilateral paresthesia → cervical spine stabilization.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> A typical unilateral stinger that fully resolves allows same-day return. **Bilateral** symptoms suggest spinal cord (not brachial plexus) involvement → immobilize. Stingers usually involve the upper trunk (C5–C6), not C7.

## Q11 — Head and Neck Injuries / Sideline (p. 543)
For a facial injury with no airway obstruction, the sideline physician should:

- A. Glue lacerations and return the athlete to play.
- B. Remove the helmet but not the pads if transport is warranted.
- C. Refer for decreased extraocular movements but not for diplopia/infraorbital hypoesthesia.
- D. Have the athlete bite down to check for malocclusion if a mandibular fracture is suspected.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Check for malocclusion (bite test) if mandible fracture is suspected.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Malocclusion is a key sign of mandibular fracture. Helmet and pads are removed together (to protect the cervical spine); diplopia and infraorbital numbness *do* signal orbital fracture and warrant referral.

## Q12 — Head and Neck Injuries / Sideline (p. 543)
Correct statement about sports-related eye injuries?

- A. Traumatic hyphema can cause permanent vision loss and needs emergent evaluation.
- B. Subconjunctival hemorrhages require emergency treatment.
- C. Apply slight pressure to an injured eye during transport.
- D. Eye protection has not been shown to reduce traumatic eye injuries.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Traumatic hyphema → emergent evaluation.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Hyphema risks rebleeding, glaucoma, and permanent vision loss. Subconjunctival hemorrhage is benign; **never** apply pressure to a possibly ruptured globe (use a shield); protective eyewear does reduce injuries.

## Q13 — Enuresis in Children (p. 549)
Correct statement about primary monosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis?

- A. Spontaneous resolution is rare.
- B. Frequency of bedwetting does not affect prognosis.
- C. A positive family history is the most significant risk factor.
- D. It is benign and does not affect quality of life.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Family history is the most significant risk factor.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Strong heritability (≈75% if both parents affected). Spontaneous resolution is common (~15%/year); higher frequency predicts worse prognosis; enuresis meaningfully harms self-esteem/QoL.

## Q14 — Enuresis in Children (p. 549)
A 6-year-old has 2–3 bedwetting episodes per night, no daytime symptoms, never >1 week dry, normal exam. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Primary monosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis.
- B. Secondary monosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis.
- C. Nonmonosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis.
- D. Too young for the diagnosis of enuresis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Primary monosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> "Primary" = never achieved a sustained dry period; "monosymptomatic" = night-only, no daytime urinary symptoms. Enuresis can be diagnosed from age 5.

## Q15 — Enuresis in Children (p. 549)
Correct statement about treatment of primary monosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis?

- A. Enuresis alarms are no better than placebo for sustained dryness after stopping.
- B. Alarms are more effective when the family is highly motivated.
- C. Intranasal desmopressin is the preferred delivery method.
- D. If desmopressin fails, switch to oxybutynin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Alarms work best with a motivated family.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Enuresis alarms have the best long-term cure rate but demand family commitment. They *do* maintain dryness after discontinuation (so A is wrong); **oral** (not intranasal) desmopressin is preferred (hyponatremia risk); oxybutynin is for nonmonosymptomatic/overactive bladder.

## Q16 — Peripheral Edema (p. 557)
A patient has hypertension and chronic venous insufficiency. Which antihypertensive class controls BP without worsening edema?

- A. Calcium channel blockers (amlodipine).
- B. ACE inhibitors (lisinopril).
- C. Beta blockers (metoprolol).
- D. Central alpha₂ agonists (clonidine).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — ACE inhibitor.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Dihydropyridine CCBs (amlodipine) cause dose-dependent peripheral edema and would worsen this patient. ACE inhibitors lower BP without aggravating edema.

## Q23 — Practice Guidelines: Surviving Sepsis (p. 589)
Which is recommended by the Surviving Sepsis Campaign to reduce mortality in septic shock?

- A. After resuscitation, guide fluids by central venous pressure.
- B. In respiratory failure, use high-flow nasal oxygen instead of NIV.
- C. Initially resuscitate with 30 mL/kg crystalloid within the first three hours.
- D. Use procalcitonin with clinical evaluation to decide on antibiotics.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — 30 mL/kg crystalloid within the first 3 hours.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The bundle calls for ≥30 mL/kg IV crystalloid within 3 hours, then dynamic measures (not static CVP) to guide further fluids. SSC suggests *against* using procalcitonin to decide whether to start antibiotics.
