## Q1 — Gallstone Disease: Common Questions and Answers (p. 518)
Which one of the following factors is associated with the highest risk of gallstones?

- A. Having type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- B. Having nonalcoholic fatty liver disease.
- C. Having a body mass index greater than 35 kg per m².
- D. Using a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — BMI > 35 kg/m².** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Obesity is the strongest classic risk factor for cholesterol gallstones (lithogenic bile, supersaturation). T2DM and NAFLD are associated but weaker; GLP-1 agonists raise gallstone risk chiefly via rapid weight loss. **⚠️ Verify** (GLP-1/rapid-weight-loss is the tempting distractor).

## Q2 — Gallstone Disease (p. 518)
Which one of the following statements about the management of acute biliary pain is supported by evidence?

- A. Opiates are first-line treatment because they provide superior pain control.
- B. Ursodeoxycholic acid and chenodeoxycholic acid are effective for the relief of acute biliary pain.
- C. Intravenous lidocaine demonstrated faster pain relief compared with intravenous morphine in a randomized controlled trial.
- D. Ketorolac is less effective than butorphanol in controlling biliary pain in the emergency department.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — IV lidocaine showed faster pain relief than IV morphine in an RCT.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> NSAIDs (e.g., ketorolac) are first-line and at least as effective as opioids (D wrong); opioids are not superior and may cause sphincter-of-Oddi spasm (A wrong); urso/chenodeoxycholic acid dissolve stones over months and don't relieve acute pain (B wrong). A trial supported IV lidocaine for biliary colic. **⚠️ Verify.**

## Q3 — Management of Spontaneous Vaginal Delivery (p. 525)
A patient is admitted for full-term labor at 6 cm dilation and requests intermittent auscultation instead of continuous electronic fetal monitoring. Which one of the following outcomes is an advantage of continuous electronic fetal monitoring?

- A. Reduction of cerebral palsy.
- B. Reduction of neonatal seizures.
- C. Reduction of perinatal mortality.
- D. Reduction of unnecessary operative delivery.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Reduction of neonatal seizures.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane evidence shows continuous EFM reduces neonatal seizures but does **not** reduce cerebral palsy or perinatal mortality, and it *increases* operative (cesarean/instrumental) delivery — so D is the opposite of the truth.

## Q4 — Management of Spontaneous Vaginal Delivery (p. 525)
Which one of the following interventions increases the chance of spontaneous vaginal delivery for a patient in active labor?

- A. Amniotomy.
- B. Continuous nonmedical support.
- C. Delayed pushing.
- D. Food intake.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Continuous nonmedical (doula/support-person) support.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Continuous labor support increases spontaneous vaginal delivery and reduces cesarean/instrumental delivery and analgesia use. Routine amniotomy and delayed pushing don't increase spontaneous delivery; food intake has no proven effect on mode of delivery.

## Q5 — Rosacea: Common Questions and Answers (p. 533)
A patient is diagnosed with rosacea with fixed erythema, papules, pustules, and telangiectasia. Which one of the following lifestyle interventions should be recommended as a conservative treatment to reduce symptoms?

- A. Alcohol cessation.
- B. Daily sun exposure.
- C. Avoidance of skin moisturizers.
- D. Tobacco cessation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Alcohol cessation.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Alcohol is a classic rosacea flushing trigger. Sun exposure is a trigger (so photoprotection, not "daily sun," is advised — B wrong); gentle moisturizers/emollients help the barrier (C wrong); tobacco is not a recognized rosacea trigger (D wrong).

## Q6 — Rosacea (p. 533)
A patient presents to your office and reports flushing from erythema. Which one of the following topical treatments is most likely to be beneficial?

- A. Azelaic acid.
- B. Brimonidine.
- C. Ivermectin.
- D. Metronidazole (Metrogel).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Brimonidine.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Brimonidine (topical α₂-agonist) causes vasoconstriction and specifically reduces persistent erythema/flushing. Azelaic acid, ivermectin, and metronidazole target the inflammatory papulopustular component, not flushing.

## Q7 — Testosterone Replacement Therapy for Male Hypogonadism (p. 543)
A 70-year-old man with well-controlled hypertension and hyperlipidemia presents to discuss persistently decreased sex drive and erectile dysfunction despite as-needed sildenafil. Fasting total testosterone is 250 ng/dL (8.68 nmol/L). If testosterone deficiency is confirmed on repeat measurement, which one of the following is an appropriate next step?

- A. Obtain luteinizing hormone and hematocrit levels.
- B. Tell the patient TRT is contraindicated due to his cardiovascular risk factors.
- C. Tell the patient TRT is unlikely to improve his sexual function.
- D. Administer the ADAM questionnaire to confirm the diagnosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Obtain LH and hematocrit.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Once low testosterone is confirmed, check LH (± FSH) to localize primary vs secondary hypogonadism and a baseline hematocrit (erythrocytosis is the key TRT safety parameter). TRT isn't contraindicated by CV risk factors alone (B), it does improve libido/erectile function (C), and the ADAM questionnaire is sensitive but nonspecific — used to prompt testing, not confirm diagnosis (D).

## Q8 — Testosterone Replacement Therapy (p. 543)
The TRAVERSE study evaluated the cardiovascular safety of testosterone replacement therapy. Which one of the following statements about the study and its findings is correct?

- A. The incidence of atrial fibrillation was similar between the treatment and placebo groups.
- B. The incidence of acute kidney injury was similar between the treatment and placebo groups.
- C. The incidence of pulmonary embolism was similar between the treatment and placebo groups.
- D. The incidence of myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular mortality was similar between the treatment and placebo groups.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — MI, stroke, and CV mortality were similar (TRT non-inferior for MACE).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> TRAVERSE found testosterone non-inferior to placebo for major adverse cardiovascular events. However, it signaled *more* atrial fibrillation, pulmonary embolism, and acute kidney injury in the testosterone arm — so A, B, and C ("similar") are incorrect.

## Q9 — Salivary Gland Disorders: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 550)
Which one of the following statements about inflammatory nonsuppurative sialadenitis is **incorrect**?

- A. Bacterial causes are common, and empiric antibiotics are recommended.
- B. A third dose of MMR vaccine is recommended for those at risk of exposure during disease outbreaks.
- C. Viral causes may include mumps virus, SARS-CoV-2, Epstein-Barr virus, adenovirus, and HIV.
- D. Sjögren syndrome and systemic lupus erythematosus may present with salivary gland edema or xerostomia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — "Bacterial causes are common, empiric antibiotics recommended" (this is the INCORRECT statement).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Nonsuppurative sialadenitis is predominantly viral or autoimmune, not bacterial, so empiric antibiotics are not indicated (that's suppurative disease). B, C, and D are all true statements.

## Q10 — Salivary Gland Disorders (p. 550)
Which one of the following statements about the treatment of acute suppurative sialadenitis is correct?

- A. The most common bacterial cause is *Streptococcus*.
- B. Treatment starts with sialagogues; antibiotics are only used in patients who do not improve.
- C. If antibiotics are used, oral antibiotics should always be used first.
- D. Intravenous antibiotics are recommended as initial therapy for patients with systemic symptoms of infection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — IV antibiotics for patients with systemic symptoms.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Acute suppurative sialadenitis is usually *Staphylococcus aureus* (A wrong). It requires antibiotics plus hydration, sialagogues, warm compresses, and massage — not sialagogues alone (B wrong). Systemically ill patients warrant IV antibiotics rather than mandatory oral-first therapy (C wrong).

## Q18 — Practice Guidelines: Management of Bipolar Disorder — VA/DoD (p. 585)
A 21-year-old with bipolar disorder presents with depressive symptoms; 3 weeks ago he stopped olanzapine (Zyprexa) because of weight gain. Per the VA/DoD guideline, which one of the following medications is recommended as monotherapy to prevent future episodes of bipolar depression in this patient?

- B. Ketamine.
- C. Lamotrigine.
- D. Quetiapine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Lamotrigine.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Lamotrigine has the strongest evidence for preventing the *depressive* pole of bipolar disorder and is well tolerated (no weight gain — fitting this patient). Antidepressant monotherapy (fluoxetine) risks mood switching and is avoided; ketamine is for acute/refractory depression, not maintenance; quetiapine works but caused the weight-related discontinuation pattern he's trying to avoid. **⚠️ Verify** (quetiapine is the tempting distractor).
