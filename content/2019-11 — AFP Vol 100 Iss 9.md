## Q1 — Care of the Military Veteran: Selected Health Issues (p. 544)

Which one of the following statements about posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in military veterans is correct?

- A. Only veterans who have been exposed to combat meet criteria for PTSD.
- B. Benzodiazepines are first-line therapy for PTSD-associated anxiety.
- C. Several nonpharmacologic therapies produce meaningful clinical improvement in people with PTSD.
- D. PTSD is a generally self-limiting condition that resolves quickly.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Several nonpharmacologic therapies produce meaningful improvement in PTSD.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Trauma-focused psychotherapies (prolonged exposure, CPT, EMDR) are first-line. Non-combat trauma also qualifies (A wrong); **benzodiazepines are harmful/avoided** in PTSD (B wrong); PTSD is often **chronic** (D wrong).

## Q2 — Care of the Military Veteran (p. 544)

Which one of the following statements about military sexual trauma is correct?

- A. Physicians should refer veterans to be screened for military sexual trauma.
- B. Veterans can receive military sexual trauma-related care from the Veterans Administration even if they are not otherwise eligible for Veterans Administration care.
- C. Most veterans with a history of military sexual trauma reported it while in the military and have already received some form of therapy.
- D. Male veterans are twice as likely to have experienced military sexual trauma as female veterans.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Veterans can receive MST-related VA care even if otherwise ineligible for VA care.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> MST care is provided free regardless of eligibility. The VA **universally screens** (no referral needed, A); MST is **underreported** during service (C wrong); **female** veterans are far more likely than male veterans to report MST (D reversed, though absolute male numbers are significant).

## Q3 — Care of the Military Veteran (p. 544)

A 43-year-old Iraq war veteran presents with a history of combat-related traumatic brain injury, PTSD, and chronic back pain. Which one of the following statements about this patient's treatment is correct?

- A. Any pain medicine prescribed should be initiated at a higher dose than in patients without a traumatic brain injury.
- B. Tricyclic antidepressants are first-line therapy.
- C. Long-acting opioids are the first-line treatment option for the patient's chronic pain.
- D. Because of the patient's traumatic brain injury, the likelihood of a positive response to cognitive behavior therapy for PTSD is decreased by 50%.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Comorbid TBI reduces the likelihood of a positive CBT response for PTSD.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Medications should be started at **lower** doses with TBI (A reversed); TCAs aren't first-line for this triad (B); **long-acting opioids are not first-line** chronic-pain therapy (C wrong). By elimination, the comorbid TBI dampening CBT response is the intended answer. *(Teaching nuance: the precise "50%" figure is article-specific; confirmed by official key.)*

## Q4 — Stuttering: Understanding and Treating a Common Disability (p. 556)

Which one of the following statements about stuttering is correct?

- A. Stressful situations can exacerbate disfluencies.
- B. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends routinely screening for speech disorders.
- C. Specific speech patterns can predict which children will develop lifelong disfluency.
- D. Referral to a speech-language pathologist is not recommended in children younger than seven years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Stress can worsen disfluencies.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Anxiety/stress increase stuttering. The USPSTF gives an **I statement** on speech-disorder screening (B wrong); no speech pattern reliably predicts persistence (C wrong); **early referral** (even <7 years) is encouraged (D wrong).

## Q5 — Stuttering (p. 556)

Which one of the following strategies is effective for treating stuttering in young children?

- A. Using secondary strategies, such as word avoidance.
- B. Delaying therapy to allow compensatory changes in the brain to occur.
- C. Training parents to increase fluency-enhancing behaviors.
- D. Pharmacologic therapies.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Training parents to increase fluency-enhancing behaviors.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Parent-administered programs (e.g., the Lidcombe approach) are effective in young children. Word avoidance is a maladaptive **secondary** behavior (A); delaying therapy isn't advised (B); no drug is established for stuttering (D).

## Q6 — Stuttering (p. 556)

Which one of the following is an appropriate role for family physicians in the management of stuttering?

- A. Counseling parents to fill in missing words to help the child correct the speech.
- B. Identifying and treating associated social anxiety.
- C. Emphasizing the psychological etiology of stuttering.
- D. Ensuring that a 12-month waiting period has passed before referral for speech therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Identifying and treating associated social anxiety.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Stuttering commonly carries social anxiety the physician can address. Don't finish a child's words (A); stuttering is **neurodevelopmental**, not primarily psychological (C); **don't delay** referral (D wrong).

## Q7 — Hidradenitis Suppurativa: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 562)

Which one of the following lifestyle changes should physicians recommend to decrease the frequency of hidradenitis suppurativa flare-ups?

- A. Smoking cessation.
- B. Weight loss if overweight.
- C. Wearing loose-fitting clothing to prevent friction.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Smoking and obesity strongly drive hidradenitis suppurativa, and friction worsens flares — so smoking cessation, weight loss, and loose clothing are all recommended.

## Q8 — Hidradenitis Suppurativa (p. 562)

Which one of the following therapies is a tumor necrosis factor alpha inhibitor with the best evidence for the treatment of moderate to severe hidradenitis suppurativa?

- A. Etanercept (Enbrel).
- B. Adalimumab (Humira).
- C. Infliximab (Remicade).
- D. Apremilast (Otezla).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Adalimumab (Humira).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Adalimumab is FDA-approved** and has the best evidence for moderate-severe HS. Etanercept is ineffective for HS; infliximab is off-label; apremilast is a **PDE4** inhibitor, not a TNF-α inhibitor.

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibitors vs. Other Antihypertensive Drug Classes (p. 540)

A 45-year-old man has a blood pressure consistently greater than 150/100 mm Hg with no diabetes or secondary causes. Which one of the following would be a first-line treatment to reduce stroke and heart-failure hospitalizations?

- A. Losartan (Cozaar).
- B. Lisinopril.
- C. Hydrochlorothiazide.
- D. Metoprolol.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Hydrochlorothiazide (a thiazide diuretic).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The review favored **first-line thiazides** over RAS inhibitors for reducing stroke and heart-failure events. Beta blockers aren't first-line for uncomplicated hypertension (D).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Oral H₁ Antihistamines as Add-on Therapy to Topical Treatment for Eczema (p. 541)

A four-year-old presents with a scaly pruritic rash in the bilateral antecubital fossae and back of the neck, with a family history of atopy and no infection. Which one of the following would be an appropriate therapy?

- A. Topical corticosteroids.
- B. Oral H₁ antihistamines.
- C. Topical antihistamines.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Topical corticosteroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This is atopic dermatitis — **topical corticosteroids + emollients** are first-line. Oral H₁ antihistamines add **no benefit** as adjuncts (B); topical antihistamines aren't recommended (sensitization risk, C).

## Q11 — AHRQ Review: The Effectiveness of Outpatient Telehealth Consultations (p. 575)

Which one of the following statements about outpatient telehealth consultations is correct?

- A. Patient satisfaction is generally decreased.
- B. Patients with skin conditions who had telehealth dermatology consultations recovered faster.
- C. Telehealth has positive effects on some chronic disease clinical outcomes.
- D. Cost savings from telehealth are unrelated to saving patients travel and time.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Telehealth has positive effects on some chronic disease clinical outcomes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Benefit is clearest for **chronic disease management** (e.g., diabetes, heart failure). Satisfaction is generally **high** (A wrong); teledermatology doesn't clearly speed recovery (B); much of the cost benefit **comes from** reduced travel/time (D wrong).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Pilonidal Disease Management — ASCRS (p. 582)

According to the ASCRS, which one of the following statements about nonoperative treatment of pilonidal disease is correct?

- A. Frequent shaving around the gluteal cleft is ineffective.
- B. A single treatment of laser hair removal is painless and effective.
- C. Phenol application into the cyst and tracts in addition to hair removal and cyst curettage can result in disease resolution.
- D. Fibrin glue should be used when pilonidal disease presents with an abscess.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Phenol application plus hair removal and curettage can resolve disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Phenol injection with hair control/curettage is an effective nonoperative option. Hair removal (including **laser**, which needs multiple sessions, B) **helps** (A wrong); fibrin glue is **not** for an acute abscess, which needs incision and drainage (D wrong).
