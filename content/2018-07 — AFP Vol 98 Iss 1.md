## Q1 — Acute Appendicitis: Efficient Diagnosis and Management (p. 25)

Which one of the following statements about antibiotics as a sole treatment for appendicitis is correct?

- A. It is appropriate only for patients with perforated appendicitis.
- B. It is indicated only for patients who are not surgical candidates.
- C. Most patients with uncomplicated appendicitis treated with antibiotics do not require surgery.
- D. Antibiotics are an option for adults but not for children with uncomplicated appendicitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Most patients with uncomplicated appendicitis treated with antibiotics avoid surgery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Antibiotics-first is an option for **uncomplicated** appendicitis (most avoid appendectomy, though some recur); it's used in adults **and** selected children (D wrong) and isn't limited to nonsurgical candidates or perforated cases (A, B wrong).

## Q2 — Acute Appendicitis (p. 25)

Which one of the following signs and symptoms best rules in acute appendicitis in children?

- A. Right lower quadrant pain.
- B. Abdominal rigidity.
- C. Absent or decreased bowel sounds.
- D. Radiation of periumbilical pain to right lower quadrant.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Absent or decreased bowel sounds (highest positive likelihood ratio in children).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Per the article's likelihood-ratio data, decreased/absent bowel sounds best "rules in" pediatric appendicitis. RLQ pain and migration are sensitive but less specific (A, D); rigidity is helpful but lower LR here (B). *(Confirmed by official key.)*

## Q3 — Allergy Testing: Common Questions and Answers (p. 34)

Which one of the following statements about allergy testing is correct?

- A. To exclude mast cell activation syndrome, tryptase levels should be measured in patients with anaphylactic reactions to insect stings.
- B. Immunoglobulin E (IgE)-specific testing should be used to test for penicillin allergy.
- C. Patients with inhalant reactions should be treated with antihistamines before skin testing.
- D. Oral food challenges should be completed at home.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Measure tryptase to evaluate for mast cell activation in insect-sting anaphylaxis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Penicillin allergy is evaluated by **skin testing** (IgE serology is unreliable, B); **antihistamines must be stopped** before skin testing (C wrong); oral food challenges are done **under medical supervision**, never at home (D wrong).

## Q4 — Allergy Testing (p. 34)

Which one of the following patient groups requires immunotherapy for insect stings?

- A. Persons with large local reactions.
- B. Persons younger than 17 years with systemic cutaneous reactions.
- C. Patients with local venom reactions.
- D. Persons 17 years or older with systemic cutaneous reactions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Adults (17 years or older) with systemic cutaneous reactions.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Venom immunotherapy is recommended for **adults with systemic (even cutaneous-only) reactions**; **children <16–17 with cutaneous-only systemic reactions have a favorable prognosis and do NOT require it** (so B is incorrect by board-standard teaching). Large local and local venom reactions don't warrant VIT (A, C). **⚠️ The official key prints "B" for this item — an option-order scramble; the clinically correct answer is D.**

## Q5 — Allergy Testing (p. 34)

Which one of the following testing methods should be used to diagnose contact dermatitis?

- A. IgE-specific testing.
- B. Patch testing.
- C. Oral food challenge.
- D. Elimination diet.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Patch testing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Allergic contact dermatitis is a **type IV (delayed) T-cell** reaction diagnosed by **patch testing** — not IgE-based tests, which assess immediate (type I) allergy (A, C, D).

## Q6 — Evaluating Medical Decision-Making Capacity in Practice (p. 40)

Which one of the following tools has been validated in a large study and is considered one of the best instruments available online to assess a patient's capacity to make decisions about undergoing a treatment or procedure?

- A. Aid to Capacity Evaluation.
- B. Hopkins Competency Assessment Test.
- C. Mini-Mental State Examination.
- D. Montreal Cognitive Assessment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — The Aid to Capacity Evaluation (ACE).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The ACE is well-validated, freely available, and decision-specific. The MMSE and MoCA screen **cognition**, not decision-making capacity (C, D).

## Q7 — Evaluating Medical Decision-Making Capacity (p. 40)

Which one of the following population groups has the highest prevalence of impaired medical decision-making capacity?

- A. Persons with learning disabilities.
- B. Inpatients on medical wards.
- C. Healthy older adults.
- D. Patients with Alzheimer disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Persons with learning (intellectual) disabilities.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Per the article's prevalence data, people with learning disabilities have the highest rate of impaired capacity. (Alzheimer disease and medical inpatients also have high rates; healthy older adults generally retain capacity. Confirmed by official key.)

## Q8 — Evaluating Medical Decision-Making Capacity (p. 40)

Which one of the four components of medical decision-making capacity is assessed by the question: "Why do you think your doctor has recommended this (a particular treatment or test) for you?"

- A. Understanding the benefits and risks of, and alternatives to, a proposed treatment or intervention.
- B. An appreciation of how the benefits, risks, and alternatives apply to the patient's own situation.
- C. Reasoning in making a decision.
- D. Communicating a choice.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Appreciation (how the information applies to the patient's own situation).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Asking why the doctor recommended it for *them* probes **appreciation**. The four capacity components are understanding, **appreciation**, reasoning, and communicating a choice.

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Procalcitonin to Guide Antibiotic Therapy in Acute Respiratory Infections (p. 20)

Which one of the following statements about procalcitonin-guided antibiotic therapy in patients with acute respiratory infection is correct?

- A. It does not improve mortality compared with routine treatments.
- B. It reduces the overall exposure to antibiotics compared with routine treatments.
- C. It does not improve the risk of antibiotic-related adverse effects compared with routine treatments.
- D. It significantly decreases the risk of treatment failure—including the need for hospitalization and patients showing symptoms at follow-up—compared with routine treatments.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Procalcitonin guidance reduces overall antibiotic exposure.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> It cuts antibiotic use **and** reduces adverse effects (C wrong) and was associated with **lower** mortality (A wrong) without increasing treatment failure (D overstates a "decrease in failure").

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Individual Behavioral Counseling for Smoking Cessation (p. 21)

A 26-year-old man has been smoking for five years but would like to quit. Which one of the following statements about the use of counseling therapy to aid in smoking cessation is correct?

- A. Individual behavioral counseling by a trained therapist is more effective than usual care.
- B. Individual behavioral counseling is likely to add to the effect of pharmacotherapy.
- C. The Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality Treating Tobacco Use and Dependence guideline recommends both pharmacotherapy and counseling to aid in smoking cessation.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Individual counseling beats usual care, **adds** to pharmacotherapy, and the AHRQ guideline recommends **combining** counseling with medication — the most effective approach.

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Bleeding with Anticoagulants — ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway (p. 57)

Which one of the following statements about managing bleeding episodes in patients taking anticoagulants is correct?

- A. At least two of the following factors are needed to qualify bleeding as a major event: critical site location, hemodynamic instability, and anemia requiring transfusion.
- B. In patients with nonmajor bleeding, discontinuation of anticoagulants is always necessary.
- C. All patients with nonmajor bleeding should receive intravenous vitamin K with a reversal agent.
- D. Major life-threatening bleeding or bleeding at a critical site warrants a reversal agent.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Major/life-threatening or critical-site bleeding warrants a reversal agent.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Any one** major-bleeding criterion qualifies (A wrong); nonmajor bleeding doesn't **always** require stopping anticoagulation (B) or reversal agents/IV vitamin K (C wrong).
