## Q1 — Heat-Related Illnesses (p. 482)

Which one of the following options lists major risk factors for heat-related illness?

- A. Mild to moderate exercise, exposure to high humidity, and wearing extra gear such as football pads.
- B. Strenuous exercise, poor physical fitness, and exposure to high temperature.
- C. Moderate exercise, high level of physical fitness, and exposure to high temperature.
- D. Mild exercise, exposure to high humidity, and wearing loose-fitting clothing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Strenuous exercise, poor physical fitness, and high ambient temperature.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Heat illness risk rises with **exertion intensity, deconditioning, heat/humidity, dehydration, and occlusive gear**. Good fitness and loose clothing are *protective* (C, D).

## Q2 — Heat-Related Illnesses (p. 482)

Which one of the following syndromes is a type of mild heat-related illness?

- A. Exercise-associated collapse.
- B. Exertional heat stroke.
- C. Heat exhaustion.
- D. Exercise-associated muscle cramps.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Exercise-associated muscle cramps.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Heat cramps are a **mild** heat illness. Heat **exhaustion** is moderate, and heat **stroke** (CNS dysfunction + core temp >40°C) is the severe, life-threatening form (B, C).

## Q3 — Heat-Related Illnesses (p. 482)

Which one of the following is the most effective treatment for heat stroke?

- A. Ice water immersion.
- B. Misting with water.
- C. Ice packs on major arteries.
- D. Application of cool, wet towels.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Ice (cold) water immersion.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Cold-water immersion** achieves the fastest cooling and lowest mortality in exertional heat stroke ("cool first, transport second"). Misting, ice packs, and towels cool far more slowly (B, C, D).

## Q4 — Kidney Stones: Treatment and Prevention (p. 490)

A patient is diagnosed with renal colic due to an 8-mm kidney stone. Which one of the following is the most appropriate management strategy?

- A. Corticosteroids to hasten expulsion.
- B. Administration of an alpha blocker.
- C. Increasing fluid intake to aid stone passage.
- D. Opioids as the first-line medication for pain management.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — An alpha blocker (medical expulsive therapy).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For stones ~5–10 mm, an **alpha blocker** (tamsulosin) aids passage. **NSAIDs are first-line analgesia** (not opioids, D); aggressive fluids don't speed passage (C); steroids aren't used alone for expulsion (A).

## Q5 — Kidney Stones (p. 490)

A patient with renal colic is stable and does not have any conditions requiring immediate referral to a urologist. Which one of the following is recommended?

- A. Standard radiography is the imaging modality of choice.
- B. Recurrent stones should not be analyzed because they can be assumed to have the same composition as the initial stone.
- C. Parathyroid hormone should be assessed only if the serum calcium level is low.
- D. Ultrasonography should be performed within 14 days.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Ultrasonography should be performed within 14 days.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Ultrasound (or low-dose CT) is preferred over plain radiography (A); **recurrent stones should be analyzed** (composition can change, B); check PTH when **calcium is high** (suspecting hyperparathyroidism), not low (C).

## Q6 — Kidney Stones (p. 490)

Which one of the following should be advised to prevent recurrence of kidney stones?

- A. Increasing daily fluid intake.
- B. Increasing intake of animal protein.
- C. Avoidance of citrates, especially lemon juice.
- D. Increasing intake of carbonated drinks acidified with phosphoric acid.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Increase daily fluid intake (aim for >2–2.5 L urine/day).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> High fluid intake is the strongest prevention. Reduce **animal protein** and sodium (B wrong); **citrate (lemon juice) inhibits** stone formation — encourage it (C reversed); phosphoric-acid colas **promote** stones (D wrong).

## Q7 — Common Breast Problems (p. 505)

A 42-year-old woman presents with a small, mobile lump on her left breast. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. A clinical breast examination is of minimal value in this setting.
- B. The description suggests a malignancy.
- C. Mammography is typically performed in this setting.
- D. A Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System score of 2 on mammography warrants a core needle biopsy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Mammography is typically performed for a palpable breast mass at this age.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A palpable lump in a woman ≥40 is worked up with **mammography** (± ultrasound). Clinical exam is valuable (A wrong); a mobile mass is more often **benign** (B); a **BI-RADS 2 is benign** — no biopsy (D wrong).

## Q8 — Common Breast Problems (p. 505)

A 40-year-old woman has a two-month history of right breast pain unrelated to menses. Which one of the following treatments should be tried first?

- A. Topical diclofenac.
- B. Dietary modifications.
- C. Vitamin E.
- D. Evening primrose oil.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Topical diclofenac (a topical NSAID).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Topical NSAIDs are first-line and effective for mastalgia. **Vitamin E and evening primrose oil lack proven benefit** (C, D); dietary changes have weak evidence (B).

## Q9 — Common Breast Problems (p. 505)

A 29-year-old woman who delivered her first child one year ago stopped breastfeeding two months ago but now has intermittent leaking from her breasts. Which one of the following should you recommend?

- A. Nipple expression.
- B. Mammography.
- C. Duct excision.
- D. Human chorionic gonadotropin testing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Human chorionic gonadotropin (pregnancy) testing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Persistent galactorrhea after weaning warrants ruling out **pregnancy** (β-hCG), plus prolactin and TSH. **Nipple expression perpetuates** secretion (A); mammography/duct excision aren't first steps for physiologic galactorrhea (B, C).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Behavioral Weight Loss Interventions (p. 515)

A 32-year-old woman with a body mass index of 34 kg/m² and otherwise normal findings presents for her annual visit. Based on the USPSTF recommendation statement, which one of the following interventions should you recommend?

- A. Offer or refer her to combined pharmacotherapy and behavioral intervention.
- B. Offer to start pharmacotherapy alone.
- C. Refer her for weight loss surgery.
- D. Offer or refer her to an intensive, multicomponent behavioral intervention.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Offer/refer to an intensive, multicomponent behavioral intervention.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF (grade **B**) recommends **intensive behavioral interventions** for adults with obesity (BMI ≥30). Pharmacotherapy and surgery are later-step options, not the recommended first intervention here (A, B, C).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Child Passenger Safety — AAP (p. 525)

Which one of the following statements about the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) best practice recommendations for child car safety is correct?

- A. Children 12 years and older may ride in the front seat as long as the seatbelt fits properly.
- B. Children who are small for their age should still be evaluated based on age rather than size.
- C. Infants and toddlers should ride in a rear-facing car seat until they reach the highest weight or height allowed by the car seat manufacturer.
- D. Booster seats can be discontinued once a child is 8 years of age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Keep infants/toddlers rear-facing until they reach the seat's height/weight limit.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Progression is **by size, not age** (B reversed); children should stay in the **back seat through age 12** (A wrong); boosters continue until the seat belt fits (~4'9", often well past age 8, D wrong).
