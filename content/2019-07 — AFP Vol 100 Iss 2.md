## Q1 — Erythema Multiforme: Recognition and Management (p. 82)

Which one of the following best describes a typical erythema multiforme lesion?

- A. Atypical macular target lesions with central dusky erythema.
- B. Pruritic, sharply demarcated papules.
- C. A target lesion with a dark center, surrounded by a lighter pink ring, both of which are surrounded by a red ring.
- D. Scaly, erythematous plaques following herald patch formation on the trunk.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A three-zone "typical target": dark center, pale ring, outer red ring.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Classic EM target lesions have **three concentric zones**. Atypical *macular* targets (A) point toward Stevens-Johnson spectrum; herald patch + trunk plaques describe **pityriasis rosea** (D).

## Q2 — Erythema Multiforme (p. 82)

Which one of the following infections is most commonly associated with erythema multiforme?

- A. Mycoplasma pneumoniae.
- B. Vulvovaginal candidiasis.
- C. Hepatitis C.
- D. Herpes simplex virus type 1.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Herpes simplex virus type 1.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **HSV (especially HSV-1)** is the leading trigger of recurrent EM. Mycoplasma is the next most common (more so in children); recurrent EM may warrant suppressive antiviral therapy.

## Q3 — Acupuncture for Pain (p. 89)

Which one of the following statements about acupuncture for low back pain is correct?

- A. There is a clear, clinically significant benefit compared with sham acupuncture for acute low back pain.
- B. There is a clear, clinically significant benefit in the short term compared with sham acupuncture for chronic low back pain.
- C. There is no benefit compared with no treatment for chronic low back pain.
- D. It is not effective for acute or chronic low back pain based on available studies.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Short-term benefit over sham acupuncture for chronic low back pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Evidence supports modest **short-term** improvement in chronic LBP versus sham, and benefit versus no treatment (C wrong). Evidence for **acute** LBP is weak (A overstated).

## Q4 — Acupuncture for Pain (p. 89)

Which one of the following statements about acupuncture to treat headache is correct?

- A. It is only effective for migraine headache prophylaxis.
- B. It is not cost-effective due to the need for multiple treatments.
- C. It is effective for tension headache but not migraine.
- D. It is somewhat effective for both migraine and tension headache when compared with sham acupuncture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Somewhat effective for both migraine and tension-type headache vs. sham.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Acupuncture reduces frequency of both **migraine and tension-type** headaches and can be cost-effective as prophylaxis (A, B, C wrong).

## Q5 — Hearing Loss in Adults: Differential Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 98)

A 65-year-old woman presents for her initial Medicare visit with no symptoms of hearing loss. Which one of the following statements about screening for hearing loss in this patient is correct?

- A. She should be screened with audiometry.
- B. She should be screened with a self-administered questionnaire.
- C. She should be screened with the whispered voice test.
- D. There is insufficient evidence to assess the balance of benefits and harms of screening for hearing loss in asymptomatic adults 50 years or older.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — The USPSTF finds insufficient evidence (I statement) for screening asymptomatic adults ≥50.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Routine screening of **asymptomatic** older adults isn't established as beneficial. Evaluate (audiometry/whispered voice/questionnaire) when patients **report** hearing concerns.

## Q6 — Hearing Loss in Adults (p. 98)

Which one of the following is the most common type of sudden sensorineural hearing loss?

- A. Idiopathic.
- B. Infectious.
- C. Traumatic.
- D. Vascular.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Idiopathic.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The vast majority of sudden sensorineural hearing loss is **idiopathic** — treat promptly with corticosteroids and obtain MRI to exclude retrocochlear pathology (e.g., vestibular schwannoma).

## Q7 — Hearing Loss in Adults (p. 98)

Which one of the following statements about the use of cochlear implants in older adults is correct?

- A. Cochlear implants are the preferred treatment for patients with middle ear disease.
- B. Cochlear implants do not improve speech perception.
- C. Cochlear implants should be considered for patients with cognitive impairment that will make it difficult to manage a conventional hearing aid.
- D. Cochlear implants are used for moderate to profound bilateral sensorineural hearing loss.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Used for moderate-to-profound bilateral sensorineural hearing loss.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cochlear implants treat severe sensorineural loss inadequately helped by hearing aids — they **improve speech perception** (B wrong), don't treat conductive/middle-ear disease (A wrong), and require the ability to participate in rehabilitation (C wrong).

## Q8 — Practice Guidelines: Interpregnancy Care — ACOG and SMFM (p. 121)

According to guidelines on interpregnancy care from the ACOG and the SMFM, which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Interpregnancy intervals shorter than 24 months should be avoided.
- B. Women at high risk of preterm birth should be screened for asymptomatic genitourinary infections in the interpregnancy period.
- C. Bariatric surgery is not recommended for any woman in the interpregnancy period.
- D. Smokers should be offered smoking cessation support before pregnancy through behavioral interventions, pharmacotherapy, or both.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Offer smokers cessation support before pregnancy (behavioral, pharmacotherapy, or both).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The recommended minimum interval is avoiding **<6 months** (ideally ~18 months), not <24 (A overstated); routine screening for **asymptomatic** GU infections isn't recommended (B); bariatric surgery **can** be appropriate for selected women (C wrong).
