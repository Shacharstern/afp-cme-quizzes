## Q1 — Osteoporosis (p. 238)
Which one of the following groups should be screened for osteoporosis according to the USPSTF?

- A. Men older than 50 years with a traumatic fracture.
- B. All men older than 70 years.
- C. All women 65 years and older.
- D. Screening should be based on risk factors alone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — All women 65 years and older.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF: screen all women ≥65, and younger postmenopausal women at increased risk (by a tool such as FRAX). There is no recommendation to screen all men; the USPSTF found insufficient evidence for men.

## Q2 — Osteoporosis (p. 238)
Which one of the following statements about pharmacologic treatments for osteoporosis is correct?

- A. Prolonged therapy with parathyroid hormone analogues is associated with osteonecrosis of the jaw.
- B. Bisphosphonates should be taken with food to improve their absorption.
- C. A drug holiday from oral bisphosphonates should be considered after five years of treatment.
- D. The typical length of a drug holiday is 10 years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A drug holiday should be considered after five years of oral bisphosphonates.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PTH analogues carry an osteosarcoma (not ONJ) signal; bisphosphonates must be taken on an empty stomach; a holiday typically lasts 1–3 years, not 10.

## Q3 — Asthma vs COPD (p. 247)
A 63-year-old with extensive smoking history, FEV₁/FVC 60%; post-albuterol FEV₁ improves by 5% and 170 mL. Most appropriate diagnosis?

- A. Asthma.
- B. COPD.
- C. Asthma and COPD.
- D. Does not meet criteria for asthma or COPD.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — COPD.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Fixed obstruction (FEV₁/FVC <0.70) with a *non-significant* bronchodilator response — reversibility requires both ≥12% **and** ≥200 mL. 5%/170 mL fails both thresholds, so this is COPD, not asthma.

## Q4 — Asthma vs COPD (p. 247)
A 66-year-old diagnosed with asthma and COPD. Which statement about treatment is correct?

- A. The patient should receive the pneumococcal vaccine at 70 years of age.
- B. Because the patient already has pulmonary damage from smoking, smoking cessation is not necessary.
- C. The patient should primarily follow existing pharmacotherapy guidelines for asthma.
- D. Pulmonary rehabilitation is unlikely to be helpful for this patient.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Primarily follow asthma pharmacotherapy guidelines.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In asthma–COPD overlap, ICS-containing therapy (asthma framework) is started first — never LABA/LAMA alone, because of asthma's steroid-responsive inflammation and exacerbation risk. Smoking cessation and pulmonary rehab remain essential.

## Q5 — Glaucoma (p. 253)
Chronic use of which medication is a risk factor for primary open-angle glaucoma?

- A. Corticosteroids.
- B. Beta blockers.
- C. Anticholinergics.
- D. Histamine H₂ blockers.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Corticosteroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Chronic steroids (any route) raise intraocular pressure → open-angle glaucoma. Anticholinergics are the classic precipitant of *angle-closure* glaucoma — a useful contrast pair.

## Q6 — Glaucoma (p. 253)
Which statement about angle-closure glaucoma is correct?

- A. Suspected angle-closure should be referred for ophthalmology evaluation no later than 24 hours.
- B. Myopia is the most important risk factor.
- C. Definitive therapy typically involves surgical intervention to promote aqueous drainage.
- D. Angle-closure is more common than primary open-angle glaucoma in the U.S.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Definitive therapy involves a procedure to promote aqueous drainage.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Laser peripheral iridotomy is definitive. Acute angle closure is an *emergency* (refer immediately, not within 24 h); *hyperopia* (not myopia) is the key risk factor; open-angle glaucoma is far more common.

## Q7 — PCOS (p. 264)
A 26-year-old (G0), irregular periods, 2 years of infertility, BMI 31. Which test helps confirm PCOS?

- A. Prolactin level.
- B. Free testosterone level.
- C. A1C level.
- D. 17-hydroxyprogesterone level.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Free testosterone level.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Free testosterone documents biochemical hyperandrogenism (a Rotterdam criterion). Prolactin and 17-OHP are used to *exclude* mimics (prolactinoma, nonclassic CAH); A1C screens comorbidity, not diagnosis.

## Q8 — PCOS (p. 264)
Which statement about treatment of PCOS is correct?

- A. Higher-dose estrogen (35 mcg ethinyl estradiol) is first-line for oligomenorrhea.
- B. Metformin is first-line drug therapy for hirsutism.
- C. Clomiphene is preferred over letrozole for ovulation induction.
- D. Spironolactone for hirsutism should not be prescribed without effective contraception.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Spironolactone requires effective contraception.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Spironolactone is teratogenic (feminization of a male fetus). Low-dose (not high-dose) estrogen OCPs are first-line; letrozole is now preferred over clomiphene for ovulation induction; metformin is not first-line for hirsutism.

## Q9 — PTSD (p. 273)
Which statement about PTSD is correct?

- A. Direct exposure to trauma is required for diagnosis.
- B. One-half of people who experience significant trauma will develop PTSD.
- C. PTSD rarely occurs with other anxiety disorders.
- D. Substance use disorder is a common comorbid condition.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Substance use disorder is a common comorbidity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Diagnosis allows *witnessed* or *learned-of* trauma (not only direct exposure); only a minority who experience trauma develop PTSD; comorbid anxiety, depression, and SUD are common.

## Q10 — PTSD (p. 273)
Which statement about psychotherapy for PTSD is correct?

- A. Psychotherapy does not seem effective for prevention after trauma.
- B. Psychotherapy is primarily used as an adjunct to medication.
- C. Non–trauma-focused therapy is as effective as trauma-focused psychotherapy.
- D. Internet-based cognitive behavior therapy has evidence of benefit.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Internet-based CBT has evidence of benefit.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Trauma-focused psychotherapy is first-line (more effective than non-trauma-focused) and stands on its own, not merely as a medication adjunct. Internet-delivered CBT shows benefit, expanding access.

## Q11 — Pulmonary Nodules (p. 282)
Which patient should be screened for lung cancer per USPSTF?

- A. A 63-year-old never-smoker with a family history of COPD.
- B. A 58-year-old with a 24-pack-year history who quit at age 40.
- C. A 36-year-old who vapes daily with a chronic cough.
- D. A 62-year-old with a 40-pack-year history who quit at age 60.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — 62-year-old, 40 pack-years, quit at 60.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF criteria: age 50–80, ≥20 pack-years, current smoker **or quit within the past 15 years**. D meets all. B fails — quitting at 40 (18 years ago) is beyond the 15-year window.

## Q12 — Pulmonary Nodules (p. 282)
Which nodule characteristic suggests higher malignancy risk?

- A. Concentric calcifications.
- B. 3 mm total diameter.
- C. Ground-glass appearance.
- D. Located in the lower lobe.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ground-glass appearance.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Ground-glass/part-solid nodules carry higher malignancy risk. Concentric/central calcification and small size (3 mm) favor benignity; *upper*-lobe location (not lower) is the higher-risk site.

## Q13 — Cochrane: Antiplatelet Agents for DVT (p. 231)
Adding an antiplatelet agent to standard DVT treatment — which statement is correct?

- A. It decreases mortality.
- B. It decreases progression to pulmonary embolism.
- C. It does not increase the risk of bleeding or other adverse effects.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — It does not increase the risk of bleeding or other adverse effects.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The Cochrane review found no clear mortality or PE-progression benefit from added antiplatelet therapy, but also no significant increase in bleeding. Flagged ⚠️ only because the "no proven benefit" framing is easy to misread — the verified answer is C.

## Q14 — Cochrane: Foot Orthoses for Flat Feet in Children (p. 232)
Which statement about flat feet in children is correct?

- A. Among children 3–6 years, up to 70% have flat feet.
- B. Customized and prefabricated orthoses do not improve pain, function, or QoL scores.
- C. Asymptomatic flat feet in children should not be routinely treated.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Flat feet are developmentally normal and common (up to 70% at ages 3–6), orthoses lack evidence of benefit on pain/function/QoL, and asymptomatic flat feet need no routine treatment.

## Q15 — Lown Right Care: ADHD Overdiagnosis (p. 292)
Which statement about ADHD in children is correct?

- A. Diagnostic criteria have become more stringent over time.
- B. The frequency and severity of symptoms have increased.
- C. Few children with ADHD have comorbid learning or psychiatric disorders.
- D. An ADHD diagnosis is more likely in children who are young for their school grade.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — More likely in children young for their grade.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The "relative age effect": the youngest children in a grade are more often (over)diagnosed, normal immaturity mistaken for ADHD. Criteria have actually *loosened* over time, and comorbid conditions are common.

## Q16 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: Screening for OSA (p. 297)
Which population does the USPSTF recommendation statement on OSA address?

- A. People undergoing workplace fitness-for-duty evaluations.
- B. People presenting with symptoms of or concerns about OSA.
- C. People who are not aware of their symptoms or do not report symptoms as being a concern.
- D. People with acute conditions that could trigger OSA (e.g., stroke).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Asymptomatic adults who do not report symptoms.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The USPSTF statement applies to *asymptomatic* general-population adults — for whom it issued an **I statement** (insufficient evidence to screen). People who already have symptoms (B) fall outside the screening question and are evaluated diagnostically. Flagged ⚠️ because the stem's "should be screened" wording is a known trap.

## Q17 — Point-of-Care Guide: Appendicitis in Children (p. 301)
Which statement about appendicitis in children/adolescents with abdominal pain is correct?

- A. Appendicitis is more common in females.
- B. Appendectomy without appendicitis is more common in males.
- C. Abdominal pain with cough or percussion is not suggestive of appendicitis.
- D. Pain localizing to the right lower quadrant is suggestive of appendicitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — RLQ pain localization is suggestive.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> RLQ pain raises the likelihood of appendicitis, as does pain with cough/percussion/hopping (peritoneal signs). Appendicitis is slightly more common in males; negative appendectomy is more common in females.

## Q18 — Practice Guidelines: ACIP 2023 Immunization Schedules (p. 319)
Which patient should receive PCV15 or PCV20?

- A. A 56-year-old with hypertension and no prior pneumococcal vaccination.
- B. A 64-year-old with hyperlipidemia and no prior pneumococcal vaccination.
- C. A 69-year-old who received PCV13 and PPSV23 two years ago.
- D. A 71-year-old who received only PPSV23 three years ago.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — 71-year-old who received only PPSV23 three years ago.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Adults ≥65 who previously got *only* PPSV23 may receive PCV20 (or PCV15) ≥1 year later. A and B are <65 with no risk-condition indication; C already completed a PCV13 + PPSV23 series.

## Q19 — Practice Guidelines: VA/DoD Major Depression (p. 323)
A 49-year-old in remission on sertraline with three prior episodes, worried about relapse. Per VA/DoD, which may help avoid relapse?

- A. Acupuncture.
- B. Bibliotherapy.
- C. Cognitive behavior therapy.
- D. Continuing sertraline for one month after resolution of symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Cognitive behavior therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CBT (and mindfulness-based cognitive therapy) reduces relapse, especially with recurrent depression. Continuing an antidepressant only *one month* after remission is far too short — maintenance runs at least 6–12 months (longer with recurrent disease).
