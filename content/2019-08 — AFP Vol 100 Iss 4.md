## Q1 — Health Maintenance in School-Aged Children, Part I (p. 213)

Which one of the following is the definition of obesity for school-aged children?

- A. Body mass index at or above the 85th percentile for age and sex.
- B. Body mass index at or above the 95th percentile for age and sex.
- C. Weight at or above the 85th percentile for age and sex.
- D. Weight at or above the 95th percentile for age and sex.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — BMI at or above the 95th percentile for age and sex.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In children, **obesity = BMI ≥95th percentile** and **overweight = BMI 85th–94th** (for age/sex). Definitions use BMI percentile, not raw weight (C, D wrong).

## Q2 — Health Maintenance in School-Aged Children, Part I (p. 213)

Which one of the following statements about hypertension and dyslipidemia screening in school-aged children is correct?

- A. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommend annual screening for both conditions.
- B. The AAP and USPSTF recommend one-time screening for both conditions between nine and 11 years of age.
- C. The AAP and USPSTF recommend screening for hypertension only in children with risk factors for elevated blood pressure.
- D. The AAP recommends screening for both conditions, but the USPSTF concludes that there is insufficient evidence to assess the balance of benefits and harms of routine screening for either condition.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — The AAP recommends screening; the USPSTF finds insufficient evidence (I statements).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A classic **guideline conflict**: AAP supports universal lipid (ages 9–11) and BP screening, while the USPSTF gives **I statements** for both in asymptomatic children.

## Q3 — Health Maintenance in School-Aged Children, Part I (p. 213)

Which one of the following childhood vaccine series has the lowest completion rate?

- A. Measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR).
- B. Meningococcal.
- C. Tetanus toxoid, reduced diphtheria toxoid, and acellular pertussis (Tdap).
- D. Varicella.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Meningococcal.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Adolescent meningococcal series (especially the **booster/MenB**) has the lowest completion rate, lagging behind well-established childhood vaccines like MMR, varicella, and Tdap.

## Q4 — Health Maintenance in School-Aged Children, Part II (p. 219)

Which one of the following is recommended by the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force?

- A. Screen children 11 years and younger for depression.
- B. Screen children 12 years and older for drug use.
- C. Screen children 12 years and older for alcohol use.
- D. Screen children 12 years and older for depression.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Screen children 12 years and older for depression.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends **depression screening for adolescents 12–18** (B recommendation). Evidence is insufficient for depression screening <12 (A) and for drug/alcohol screening in adolescents (B, C are I statements).

## Q5 — Health Maintenance in School-Aged Children, Part II (p. 219)

Which one of the following should be recommended as part of dietary counseling for school-aged children?

- A. Increase intake of refined grains.
- B. Limit fruits to three times weekly.
- C. Minimize intake of sugary beverages.
- D. Limit fish intake to once weekly.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Minimize intake of sugary beverages.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Reducing sugar-sweetened drinks is core dietary counseling. Children should eat **whole** grains, plenty of fruits/vegetables, and **adequate** fish (A, B, D are unhealthy restrictions/recommendations).

## Q6 — Health Maintenance in School-Aged Children, Part II (p. 219)

Which one of the following is an appropriate recommendation for school-aged children?

- A. Bone-strengthening activities at least five days a week.
- B. Moderate to vigorous physical activity for 60 minutes seven days a week.
- C. Vigorous-intensity aerobic activity at least once a week.
- D. Muscle-strengthening activities at least once a week.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity every day.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Guidelines call for **≥60 minutes daily**, including vigorous aerobic activity and bone- and muscle-strengthening on **≥3 days/week** (so the once-weekly options A, C, D are too infrequent).

## Q7 — Electronic Cigarettes: Common Questions and Answers (p. 227)

Which one of the following is a health benefit of e-cigarettes over traditional cigarettes?

- A. Reduced exposure to non-nicotine toxic chemicals.
- B. No exposure to nicotine.
- C. Children exposed to e-cigarette liquid have less risk of hospital admission than children exposed to cigarettes.
- D. No exposure to heavy metals and toxicants.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Reduced exposure to non-nicotine toxic chemicals.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> e-Cigarettes deliver **fewer combustion toxicants** than cigarettes, but still contain **nicotine and some heavy metals/toxicants** (B, D wrong). Concentrated e-liquid ingestion is **more** dangerous to children (C wrong).

## Q8 — Electronic Cigarettes (p. 227)

Which one of the following accurately describes an outcome of e-cigarette use?

- A. Precise dosing calibration facilitates stepwise decreases in e-cigarette use.
- B. Many people who quit cigarette smoking continue to use e-cigarettes at one year.
- C. Continued e-cigarette use reduces long-term cardiovascular mortality.
- D. e-Cigarettes help prevent teens from trying traditional cigarettes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Many who quit cigarettes with e-cigarettes keep using e-cigarettes at one year.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Sustained dependence is a downside. Nicotine dosing is **inconsistent** (A wrong); long-term cardiovascular benefit isn't established (C wrong); e-cigarettes are a **gateway** that increases teen cigarette initiation (D reversed).

## Q9 — Electronic Cigarettes (p. 227)

Which one of the following statements about e-cigarettes is correct?

- A. Accidental ingestion of e-cigarette liquid is less hazardous than cigarette ingestion.
- B. They are recommended as an alternative to traditional cigarettes in pregnant smokers.
- C. Secondhand exposure may provoke asthma attacks in youth with asthma.
- D. They are approved as smoking cessation devices by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Secondhand e-cigarette aerosol may provoke asthma attacks in youth.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> e-Liquid ingestion is **more** hazardous (concentrated nicotine, A wrong); e-cigarettes are **not** recommended in pregnancy (B wrong) and are **not FDA-approved** cessation devices (D wrong).

## Q10 — Point-of-Care Guides: Identifying Outpatients with Acute Cough at Very Low Risk of Pneumonia (p. 246)

Which one of the following identifies patients with acute cough who are at very low risk of community-acquired pneumonia?

- A. Normal blood pressure and fever higher than 100.5°F (38°C).
- B. Normal vital signs and normal lung examination findings.
- C. Normal vital signs and no history of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
- D. Normal vital signs with C-reactive protein level of 35 mg per L (333.3 nmol per L).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Normal vital signs and a normal lung examination.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Normal vitals (no tachycardia/tachypnea/fever) **plus a normal lung exam** make pneumonia very unlikely — no chest radiograph needed. Fever (A) and elevated CRP (D) raise risk.

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Maintenance Intravenous Fluids in Children — AAP (p. 251)

According to the AAP, which one of the following intravenous maintenance fluids would be most appropriate for a two-year-old child admitted for vomiting?

- A. Normal saline with 5% dextrose and 20 mEq of potassium chloride per liter.
- B. 0.2% sodium chloride solution.
- C. 1/2 normal saline with 5% dextrose.
- D. 1/2 normal saline with 5% dextrose and 20 mEq of potassium chloride per liter.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Isotonic normal saline with 5% dextrose and 20 mEq/L potassium chloride.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The AAP recommends **isotonic** maintenance fluids (with dextrose and appropriate potassium) for children 28 days–18 years to prevent **iatrogenic hyponatremia**. Hypotonic fluids (0.2% or 1/2 NS, B–D) are no longer preferred.
