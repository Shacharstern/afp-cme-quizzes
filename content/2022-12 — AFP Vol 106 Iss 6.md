## Q1 — Antibiotics in Acute URTIs (p. 628)
A 21-year-old with fever and sore throat, no cough/rhinorrhea, no nodes or exudate, negative rapid strep test. Next step?

- A. Immediately start penicillin V.
- B. Provide a delayed amoxicillin prescription.
- C. Recommend symptomatic treatment only.
- D. Send a specimen for throat culture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Symptomatic treatment only.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In **adults**, a negative rapid antigen test does not require confirmatory throat culture (unlike children/adolescents). With a negative test, treat symptomatically — no antibiotics.

## Q2 — Antibiotics in Acute URTIs (p. 628)
For which patient with acute rhinosinusitis is antibiotic treatment most appropriate?

- A. Day 2 with fever 101°F, headache, nasal discharge.
- B. One week of symptoms that were improving, now worsening headache and nasal discharge for three days.
- C. Only mild improvement after seven days.
- D. Bilateral facial pain for two days with purulent discharge.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — "Double sickening": improvement, then worsening.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Antibiotics for acute *bacterial* rhinosinusitis are indicated when symptoms persist ≥10 days without improvement, are severe (≥3–4 days of high fever + purulence), or follow the **double-worsening** pattern. Early symptoms (A, D) are viral.

## Q3 — Medication Safety in Breastfeeding (p. 638)
Which one can reduce infant risk from maternal medications during breastfeeding?

- A. Choose medications safe in pregnancy (this confirms breastfeeding safety).
- B. Choose oral over topical medications.
- C. Take daily medications shortly before feeding.
- D. Take daily medications before the infant's longest sleep interval.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Dose before the infant's longest sleep interval.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Timing the dose so the peak occurs during the longest interval between feeds minimizes infant exposure. Pregnancy safety ≠ lactation safety; *topical* is preferred over oral (less systemic absorption).

## Q4 — Medication Safety in Breastfeeding (p. 638)
Safest antidepressant for postpartum depression while breastfeeding?

- A. Duloxetine (SNRI).
- B. Fluoxetine (SSRI).
- C. Sertraline (SSRI).
- D. Venlafaxine (SNRI).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Sertraline.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Sertraline has the most data and very low milk/infant serum levels — first-line in lactation. Fluoxetine's long half-life and active metabolite make it less preferred.

## Q5 — Medication Safety in Breastfeeding (p. 638)
Safest option for URI symptoms while breastfeeding, least likely to reduce milk supply?

- A. Diphenhydramine.
- B. Loratadine.
- C. Oxymetazoline.
- D. Pseudoephedrine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Oxymetazoline (topical nasal).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Topical oxymetazoline has minimal systemic absorption and no effect on supply. Pseudoephedrine reduces milk supply; first-generation diphenhydramine is sedating and may reduce supply.

## Q6 — Anxiety Disorders in Children (p. 657)
Recommended initial therapy for mild-to-moderate anxiety in children/adolescents?

- A. SSRI.
- B. SNRI.
- C. Cognitive behavior therapy.
- D. CBT plus an SSRI or SNRI.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Cognitive behavior therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CBT is first-line for mild-to-moderate pediatric anxiety. Medication (SSRI) or combination is reserved for moderate-to-severe or CBT-refractory cases.

## Q7 — Anxiety Disorders in Children (p. 657)
For which age group does the USPSTF recommend screening for anxiety?

- A. Children/adolescents 3–18 years.
- B. Children/adolescents 8–18 years.
- C. Insufficient evidence in children/adolescents.
- D. Screening not recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — 8 to 18 years.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF (2022) recommends screening for anxiety in children/adolescents **8–18** (Grade B). For ages 7 and under, evidence was insufficient.

## Q8 — Anxiety Disorders in Children (p. 657)
Which test should be ordered routinely in evaluating a child with suspected anxiety?

- A. Thyroid-stimulating hormone.
- B. Neuroimaging.
- C. Serum cortisol.
- D. Diagnostic testing is not routinely recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — No routine diagnostic testing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Pediatric anxiety is a clinical diagnosis; labs/imaging are ordered only when history or exam suggests a specific organic cause.

## Q9 — Hip Fractures (p. 675)
Which statement about antibiotic therapy for hip-fracture surgery is correct?

- A. A single preop dose; no postoperative antibiotics absent infection/immunocompromise.
- B. Anti-S. aureus antibiotics 1–2 h before surgery, continued 24 hours postoperatively.
- C. Anti-S. aureus antibiotics started immediately postop, continued 48 hours.
- D. No antibiotics needed if repaired within 48 hours of injury.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Anti-staph prophylaxis 1–2 h preop, continued 24 h.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Standard orthopedic surgical prophylaxis (cefazolin) is given within an hour or two before incision and continued for ≤24 h. Option A (single dose only) is also a defensible modern stance — flagged for nuance, but the keyed answer is B.

## Q10 — Hip Fractures (p. 675)
Which statement about perioperative care of older adults with hip fracture is correct?

- A. Delirium is common and guideline measures can help prevent it.
- B. General anesthesia is preferred over spinal.
- C. LMWH is the anticoagulant of choice for VTE prevention.
- D. Transfuse preoperatively if hemoglobin <9 g/dL.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Delirium is common and preventable with guideline-based measures.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Multicomponent delirium-prevention bundles help. Neuraxial and general anesthesia are comparable (no clear preference); a *restrictive* transfusion threshold (~<8 g/dL) is used.

## Q11 — Care of People Experiencing Homelessness (p. 684)
Which statement about people experiencing homelessness is correct?

- A. DV victims who are housed (even with their abuser) are not considered homeless.
- B. A family notified of eviction next month with no subsequent housing should be considered homeless.
- C. Three months of continuous homelessness meets the definition of chronic homelessness.
- D. Two episodes of homelessness over two years meets chronic homelessness.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Imminent loss of housing without alternative = homeless.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The expanded definition includes those facing imminent housing loss with no resources to obtain other housing, and those fleeing domestic violence (so A is wrong). *Chronic* homelessness = ≥12 months continuous **or** ≥4 episodes in 3 years (so C, D are wrong). The strict HUD "imminent" window is 14 days — minor tension with "next month" — flagged, but B is the keyed best answer.

## Q12 — Care of People Experiencing Homelessness (p. 684)
Which statement about chronic disease in this population is correct?

- A. Smoking rates are lower than the general population.
- B. STIs rarely occur.
- C. TB incidence is markedly higher than the general population.
- D. Mental health disorders are only minimally higher.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — TB incidence is markedly higher.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Crowded shelters drive much higher TB rates. Smoking, STIs, substance use, and mental illness are all substantially *more* common, not less.

## Q13 — Care of People Experiencing Homelessness (p. 684)
Which statement about homelessness in special populations is correct?

- A. ~30% of homeless youth identify as LGBTQ.
- B. Homeless veterans have fewer mental health disorders than other homeless people.
- C. Nearly half of U.S. refugees are homeless.
- D. Homelessness has no independent impact on pregnancy outcomes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — About 30% of homeless youth identify as LGBTQ.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> LGBTQ youth are markedly over-represented among homeless youth (family rejection a major driver). Homelessness independently worsens pregnancy outcomes; veterans have high mental-illness burden.

## Q14 — Galactorrhea (p. 695)
Which patient with milky breast discharge requires further workup?

- A. 25-year-old, last pregnant 4 years ago, stopped breastfeeding 6 months ago.
- B. 31-year-old, last pregnant 9 months ago, did not breastfeed.
- C. 37-year-old, last pregnant 3 years ago, stopped breastfeeding 18 months ago.
- D. 43-year-old, last pregnant 6 months ago, still breastfeeding.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Discharge 18 months after weaning, 3 years post-pregnancy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Lactation-related galactorrhea can persist for up to ~6–12 months after weaning (physiologic). Discharge well beyond that window (C) is non-physiologic → check prolactin, TSH, pregnancy test, review meds.

## Q15 — Galactorrhea (p. 695)
Most effective first-line therapy for symptomatic hyperprolactinemia caused by medication?

- A. Cabergoline.
- B. Bromocriptine.
- C. Transsphenoidal surgery.
- D. Discontinuing the causative medication.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Stop the offending medication.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Drug-induced hyperprolactinemia (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, etc.) is treated by stopping/switching the agent — not by adding a dopamine agonist.

## Q16 — Galactorrhea (p. 695)
Most effective first-line therapy for symptomatic hyperprolactinemia caused by a prolactinoma?

- A. Cabergoline.
- B. Bromocriptine.
- C. Transsphenoidal surgery.
- D. Regular exercise.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Cabergoline.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Dopamine agonists are first-line for prolactinoma; cabergoline is more effective and better tolerated than bromocriptine. Surgery is reserved for intolerance or resistance.

## Q17 — Cochrane: Pharmacotherapy for PTSD (p. 623)
Which medication class is first-line for PTSD?

- A. Anticonvulsants (e.g., gabapentin).
- B. Antipsychotics (e.g., quetiapine).
- C. SSRIs (e.g., fluoxetine).
- D. No evidence supports medication for PTSD.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — SSRIs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SSRIs (and venlafaxine) are first-line pharmacotherapy for PTSD, though trauma-focused psychotherapy remains the overall preferred treatment.

## Q18 — Cochrane: Antiplatelet Therapy in CKD (p. 624)
A patient with CKD and no CAD asks about antiplatelet therapy. Which statement is correct?

- A. It reduces the risk of myocardial infarction.
- B. It increases the risk of bleeding.
- C. KDIGO supports aspirin use for adults with CKD.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The Cochrane review found antiplatelet therapy reduces MI but increases bleeding, with uncertain mortality benefit; KDIGO supports aspirin in CKD (largely secondary prevention). The "all of the above" framing of the KDIGO endorsement is the nuance — flagged, but the keyed answer is D.

## Q19 — Lown Right Care: Overdiagnosis of NAFLD (p. 704)
Which statement about NAFLD is correct?

- A. Most patients develop serious hepatic complications.
- B. Pioglitazone improves clinical outcomes.
- C. Prevalence and poor outcomes decrease with age.
- D. The most common cause of death is vascular disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cardiovascular (vascular) disease is the leading cause of death.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most NAFLD patients never develop cirrhosis/HCC; they die of cardiovascular disease. Prevalence rises with age; pioglitazone improves histology but its effect on hard outcomes is unproven.

## Q20 — Point-of-Care Guide: Testicular Torsion (p. 712)
Which is NOT an independent predictor of testicular torsion?

- A. Right-sided symptoms.
- B. Testicular swelling.
- C. Absent cremasteric reflex.
- D. Nausea or vomiting.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Right-sided symptoms.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Independent predictors include nausea/vomiting, testicular swelling, high/horizontal lie, and an absent cremasteric reflex. Which side is affected carries no predictive value.

## Q21 — Practice Guidelines: AAFP Blood Pressure Targets (p. 721)
Per AAFP, what is reduced with a BP target ≤130/80 vs. 140/90?

- A. Adverse events.
- B. All-cause mortality.
- C. Cardiovascular mortality.
- D. Myocardial infarction.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Myocardial infarction.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The AAFP review found the lower target reduced MI but **not** all-cause or cardiovascular mortality, while *increasing* treatment-related adverse events — hence its more conservative stance than ACC/AHA.

## Q22 — Practice Guidelines: AASM Surgical Treatment of OSA (p. 723)
A 26-year-old with severe OSA, large tonsils, redundant soft palate, small jaw. AASM-recommended initial therapy?

- A. Behavior therapy.
- B. Oral/maxillofacial referral for a mandibular advancement device.
- C. Otolaryngology referral for surgical repair.
- D. Positive airway pressure.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Positive airway pressure.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PAP is first-line for OSA regardless of favorable surgical anatomy. Surgery (and the AASM surgical guideline) applies to patients who are intolerant of or fail PAP.
