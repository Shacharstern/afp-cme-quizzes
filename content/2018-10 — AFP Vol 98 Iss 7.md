## Q1 — Mechanical Low Back Pain (p. 421)

Which one of the following statements about the pharmacologic treatment of low back pain is correct?

- A. Systemic corticosteroids are more effective than placebo for acute low back pain from a herniated lumbar disc.
- B. Opioids are no more effective than nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).
- C. Acetaminophen is more effective than placebo for acute and chronic low back pain.
- D. Cyclobenzaprine (Flexeril) plus an NSAID is more effective than an NSAID alone for nonradicular pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Opioids are no more effective than NSAIDs for low back pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs are first-line; opioids add risk without superior efficacy. Systemic steroids don't help disc-herniation pain (A); **acetaminophen is no better than placebo** for LBP (C wrong); adding cyclobenzaprine to an NSAID doesn't improve nonradicular pain (D wrong).

## Q2 — Mechanical Low Back Pain (p. 421)

Which one of the following statements about the McKenzie method (mechanical diagnosis and therapy) for treatment of low back pain is correct?

- A. Correct classification of patients regarding the centralization phenomenon and directional preference is recommended.
- B. The same treatment plan is recommended for all patients.
- C. Directional preference refers to the patient's preference for pharmacotherapy vs. physical therapy.
- D. Minimal training is needed to use the McKenzie method in practice.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Correctly classifying centralization/directional preference is recommended.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The McKenzie method is **individualized** by movement response (B wrong); **directional preference** = the direction of movement that reduces/centralizes pain (C wrong); it requires **specific training** (D wrong).

## Q3 — Mechanical Low Back Pain (p. 421)

Which one of the following statements about imaging for patients with low back pain is correct?

- A. Plain radiography is recommended as the initial imaging study within two weeks of presentation or the onset of pain.
- B. Early imaging is associated with better long-term outcomes.
- C. Early use of magnetic resonance imaging has been shown to decrease the likelihood of surgery.
- D. Early imaging should be reserved for patients with red flags suggesting possible infection, malignancy, fracture, or other systemic cause.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Reserve early imaging for patients with red flags.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Routine early imaging doesn't improve outcomes (B wrong) and **early MRI is associated with MORE surgery** (C reversed). Image only with red flags (infection, malignancy, fracture, neurologic deficit) — not routinely at 2 weeks (A).

## Q4 — Abdominal Wall Pain: Clinical Evaluation, Differential Diagnosis, and Treatment (p. 429)

A patient presents with a two-week history of abdominal pain. Which one of the following findings best supports the diagnosis of abdominal wall pain?

- A. Positive Carnett test.
- B. A change in bowel habits.
- C. Multiple sites of pain.
- D. Constitutional symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A positive Carnett test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In the **Carnett test**, pain that **persists or worsens** when the patient tenses the abdominal wall (head/leg lift) points to a **wall** (not visceral) source. Changed bowel habits, multiple sites, and constitutional symptoms suggest visceral/systemic disease (B, C, D).

## Q5 — Abdominal Wall Pain (p. 429)

A patient with localized, unilateral pain that worsens with abdominal muscle movement scores 14 on the 18-item questionnaire for anterior cutaneous nerve entrapment syndrome. Which one of the following is the next best step to establish the diagnosis?

- A. Electromyelography.
- B. Ultrasound-guided local injection.
- C. Computed tomography.
- D. Magnetic resonance imaging.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — An ultrasound-guided local anesthetic injection.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In ACNES, a **trigger-point local anesthetic injection** is both diagnostic (pain relief confirms it) and therapeutic. Imaging/EMG don't establish the diagnosis (A, C, D).

## Q6 — Abdominal Wall Pain (p. 429)

Which one of the following is the preferred initial treatment for abdominal wall pain caused by nerve entrapment?

- A. Analgesics.
- B. Transversus abdominus plane block.
- C. Ultrasound-guided trigger point injection.
- D. Surgical neurectomy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ultrasound-guided trigger-point (local anesthetic ± corticosteroid) injection.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Trigger-point injection is the preferred initial treatment for ACNES. Oral analgesics are often inadequate (A); TAP blocks and **neurectomy** are reserved for refractory cases (B, D).

## Q7 — Anemia in Older Adults (p. 437)

Which one of the following tests is recommended to help determine the cause of anemia in a 67-year-old Black woman with a hemoglobin level of 11 g/dL?

- A. Glomerular filtration rate.
- B. Serum ferritin level.
- C. Mean corpuscular volume.
- D. Fecal occult blood test.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Mean corpuscular volume (MCV).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The MCV (micro-/normo-/macrocytic) is the first branch point that **directs the rest of the workup** (e.g., ferritin if microcytic, B₁₂/folate if macrocytic). Ferritin, GFR, and FOBT are subsequent, cause-specific tests.

## Q8 — Anemia in Older Adults (p. 437)

Which one of the following statements about iron replacement therapy in older adults with iron deficiency anemia is correct?

- A. Lower doses have less risk of adverse effects.
- B. Orange juice should be avoided when taking oral iron preparations.
- C. Iron should be given simultaneously with meals to maximize absorption.
- D. Treatment must continue indefinitely.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Lower (or alternate-day) iron doses have fewer adverse effects.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Lower-dose/alternate-day dosing improves tolerability with comparable absorption. **Vitamin C/orange juice enhances** absorption (B wrong); iron is best taken **on an empty stomach** (C wrong); treatment continues ~3 months **after** repletion, not indefinitely (D wrong).

## Q9 — Anemia in Older Adults (p. 437)

Which one of the following statements about parenteral iron replacement is correct?

- A. Its effects are reduced in patients who have undergone gastric bypass surgery.
- B. Parenteral therapy should be attempted before oral therapy in most patients.
- C. There is a broad range of effectiveness depending on the product.
- D. Laboratory studies are repeated six to eight weeks after one to five infusions are given.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Recheck labs about six to eight weeks after the infusion course.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> IV iron **bypasses the gut**, so it works well **after gastric bypass/malabsorption** (A reversed); **oral iron is first-line** for most (B wrong); modern IV products are broadly effective (C overstated).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Interventions for Increasing Fruit and Vegetable Consumption in Children Five Years and Younger (p. 419)

Which one of the following interventions is most likely in the short term to lead to an increase in a four-year-old's daily vegetable consumption?

- A. Parental nutrition education.
- B. Multicomponent interventions.
- C. Child nutrition education.
- D. Child feeding interventions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Child feeding interventions (e.g., repeated taste exposure).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Direct **child-feeding** strategies (repeated exposure, offering vegetables) most reliably increase short-term vegetable intake in young children — more than education-based approaches (A, B, C).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Depression in Adolescents — AAP Updated Guidelines (p. 462)

Which one of the following statements about the American Academy of Pediatrics' recommendation on diagnosing and treating depression in adolescents is correct?

- A. After symptoms have resolved, all patients should be monitored monthly for three years to evaluate for recurrence.
- B. Physicians should screen adolescents who are at least 10 years of age for depression annually via a formal self-report tool.
- C. After a patient is diagnosed with mild depression, physicians should consider active support and monitoring for three to six months before initiating treatment.
- D. Only treatments that have been proven effective for depression, such as cognitive behavior therapy and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, should be recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Recommend only proven treatments — CBT and SSRIs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> GLAD-PC/AAP advise **annual screening from age 12** (not 10, B), **active support/monitoring for ~6–8 weeks** (not 3–6 months) in mild depression (C wrong), and individualized follow-up (not fixed monthly monitoring for 3 years, A).
