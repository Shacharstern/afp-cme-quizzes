## Q1 — Topical Corticosteroids: Choice and Application (p. 337)

Which one of the following statements about topical corticosteroid use is correct?

- A. Super-high-potency topical corticosteroids can be used safely for several months in children.
- B. Topical corticosteroids should not be prescribed to women who are lactating.
- C. Adverse effects are more likely on areas of thicker skin.
- D. Cushing syndrome is a potential systemic adverse effect.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cushing syndrome is a potential systemic adverse effect.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Enough potent topical steroid (large area, occlusion, prolonged use) can be absorbed to suppress the HPA axis → iatrogenic Cushing. Adverse effects are greater on *thin* skin (face, flexures) — A, C wrong; topical steroids are acceptable in lactation (B wrong).

## Q2 — Topical Corticosteroids (p. 337)

Which one of the following statements about using topical corticosteroids for mild psoriasis on the elbows of a 31-year-old is correct?

- A. There is no good evidence to support topical corticosteroids for psoriasis.
- B. Super-high-potency topical corticosteroids are contraindicated for psoriasis.
- C. If each elbow area is about a palm in size, one fingertip unit is appropriate for both elbows per application.
- D. Medium-potency topical corticosteroids should not be used longer than three weeks at a time.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — One fingertip unit covers ~two palm-sized areas (both elbows).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> One fingertip unit (~0.5 g) treats about two adult palm areas. Topical steroids are evidence-based first-line for localized psoriasis (A wrong); high-potency agents are used for thick plaques (B wrong).

## Q3 — Topical Corticosteroids (p. 337)

Which one of the following statements about topical corticosteroid formulations is correct?

- A. Gels can dry the skin.
- B. The formulation does not affect absorption.
- C. Solutions and oils are more allergenic than lotions.
- D. Creams have a higher water content than lotions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Gels can dry the skin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Gels/solutions (alcohol-based) are drying and suit hairy/oily areas. Formulation strongly affects potency/absorption (ointments > creams > lotions) (B wrong); lotions have higher water content than creams (D reversed).

## Q4 — Common Fractures of the Radius and Ulna (p. 345)

Which one of the following statements about using ultrasonography to diagnose forearm fractures is correct?

- A. It is an alternative to radiography with high sensitivity and specificity.
- B. Its use should be limited to adults.
- C. It has low sensitivity for detecting fractures.
- D. It cannot detect buckle (torus) fractures in children.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A radiography alternative with high sensitivity and specificity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Point-of-care ultrasound has high sensitivity/specificity for forearm fractures, including pediatric buckle fractures, and reduces radiation exposure — particularly valuable in children (B, C, D wrong).

## Q5 — Common Fractures of the Radius and Ulna (p. 345)

Which one of the following fractures is most likely to be associated with a median nerve injury?

- A. Distal radius fracture.
- B. Isolated displaced midshaft ulna fracture.
- C. Displaced radial head fracture.
- D. Ulnar styloid fracture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Distal radius fracture.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The median nerve runs through the carpal tunnel adjacent to the distal radius, so distal radius fractures (e.g., Colles) classically threaten it → acute carpal tunnel syndrome.

## Q6 — Common Fractures of the Radius and Ulna (p. 345)

Which one of the following statements about fractures of the radial head is correct?

- A. Varus/valgus stress testing of the elbow is contraindicated.
- B. They are an uncommon type of elbow fracture.
- C. Elbow joint effusion is rarely associated with these fractures.
- D. Radiograph findings sometimes appear normal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Radiograph findings sometimes appear normal.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Radial head fractures may be occult; an elbow effusion shown by the **fat pad sign** (sail sign / posterior fat pad) may be the only clue. They are the *most common* adult elbow fracture (B wrong) and effusions are typical (C wrong).

## Q7 — Pain Management in Labor (p. 355)

Which one of the following nonpharmacologic approaches to pain management in labor leads to higher rates of spontaneous vaginal delivery?

- A. Continuous labor support.
- B. Immersion in water in the first stage of labor.
- C. Hypnosis.
- D. Audioanalgesia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Continuous labor support.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Continuous one-to-one support (e.g., doula) increases spontaneous vaginal delivery and reduces cesarean, instrumental delivery, and analgesia use. The other modalities lack this delivery-mode benefit.

## Q8 — Pain Management in Labor (p. 355)

Which one of the following statements about the effectiveness of parenteral opioids to manage pain during labor is correct?

- A. An advantage is the low rate of pain recurrence.
- B. Maternal satisfaction is considerably higher than with regional anesthesia.
- C. They provide superior pain relief to regional analgesia.
- D. Ultra-rapid-acting formulations result in less neonatal sedation than other opioids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Ultra-rapid-acting formulations (e.g., remifentanil) cause less neonatal sedation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Parenteral opioids give inferior analgesia to neuraxial techniques (B, C wrong) with frequent recurrence (A wrong). Ultra-short-acting agents are rapidly metabolized → less placental transfer/neonatal sedation.

## Q9 — Pain Management in Labor (p. 355)

Which one of the following statements about opioid use for pain management after cesarean delivery is correct?

- A. Systemic opioids are superior to nonopioid medications for pain control.
- B. Patients on medication-assisted therapy for OUD should not require additional postoperative pain medications.
- C. Subcutaneous wound infiltration with local anesthetic during delivery may decrease postoperative opioid requirements.
- D. Spinal/epidural opioids should be discontinued immediately after delivery.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Local-anesthetic wound infiltration may reduce postoperative opioid needs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Multimodal analgesia (local infiltration, NSAIDs/acetaminophen, neuraxial morphine) lowers opioid requirements. Nonopioids are co-first-line (A wrong); OUD patients often need *more* analgesia (B wrong); neuraxial opioids provide valuable post-op analgesia (D wrong).

## Q10 — Lown Right Care: Helping Ambivalent Patients Make Healthy Decisions About COVID-19 (p. 334)

A patient with well-controlled comorbidities is skeptical about COVID-19. Which statement about using motivational interviewing is correct?

- A. The benefits of MI are limited to reducing alcohol consumption.
- B. A series of small steps toward a goal is unlikely to produce significant change over time.
- C. Patients are more likely to defend the importance of change when the clinician does not take sides in their ambivalence.
- D. Follow-up is most effective when done by the physician in person.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Patients defend change more when the clinician stays neutral on their ambivalence.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In MI, "rolling with resistance" and evoking the patient's own change talk (rather than arguing for change) elicits self-motivation. MI applies broadly (A wrong), incremental steps work (B wrong), and follow-up need not be in person by the physician (D wrong).

## Q11 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for High Blood Pressure in Children and Adolescents (p. 371)

A 10-year-old boy with obesity and a family history of hypertension/hyperlipidemia presents for a well-child exam. According to the USPSTF, which is the most important risk factor for hypertension in this child?

- A. Obesity.
- B. Male sex.
- C. Age.
- D. Family history of hyperlipidemia.
- E. He does not exhibit any risk factors.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Obesity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Obesity is the strongest modifiable risk factor for pediatric hypertension and its prevalence rises with BMI. (Note: USPSTF gives an I statement on routine BP screening in asymptomatic children — insufficient evidence — but obesity remains the key risk factor.)

## Q12 — Point-of-Care Guides: Canadian Syncope Risk Score (p. 375)

According to the Canadian Syncope Risk Score, which one of the following is *not* a risk factor for a serious outcome in patients with syncope?

- A. History of heart disease.
- B. History consistent with vasovagal syncope.
- C. Elevated troponin level.
- D. QRS duration greater than 480 ms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A history consistent with vasovagal syncope.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In the CSRS, a vasovagal predisposition *lowers* the score (negative points) — it is reassuring, not a risk factor. Heart disease, elevated troponin, and abnormal QRS/QT all add risk points.

## Q13 — Practice Guidelines: Medications for Smoking Cessation — American Thoracic Society (p. 380)

According to the ATS, which one of the following statements about a 47-year-old with bipolar disorder and a 14-year smoking history is correct?

- A. Varenicline (Chantix) is not recommended because of psychiatric adverse-event risk.
- B. Treatment for tobacco cessation is not useful unless the patient is ready to quit.
- C. Nicotine patches are least likely to cause adverse events.
- D. Varenicline is the most effective treatment for achieving abstinence.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Varenicline is the most effective treatment for abstinence.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The 2020 ATS guideline recommends varenicline over patch and over bupropion, and varenicline **even in patients with psychiatric comorbidity** (EAGLES trial reassured on safety, A wrong). Treatment helps even those not yet ready to quit (B wrong).
