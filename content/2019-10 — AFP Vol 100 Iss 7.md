## Q1 — Hand-Foot-and-Mouth Disease: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 408)

Which one of the following is the most effective way to prevent the transmission of hand-foot-and-mouth disease?

- A. Discontinue breastfeeding.
- B. Vaccination or chemoprophylaxis.
- C. Handwashing after toileting and diaper changes and before eating.
- D. Exclusion from childcare immediately upon diagnosis and until afebrile.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Handwashing after toileting/diaper changes and before eating.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Hand hygiene is the cornerstone of preventing fecal-oral/contact spread. There's **no widely available vaccine** in the US (B), breastfeeding should continue (A), and exclusion until afebrile doesn't stop transmission (prolonged viral shedding, D).

## Q2 — Hand-Foot-and-Mouth Disease (p. 408)

Which one of the following is an appropriate treatment for hand-foot-and-mouth disease?

- A. Weight-based antivirals.
- B. Intravenous immunoglobulin infusion.
- C. Fever reduction with weight-based acetaminophen or ibuprofen.
- D. Hospitalization at the first sign of oral lesions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Fever/pain reduction with weight-based acetaminophen or ibuprofen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Treatment is **supportive** (analgesia/antipyretics, hydration). No routine antivirals (A); IVIG is reserved for severe complications (B); most cases are self-limited and don't require admission (D).

## Q3 — Hand-Foot-and-Mouth Disease (p. 408)

How long can the active virus that causes hand-foot-and-mouth disease be spread by stool?

- A. The first week after oral lesions develop.
- B. Three to six days.
- C. Up to four to eight weeks.
- D. Up to six months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Up to four to eight weeks.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Enteroviruses are shed in stool for **weeks** after symptoms resolve — which is why exclusion policies don't reliably prevent spread and **hand hygiene** is key.

## Q4 — Opioid Use Disorder: Medical Treatment Options (p. 416)

Which one of the following statements about initiating medication-assisted treatment for opioid use disorder is correct?

- A. Patients do not have to be in withdrawal from opioids before initiating buprenorphine.
- B. Patients do not have to completely withdraw from opioids before initiating naltrexone (Vivitrol).
- C. Patients taking naltrexone should have regular electrocardiographic monitoring.
- D. Methadone may be prescribed for opioid use disorder only in a federally certified opioid treatment program.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Methadone for OUD may be dispensed only through a federally certified opioid treatment program.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Buprenorphine **requires mild-moderate withdrawal first** to avoid precipitated withdrawal (A wrong); naltrexone requires **full opioid abstinence (~7–10 days)** before starting (B wrong); naltrexone needs no routine ECG (C wrong).

## Q5 — Opioid Use Disorder (p. 416)

A 35-year-old man with opioid use disorder wants to begin medication-assisted treatment. Which one of the following statements about buprenorphine therapy is correct?

- A. If starting buprenorphine without abstaining from opioids for several hours to days, abrupt withdrawal symptoms can occur.
- B. Accidental overdose from buprenorphine is common in adults.
- C. Simultaneous use of naloxone is contraindicated.
- D. The patient must undergo directly observed therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Starting buprenorphine too soon can precipitate abrupt withdrawal.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> As a **partial agonist**, buprenorphine displaces full agonists → precipitated withdrawal if started before adequate abstinence. Its ceiling effect makes overdose **uncommon** (B); buprenorphine/**naloxone** combinations are standard (C wrong); DOT isn't required (D).

## Q6 — Anticoagulation: Updated Guidelines for Outpatient Management (p. 426)

A 55-year-old patient is started on warfarin (Coumadin) after developing a venous thromboembolism after surgery. Which one of the following statements about treatment is correct?

- A. The patient will require at least one year of anticoagulant therapy.
- B. It is recommended that the patient undergo genetic testing to determine the proper dose.
- C. The patient should be prescribed aspirin after the completion of anticoagulant therapy.
- D. Foods with high concentrations of vitamin K are contraindicated.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Consider aspirin after completing anticoagulation (to reduce VTE recurrence).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A **provoked** (post-surgical) VTE needs only ~3 months of anticoagulation (A wrong); routine **pharmacogenetic testing is not recommended** (B wrong); patients should keep vitamin K intake **consistent**, not eliminate it (D wrong).

## Q7 — Anticoagulation (p. 426)

A patient with gastric cancer develops a venous thromboembolism and is treated with low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH). Which one of the following statements about LMWH is correct?

- A. LMWH has a higher heparin-induced thrombocytopenia risk compared with unfractionated heparin.
- B. Routine monitoring with anti–factor Xa levels is recommended.
- C. Subcutaneous LMWH has a predictable absorption and degree of anticoagulation.
- D. LMWH is no longer recommended for patients with cancer.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Subcutaneous LMWH has predictable absorption and anticoagulant effect.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Predictable pharmacokinetics mean **no routine monitoring** is needed (B wrong). LMWH carries **lower** HIT risk than unfractionated heparin (A reversed) and remains a mainstay for **cancer-associated** VTE (D wrong).

## Q8 — Anticoagulation (p. 426)

Which one of the following approaches for bleeding events in patients being treated with an anticoagulant is correct?

- A. Fresh frozen plasma is the preferred treatment for a major bleed attributed to warfarin anticoagulation.
- B. Holding the anticoagulant dose is an acceptable initial approach in nonmajor bleeding.
- C. There is currently no available agent to reverse the anticoagulant effect of dabigatran (Pradaxa).
- D. Andexanet alfa (Andexxa) has been found to augment the anticoagulant effects of rivaroxaban (Xarelto) and apixaban (Eliquis).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Holding the anticoagulant is an acceptable initial step in nonmajor bleeding.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For major warfarin bleeds, **4-factor PCC** (with vitamin K) is preferred over FFP (A wrong); **idarucizumab reverses dabigatran** (C wrong); andexanet alfa **reverses** (not augments) factor Xa inhibitors (D reversed).

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Antihypertensive Drug Therapy for Mild to Moderate Hypertension During Pregnancy (p. 403)

Your pregnant patient has a blood pressure of 150/85 mm Hg, and two days later it is 152/85 mm Hg. Which one of the following outcomes is significantly reduced in pregnant women receiving antihypertensive therapy for moderate hypertension compared with those who receive placebo?

- A. Miscarriage or neonatal death.
- B. Severe hypertension.
- C. Preterm birth.
- D. Infants who are small for gestational age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Severe hypertension.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Treating mild-to-moderate hypertension in pregnancy halves the risk of progressing to **severe** hypertension, but did not clearly reduce miscarriage/neonatal death, preterm birth, or SGA in this review (A, C, D).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Effectiveness and Safety of Factor Xa Inhibitors in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation (p. 405)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of atrial fibrillation with factor Xa inhibitors is correct?

- A. Fewer patients develop stroke and other embolic events compared with those treated with warfarin (Coumadin).
- B. There is no difference in all-cause mortality compared with warfarin therapy.
- C. There is no difference in intracranial hemorrhage compared with warfarin therapy.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Fewer strokes/embolic events than with warfarin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Factor Xa inhibitors modestly reduce stroke/systemic embolism, **lower all-cause mortality**, and **lower intracranial hemorrhage** versus warfarin — so B and C (and therefore "all of the above") are incorrect.

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Perinatal Depression — AAP Policy Statement (p. 443)

Which one of the following does the American Academy of Pediatrics recommend regarding screening for postpartum depression in the mother's partner?

- A. No screening is recommended.
- B. Screen at every visit attended by the mother's partner using the Patient Health Questionnaire-2 (PHQ-2) tool.
- C. Screen at the six-month well-child visit using the Edinburgh Postpartum Depression Scale.
- D. Screen at the two-week visit using the PHQ-2.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Screen the partner at the six-month well-child visit using the Edinburgh Postpartum Depression Scale.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The AAP recognizes **paternal/partner** perinatal depression and recommends EPDS screening at the 6-month visit (mothers are screened at the 1-, 2-, 4-, and 6-month visits).
