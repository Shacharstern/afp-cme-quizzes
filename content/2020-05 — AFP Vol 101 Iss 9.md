# AFP CME Quiz — May 1, 2020 (Vol 101, Issue 9)

## Q1 — Tickborne Diseases: Diagnosis and Management (p. 530)

A 12-year-old boy is bitten by a blacklegged tick during a weekend camping trip to Connecticut in early summer. Which one of the following is a symptom of early disseminated Lyme disease?

- A. Fever.
- B. Myalgias.
- C. Multiple erythema migrans lesions.
- D. Persistent atrioventricular block.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Multiple erythema migrans lesions.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Multiple EM lesions** mark early *disseminated* Lyme (along with cranial neuropathy and carditis). Fever/myalgias are nonspecific early-localized symptoms (A, B); Lyme carditis AV block is typically **transient/reversible**, not persistent (D).

## Q2 — Tickborne Diseases (p. 530)

Which one of the following antibiotics should be used as a first-line treatment for a pregnant woman with Rocky Mountain spotted fever?

- A. Doxycycline.
- B. Chloramphenicol.
- C. Cefuroxime axetil (Ceftin).
- D. Amoxicillin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Doxycycline.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> RMSF can be fatal, and **doxycycline is first-line for all ages and in pregnancy** — its life-saving benefit outweighs theoretical fetal/dental concerns. Chloramphenicol is less effective and toxic; beta-lactams don't treat rickettsiae.

## Q3 — Tickborne Diseases (p. 530)

Which one of the following should be used to treat Colorado tick fever?

- A. Tetracyclines.
- B. Macrolides.
- C. Clindamycin.
- D. Supportive care.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Supportive care.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Colorado tick fever is a **viral** illness — antibiotics are ineffective; management is supportive. (Antibiotics treat bacterial/rickettsial tickborne diseases; clindamycin ± quinine treats babesiosis.)

## Q4 — Chronic Dyspnea: Diagnosis and Evaluation (p. 542)

A patient who has a slow and progressive course of dyspnea that is worsening comes for an office visit. Which one of the following conditions is most consistent with this presentation?

- A. Interstitial fibrosis.
- B. Bronchiectasis.
- C. Chronic bronchitis.
- D. Asthma.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Interstitial fibrosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A **slowly progressive, relentlessly worsening** dyspnea fits interstitial lung disease/pulmonary fibrosis. Asthma is episodic/variable (D); chronic bronchitis and bronchiectasis feature chronic productive cough with a more fluctuating course (B, C).

## Q5 — Chronic Dyspnea (p. 542)

A 54-year-old woman has had a sensation of not being able to take a full breath for the past seven months. Which one of the following would not be recommended for this patient?

- A. Taking a detailed smoking and exposure history.
- B. Obtaining electrocardiography if heart failure is suspected.
- C. Obtaining chest radiography and spirometry if a pulmonary cause is suspected.
- D. Initiating supplemental oxygen.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Initiating supplemental oxygen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Oxygen isn't a diagnostic step and isn't indicated without documented hypoxemia. The chronic dyspnea workup is **history + targeted testing** (ECG, CXR, spirometry) to find the cause first (A, B, C are appropriate).

## Q6 — Endometrial Biopsy: Tips and Pitfalls (p. 551)

A 58-year-old postmenopausal woman has three days of new-onset vaginal bleeding and no risk factors for endometrial hyperplasia or cancer. Which one of the following initial evaluations is most appropriate?

- A. Hysteroscopy.
- B. Endometrial biopsy.
- C. Transvaginal ultrasonography.
- D. Endometrial biopsy or transvaginal ultrasonography.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Endometrial biopsy or transvaginal ultrasonography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Postmenopausal bleeding must be evaluated; **either** an endometrial biopsy **or** TVUS (endometrial stripe ≤4 mm is reassuring) is an acceptable first step. Hysteroscopy is reserved for nondiagnostic or persistent cases (A).

## Q7 — Endometrial Biopsy (p. 551)

The administration of oral misoprostol (Cytotec) before endometrial biopsy shows which one of the following effects in studies?

- A. It dilates cervical stenosis.
- B. It reduces patient discomfort.
- C. It increases uterine cramping.
- D. It increases procedural success.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — It increases uterine cramping.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Routine pre-procedure misoprostol does **not** improve success or ease cervical passage for office endometrial biopsy and **increases cramping** and side effects (A, B, D wrong).

## Q8 — Endometrial Biopsy (p. 551)

Which one of the following statements about insufficient samples from endometrial biopsy is correct?

- A. Uterine polyps can lead to insufficient samples.
- B. Insufficient samples are caused primarily by poor procedural technique.
- C. Endometrial atrophy will not lead to an insufficient sample.
- D. Insufficient samples are rare in postmenopausal women.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Uterine polyps can lead to insufficient samples.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Focal lesions (polyps) and **atrophy** commonly yield scant/insufficient tissue — so atrophy *does* cause it (C wrong) and insufficient samples are **more** common in postmenopausal women (D wrong); it's not chiefly a technique problem (B wrong).

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Antidepressants Plus Benzodiazepines for Adults with Major Depression (p. 527)

Which one of the following statements about adults with major depression treated with a combination of benzodiazepines and antidepressants vs. antidepressant monotherapy is correct?

- A. Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression scores improve over five to 12 weeks of combination therapy.
- B. Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression scores improve after 12 weeks or more of combination therapy.
- C. Patients on combination therapy are at increased risk of adverse effects.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Combination therapy increases the risk of adverse effects.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Adding a benzodiazepine gives only an **early, short-term** symptom edge (first few weeks) that **disappears by 6–12 weeks** (so A, B overstate durable benefit) while adding dependence and adverse-effect risk.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Lithium for Acute Mania (p. 528)

Which one of the following statements about the use of lithium to treat acute mania in patients with bipolar disorder is correct?

- A. It is not effective at treating acute mania and does not increase the risk of adverse effects.
- B. Olanzapine (Zyprexa) is more effective than lithium at reducing symptoms on the Young Mania Rating Scale.
- C. Topiramate (Topamax) is more effective than lithium at treating acute mania.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Olanzapine is more effective than lithium on the Young Mania Rating Scale.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Lithium **is** effective for acute mania (A wrong); **topiramate is not** effective for mania (C wrong). Olanzapine showed somewhat greater symptom reduction in this review.

## Q11 — AHRQ Review: Therapies for COPD Exacerbations in Adults (p. 557)

Which one of the following statements about therapies for acute exacerbations of COPD is correct?

- A. Systemic corticosteroids increase the risk of serious adverse events.
- B. Resistance training has no effects on dyspnea or quality of life.
- C. Antibiotic therapy improves clinical cure rates.
- D. High flow oxygen improves mortality compared with titrated oxygen.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Antibiotic therapy improves clinical cure rates.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Antibiotics help in COPD exacerbations (especially with increased sputum purulence). Short-course systemic steroids are beneficial without excess serious harm (A wrong); pulmonary rehab/resistance training **improves** dyspnea/QoL (B wrong); **titrated** (not high-flow) oxygen improves mortality (D reversed).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Migraines in Children — Acute and Preventive Treatment (p. 569)

Which one of the following treatments is most likely to benefit a 16-year-old female with episodic migraines 10 days per month?

- A. Divalproex (Depakote), 50 mg per day.
- B. Cognitive behavior therapy.
- C. Amitriptyline, 50 mg per day.
- D. OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) injections.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Cognitive behavior therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In children/adolescents, **CBT** (and lifestyle measures) has the best evidence for migraine prevention — and CBT plus amitriptyline outperforms medication alone. Pediatric preventive **drugs largely match placebo**; Botox/divalproex aren't first-line here.
