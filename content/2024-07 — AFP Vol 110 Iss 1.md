## Q1 — Obstructive Sleep Apnea in Adults (p. 27)
A 62-year-old with HFrEF and obesity has worsening fatigue; STOP-BANG shows high pretest probability and polysomnography confirms OSA. Best first-line treatment?

- A. Positive airway pressure (PAP).
- B. A mandibular advancement device.
- C. Hypoglossal nerve stimulation.
- D. Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Positive airway pressure (PAP).** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> PAP is first-line for moderate-to-severe OSA, especially with cardiac comorbidity. Mandibular devices suit mild disease/PAP intolerance; hypoglossal stimulation and UPPP are later-line surgical options.

## Q2 — Obstructive Sleep Apnea in Adults (p. 27)
Which one of the following is most likely to be a comorbid condition with OSA?

- A. Major depressive disorder.
- B. Hypertension.
- C. Obesity-hypoventilation syndrome.
- D. Opioid therapy for chronic spinal pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Obesity-hypoventilation syndrome.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> OHS is the condition most intrinsically linked to OSA — roughly 90% of OHS patients have coexisting OSA. (Hypertension is highly *prevalent* in OSA, but the keyed answer is OHS for its near-obligate association.)

## Q3 — Primary Care of Adult Cancer Survivors (p. 37)
A 52-year-old breast cancer survivor (curative treatment completed 2 years ago) asks about mammography frequency. Correct screening frequency for breast cancer survivors?

- A. Mammography annually.
- B. Mammography twice per year for 5 years, then annually.
- C. Mammography annually for 5 years, then every 2 years.
- D. Magnetic resonance imaging annually.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Mammography annually.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> Surveillance after breast cancer treatment is annual mammography of the remaining/treated breast tissue. Routine MRI and more/less frequent intervals are not standard for average survivors.

## Q4 — Primary Care of Adult Cancer Survivors (p. 37)
A 72-year-old man treated for non-Hodgkin lymphoma 10 years ago (anthracycline-containing regimen). Common long-term complication of this treatment?

- A. Cardiovascular disease.
- B. Bladder cancer.
- C. Lymphedema.
- D. Peripheral neuropathy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Cardiovascular disease.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> Anthracyclines (e.g., doxorubicin) cause dose-dependent cardiotoxicity/cardiomyopathy, a key long-term survivorship concern. Bladder cancer follows cyclophosphamide, lymphedema follows nodal surgery/radiation, and neuropathy follows vinca alkaloids/platinums/taxanes.

## Q5 — Acute and Chronic Prostatitis (p. 45)
Which one of the following presentations suggests acute bacterial prostatitis?

- A. Urinary urgency and frequency, suprapubic pain, and fever.
- B. Perineal pain and recurrent urinary tract infection.
- C. Pelvic pain and sexual dysfunction in the absence of urinary symptoms.
- D. Inflammatory cells incidentally found on prostate biopsy for an elevated PSA level.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Urgency/frequency, suprapubic pain, and fever.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> Acute bacterial prostatitis is an acute febrile illness with obstructive/irritative urinary symptoms. B describes chronic bacterial prostatitis, C describes chronic pelvic pain syndrome, and D describes asymptomatic inflammatory prostatitis.

## Q6 — Acute and Chronic Prostatitis (p. 45)
A 65-year-old (not sexually active) has perineal pain, hesitancy, and recurrent UTIs over 4 months. Appropriate empiric first-line treatment?

- A. Azithromycin for 3 weeks.
- B. Doxycycline for 3 weeks.
- C. Ciprofloxacin for 4 weeks.
- D. Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole for 4 weeks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ciprofloxacin for 4 weeks.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> This is chronic bacterial prostatitis. Fluoroquinolones penetrate prostatic tissue well and are first-line for a prolonged (4–6 week) course. TMP/SMX is an alternative; azithromycin/doxycycline target atypicals more relevant to a younger, sexually active patient.

## Q7 — Carpal Tunnel Syndrome: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 52)
Which one of the following statements about the diagnosis of CTS is correct?

- A. The Phalen test and Tinel sign have good sensitivity and specificity for CTS.
- B. Patients typically present with symptoms in the radial nerve distribution.
- C. Thenar atrophy is highly specific for CTS.
- D. A normal nerve conduction study excludes CTS.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Thenar atrophy is highly specific for CTS.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> Thenar wasting is a specific (though late, insensitive) sign. Phalen/Tinel have only modest accuracy (A wrong); CTS affects the *median* nerve distribution (B wrong); and a normal nerve conduction study does not rule out CTS (D wrong).

## Q8 — Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (p. 52)
Which one of the following statements about the treatment of CTS is correct?

- A. Splints are most effective when worn 24 hours a day.
- B. Symptom relief from splints typically occurs within 1 week.
- C. NSAIDs can be helpful for short-term pain relief.
- D. Local corticosteroid injections provide relief and decrease the need for surgery 1 year after injection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Corticosteroid injection relieves symptoms and reduces the need for surgery at 1 year.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> Local corticosteroid injection has the best short-to-medium-term evidence. Night splinting (not 24-hour wear) helps but takes weeks, not 1 week, and NSAIDs lack proven benefit for CTS.

## Q9 — Hematologic Emergencies (p. 58)
A trauma patient required transfusion. Which presentation is most consistent with an acute hemolytic transfusion reaction?

- A. Fever, renal pain, and hemoglobinuria.
- B. Fever, chest pain, respiratory distress, hypoxemia, cough, or tachypnea with a new pulmonary infiltrate.
- C. Bruising, gingival bleeding, and hematomas from minor trauma.
- D. Fatigue on exertion, dark urine, and acute jaundice.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Fever, flank/renal pain, and hemoglobinuria.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> Acute hemolytic (ABO-incompatibility) reactions cause fever, flank pain, and red urine (hemoglobinuria) with risk of DIC/renal failure. B is TRALI, C suggests a bleeding disorder/thrombocytopenia, and D fits a delayed hemolytic reaction.

## Q10 — Hematologic Emergencies (p. 58)
Which treatment is recommended for patients with hemophilia A or B per the National Bleeding Disorders Foundation?

- A. For severe bleeding, hemophilia A → factor IX 30 units/kg and hemophilia B → factor VIII 50 units/kg.
- B. Fresh frozen plasma should be used for hemophilia A and B.
- C. When bleeding is suspected, clotting factor replacement should be initiated before diagnostic testing.
- D. Cryoprecipitate plasma should be used for hemophilia A and B.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Start clotting factor replacement before diagnostic testing when bleeding is suspected.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> In known hemophilia, "treat first" — give factor immediately rather than awaiting workup. Option A swaps the factors (A needs factor VIII, B needs factor IX); FFP and cryoprecipitate are not the preferred replacement when specific factor concentrates are available.

## Q11 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2023 (p. 65)
Which one of the following statements about the treatment of cardiovascular disease is correct?

- A. Antihypertensive medications are most effective when taken at bedtime.
- B. A treat-to-target approach to statins is superior to a fixed-dose strategy in adults with CAD.
- C. Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring is better than office measurements in predicting mortality.
- D. Vitamin K antagonists have a better net clinical benefit than DOACs in older adults with atrial fibrillation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ambulatory BP monitoring predicts mortality better than office measurements.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> Bedtime dosing was not shown superior (TIME trial — A wrong); treat-to-target was non-inferior, not superior, to fixed-dose statins (B wrong); and DOACs are preferred over VKAs in older AF patients (D wrong).

## Q12 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2023 (p. 65)
Which one of the following statements about infections is correct?

- A. Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid) reduced hospitalizations and deaths from COVID-19 in the Omicron phase.
- B. A lower leukocyte cutoff should be used to define pyuria in older women.
- C. A 4-week course of terbinafine is the best treatment for toenail onychomycosis.
- D. Antitussives, honey, and anticholinergics all reduce cough more than placebo in acute cough.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Paxlovid reduced COVID-19 hospitalizations and deaths in the Omicron phase.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> This was a key 2023 finding (benefit concentrated in higher-risk patients). Toenail onychomycosis needs ~12 weeks of terbinafine, not 4 (C wrong); the pyuria-cutoff and "all antitussives work" statements (B, D) are not supported.

## Q13 — Cochrane: Breastfeeding Support for Healthy Mothers and Infants (p. 24)
Which one of the following statements about breastfeeding support services is correct?

- A. They promote exclusive breastfeeding at 6 weeks.
- B. They promote any breastfeeding at 6 months.
- C. They are effective even when they are not delivered in person.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found that structured breastfeeding support increases both exclusive and any breastfeeding rates and is effective whether delivered in person or remotely.

## Q14 — Cochrane: Cognitive Rehabilitation for Patients With Dementia (p. 25)
Which one of the following statements about cognitive rehabilitation is correct?

- A. It can improve participant self-ratings of goal attainment and satisfaction with goal attainment.
- B. It has no effect on caregiver quality of life.
- C. It significantly improves sustained attention, memory, and delayed recall.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — It improves self-rated goal attainment and satisfaction.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> Cognitive rehabilitation's strongest, most consistent benefit is on personally meaningful, goal-oriented functioning (self-rated goal attainment/satisfaction) rather than on standardized neuropsychological measures of attention/memory (C overstates) — so D doesn't hold.

## Q15 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: Screening for Anxiety, Depression, and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents (p. 93)
A well 13-year-old with controlled eczema presents for a well-child exam. Per the USPSTF, for which should he be screened?

- A. Anxiety.
- B. Depression.
- C. Both depression and anxiety.
- D. The patient should not be screened for anxiety or depression.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Both depression and anxiety.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends screening for anxiety in children/adolescents aged 8–18 and for major depression in those aged 12–18. A 13-year-old falls within both windows, so both are recommended (suicide-risk screening has insufficient evidence).

## Q16 — Practice Guidelines: Management of Pregnancy (VA/DoD) (p. 95)
A 26-year-old at 28 weeks (history of postpartum depression) has a positive Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale and admits anxiety/depressive symptoms, no active suicidal ideation. Per VA/DoD, recommended primary treatment?

- A. Exercise.
- B. Interpersonal psychotherapy.
- C. Peer support.
- D. Yoga.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Interpersonal psychotherapy.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> For perinatal depression, evidence-based psychotherapy (interpersonal therapy or CBT) is the recommended primary treatment. Exercise, yoga, and peer support are adjuncts without the same evidence for primary management.

## Q17 — Practice Guidelines: Postdural Puncture Headache (Multisociety International Working Group) (p. 97)
A 31-year-old, 7 days postpartum (epidural analgesia), has a 3-day positional headache (worse upright, relieved lying down) impairing infant care. Most likely to improve symptoms?

- A. Bed rest.
- B. Caffeine.
- C. Epidural blood patch.
- D. Epidural dextran injection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Epidural blood patch.** ✅ Official · Confidence: High
> The history is classic postdural puncture headache (positional, post-epidural). For headache that is severe or impairs functioning, an epidural blood patch is the most effective treatment. Bed rest and caffeine offer limited/temporary relief; epidural dextran is not standard.
