## Q1 — Childhood and Adolescent Sports-Related Overuse Injuries (p. 544)
12-year-old baseball pitcher with throwing-arm shoulder pain and decreased velocity/accuracy. Which statement is correct?

- A. MRI should be performed initially.
- B. The pathophysiology involves a fracture of the metaphysis.
- C. Management does not require refraining from pitching.
- D. Prevention includes rest cycles from throwing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Prevention includes rest cycles from throwing.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> This is "Little League shoulder" (proximal humeral physeal stress, not a metaphyseal fracture). Plain radiographs (not MRI) are first-line; management *requires* rest from pitching; pitch-count limits and rest cycles prevent it.

## Q2 — Childhood and Adolescent Sports-Related Overuse Injuries (p. 544)
Young gymnast with back pain, concern for spondylolysis. Which statement is correct?

- A. Spondylolysis more commonly affects thoracic than lumbar vertebrae.
- B. Back flexion more commonly causes pain than extension.
- C. The stork test is sensitive and specific for spondylolysis.
- D. Early detection and conservative management can lead to high healing rates in early-stage defects.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Early detection + conservative care → high healing of early-stage defects.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Spondylolysis is a stress injury of the **lumbar** pars (L5), provoked by **extension** (not flexion). The stork (single-leg hyperextension) test is suggestive but not sensitive/specific. Early-stage lesions heal well with activity modification/bracing.

## Q3 — Chronic Kidney Disease (p. 554)
64-year-old man with mild hypertension. Which CKD assessment statement is correct?

- A. eGFR should be assessed using the CKD-EPI creatinine equation without the race variable.
- B. Urine albumin/creatinine ratio is only reliable above 300 mg/g.
- C. An abnormal eGFR must be present for at least six months.
- D. Serum cystatin C is preferred in the setting of acute kidney injury.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Use the race-free CKD-EPI creatinine equation.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The 2021 refit CKD-EPI equation removed the race coefficient. ACR is useful well below 300 mg/g (detects A2 30–300); CKD requires abnormality ≥3 months (not 6); cystatin C is *not* reliable in unstable/AKI states.

## Q4 — Chronic Kidney Disease (p. 554)
In patients with CKD stage 4 or greater, which intervention is most appropriate?

- A. Continuation of metformin regardless of glycemic status.
- B. Continuation of an ACE inhibitor or ARB in patients with hypertension.
- C. Administration of calcimimetics to lower elevated parathyroid levels.
- D. Radiologic assessment with IV iodinated contrast media.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Continue ACEi/ARB in hypertensive CKD.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> RAAS blockade is reno-protective (slows progression, reduces albuminuria) and should be continued. Metformin is contraindicated at low eGFR (lactic acidosis risk); IV iodinated contrast risks nephrotoxicity; calcimimetics treat secondary hyperparathyroidism but aren't the general "most appropriate" step.

## Q5 — Psoriasis (p. 562)
Which form of psoriasis is considered a medical emergency?

- A. Plaque psoriasis.
- B. Guttate psoriasis.
- C. Type 2 erythrodermic psoriasis.
- D. Inverse psoriasis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Erythrodermic psoriasis.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Erythrodermic (and generalized pustular) psoriasis is a dermatologic emergency — impaired thermoregulation, fluid/protein loss, infection/sepsis risk → urgent referral/admission.

## Q6 — Psoriasis (p. 562)
Which statement about psoriatic arthritis is correct?

- A. It most commonly develops more than 20 years after skin onset.
- B. It does not occur in children.
- C. Arthritis may develop years before skin abnormalities.
- D. The PASI score is recommended for distinguishing psoriatic arthritis from other arthritides.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Arthritis can precede skin disease by years.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> In a minority (~15%) joint disease appears before skin lesions. PsA usually develops *within* ~10 years of skin disease; it *does* occur in children (juvenile PsA); PASI measures skin severity, not arthritis diagnosis.

## Q7 — Adnexal Masses (p. 580)
43-year-old: bloating, early satiety, 2.3 kg weight loss, increasing abdominal girth, urinary frequency, negative pregnancy test — concern for ovarian cancer. Most appropriate evaluation?

- A. CT.
- B. MRI.
- C. Transvaginal ultrasonography.
- D. Surveillance only.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Transvaginal ultrasonography.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> TVUS is the first-line imaging study for a suspected adnexal/ovarian mass — characterizes morphology and guides risk stratification. CT/MRI are adjuncts for staging or indeterminate masses.

## Q8 — Adnexal Masses (p. 580)
33-year-old pregnant patient, 2.5 cm simple cystic ovarian mass at 20-week scan, no suspicious features. Most appropriate option?

- B. Repeat ultrasonography at 34 weeks.

A later third-trimester rescan documents resolution/stability and aids delivery planning. ⚠️⚠️ Interval (24 vs 34 wk) uncertain — verify against print key.

## Q9 — Preventing CVD in Women (p. 595)
55-year-old: prior preterm birth at 35 4/7 weeks and GDM in second pregnancy, normal BMI, A1C 5.6%, on pantoprazole, regular periods. Which conveys the greatest CVD risk?

- A. Preterm delivery at 35 4/7 weeks.
- B. Esophageal reflux.
- C. GDM in her second pregnancy.
- D. Late onset of menopause.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Gestational diabetes.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> GDM is a strong, durable marker of future cardiometabolic and cardiovascular disease (and T2DM). Preterm birth is also a risk-enhancer but weaker than GDM here; reflux is irrelevant; late menopause is relatively protective. ⚠️ Verify against print key (A vs C).

## Q10 — Preventing CVD in Women (p. 595)
43-year-old, induced at 33 weeks for preeclampsia with severe features 5 years ago; A1C 5.8%, BP 119/79, 10-year CVD risk 11.1%. Recommendation to reduce CVD risk?

- A. Shared decision-making to consider low-dose aspirin.
- B. Shared decision-making to consider a moderate-intensity statin.
- C. Shared decision-making to consider both aspirin and a moderate-intensity statin.
- D. Do not discuss aspirin or a statin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Shared decision-making about a moderate-intensity statin.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Her 10-year ASCVD risk (11.1%) is intermediate, with a risk-enhancer (preeclampsia) → statin is reasonable via shared decision-making. Routine aspirin for *primary* prevention is no longer favored (bleeding risk outweighs benefit in most). ⚠️ Verify against print key.

## Q11 — Preconception Counseling and Care (p. 605)
32-year-old with morbid obesity planning bariatric surgery, on OCPs, considering pregnancy. Which statement about bariatric surgery and pregnancy risks is correct?

- A. Pregnancy should be avoided for 12 to 24 months after bariatric surgery.
- B. Bariatric surgery has no impact on preterm birth, preeclampsia, or GDM rates.
- C. Bariatric surgery is not generally performed in individuals with reproductive potential.
- D. Oral contraceptives are the best postoperative contraceptive method until pregnancy is desired.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Avoid pregnancy for 12–24 months post-surgery.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Delaying conception through the rapid-weight-loss phase optimizes nutrition and outcomes. Surgery *does* alter pregnancy risks (lowers GDM/preeclampsia, may raise SGA/preterm); malabsorption reduces oral contraceptive reliability → prefer non-oral/LARC methods.

## Q12 — Preconception Counseling and Care (p. 605)
Patient on a DOAC for VTE prophylaxis, planning pregnancy within a year. First-line anticoagulant during pregnancy?

- A. Apixaban (Eliquis).
- B. Rivaroxaban (Xarelto).
- C. Warfarin.
- D. Low-molecular-weight heparin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Low-molecular-weight heparin.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> LMWH does not cross the placenta and is the anticoagulant of choice in pregnancy. Warfarin is teratogenic; DOACs lack safety data and are not recommended in pregnancy.

## Q13 — Cochrane: Spinal Cord Stimulation for Low Back Pain (p. 542)
Adding spinal cord stimulation to physical therapy / optimal medical management — which statement is correct?

- A. It slightly improves back function over 12 months.
- B. It slightly reduces opioid use over 12 months.
- C. It is associated with risk of adverse effects — infection, lead migration, spine/nerve damage, and revision surgery — over 12 months.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — It carries real harms (infection, lead migration, revision surgery).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found SCS provides little to no benefit for function or opioid use over 12 months while exposing patients to procedural harms → A and B are false, so "all of the above" is wrong.

## Q14 — Stable COPD and the Role of Inhaled Corticosteroids (online)
Which statement about ICS use in stable COPD is correct?

- A. They decrease the likelihood of exacerbations.
- B. They do not improve quality of life.
- C. They are neutral with respect to pneumonia risk.
- D. They do not affect FEV₁.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — ICS reduce exacerbations.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> In appropriate patients (frequent exacerbations, blood eosinophilia) ICS reduce exacerbation frequency. They *increase* pneumonia risk (so C is false), modestly improve QoL/lung function — making B and D false.

## Q15 — Lown Right Care: Overuse of Antireflux Medications in Infants (p. 614)
Which statement about gastroesophageal reflux in infants is correct?

- A. It occurs in a small percentage of healthy infants.
- B. It begins two to three weeks after birth and peaks between four and five months of age.
- C. Symptoms often continue throughout childhood.
- D. H₂ receptor antagonists or PPIs reduce the frequency and volume of regurgitation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Begins in the first weeks and peaks around 4 months.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Physiologic GER affects *most* healthy infants (so A is false), typically starts in the early weeks, peaks ~4 months, and resolves by 12–18 months (so C is false). The Lown teaching point: acid suppression does **not** reduce regurgitation (D false) → avoid overuse. ⚠️ Verify exact wording of B against print key.

## Q16 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: PrEP to Prevent HIV (p. 617)
Which best describes the USPSTF recommendation on PrEP?

- A. Clinicians should selectively offer PrEP to people at increased risk.
- B. Clinicians should prescribe PrEP to people at increased risk.

## Q17 — Point-of-Care Guides: Initial Evaluation of Clinically Suspected Dementia (p. 624)
Which is consistent with a positive Mini-Cog screen for dementia?

- A. Inability to name three animals from pictures.
- B. Inability to recall any of three provided words after five minutes.
- C. Inability to repeat a phrase like "no ifs, ands, or buts."
- D. Inability to count backwards from 100 by sevens.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Failure to recall any of the three words.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The Mini-Cog = 3-word registration + clock-drawing (distractor) + delayed recall. Recall of 0 words is a positive screen; recall of 1–2 is positive if the clock is abnormal. The other tasks (naming, repetition, serial 7s) belong to other instruments. ⚠️ Verify timing wording against print key.

## Q18 — Practice Guidelines: Caring for Transgender and Gender-Diverse People (WPATH) (p. 626)
50-year-old transgender man: chest (top) surgery removing breast tissue at 30, retained vagina/uterus/cervix, vaginal intercourse. Per WPATH, which screening is recommended?

- A. Breast cancer screening.
- B. Cervical cancer screening.
- C. Combined breast and cervical cancer screening.
- D. No screening is required.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Cervical cancer screening.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Organ-based screening: a retained cervix → continue cervical (Pap/HPV) screening per guidelines. After chest masculinization (breast tissue removed), routine mammographic breast screening is generally not indicated (chest-wall/clinical exam instead).
