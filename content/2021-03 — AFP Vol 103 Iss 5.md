## Q1 — Peripheral Nerve Entrapment and Injury in the Upper Extremity (p. 275)

Injury to the long thoracic nerve is most likely to cause which one of the following signs?

- A. Wrist drop.
- B. Pronator syndrome.
- C. Scapular winging.
- D. Hand numbness.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Scapular winging.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The long thoracic nerve innervates serratus anterior; injury → medial (winged) scapula. Wrist drop = radial nerve; pronator syndrome and hand numbness = median/ulnar issues.

## Q2 — Peripheral Nerve Entrapment (p. 275)

A patient has numbness/tingling in the wrist with loss of sensation in the little and ring fingers and at the base of the little finger. Which test is likely to be positive?

- A. Froment sign.
- B. Pronator syndrome test.
- C. OK sign.
- D. Phalen test.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Froment sign.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The sensory pattern (5th + ulnar half of 4th finger) is **ulnar** nerve. Froment sign (thumb IP flexion when pinching paper, compensating for weak adductor pollicis) tests ulnar function. Phalen tests median/carpal tunnel.

## Q3 — Peripheral Nerve Entrapment (p. 275)

For a patient unable to perform the OK sign and unsure when symptoms started, which one of the following is correct?

- A. Immediate surgery is required.
- B. Pain is the predominant symptom in most cases.
- C. Ultrasonography should look for nerve entrapment in the Guyon canal.
- D. Electrodiagnostic testing can help confirm the diagnosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Electrodiagnostic testing can help confirm the diagnosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Inability to make the "OK" sign indicates anterior interosseous (median) nerve dysfunction. EMG/nerve conduction studies help confirm and time the lesion. Surgery is not immediate (A); Guyon canal is *ulnar*, not relevant here (C).

## Q4 — Difficult-to-Treat and Severe Asthma: Management Strategies (p. 286)

Which one of the following is the first step in the assessment of difficult-to-treat asthma?

- A. Optimize management.
- B. Confirm the diagnosis of asthma.
- C. Address contributing factors.
- D. Assess clinical response to therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Confirm the diagnosis of asthma.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Before escalating, verify it truly is asthma (spirometry/reversibility) — many "difficult" cases are misdiagnosis or comorbidity. Then address contributors/adherence/inhaler technique, optimize therapy, and reassess.

## Q5 — Difficult-to-Treat and Severe Asthma (p. 286)

Which one of the following test results suggests a patient with severe asthma will likely respond to biologic therapy?

- A. Decreased fractional exhaled nitric oxide.
- B. Negative allergy skin testing.
- C. Elevated blood or sputum eosinophils.
- D. Anatomic obstruction on chest CT.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Elevated blood or sputum eosinophils.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Type 2-high biomarkers — eosinophilia, elevated FeNO, and allergic sensitization — predict response to biologics (anti-IgE, anti-IL5, anti-IL4/13). Low FeNO and negative allergy testing argue against type 2 inflammation.

## Q6 — Difficult-to-Treat and Severe Asthma (p. 286)

Which one of the following nonpharmacologic treatments improves resting lung function in patients with asthma?

- A. Breathing exercises.
- B. Asthma self-management smartphone/tablet apps.
- C. High-dose vitamin D.
- D. Soy supplements.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Breathing exercises.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Breathing-retraining techniques improve lung function and quality of life. Vitamin D and soy supplements lack benefit; apps may aid self-management but don't improve resting spirometry.

## Q7 — Initiating Hormonal Contraception (p. 291)

Which one of the following patients lacks a contraindication for an estrogen-containing oral contraceptive?

- A. A 22-year-old who has never had a Pap test.
- B. A 42-year-old with a history of deep venous thrombosis.
- C. A 37-year-old who smokes a pack of cigarettes daily.
- D. A 42-year-old who is one month postpartum and breastfeeding.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — The 22-year-old who has never had a Pap test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> No pelvic exam or Pap is required before starting OCPs. The others are CDC MEC category 3–4: prior VTE (B), age ≥35 + smoking (C), and <21 days postpartum/breastfeeding (D) all contraindicate estrogen.

## Q8 — Initiating Hormonal Contraception (p. 291)

A 24-year-old with normal vital signs messages requesting transdermal hormonal contraception. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. The patient needs to come in for a blood pressure recheck.
- B. If she had an abnormal Pap in the past, repeat it before prescribing.
- C. With no significant medical history, it is reasonable to prescribe after proper counseling.
- D. Transdermal patches have a different adverse-effect profile, so guide her to a different choice.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — With no significant history, prescribe after counseling.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Only a recent blood pressure and a focused history are needed to start combined contraception; a recorded normal BP suffices (A wrong). Pap status doesn't gate contraception (B wrong). The patch is a valid option (D wrong).

## Q9 — Initiating Hormonal Contraception (p. 291)

Which one of the following statements about postpartum hormonal contraception is correct?

- A. A copper IUD can be placed within 10 minutes of delivery of the placenta.
- B. Lactation is nearly 100% effective as long as ≤50% of the infant's calories come from breast milk.
- C. The CDC supports starting estrogen-containing contraceptives in the immediate postpartum period.
- D. Progestin-only pills carry the same VTE risk as estrogen-containing pills.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A copper IUD can be placed within 10 minutes of placental delivery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Immediate postplacental IUD insertion is safe and recommended. The lactational amenorrhea method requires *exclusive* breastfeeding, amenorrhea, and <6 months (B wrong); estrogen is contraindicated early postpartum due to VTE risk (C wrong); POPs do **not** carry estrogen's VTE risk (D wrong).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Alcoholics Anonymous and Other 12-Step Facilitation Programs for Alcohol Use Disorder (p. 272)

A patient screens positive and is diagnosed with alcohol use disorder. Which statement about treatment is correct?

- A. When clinically indicated, offer pharmacotherapy and referral to behavioral support.
- B. AA and manualized 12-step facilitation programs improve continuous-abstinence rates compared with other established treatments.
- C. AA is free, available in most communities, and increasingly available online.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The updated Cochrane review found manualized AA/TSF achieves abstinence rates at least as good as (often better than) other treatments, AA is free/accessible, and pharmacotherapy plus behavioral support remain core management.

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Structural MRI for Early Diagnosis of Alzheimer Disease in Patients with MCI (p. 273)

Which one of the following statements about early diagnosis of Alzheimer disease in patients with MCI is correct?

- A. Patients with MCI progress to Alzheimer disease at ~10–15% per year vs. 1–2% per year in the general population.
- B. Structural brain MRI does not reliably predict development of Alzheimer disease in patients with MCI.
- C. Alzheimer disease is a clinical diagnosis based on impairment in memory, executive function, attention, or language.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The review found structural MRI has insufficient accuracy to predict MCI→Alzheimer progression (B); MCI converts at ~10–15%/yr (A); and Alzheimer remains a clinical diagnosis (C).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Colonoscopy Follow-up — U.S. Multi-Society Task Force on Colorectal Cancer (p. 314)

According to the USMSTF, which one of the following initial colonoscopy findings should result in repeat colonoscopy in one year?

- A. 12-mm adenoma with high-grade dysplasia.
- B. 14-mm hyperplastic polyp.
- C. 14 hyperplastic polyps each <10 mm.
- D. 12 tubular adenomas each <10 mm.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — 12 tubular adenomas (<10 mm) — i.e., ≥10 adenomas → 1-year follow-up.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Finding ≥10 adenomas warrants a 1-year interval (and consideration of a polyposis syndrome). A 12-mm adenoma with HGD → 3 years; small distal hyperplastic polyps → routine (10 years).
