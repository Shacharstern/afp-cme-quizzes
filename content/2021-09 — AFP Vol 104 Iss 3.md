## Q1 — Home Blood Pressure Monitoring (p. 237)
Which is considered the most accurate measure of a patient's blood pressure?

- A. Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring.
- B. Home blood pressure monitoring.
- C. In-office measurement with a mercury or aneroid cuff.
- D. In-office measurement with an automated device.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Ambulatory (24-hour) BP monitoring.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ABPM is the reference standard; it captures out-of-office readings and nocturnal dipping, predicts outcomes best, and detects white-coat and masked hypertension.

## Q2 — Home Blood Pressure Monitoring (p. 237)
Office BP is normal; the patient's correctly performed home readings are consistently elevated. This best describes:

- A. Confirmed hypertension.
- B. No hypertension.
- C. Masked hypertension.
- D. White coat hypertension.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Masked hypertension.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Masked HTN = normal in office, high out of office (mirror image of white-coat HTN). It carries elevated cardiovascular risk and is easily missed.

## Q3 — Home Blood Pressure Monitoring (p. 237)
Which statement about best practices for BP monitoring is correct?

- A. Wrist cuffs are a validated alternative to arm cuffs.
- B. Patients should be seated with back supported, feet on the floor, and arm resting on a flat surface.
- C. Patients should not measure BP for at least one hour after physical activity.
- D. Store BP kiosks can be used instead of home monitoring.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Seated, back supported, feet flat, arm on a flat surface at heart level.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Proper positioning is essential. Wrist cuffs and kiosks are not validated substitutes; wait ~30 min (not a full hour) after exercise/caffeine.

## Q4 — Kawasaki Disease vs MIS-C (p. 244)
A 3-year-old has 1 week of fever, diffuse maculopapular rash, and a 17-mm left cervical node. Labs: mildly elevated ESR, leukocytosis, anemia, thrombocytosis. Which statement is correct?

- A. Meets criteria for classic Kawasaki disease.
- B. Meets criteria for incomplete Kawasaki disease.
- C. Cannot diagnose KD until a negative SARS-CoV-2 test.
- D. Meets the case definition of MIS-C.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Incomplete Kawasaki disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Classic KD needs ≥4 of 5 principal criteria with fever; here only some are met, but fever ≥5 days plus supportive labs (↑ESR, anemia, thrombocytosis, leukocytosis) fulfills *incomplete* KD — important because coronary aneurysm risk persists.

## Q5 — Kawasaki Disease vs MIS-C (p. 244)
Which clarification helps distinguish KD from MIS-C?

- A. Cardiac manifestations are common in KD but rare in MIS-C.
- B. GI symptoms are common in KD but rare in MIS-C.
- C. KD patients rarely test positive for respiratory viruses, but MIS-C patients commonly do.
- D. COVID-19 exposure or positive testing is included in the MIS-C case definition but not KD.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — SARS-CoV-2 exposure/positivity is part of the MIS-C definition.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> MIS-C requires evidence of recent COVID-19. MIS-C tends to feature *more* GI and cardiac (myocardial dysfunction) involvement and older age than classic KD.

## Q6 — Kawasaki Disease vs MIS-C (p. 244)
Which statement about treatment is correct?

- A. Low-dose aspirin is recommended in KD and MIS-C to reduce coronary thrombosis risk.
- B. Steroids have no role in KD.
- C. IVIG is helpful in KD but not MIS-C.
- D. MIS-C can be safely treated as an outpatient.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Low-dose aspirin in both KD and MIS-C.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Aspirin (antithrombotic) is used in both. Steroids are adjuncts in high-risk/refractory KD; IVIG is first-line for both; MIS-C requires inpatient (often ICU) care.

## Q7 — Alcohol Withdrawal Syndrome: Outpatient Management (p. 253)
Which meets the diagnostic criteria for alcohol withdrawal syndrome?

- A. Reducing or stopping weekend alcohol use.
- B. A history of seizure disorder in the setting of alcohol use disorder.
- C. Development of agitation and anxiety one day after quitting alcohol use.
- D. Increased insomnia, anxiety, and alcohol use associated with a new job.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Agitation/anxiety appearing ~1 day after cessation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> AWS = autonomic and neuropsychiatric symptoms (tremor, anxiety, agitation) emerging after reduction/cessation in a dependent drinker, typically within 6–24 hours.

## Q8 — Alcohol Withdrawal Syndrome (p. 253)
Which patient could potentially be a candidate for outpatient treatment of AWS?

- A. A 42-year-old who drank in a suicide attempt.
- B. A 25-year-old with a seizure disorder requiring two medications.
- C. A 57-year-old with severe AWS symptoms 7 months prior.
- D. A 32-year-old with moderate agitation, accompanied by their spouse.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Moderate symptoms with a supportive companion at home.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Outpatient management suits mild–moderate AWS with reliable support and no high-risk features. Suicidality, seizure disorder, and prior severe withdrawal/DTs warrant inpatient care.

## Q9 — Alcohol Withdrawal Syndrome (p. 253)
Preferred monotherapy for patients with moderate AWS?

- A. Benzodiazepines.
- B. Acamprosate.
- C. Gabapentin (Neurontin).
- D. Motivational interviewing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Benzodiazepines.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Benzodiazepines are first-line for AWS (cross-tolerant with alcohol, reduce seizures/DTs). Gabapentin is an alternative for mild cases; acamprosate is for maintenance of abstinence, not acute withdrawal.

## Q10 — Hereditary Hemochromatosis: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 263)
Which statement about screening for hereditary hemochromatosis is correct?

- A. One-time ferritin + transferrin saturation screening is recommended for all adults.
- B. One-time genetic testing is recommended for all adults of European ancestry.
- C. Genetic testing is recommended starting at age 18 for first-degree relatives of affected people.
- D. More than 95% of C282Y homozygotes are symptomatic.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Test first-degree relatives genetically beginning at age 18.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Population screening is *not* recommended; penetrance is low (most C282Y homozygotes are asymptomatic). Cascade genetic testing of first-degree relatives is advised.

## Q11 — Hereditary Hemochromatosis (p. 263)
Recommended initial testing if hereditary hemochromatosis is clinically suspected?

- A. Genetic testing for C282Y and H63D mutations.
- B. Random serum ferritin and transferrin saturation.
- C. Fasting serum ferritin and transferrin saturation.
- D. Transferrin saturation, followed by ferritin if abnormal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Random serum ferritin and transferrin saturation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Iron studies (ferritin + transferrin saturation) are the initial tests; genetic testing follows abnormal iron studies. *Nuance:* classic teaching favored fasting morning samples, but this review treats random measurements as adequate for initial evaluation. Answer confirmed.

## Q12 — Hereditary Hemochromatosis (p. 263)
Generally considered first-line therapy for hereditary hemochromatosis?

- A. Phlebotomy, beginning ~500 mL/week until serum ferritin normalizes.
- B. Lifelong monthly phlebotomy, removing 500 mL/week initially.
- C. Chelation therapy.
- D. Combination of phlebotomy and chelation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Induction phlebotomy ~500 mL weekly until ferritin normalizes.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Therapeutic phlebotomy is first-line: weekly until iron stores normalize, then less-frequent maintenance. Chelation is reserved for those who cannot tolerate phlebotomy (e.g., severe anemia).

## Q13 — Splenomegaly: Diagnosis and Management (p. 271)
Best physical exam maneuver for detecting splenomegaly?

- A. Palpation in the left lateral decubitus position.
- B. Percussion in the lowest left intercostal space.
- C. Palpation while supine with legs flat.
- D. Percussion during deep inhalation while standing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Percussion of the lowest left intercostal space (Castell sign / Traube space dullness).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Percussion is more sensitive than palpation for detecting an enlarged spleen; palpation (best in right lateral decubitus) confirms larger spleens.

## Q14 — Splenomegaly (p. 271)
A 19-year-old refugee from Cameroon living in southern California has an enlarged spleen. Most likely cause?

- A. Heart failure.
- B. Babesiosis.
- C. Lymphoma.
- D. Chronic malaria.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Chronic malaria.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In patients from malaria-endemic regions, chronic/hyperreactive malarial splenomegaly is the leading cause — epidemiology drives the answer.

## Q15 — Splenomegaly (p. 271)
A 42-year-old has splenomegaly with no cause suggested by history or exam. Which initial evaluation is most likely to suggest the diagnosis?

- A. CBC and complete metabolic panel.
- B. Heterophile antibody test.
- C. Tuberculosis testing.
- D. C-reactive protein.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — CBC and CMP.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> First-line workup of unexplained splenomegaly is a CBC (with smear) and CMP/LFTs, which point toward hematologic, hepatic, or infectious causes and direct further testing.

## Q16 — Neglected Parasitic Infections (CDC Update) (p. 277)
Which parasitic disease may cause serious cardiac disease (conduction abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, aneurysms, heart failure)?

- A. Chagas disease.
- B. Cysticercosis.
- C. Taeniasis.
- D. Toxoplasmosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Chagas disease (*Trypanosoma cruzi*).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Chronic Chagas causes cardiomyopathy, conduction blocks, apical aneurysms, and heart failure — increasingly relevant in immigrants from Latin America.

## Q17 — Neglected Parasitic Infections (p. 277)
Which parasitic infection is the most common cause of ED visits for seizures?

- A. Chagas disease.
- B. Neurocysticercosis.
- C. Taeniasis.
- D. Toxoplasmosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Neurocysticercosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Neurocysticercosis (larval *Taenia solium* in the CNS) is a leading cause of acquired epilepsy worldwide and a common cause of seizure-related ED visits.

## Q18 — Neglected Parasitic Infections (p. 277)
Which parasitic infection is commonly acquired by ingesting food or water contaminated by cat feces?

- A. Chagas disease.
- B. Cysticercosis.
- C. Taeniasis.
- D. Toxoplasmosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Toxoplasmosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> *Toxoplasma gondii* oocysts are shed in cat feces; ingestion (contaminated food/water/litter) or undercooked meat transmits it — major concern in pregnancy and immunosuppression.

## Q19 — Cochrane: Pelvic Floor Muscle Training (Antenatal/Postnatal) (p. 234)
A patient 3 months postpartum has involuntary loss of urine and occasionally stool. Which statement about pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT) is correct?

- A. Starting PFMT after symptoms develop will not improve incontinence.
- B. Starting PFMT early in pregnancy may prevent incontinence later in pregnancy.
- C. Starting PFMT early in pregnancy may prevent postpartum incontinence.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Per this Cochrane review, early antenatal PFMT prevents both late-pregnancy and postpartum incontinence (B, C). *Nuance:* statement A reflects the review's finding that evidence for *treating* established incontinence with PFMT was less certain — accept as keyed. Answer confirmed.

## Q20 — Cochrane: Reducing Saturated Fat Intake (p. 235)
Which statement about reducing dietary saturated fat for ≥2 years is correct?

- A. It decreases all-cause mortality.
- B. It decreases cardiovascular mortality.
- C. It decreases the risk of combined cardiovascular events.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Reduces combined cardiovascular events.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cochrane found reducing saturated fat lowers combined CV events (~17%) but shows *no significant* effect on all-cause or cardiovascular mortality — so "all of the above" is wrong.

## Q21 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Colorectal Cancer (p. 295)
A 47-year-old, no personal/family CRC history, BMI 29, A1C 5.9%, never screened. Per the USPSTF, how should this patient be counseled?

- A. Only increased-risk adults should begin at 45.
- B. Regardless of risk factors, all patients should be screened starting at 45.
- C. This patient is at increased risk because of BMI and A1C and should be screened today.
- D. With no personal/family history, routine screening begins at 50.
- E. The net benefit is small; refer only with a strong preference after shared decision-making.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Begin CRC screening at age 45 for all average-risk adults.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The 2021 USPSTF update lowered the routine screening start age to 45 (grade B for 45–49). BMI and A1C don't change the average-risk classification here.

## Q22 — Lown Right Care: Evaluation of Syncope (p. 305)
Which statement about the evaluation of a patient with syncope is correct?

- A. Continuous telemetry monitoring improves survival.
- B. Less than 10% of syncope episodes have no identifiable cause.
- C. Hospitalization for evaluation of syncope is associated with increased adverse events.
- D. Carotid artery imaging is routinely recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Hospitalization for syncope workup is associated with increased adverse events.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Low-risk syncope is over-hospitalized; admission adds iatrogenic risk without clear benefit. Telemetry doesn't improve survival, ~one-third remain unexplained, and carotid imaging is not routine (syncope ≠ stroke mechanism).

## Q23 — Point-of-Care Guides: Lower Extremity Cellulitis vs Mimics (p. 309)
Which is NOT an independent predictor of lower extremity cellulitis?

- A. Age 70 years or older.
- B. Involvement of only one leg.
- C. Elevated white blood cell count.
- D. Heart rate below 90 beats per minute.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Heart rate below 90 bpm.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The prediction rule's predictors are older age (≥70), asymmetry/unilateral involvement, leukocytosis, and *tachycardia*. A normal/low heart rate is not predictive (it argues against, if anything).

## Q24 — Practice Guidelines: Cervical Cancer Screening (ACS) (p. 314)
Per the American Cancer Society, the most effective screening interval to prevent cervical cancer in patients with a cervix?

- A. Cytology alone every 3 years starting at 21.
- B. Primary HPV testing (without cytology) every 5 years starting at 25.
- C. Cotesting (cytology + HPV) every 5 years starting at 25.
- D. Cytology every 3 years from 21, shifting to cotesting at 30.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Primary HPV testing every 5 years starting at age 25.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The 2020 ACS guideline prefers primary HPV testing every 5 years beginning at 25 (cotesting or cytology are acceptable alternatives where primary HPV testing isn't available). Note USPSTF still allows starting cytology at 21.

## Q25 — Practice Guidelines: VA/DoD Headache Management (p. 316)
Per the VA/DoD, which medication class is recommended for migraine prophylaxis, especially in patients with hypertension?

- A. Diuretics.
- B. Calcium channel blockers.
- C. Alpha blockers.
- D. Angiotensin II receptor blockers.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Angiotensin II receptor blockers (e.g., candesartan).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Candesartan has evidence for migraine prophylaxis and conveniently treats coexisting hypertension. (Beta-blockers are also classic prophylactics with an antihypertensive bonus.)
