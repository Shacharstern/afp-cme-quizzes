## Q1 — Current Concepts in Concussion: Initial Evaluation and Management (p. 426)

Which one of the following statements about concussion is correct?

- A. Loss of consciousness is required for diagnosis.
- B. Headache is the most common symptom.
- C. Loss of consciousness is highly predictive of prolonged recovery.
- D. Symptoms typically present 72 hours after injury.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Headache is the most common concussion symptom.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most concussions occur **without** loss of consciousness (A), and brief LOC doesn't strongly predict prolonged recovery (C). Symptoms usually appear **immediately to within hours**, not 72 hours later (D).

## Q2 — Current Concepts in Concussion (p. 426)

A family physician has volunteered to treat injuries for a school sports team. Which one of the following statements about evaluating concussions is correct?

- A. Preinjury baseline testing is recommended, especially in younger children.
- B. The tests in the Sport Concussion Assessment Tool 5 series are considered the most comprehensive sideline assessment tools available.
- C. Magnetic resonance imaging is the modality of choice.
- D. Plain radiography should be obtained if concussion is suspected.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — The SCAT5 series is the most comprehensive sideline assessment tool.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Routine **baseline testing is not recommended** (especially young children, A); concussion is a **clinical** diagnosis — imaging (CT for red flags, not routine MRI/x-ray) is to exclude structural injury (C, D wrong).

## Q3 — Current Concepts in Concussion (p. 426)

An athlete was unconscious for a short period after a blow to the head. He is sent home the same day, and his main symptom is a headache. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. He may return to play in 24 to 48 hours.
- B. A five-day period of strict rest is recommended.
- C. He will have a prolonged recovery because of loss of consciousness.
- D. Gradual resumption of activities may expedite recovery.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Gradual, symptom-limited return to activity may speed recovery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> After a brief (24–48 h) relative rest, **graded return** is favored over prolonged strict rest (B wrong); never same-week return to play (A); brief LOC doesn't reliably predict prolonged recovery (C wrong).

## Q4 — Abnormal Uterine Bleeding in Premenopausal Women (p. 435)

Which one of the following statements about endometrial sampling in women with abnormal uterine bleeding is correct?

- A. It is indicated for all women with abnormal uterine bleeding.
- B. It is generally indicated only for women 45 years and older.
- C. It is indicated for all women 45 years and older, and for selected younger women at increased risk.
- D. Endometrial biopsy is not recommended in the primary care setting.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Sample all women ≥45, plus younger women with risk factors.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Endometrial biopsy is indicated at **≥45** and in younger women with risk factors for hyperplasia/cancer (unopposed estrogen, obesity, PCOS, tamoxifen, Lynch syndrome, failed therapy). It's not needed for *every* AUB patient (A) and **can** be done in primary care (D wrong).

## Q5 — Abnormal Uterine Bleeding in Premenopausal Women (p. 435)

Which one of the following is the most effective medical therapy for patients with chronic abnormal uterine bleeding?

- A. Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system (Mirena).
- B. Estrogen-progestin oral contraceptives.
- C. Oral tranexamic acid (Lysteda).
- D. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — The levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system (Mirena).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The LNG-IUS reduces menstrual blood loss the most among medical options and is first-line for chronic heavy bleeding. OCPs, tranexamic acid, and NSAIDs are effective but less so.

## Q6 — Abnormal Uterine Bleeding in Premenopausal Women (p. 435)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment for heavy or emergent uterine bleeding is correct?

- A. Hysterectomy is often needed.
- B. Uterine tamponade is not recommended because of a high risk of infection.
- C. Uterine artery embolization is not a treatment option in this setting.
- D. Oral treatment is sufficient for most hemodynamically stable patients.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Most hemodynamically stable patients can be managed with oral therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Stable acute bleeding responds to oral hormonal therapy (e.g., high-dose estrogen/progestin). Hysterectomy is a **last resort** (A); **tamponade and uterine artery embolization are valid** options for refractory bleeding (B, C wrong).

## Q7 — Febrile Seizures: Risks, Evaluation, and Prognosis (p. 445)

Which one of the following diagnostic tests should be included in the routine evaluation of a well-appearing child with a simple febrile seizure?

- A. Laboratory tests for serum sodium and blood glucose levels.
- B. No routine diagnostic testing is recommended.
- C. Electroencephalography within two weeks.
- D. Magnetic resonance imaging of the head within two weeks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — No routine diagnostic testing is recommended.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A simple febrile seizure in a well-appearing child needs **no routine labs, EEG, or neuroimaging** — focus on identifying the source of fever.

## Q8 — Febrile Seizures (p. 445)

Which one of the following statements about the use of antipyretic medications after immunizations to prevent febrile seizures is correct?

- A. They should be given after all childhood immunizations.
- B. They should be given only after measles-mumps-rubella-varicella vaccination.
- C. They should be given only after influenza vaccination.
- D. They are not recommended after any immunizations.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Prophylactic antipyretics after immunizations are not recommended.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Prophylactic antipyretics don't prevent febrile seizures and may **blunt the immune response** to vaccines — so they're not recommended for this purpose.

## Q9 — Febrile Seizures (p. 445)

Which one of the following is **not** a feature of a complex seizure?

- A. Duration of 15 minutes or more.
- B. Focal neurologic signs.
- C. Patient age younger than three years.
- D. Recurs within 24 hours.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Patient age younger than three years.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A **complex** febrile seizure is defined by being **focal, prolonged (≥15 min), or recurrent within 24 hours** (A, B, D). Age is not a defining criterion.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Direct Oral Anticoagulants vs. Warfarin in Atrial Fibrillation and CKD (p. 422)

Which one of the following statements about the use of direct oral anticoagulants vs. warfarin for the prevention of stroke and thromboembolism in patients with atrial fibrillation and chronic kidney disease (CKD) is correct?

- A. Patients treated with direct oral anticoagulants have an equivalent risk of stroke.
- B. Patients treated with direct oral anticoagulants have a decreased risk of major bleeding events.
- C. Patients treated with direct oral anticoagulants have a decreased risk of all-cause mortality.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — DOACs confer an equivalent stroke risk vs. warfarin in AF with CKD.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In mild-moderate CKD, DOACs are at least as effective for stroke prevention. The review did **not** demonstrate clear reductions in major bleeding or all-cause mortality (B, C), so "all of the above" is wrong (D).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Mild TBI in Children — CDC Guidance (p. 462)

According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), which one of the following statements about the evaluation and management of mild traumatic brain injury (TBI) in children is correct?

- A. Magnetic resonance imaging is the modality of choice in children with acute symptoms.
- B. Hypertonic saline 3% is the preferred rehydration solution for acute headache in the emergency department setting.
- C. There are no validated clinical decision rules to determine the appropriateness of computed tomography.
- D. Most symptoms resolve in one to three months after TBI, with significant difficulties rarely lasting longer.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Most symptoms resolve within one to three months; lasting difficulty is uncommon.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Reassuring recovery counseling is key. MRI isn't the acute modality (A); IV hypertonic saline isn't standard ED headache therapy (B); **validated CT decision rules (e.g., PECARN) do exist** (C wrong).
