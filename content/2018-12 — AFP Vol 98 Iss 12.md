## Q1 — Counseling Patients in Primary Care: Evidence-Based Strategies (p. 719)

Which one of the following strategies assesses a patient's degree of commitment to change and can guide physicians in choosing a counseling technique?

- A. FRAMES (feedback, responsibility, advice, menu, empathy, self-efficacy).
- B. Five A's (ask, advise, assess, assist, arrange).
- C. Transtheoretical (stages of change) model.
- D. BATHE (background, affect, troubles, handling, empathy) strategy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The transtheoretical (stages of change) model.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Staging readiness (precontemplation → maintenance) tells you which counseling technique to use. FRAMES and the Five A's are intervention frameworks; BATHE is a brief psychosocial interview.

## Q2 — Counseling Patients in Primary Care (p. 719)

Which one of the following is the most appropriate question to ask a patient in the precontemplation stage of smoking cessation?

- A. "What do you see as the pros and cons of continuing to smoke?"
- B. "Is it okay if I talk to you about your smoking?"
- C. "What would be the best day in the next month to stop smoking?"
- D. "How can I help you be successful in quitting smoking?"

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — "Is it okay if I talk to you about your smoking?"** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> In **precontemplation** the patient isn't yet considering change, so **ask permission** and raise awareness gently. Decisional-balance (A) suits contemplation; setting a quit day (C) and "how can I help you quit" (D) suit preparation/action.

## Q3 — Counseling Patients in Primary Care (p. 719)

Which one of the following statements about the BATHE strategy is correct?

- A. "Tell me more about that" would be a useful background prompt.
- B. Close monitoring after the initial intervention is not necessary.
- C. BATHE takes too long to use in the primary care setting.
- D. BATHE improves patient satisfaction.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — BATHE improves patient satisfaction.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> BATHE is **brief** and enhances the therapeutic relationship/satisfaction (so C is wrong). The background prompt is "What is going on in your life?" (A mislabels it); follow-up still matters (B wrong).

## Q4 — The Adult Well-Male Examination (p. 729)

Which one of the following statements about screening men for dyslipidemia is correct?

- A. The preferred screening test is the nonfasting lipoprotein profile.
- B. Lipid screening should start at 20 years of age.
- C. Lipid screening should stop at 65 years of age.
- D. Screening intervals are uncertain according to the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — The USPSTF does not specify a screening interval (intervals are uncertain).** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The USPSTF assesses cardiovascular risk (≈ages 40–75) without a defined repeat interval. Screening doesn't start at 20 or stop at 65 as fixed rules (B, C wrong); nonfasting testing is acceptable but not framed as the single "preferred" test here (A).

## Q5 — The Adult Well-Male Examination (p. 729)

Which one of the following screening recommendations for men is correct?

- A. The USPSTF recommends screening for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease using spirometry.
- B. Fecal DNA testing is more accurate than fecal occult blood testing for colorectal cancer screening.
- C. The USPSTF recommends annual screening for lung cancer with low-dose computed tomography in at-risk smokers 55 to 80 years of age.
- D. The National Osteoporosis Foundation recommends bone mineral density testing for all men starting at 65 years of age.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Annual low-dose CT lung cancer screening for at-risk smokers 55–80.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends **against** spirometry screening for COPD (A); NOF bone density testing in men starts at **70** (D); the FIT-DNA "accuracy" claim is overstated (B). (Note: the USPSTF later lowered lung screening to age 50/20 pack-years.)

## Q6 — The Adult Well-Male Examination (p. 729)

Which one of the following screening tests does the USPSTF recommend for all men?

- A. Testicular examination for cancer.
- B. Skin examination for cancer.
- C. Body mass index measurement for obesity.
- D. Blood glucose levels for diabetes mellitus.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — BMI measurement to screen for obesity.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends **against** testicular self/clinical exam screening (A), finds skin-exam evidence insufficient (B), and recommends diabetes screening only for **overweight/older** adults, not all men (D).

## Q7 — School Absenteeism in Children and Adolescents (p. 738)

Which one of the following statements about chronic school absenteeism is correct?

- A. Excused absences are not detrimental.
- B. Nationally, less than 5% of kindergarteners and first graders are chronically absent.
- C. Absenteeism rates are similar for males and females.
- D. Absenteeism is most commonly seen in high school.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Chronic absenteeism is most common in high school.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Rates are highest in high school (with a secondary peak in the earliest grades). Excused absences still harm achievement (A wrong); early-grade chronic absence exceeds 5% (B wrong).

## Q8 — School Absenteeism in Children and Adolescents (p. 738)

Which one of the following strategies to prevent chronic absenteeism is considered a family-level intervention?

- A. Pharmacotherapy for a child with anxiety.
- B. Support for parental treatment of depression.
- C. Serving as the consulting physician to a school multidisciplinary team.
- D. Center-based preschool program.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Supporting treatment of a parent's depression (a family-level intervention).** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> Addressing **parental** mental health targets the family system. Treating the child's anxiety is **individual**-level (A); school-team consultation is **school**-level (C); preschool programs are **community/system**-level (D).

## Q9 — Point-of-Care Guides: Evaluating the Benefits and Harms of Anticoagulation in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation (p. 751)

Which one of the following is a risk factor for both stroke and major bleeding?

- A. Female sex.
- B. Hypertension.
- C. Abnormal liver function.
- D. History of vascular disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Hypertension.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> **Hypertension appears in both CHA₂DS₂-VASc (stroke) and HAS-BLED (bleeding).** Abnormal liver function and (in CHA₂DS₂-VASc) vascular disease/female sex sit in only one of the two scores.

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Peripheral Artery Disease and CVD Risk with the Ankle-Brachial Index (p. 754)

The USPSTF recommendation on screening for peripheral artery disease (PAD) and assessing cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk with the ankle-brachial index applies to which one of the following populations?

- A. All asymptomatic adults and adolescents.
- B. Adults at high risk for PAD or CVD.
- C. Asymptomatic adults without a known diagnosis of PAD, CVD, or severe chronic kidney disease.
- D. Asymptomatic men 60 years and older.
- E. Asymptomatic women 75 years and older.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Asymptomatic adults without known PAD, CVD, or severe CKD.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> That defines the population for the USPSTF **I statement** (insufficient evidence) on ABI screening. The recommendation isn't restricted to "high-risk," age, or sex subgroups (B, D, E) and doesn't cover adolescents (A).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Crohn's Disease — ACG Updated Management Guidelines (p. 756)

According to the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG), which one of the following statements about the treatment of Crohn's disease is correct?

- A. Persons with mild to moderate Crohn's disease should be treated with oral mesalamine.
- B. Antibiotics should be avoided to prevent disease flares.
- C. Short-term oral corticosteroids should be avoided in moderate to severe Crohn's disease.
- D. In persons with moderate to severe Crohn's disease who are naïve to treatment with infliximab (Remicade) or immunomodulators, treatment with these medications combined is more effective than monotherapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Combination infliximab + immunomodulator beats monotherapy in treatment-naïve moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease.** 🧠 Generated-validated · Confidence: High
> This reflects the SONIC trial. **Mesalamine is ineffective in Crohn's** (A wrong); short-term steroids **are used** to induce remission (C wrong); antibiotics have a role in specific situations (B overstated).
