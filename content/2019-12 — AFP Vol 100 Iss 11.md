## Q1 — Hypothermia and Cold Weather Injuries (p. 680)

In which one of the following circumstances is terminating or avoiding advanced cardiac life support without rewarming reasonable for hypothermic patients?

- A. Nonlethal injury.
- B. Patent airway.
- C. Snow burial for more than 35 minutes.
- D. Serum potassium level less than 12.0 mEq per L (12.0 mmol per L).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Snow (avalanche) burial for more than 35 minutes (with an obstructed airway/asystole).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> "No one is dead until warm and dead" — but resuscitation can be withheld for obviously lethal injury, a frozen chest, prolonged burial with a packed airway, or **serum potassium >12** (so **<12** favors *continuing*, D reversed).

## Q2 — Hypothermia and Cold Weather Injuries (p. 680)

Which one of the following medications reduces the risk of reperfusion injuries in people with frostbite?

- A. Ibuprofen.
- B. Amitriptyline.
- C. Empiric antibiotics.
- D. Morphine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Ibuprofen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Ibuprofen inhibits **thromboxane**, limiting the prostaglandin-mediated reperfusion/vascular injury of frostbite. The others manage pain/infection but don't reduce reperfusion injury.

## Q3 — Hypothermia and Cold Weather Injuries (p. 680)

Which one of the following is an appropriate treatment for immersion foot?

- A. Rapid rewarming.
- B. Opioids.
- C. Walking on the affected limb.
- D. Amitriptyline.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Amitriptyline (for the neuropathic pain).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Immersion (trench) foot causes painful neuropathy treated with agents like **amitriptyline**. Avoid rapid rewarming, weight-bearing on the affected limb, and rely-on-opioid strategies (A, B, C wrong).

## Q4 — Acute Kidney Injury: Diagnosis and Management (p. 687)

Hypovolemia can cause which of the following types of acute kidney injury?

- A. Prerenal and intrinsic renal.
- B. Prerenal and postrenal.
- C. Intrinsic renal and postrenal.
- D. Prerenal, intrinsic renal, and postrenal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Prerenal and intrinsic renal.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Hypovolemia first causes **prerenal** AKI (reduced perfusion); if severe/prolonged it progresses to **intrinsic** AKI (ischemic acute tubular necrosis). It does not cause postrenal (obstructive) AKI.

## Q5 — Acute Kidney Injury (p. 687)

Which one of the following is the most accurate measure of renal function in patients with acute kidney injury?

- A. Serum creatinine level.
- B. Creatinine/albumin ratio.
- C. Urine output.
- D. Creatinine clearance.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Creatinine clearance.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Measured **creatinine clearance** best estimates GFR. In dynamic AKI, a single serum creatinine **lags** behind true function (A), and urine output is nonspecific (C).

## Q6 — Acute Kidney Injury (p. 687)

Which one of the following statements about renal replacement therapy for patients with acute kidney injury is correct?

- A. Early initiation of renal replacement therapy improves 90-day mortality rates compared with delayed therapy.
- B. Delayed initiation of renal replacement therapy improves 90-day mortality rates compared with early therapy.
- C. Early nephrology consultation on when to initiate renal replacement therapy is beneficial.
- D. Patients with stage 3 acute kidney injury requiring renal replacement therapy have mortality rates less than 10%.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Early nephrology consultation about timing of renal replacement therapy is beneficial.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Trials show **no clear mortality difference** between early and delayed RRT (A, B wrong); severe AKI requiring RRT carries **high mortality** (far above 10%, D wrong) — so individualized, nephrology-guided timing is advised.

## Q7 — Nonspecific Low Back Pain and Return to Work (p. 697)

Which one of the following statements about the evaluation of patients with nonspecific low back pain is correct?

- A. Magnetic resonance imaging identifies the underlying cause of pain in most cases.
- B. Immunocompromise is a risk factor for cauda equina.
- C. Obtaining early imaging in patients with a history of steroid use.
- D. A history of malignancy is an established red flag for tumor.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A history of malignancy is an established red flag for spinal tumor.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Prior cancer strongly raises concern for metastasis → image. MRI often shows **incidental** findings, not the pain source (A); immunocompromise/steroids are red flags for **infection**, not cauda equina (B, C miscategorized).

## Q8 — Nonspecific Low Back Pain and Return to Work (p. 697)

A 32-year-old warehouse worker who lifts heavy boxes asks how to prevent low back pain. Which one of the following statements is most supported by existing evidence?

- A. He should wear a back brace every day.
- B. His exercise regimen should include core strengthening.
- C. He should wear over-the-counter insoles and replace them every six months.
- D. Back stretching routines are not helpful.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — His exercise regimen should include core strengthening.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Exercise (including core strengthening)** is the best-supported prevention. Back belts and shoe insoles do **not** prevent low back pain (A, C wrong), and stretching/exercise **is** helpful (D wrong).

## Q9 — Nonspecific Low Back Pain and Return to Work (p. 697)

A 45-year-old forklift driver hurts her back at work. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. An initial wait-and-see approach is recommended for this patient.
- B. Active litigation predicts a faster recovery.
- C. Her age is a good predictor of long-term prognosis.
- D. Being out of work for more than 30 days correlates with longer-term disability overall.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Being out of work for more than 30 days correlates with longer-term disability.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The longer a worker is off, the lower the chance of return — favor **early activity/return to modified duty**, not wait-and-see (A). Litigation predicts **worse** recovery (B); psychosocial factors outweigh age for prognosis (C).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: General Health Checks in Adults for Reducing Disease-Related Morbidity and Mortality (p. 676)

Which one of the following statements about general health checks is correct?

- A. They decrease overall mortality.
- B. They decrease cardiovascular mortality.
- C. They decrease cancer-related mortality.
- D. There is no evidence that they cause patient harm.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — There is no clear evidence that they cause patient harm.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Routine general health checks did **not** reduce overall, cardiovascular, or cancer mortality (A, B, C wrong) — but the trials also didn't demonstrate measurable harm. The takeaway: little benefit, so target specific evidence-based screening instead.

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Combined Oral Contraceptives for Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (p. 677)

Which one of the following statements about women who use combined oral contraceptives for the treatment of menorrhagia is correct?

- A. Fewer patients report menorrhagia over six months compared with placebo.
- B. Patients have improved quality-of-life scores.
- C. Patients have improved hemoglobin levels.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Combined oral contraceptives reduce reported heavy bleeding, improve quality of life, and raise hemoglobin compared with placebo — a reasonable medical option (the LNG-IUS remains most effective).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Diagnosing VTE — American Society of Hematology (p. 716)

According to the American Society of Hematology, which one of the following is the preferred initial study to diagnose a pulmonary embolism in a patient with intermediate risk based on the Wells score and a positive d-dimer result?

- A. No further testing or treatment is needed.
- B. Ventilation-perfusion scan.
- C. Computed tomography pulmonary angiography.
- D. Proximal lower extremity ultrasonography with a repeat scan in one week if negative.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Ventilation-perfusion (V/Q) scan.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ASH suggests **V/Q scanning over CTPA** when feasible (lower radiation, fewer incidental findings) for intermediate-probability PE. A positive D-dimer rules out "no testing" (A); CTPA is an alternative when V/Q isn't suitable (C).
