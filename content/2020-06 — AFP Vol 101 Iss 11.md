# AFP CME Quiz — June 1, 2020 (Vol 101, Issue 11)

## Q1 — Nerve Blocks: Part I. Upper Extremity (p. 654)

Which one of the following statements about ultrasound-guided nerve blocks is correct?

- A. They require more time for anesthesia to take effect.
- B. There is a slightly shorter duration of anesthesia than when peripheral nerve stimulation-guided nerve blocks are used.
- C. They lead to fewer complications compared with other anesthetic techniques.
- D. They are more time consuming than peripheral nerve stimulation-guided nerve blocks.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ultrasound guidance leads to fewer complications.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Real-time visualization improves accuracy and safety (fewer vascular punctures/nerve injuries), with **faster** onset, **longer** block duration, and **less** procedure time than nerve-stimulation guidance (A, B, D wrong).

## Q2 — Nerve Blocks: Part I (p. 654)

When performing ultrasound-guided nerve blocks, which one of the following accurately reflects the appropriate setup?

- A. Use of sterile gloves is required.
- B. Compared with povidone-iodine paint, 0.5% chlorhexidine skin preparation lowers rates of surgical site infections from intact skin procedures.
- C. An 18-gauge needle is recommended for most procedures.
- D. The patient should be positioned so that the line of the injection site is perpendicular to the ultrasound screen.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — 0.5% chlorhexidine lowers SSI rates compared with povidone-iodine.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Chlorhexidine is the preferred skin antiseptic. Clean (not sterile) gloves suffice for most blocks (A); a **smaller-gauge** needle is used (C); the needle/injection line should be **parallel** to the transducer for in-plane visualization (D wrong).

## Q3 — Nerve Blocks: Part I (p. 654)

A patient presents for removal of a deeply embedded foreign body in the hand using an ultrasound-guided nerve block. Which one of the following has been found to be effective in reducing postoperative pain intensity and opioid use?

- A. Lidocaine.
- B. Bupivacaine (Marcaine).
- C. Ropivacaine (Naropin).
- D. Dexamethasone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Dexamethasone (as a perineural adjuvant).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Adding **dexamethasone** to the local anesthetic prolongs block duration and reduces postoperative pain and opioid use. The anesthetics themselves (lidocaine/bupivacaine/ropivacaine) provide the block but aren't the pain-reducing *adjuvant* asked about.

## Q4 — Nerve Blocks: Part II. Lower Extremity (p. 669)

Which one of the following statements about distal sciatic nerve blocks is correct?

- A. They should not be used to manage procedures that cross multiple dermatomes.
- B. A landmark-guided injection is the preferred technique.
- C. Injecting the anesthetic into the perineural space should be avoided.
- D. Injection proximal to the bifurcation in the popliteal fossa spares the hamstrings.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Injecting proximal to the popliteal bifurcation spares the hamstrings.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A popliteal sciatic block placed proximal to the tibial/peroneal bifurcation provides broad foot/ankle anesthesia while sparing hamstring motor function. **Ultrasound** (not landmark) is preferred (B); perineural injection is the goal (C); blocks can cover multiple dermatomes (A wrong).

## Q5 — Nerve Blocks: Part II (p. 669)

Which one of the following statements about lower extremity nerve blocks is correct?

- A. Preprocedural tibial nerve blocks do not reduce the pain of plantar fascia injections.
- B. Deep peroneal nerve blocks numb the first web space of the foot.
- C. Ultrasound guidance increases complications when compared with other techniques.
- D. The anesthetic should not be injected into the superficial peroneal nerve.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Deep peroneal nerve blocks numb the first web space of the foot.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The deep peroneal nerve supplies the **first dorsal web space**. Tibial nerve blocks **do** reduce plantar fascia injection pain (A wrong); ultrasound **reduces** complications (C wrong); superficial peroneal blocks are appropriate (D wrong).

## Q6 — Nerve Blocks: Part II (p. 669)

Which one of the following nerve blocks should be used for meralgia paresthetica?

- A. Lateral femoral cutaneous.
- B. Sural.
- C. Tibial.
- D. Saphenous.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Meralgia paresthetica is entrapment of the **lateral femoral cutaneous nerve** (anterolateral thigh numbness/burning) — so that's the nerve to block.

## Q7 — Newborn Circumcision Techniques (p. 680)

Which one of the following statements about newborn male circumcision is correct?

- A. There is a higher risk of penile cancer.
- B. There is a higher risk of HIV.
- C. Circumcision outside of the newborn period is associated with less complications.
- D. There is a lower risk of urinary tract infections.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Circumcision lowers the risk of urinary tract infections.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Circumcision reduces UTIs, penile cancer, and HIV/STI transmission (A, B reversed). Performing it **outside** the newborn period carries **more** complications, not fewer (C wrong).

## Q8 — Newborn Circumcision Techniques (p. 680)

Which one of the following statements about circumcision techniques is correct?

- A. All of the techniques are equally effective.
- B. The Mogen clamp induces tissue necrosis.
- C. When using the Gomco clamp, there is no need to estimate the diameter of the glans penis.
- D. Procedures using the Plastibell usually take less time than those that use the Mogen clamp.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — All of the techniques are equally effective.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Gomco, Mogen, and Plastibell are all effective; choice depends on training/preference. The **Plastibell/Gomco** rely on pressure necrosis (not Mogen, B); Gomco requires **estimating the bell/glans size** (C wrong); the **Mogen is the fastest** technique (D wrong).

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Emergency Contraception — Safety and Effectiveness (p. 651)

Your patient wishes to discuss emergency contraception but is not interested in a copper IUD. Which one of the following is considered safe and effective for emergency contraception?

- A. Oral mifepristone (Mifeprex).
- B. Ulipristal (Ella).
- C. Ethinyl estradiol/levonorgestrel.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mid/low-dose mifepristone, ulipristal acetate, and the (older) Yuzpe combined estrogen-progestin regimen are all effective oral emergency contraceptives — ulipristal and the copper IUD being the most effective options.

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Potential of Fetal Fibronectin Testing to Prevent Preterm Birth (p. 652)

Which one of the following statements about the use of fetal fibronectin testing is correct?

- A. Knowing the results reduces preterm births before 34 weeks.
- B. Knowing the results reduces perinatal death.
- C. Knowing the results reduces admissions to neonatal intensive care units.
- D. The evidence is insufficient to recommend fetal fibronectin testing to prevent preterm birth and associated outcomes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Evidence is insufficient to recommend fetal fibronectin testing for preventing preterm birth.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Knowing fFN results has **not** been shown to reduce preterm birth, perinatal death, or NICU admissions (A, B, C wrong) — its main value is its high **negative** predictive value for ruling out imminent delivery.

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Preparticipation Physical Evaluation — AAFP and Others (p. 692)

According to the AAFP, which one of the following statements about a 14-year-old female starting high school soccer is correct?

- A. She should receive full preparticipation physical evaluations (PPEs) every year through her high school athletic career.
- B. She should receive her PPE from her primary care physician.
- C. She should receive a station-based examination with her teammates, where stations focus on specific elements of the PPE.
- D. She should receive a PPE two weeks before the start of the soccer season.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — She should receive her PPE from her primary care physician.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The PPE is best done by the athlete's **own clinician** within the medical home (continuity, full history). A full PPE every 2–3 years with interim updates suffices (A); the **office-based** exam is preferred over mass station-based screening (C); the PPE should be ~**6 weeks** before the season (D wrong).
