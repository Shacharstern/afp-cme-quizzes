## Q1 — Geriatric Assessment: An Office-Based Approach (p. 776)

Which one of the following statements about the risk of falling in older adults is correct?

- A. Reduced gait speed is associated with a lower risk of falling.
- B. The best method to determine a patient's risk of falling is to follow the Beers criteria.
- C. The question "Have you fallen in the past year?" effectively screens for future fall risk.
- D. Qualitative evaluations of gait are generally inaccurate.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Asking "Have you fallen in the past year?" effectively screens fall risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A prior fall is the strongest predictor of future falls. **Reduced** gait speed raises (not lowers) risk (A); Beers criteria address inappropriate medications, not fall-risk prediction (B); observational gait assessment **is** useful (D wrong).

## Q2 — Geriatric Assessment (p. 776)

Which one of the following statements about nutritional status in older adults is correct?

- A. Weight monitoring using body mass index is inaccurate in this population.
- B. Appetite stimulants are recommended to improve nutritional status.
- C. High-calorie supplements should be recommended to patients who unintentionally lose 5% or more of their body weight.
- D. Patients should be asked if they have lost weight within the past six months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Ask whether the patient has lost weight in the past six months.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Screening for **unintentional weight loss** is key. BMI tracking is useful (A wrong); **appetite stimulants aren't recommended** (adverse effects, B); high-calorie oral supplements have limited evidence and aren't the default for a 5% loss (C overstated).

## Q3 — Geriatric Assessment (p. 776)

In assessing for hearing and vision loss in older patients, which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends routine screening for hearing loss in patients older than 50 years.
- B. A whispered voice test is an effective screening alternative to audiometry.
- C. Hearing loss is rarely associated with cognitive or mood changes.
- D. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends routine screening for visual acuity in older adults.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — The whispered voice test is an effective screening alternative to audiometry.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF gives **I statements** for routine hearing and visual-acuity screening in asymptomatic older adults (A, D wrong); hearing loss **is** linked to cognitive decline and depression (C wrong).

## Q4 — Recurrent Abdominal Pain in Children (p. 785)

In the absence of fever, vomiting, blood in stool, history of urinary tract infection, alarm symptoms, or abnormal physical examination findings, which one of the following is the appropriate workup for recurrent abdominal pain?

- A. Abdominal computed tomography.
- B. Functional abdominal pain is likely; clinically evaluate, but no additional workup is necessary.
- C. Fecal guaiac testing, fecal ova and parasite testing, and urinalysis.
- D. Referral to pediatric gastroenterologist.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Likely functional abdominal pain — evaluate clinically without further testing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Without **red flags**, recurrent abdominal pain in children is usually functional; extensive testing, imaging, or referral isn't warranted initially (A, C, D).

## Q5 — Recurrent Abdominal Pain in Children (p. 785)

Which one of the following approaches is the most appropriate next step after dietary and behavioral therapies for constipation?

- A. Manual disimpaction.
- B. Polyethylene glycol (Miralax).
- C. Mineral oil enema.
- D. Stimulant laxative.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Polyethylene glycol (osmotic laxative).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> After diet/behavioral measures, **PEG** is first-line maintenance/cleanout therapy for childhood constipation. Enemas/disimpaction are for significant fecal impaction; stimulants are adjuncts/second-line.

## Q6 — Recurrent Abdominal Pain in Children (p. 785)

Which one of the following therapies is most likely to ease the symptoms of functional abdominal pain in children?

- A. Dopamine receptor antagonists.
- B. Amitriptyline.
- C. Citalopram (Celexa).
- D. Cognitive behavior therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Cognitive behavior therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CBT has the best evidence for pediatric functional abdominal pain. Antidepressants (amitriptyline, citalopram) show **little benefit over placebo** in children (B, C); dopamine antagonists aren't indicated (A).

## Q7 — Localized Prostate Cancer: Treatment Options (p. 798)

Which one of the following statements about radical prostatectomy for localized prostate cancer is correct?

- A. Comparative studies show that laparoscopic prostatectomy is superior to the traditional open surgical approach.
- B. Robotic-assisted surgery is not commonly performed.
- C. Progression to metastatic cancer is less likely with radical prostatectomy than with watchful waiting.
- D. Radical prostatectomy is associated with a lower risk of urinary incontinence than radiation therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Radical prostatectomy reduces progression to metastatic disease versus watchful waiting.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Laparoscopic and open approaches have **similar** outcomes (A); robotic surgery is **very common** (B wrong); prostatectomy carries a **higher** risk of urinary incontinence than radiation (D reversed, while radiation causes more bowel toxicity).

## Q8 — Localized Prostate Cancer (p. 798)

Compared with surgery for localized prostate cancer, radiation therapy is associated with a higher incidence of which one of the following adverse effects?

- A. Urinary incontinence.
- B. Bloody stools.
- C. Erectile dysfunction.
- D. Decreased penile length.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Bloody stools (radiation proctitis/bowel toxicity).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Radiation causes more **bowel/rectal** adverse effects; **surgery** causes more urinary incontinence and (early) erectile dysfunction and penile shortening (A, C, D).

## Q9 — Localized Prostate Cancer (p. 798)

Localized prostate cancer was recently diagnosed in a 70-year-old patient. The tumor is not palpable (stage T1), PSA 8 ng/mL, biopsy Gleason score 6 with four positive cores. His comorbidity-adjusted life expectancy is eight years. Based on National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines, which one of the following treatment options would you recommend?

- A. Observation.
- B. Active surveillance.
- C. Brachytherapy.
- D. Radical prostatectomy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Observation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For **low-risk** disease (T1c, Gleason 6, PSA <10) with a life expectancy **<10 years**, NCCN recommends **observation** (watchful waiting, treat only if symptomatic). **Active surveillance** (with curative intent) is for those with longer (≥10-year) life expectancy (B); definitive treatment isn't indicated here (C, D).

## Q10 — Point-of-Care Guides: Identifying Patients with Newly Diagnosed Acute Pulmonary Embolism Who Are at Low Risk of Death (p. 811)

Which one of the following statements about the management of acute pulmonary embolism is correct?

- A. Outpatient management or early discharge is appropriate for any patient with a low-risk pulmonary embolism.
- B. Outpatient management or early discharge is never appropriate.
- C. Outpatient management or early discharge is contraindicated for patients with recent bleeding.
- D. Outpatient management or early discharge is only appropriate for patients younger than 45 years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Recent bleeding contraindicates outpatient management/early discharge.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Low-risk PE (e.g., low PESI/Hestia criteria) **can** be managed outpatient — but **not "any" low-risk patient** (A overstated) — with exclusions like recent bleeding, renal/hepatic failure, or poor support. It's not age-restricted (D) or never appropriate (B).

## Q11 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Ovarian Cancer (p. 813)

A 61-year-old woman with a normal Pap last year, no medical problems, and a family history significant only for cardiovascular disease presents for a well-woman exam. According to the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF), should this woman be screened for ovarian cancer?

- A. No; the USPSTF recommends screening for ovarian cancer only in asymptomatic women 65 to 79 years of age.
- B. Yes; the USPSTF recommends screening for ovarian cancer with transvaginal ultrasonography in asymptomatic women.
- C. No; the USPSTF recommends against screening for ovarian cancer in asymptomatic women.
- D. Yes; the USPSTF recommends screening for ovarian cancer with serum cancer antigen 125 testing.
- E. Maybe; the USPSTF concluded that the current evidence is insufficient to assess the balance of benefits and harms of screening for ovarian cancer.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The USPSTF recommends against screening asymptomatic women for ovarian cancer.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This is a **D** recommendation — CA-125 and transvaginal ultrasound screening don't reduce mortality and cause harm (false positives → unnecessary surgery) (B, D wrong).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Corticosteroids for Sore Throat — BMJ Rapid Recommendation (p. 821)

Which one of the following statements about corticosteroids for treatment of acute sore throat symptoms is correct?

- A. Single-dose treatment is recommended for children three years and older.
- B. Single-dose treatment is consistently associated with serious adverse effects.
- C. Corticosteroids should not be given if antibiotics are used to treat sore throat.
- D. The typical adult dose is 10 mg of dexamethasone or alternative corticosteroid equivalent.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — The typical adult dose is a single 10-mg dose of dexamethasone (or equivalent).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A single corticosteroid dose modestly speeds sore-throat symptom relief in **adults and adolescents** (the recommendation isn't for young children, A) **with or without antibiotics** (C wrong) and **without serious adverse effects** (B wrong).
