## Q1 — Acute Migraine Headache: Treatment Strategies (p. 317)
A 23-year-old woman with a recent diagnosis of migraines states that ibuprofen and combination acetaminophen-aspirin-caffeine had been working to control her headaches, but they have not been as effective recently. According to the MIDAS questionnaire score, the severity of her migraines is grade III. Which one of the following should be recommended?

- A. She should switch to a triptan.
- B. She will need intramuscular medications, such as ketorolac or promethazine.
- C. She should add acupuncture to her treatment regimen.
- D. She should switch to a butalbital-containing medication.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Switch to a triptan.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Moderate disability (MIDAS III) with declining response to OTC analgesics → step up to migraine-specific therapy (triptan). B (IM ketorolac/promethazine) is rescue/severe/refractory use. C acupuncture is adjunct with weak evidence. D butalbital should be avoided — high medication-overuse-headache risk.

## Q2 — Acute Migraine Headache (p. 317)
A 55-year-old patient with claudication due to smoking has debilitating migraines ~4×/month treated with NSAIDs and acetaminophen. Which one of the following should be considered when escalating her treatment?

- A. Her vascular disease makes her ineligible for gepants or ditans.
- B. Lasmiditan (Reyvow) carries a US FDA boxed warning about adverse effects.
- C. There is high risk for medication overuse headache with gepants.
- D. There is high-quality evidence supporting the effectiveness of neuromodulatory devices.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Lasmiditan carries an FDA boxed/safety warning about adverse effects.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> By elimination the other three are clearly false: A is wrong — gepants/ditans are precisely the agents you CAN use in vascular disease since they don't cause vasoconstriction (unlike triptans). C is wrong — gepants have low MOH risk (some are used for prevention). D is wrong — neuromodulation evidence is low-quality. **⚠️ Verify:** confirm whether lasmiditan's warning is formally "boxed"; it definitely carries driving-impairment/CNS-depression warnings and is Schedule V.

## Q3 — Common Questions About Buprenorphine for OUD (p. 330)
Which one of the following statements about buprenorphine treatment for opioid use disorder is correct?

- A. The risk of cardiac arrhythmia is higher with buprenorphine than methadone.
- B. It is approved by the FDA for patients as young as 16 years.
- C. It should not be initiated if a patient is actively in withdrawal.
- D. It should be prescribed only if patients are also receiving behavior therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — FDA-approved from age 16.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> A is wrong — methadone carries the higher QT/arrhythmia risk. C is reversed — you initiate buprenorphine *when the patient is in withdrawal* to avoid precipitated withdrawal. D is wrong — concurrent behavioral therapy is recommended but not required.

## Q4 — Buprenorphine for OUD (p. 330)
Which one of the following statements about drug testing in patients being treated with buprenorphine is correct?

- A. Positive tests for illicit drugs do not correlate with overdose risk.
- B. The absence of norbuprenorphine suggests adulteration of the sample.
- C. ASAM recommends once-yearly urine drug testing.
- D. Specimen collection must be directly observed to be considered valid.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Absence of norbuprenorphine suggests adulteration (spiking).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Norbuprenorphine is the metabolite; buprenorphine present without its metabolite implies the drug was added to the sample rather than ingested. A wrong (positive illicit results do correlate with overdose risk). C wrong (testing should be more frequent). D wrong (observed collection not mandatory for validity).

## Q6 — Groin Pain and Injuries (p. 337)
Which one of the following types of groin pain indicates the need for radiography as part of the initial imaging?

- A. Adductor-related.
- B. Pubic-related.
- C. Inguinal-related.
- D. Iliopsoas-related.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Pubic-related.** 🧠 Generated (corrected) · Confidence: High 
> "Hip radiography and MRI are the preferred imaging modalities in patients with suspected iliopsoas-related groin pain because these patients often have concomitant hip pathology"
> 
> "In all patients with inguinal-related groin pain, dynamic ultrasonography should be performed to rule out a true hernia and evaluate for posterior abdominal wall weakness.15,31 If the ultrasonography is nondiagnostic, MRI of the pelvis can assist in diagnosis in patients with pain that is refractory to conservative management."
> 
> "Pelvic MRI is the imaging modality of choice and should be reserved for patients with symptoms who do not improve with initial conservative measures."
> 
> "In patients with adductor-related groin pain, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the pelvis is the preferred imaging modality and should be performed if the diagnosis is unclear or if symptoms have not improved after initial conservative management."



## Q7 — PMS and PMDD (p. 345)
A 30-year-old woman has headache, weight gain, bloating, irritability, and anxiety during the 5 days before her menstrual cycle. Symptoms worsen with age and interfere with daily activities. Which one of the following is first-line therapy?

- A. Oral contraceptives.
- B. Gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonists.
- C. Acupuncture.
- D. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — SSRIs.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> SSRIs are first-line for moderate-severe PMS/PMDD (effective continuously or luteal-phase dosing). OCPs (esp. drospirenone) are an option but not the single first-line answer here. GnRH agonists are reserved for refractory cases; acupuncture has weak evidence.

## Q8 — PMS and PMDD (p. 345)

## Q12 — Cochrane: Liver Fibrosis (FIB-4 / Forns) in Chronic HCV (p. 315)
A 54-year-old has positive HCV antibody and positive HCV RNA. Which one of the following about the FIB-4 score is correct?

- A. It includes GGT, platelet, and total cholesterol levels and patient age.
- B. It includes ALT, AST, and platelet levels and patient age.
- C. It is based on the results of a FibroScan.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Uses ALT, AST, platelets, and age.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> FIB-4 = (age × AST) / (platelets × √ALT). It's a non-invasive blood-based index — independent of FibroScan (transient elastography). A lists the wrong analytes (that's closer to the Forns/other indices).

## Q13 — ACOG Labor Management Guidelines (p. 379)
