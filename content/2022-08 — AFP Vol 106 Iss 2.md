## Q1 — Arthropod Bites and Stings (p. 137)
A 25-year-old with prior systemic allergic reaction to a bee sting. Most effective way to decrease risk of recurrent systemic reaction?

- A. Wearing dark-colored clothing.
- B. Applying lemon eucalyptus oil before going outdoors.
- C. DEET 20%–50% insect repellent.
- D. Venom immunotherapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Venom immunotherapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For documented systemic sting reactions, venom immunotherapy reduces recurrent systemic reaction risk by up to ~90%. Repellents/clothing reduce bites but not the immune response; DEET does not deter stinging hymenoptera well.

## Q2 — Arthropod Bites and Stings (p. 137)
Most likely clinical course for a brown recluse spider bite?

- A. Progressive skin necrosis.
- B. Erythematous target lesion at the bite site.
- C. Resolves in one week with no scarring.
- D. Multiple raised erythematous lesions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Resolves in about a week with no scarring.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most brown recluse bites are self-limited. Progressive necrosis (A) is the feared but uncommon outcome; the target lesion (B) suggests Lyme erythema migrans.

## Q3 — Arthropod Bites and Stings (p. 137)
Widow spider envenomation, pain at bite site, normal vital signs. Appropriate intervention?

- A. Opioids.
- B. Benzodiazepines.
- C. Antivenom.
- D. Tetanus vaccine prophylaxis (if indicated).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Tetanus prophylaxis (if indicated).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mild widow bites (normal vitals) get supportive care + tetanus update. Opioids/benzodiazepines are for severe pain/muscle spasm; antivenom is reserved for severe systemic envenomation.

## Q4 — Diverticular Disease (p. 150)
Which evaluation should be performed routinely in possible acute diverticulitis?

- A. Abdominal MRI.
- B. CBC, basic metabolic panel, urinalysis, and CRP.
- C. Abdominal ultrasonography.
- D. Abdominal CT.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — CBC, BMP, UA, CRP.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Basic labs are obtained *routinely*. CT (D) is the most accurate imaging and is used to confirm the diagnosis or assess complications, but is not part of the routine universal baseline the question targets. Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q5 — Diverticular Disease (p. 150)
Correct statement about initial management of *uncomplicated* diverticulitis?

- A. Start an antibiotic covering enteric bacteria.
- B. Admit ≥24 h for IV fluids and antibiotics.
- C. Antibiotics are not recommended for initial management.
- D. NPO for at least 24–48 hours.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Antibiotics are not recommended for initial management.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> AGA recommends *selective*, not routine, antibiotics in uncomplicated diverticulitis in immunocompetent patients — most can be managed without them.

## Q6 — Diverticular Disease (p. 150)
Correct statement about initial management of *complicated* diverticulitis?

- A. Stop all oral fluids/feeding on diagnosis.
- B. Place a percutaneous drain for abscesses 3 cm or larger.
- C. Ultrasonography is the preferred imaging.
- D. Start oral antibiotics.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Percutaneous drainage for abscesses ≥3 cm.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Larger abscesses are drained percutaneously (plus IV antibiotics); CT is preferred imaging; complicated disease needs IV not oral antibiotics.

## Q7 — Generalized Anxiety and Panic Disorder (p. 157)
Which history element supports DSM-5 panic disorder?

- A. Headaches during episodes.
- B. Episodes that wake the patient at night.
- C. Persistent concern for recurrence.
- D. Uncontrolled crying during episodes.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Persistent concern about recurrence.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> DSM-5 requires recurrent unexpected panic attacks plus ≥1 month of persistent worry about further attacks or maladaptive behavior change.

## Q8 — Generalized Anxiety and Panic Disorder (p. 157)
First-line therapy for generalized anxiety disorder?

- A. Alprazolam (Xanax), a benzodiazepine.
- B. Bupropion (Wellbutrin).
- C. Escitalopram (Lexapro), an SSRI.
- D. Quetiapine (Seroquel).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Escitalopram (SSRI).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SSRIs/SNRIs are first-line. Benzodiazepines are avoided long-term; bupropion is not effective for anxiety; antipsychotics are not first-line.

## Q9 — Generalized Anxiety and Panic Disorder (p. 157)
Most accurate statement about benzodiazepines for panic disorder?

- A. Association with increased overall mortality.
- B. More effective than antidepressant therapy.
- C. No benefit in symptoms compared with placebo.
- D. No increase in tolerance or withdrawal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Associated with increased overall mortality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Observational data link benzodiazepines to higher mortality. They are not superior to antidepressants (B), do help short-term vs placebo (C false), and clearly cause tolerance/withdrawal (D false). Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q10 — Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (p. 165)
Which patient should be screened for AAA per USPSTF?

- A. 50-year-old man, never smoked, with risk factors.
- B. 65-year-old woman who has smoked.
- C. 65-year-old woman, never smoked, family history of AAA.
- D. 70-year-old man who has smoked.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — 70-year-old man who has smoked.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF: one-time ultrasound screening in **men 65–75 who ever smoked** (B recommendation). Screening women who never smoked is not recommended; for women who smoked/with family history the evidence is insufficient (I).

## Q11 — Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (p. 165)
A 75-year-old former smoker diagnosed with AAA. Which recommendation is correct?

- A. Antihypertensive therapy slows aneurysm expansion.
- B. Beta blockers + statins decrease perioperative mortality.
- C. Vigorous, competitive exercise should be encouraged.
- D. Doxycycline reduces the likelihood of early rupture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Beta blockers + statins decrease perioperative mortality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Antihypertensives have not been shown to slow expansion (A); vigorous competitive exertion is discouraged with large AAA (C); doxycycline has not reduced rupture/expansion in trials (D). Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q12 — Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (p. 165)
Correct statement about surgical management of AAA?

- A. Repair is mandatory once the aneurysm exceeds 2.9 cm.
- B. EVAR and open repair have similar long-term survival rates.
- C. Open repair requires imaging follow-up.
- D. Open repair is most suited for older patients and those with comorbidities.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — EVAR and open repair have similar long-term survival.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Repair threshold is ~5.5 cm (A false); *EVAR* (not open) requires lifelong surveillance imaging (C false); EVAR — not open — suits older/comorbid patients (D false).

## Q13 — Multiple Sclerosis (p. 173)
A 33-year-old, first 1-week episode of gait disturbance, MRI shows focal demyelination matching the deficit, symptoms resolved, no prior history. Most appropriate MS subtype?

- A. Relapsing-remitting MS.
- B. Clinically isolated syndrome.
- C. Primary progressive MS.
- D. Secondary progressive MS.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Clinically isolated syndrome.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A single demyelinating episode without dissemination in time = CIS. A second clinical/MRI event (dissemination in time) would establish relapsing-remitting MS.

## Q14 — Multiple Sclerosis (p. 173)
Correct statement about long-term MS treatment?

- A. DMT reduces short-term symptoms but doesn't slow progression.
- B. All newly diagnosed patients should start interferon beta-1b.
- C. Smoking cessation can slow disease progression.
- D. Pregnant patients should continue DMT because relapse risk more than doubles in pregnancy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Smoking cessation can slow disease progression.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> DMT does slow progression (A false); therapy is individualized, not one-size interferon (B false); relapse risk actually *decreases* during pregnancy and most DMTs are stopped (D false).

## Q15 — Multiple Sclerosis (p. 173)
Correct statement about treatment of acute MS relapses?

- A. Lack of steroid response contraindicates plasmapheresis.
- B. Oral and IV steroids have similar effectiveness.
- C. A lower steroid dose is recommended to limit adverse effects.
- D. Initiating disease-modifying therapy is the first step.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Oral and IV steroids have similar effectiveness.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> High-dose oral and IV corticosteroids are equivalent for acute relapses. Plasmapheresis is actually used *for* steroid-refractory relapses (A false); DMT is for long-term prevention, not acute relapse (D false).

## Q16 — Scrotal Masses (p. 184)
Adolescent: swollen, firm, high-riding testicle, absent cremasteric reflex, nausea, onset 1 hour ago, no trauma. Recommended action?

- A. Test for C. trachomatis and N. gonorrhoeae.
- B. Urgent urologic consultation.
- C. Scrotal ultrasonography with follow-up in two days.
- D. Icing and symptomatic treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Urgent urologic consultation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Classic **testicular torsion** — a surgical emergency. Do not delay for imaging or follow-up; the salvage window is ~6 hours.

## Q17 — Scrotal Masses (p. 184)
A 30-year-old man worried about testicular cancer, never felt a lump. Appropriate action?

- A. Routine screening for N. gonorrhoeae.
- B. Physical exam of testicles to screen for cancer.
- C. Ultrasonography of the testicles.
- D. Screening for testicular cancer is not recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Screening is not recommended.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF recommends *against* screening asymptomatic adolescents/men for testicular cancer (D recommendation) — no mortality benefit, given high cure rates.

## Q18 — Scrotal Masses (p. 184)
A 30-year-old: gradual left scrotal pain, mild urinary frequency/dysuria, tenderness, positive Prehn sign. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Acute epididymitis.
- B. Testicular cancer.
- C. Inguinal hernia.
- D. Hydrocele.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Acute epididymitis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Gradual onset, dysuria/frequency, and a **positive Prehn sign** (pain relief with elevation) point to epididymitis. Torsion is sudden with a negative Prehn sign and absent cremasteric reflex.

## Q19 — Cochrane: Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (p. 134)
PROMs (e.g., CAGE, GAD-7) benefit which one of the following?

- A. Patient communication with health care professionals.
- B. General health perception and social functioning.
- C. Pain level.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Patient–clinician communication.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The Cochrane review found PROMs may improve communication, but evidence for improving health perception, social functioning, or pain is weak — so not "all of the above." Answer confirmed by the official key.

## Q20 — Cochrane: Exercise Training in Maintenance Dialysis (p. 135)
Correct statement about exercise for patients with end-stage renal disease?

- A. Exercise probably improves functional capacity.
- B. Exercise probably improves pain, depression, and quality of life.
- C. Guidelines suggest encouraging exercise between and during dialysis sessions.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Exercise improves functional capacity and patient-reported outcomes, and guidelines encourage activity including intradialytic exercise.

## Q21 — AHRQ: Cannabis for Chronic Pain (p. 202)
Correct statement about cannabis use for chronic pain?

- A. Higher THC:CBD ratios are associated with fewer adverse effects.
- B. It reduces opioid use for chronic pain.
- C. Evidence on whole-plant cannabis is insufficient to draw conclusions.
- D. Synthetic oral high THC:CBD formulations produce no pain relief.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Whole-plant cannabis evidence is insufficient.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Higher THC:CBD ratios cause *more* adverse effects (A false); opioid-sparing is not established (B false); high THC:CBD synthetics do provide some short-term pain relief (D false).

## Q22 — Practice Guidelines: Shared Decision-Making (NICE) (p. 205)
An 82-year-old after a fall, discussing independent living. What does NICE recommend for the patient and family *before* the visit?

- A. Consider the patient's life and care goals.
- B. Be directive about the best treatment plan.
- C. Ask the family to leave before discussing options.
- D. Describe chances of falling again as common, uncommon, or rare.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Consider the patient's life and care goals beforehand.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Shared decision-making is patient-centered and preparatory — the patient/family reflect on values and goals before the visit. Being directive (B) and excluding family (C) contradict the model; (D) is a during-visit risk-communication technique.

## Q23 — BMJ Rapid Recommendation: Medical Cannabis for Chronic Pain (p. 208)
A patient with 5 years of neuropathic pain. Per BMJ/MAGIC, which effect can be expected with non-inhaled cannabis/cannabinoids?

- A. Improved emotional functioning.
- B. Improved social functioning.
- C. Improved sleep quality.
- D. Improved role functioning.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Improved sleep quality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The BMJ rapid recommendation found small improvements in pain and **sleep**, with little to no benefit for emotional, social, role, or physical functioning.
