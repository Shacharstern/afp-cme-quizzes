## Q1 — Sacroiliac Joint Dysfunction (p. 239)
On physical examination, pain at which location suggests SI joint dysfunction?

- A. Lateral to the thoracic paraspinal muscles.
- B. Midline lumbar spine.
- C. Inferomedial to the posterior superior iliac spine.
- D. Perineum or scrotum.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Inferomedial to the PSIS.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Fortin finger test: patients localize SI pain just inferomedial to the PSIS.

## Q2 — Sacroiliac Joint Dysfunction (p. 239)
A 55-year-old with CKD in cardiac rehab develops SI joint dysfunction. First-line treatment?

- A. Corticosteroid injection.
- B. NSAIDs.
- C. Manipulation combined with physical therapy.
- D. Spinal fusion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Manipulation + physical therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs are relatively contraindicated in CKD; conservative PT/manipulation is first-line. Injections/surgery are later steps.

## Q3 — Sacroiliac Joint Dysfunction (p. 239)
Which diagnostic test most likely suggests SI joint dysfunction?

- A. CT of the SI joint.
- B. MRI of the SI joint.
- C. Bone scan of lumbar spine and SI joint.
- D. A minimum of three positive provocation tests.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — At least three positive provocation tests.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SI dysfunction is a clinical diagnosis; ≥3 positive provocation maneuvers (e.g., FABER, thigh thrust, compression) increase likelihood. Imaging is usually unrevealing.

## Q4 — Heart Murmurs in Children (p. 250)
Which statement about pulse oximetry screening for critical congenital heart disease is correct?

- A. False positives are less common if screened within 12 hours of birth.
- B. It should be performed only on infants with murmurs.
- C. It should ideally be performed at 24 hours of life or later.
- D. SpO₂ <95% on any one measurement is abnormal.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Ideally performed at ≥24 hours of life.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Screening before 24h raises false positives (transitional circulation); universal (not murmur-only) screening; abnormal thresholds use repeated measurements/right-hand vs foot differential.

## Q5 — Heart Murmurs in Children (p. 250)
A well newborn with an easily audible systolic murmur, normal exam/pulse ox, murmur persists next day. Appropriate management?

- A. Referral to a pediatric cardiologist.
- B. Echocardiography.
- C. Electrocardiography.
- D. Re-evaluate as outpatient in one week.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Referral to a pediatric cardiologist.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> An *easily audible/loud* persistent murmur in a newborn is a red flag (innocent murmurs are soft and often transient) → cardiology referral.

## Q6 — Heart Murmurs in Children / Still's murmur (p. 250)
Which statement about a Still's murmur is correct?

- A. Continuous (systolic and diastolic), diastolic portion louder.
- B. Diastolic.
- C. Most common in adolescents.
- D. Systolic with a twangy musical sound.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Systolic, twangy/musical quality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Still's murmur is the classic innocent vibratory systolic murmur, peaks in early school-age children. Any diastolic murmur is pathologic.

## Q7 — Health Care–Associated Infections (p. 262)
Which is an appropriate indication for placing a urinary catheter?

- A. Monitoring diuresis in acute heart failure exacerbation.
- B. Incontinence in a patient with delirium.
- C. Urinary retention from anticholinergic medication overdose.
- D. Decreased nurse-to-patient ratios during COVID-19.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Urinary retention from anticholinergic overdose.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Acute retention is a valid indication. Convenience/incontinence/diuresis monitoring are NOT — they drive CAUTIs.

## Q8 — Health Care–Associated Infections (p. 262)
Which practice reduces central line–associated bloodstream infections (CLABSI)?

- A. Preferring parenteral over enteral nutrition.
- B. Preferring femoral over subclavian site.
- C. Prophylactic antibiotics before insertion.
- D. Removing central lines as soon as no longer required.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Remove central lines as soon as no longer needed.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Subclavian (not femoral) is preferred; no prophylactic antibiotics; enteral preferred over parenteral. Minimizing line-days is key.

## Q9 — Health Care–Associated Infections / VAP (p. 262)
A 55-year-old with severe COPD exacerbation and hypoxic respiratory failure. Which VAP statement is correct?

- A. NIPPV increases VAP risk.
- B. Heavy sedation reduces VAP risk.
- C. A PPI reduces VAP risk.
- D. Daily checks for extubation readiness reduce VAP.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Daily extubation-readiness assessment reduces VAP.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> NIPPV *lowers* VAP risk (avoids intubation); minimize sedation (sedation vacations); PPIs don't prevent VAP.

## Q10 — Alpha- and Beta-thalassemia (p. 272)
Which statement about laboratory testing in possible thalassemia is correct?

- A. Normocytic, normochromic anemia is typical in mild–moderate disease.
- B. In alpha-thalassemia, MCV is low and RDW is normal.
- C. The Mentzer index (MCV/RBC) is usually >13 in thalassemia.
- D. Poikilocytosis is seen in thalassemia but not iron deficiency.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Low MCV with normal RDW.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Thalassemia: microcytosis with *normal* RDW and Mentzer index **<13** (iron deficiency gives high RDW, Mentzer >13). 🔑 "Microcytic + normal RDW + Mentzer <13 = thal."

## Q11 — Alpha- and Beta-thalassemia (p. 272)
Which statement about treating symptomatic thalassemia is correct?

- A. Maintain hemoglobin >12 g/dL.
- B. Transfusion-dependent patients usually transfuse every 2–4 months.
- C. Non–transfusion-dependent patients never need transfusions.
- D. Iron chelation is recommended for transfusion-dependent patients with ferritin >1,000 ng/mL.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Iron chelation when transfusion-dependent and ferritin >1,000.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Transfusion-dependent patients usually transfuse every 2–5 weeks; even non-dependent patients sometimes need transfusions.

## Q12 — Alpha- and Beta-thalassemia (p. 272)
Which statement about complications of thalassemia is correct?

- A. Abnormal bone growth, osteopenia, and osteoporosis are common.
- B. Splenectomy is routinely indicated to improve hemoglobin.
- C. Endocrine disorders are rare.
- D. Cardiac complications contraindicate iron chelation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Bone abnormalities (osteopenia/osteoporosis) are common.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Marrow expansion → bone changes. Endocrinopathies (from iron overload) are common; cardiac iron overload is an *indication* for chelation, not a contraindication.

## Q13 — Telemedicine in Diabetes Care (p. 281)
Which statement about remote glucose monitoring is correct?

- A. Remote monitoring improves glucose control.
- B. Continuous glucose monitoring is recommended for all patients.
- C. Improving time in range has been shown to decrease diabetic complications.
- D. Remote monitoring reduces practice workload.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Remote monitoring improves glucose control.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CGM is not for everyone; time-in-range correlation with hard outcomes is associative (not proven to reduce complications); remote monitoring adds workload.

## Q14 — Telemedicine in Diabetes Care (p. 281)
Which statement about teleretinal screening is correct?

- A. Affordable regardless of practice size/volume.
- B. Requires pupillary dilation.
- C. Improves screening rates.
- D. Guidelines recommend against it.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Teleretinal screening improves screening rates.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cost-effectiveness depends on volume; modern cameras are often nonmydriatic; guidelines support it.

## Q15 — Telemedicine in Diabetes Care (p. 281)
Which statement about telemedicine and nonglycemic outcomes is correct?

- A. Telemedicine doesn't benefit prediabetes.
- B. Telemedicine improves weight in prediabetes more than in-person care.
- C. Telemedicine improves blood pressure control through lifestyle changes.
- D. Telemedicine interventions are not covered by Medicare.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Improves BP control via lifestyle support.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Telemedicine benefits prediabetes; weight outcomes are comparable (not superior) to in-person; Medicare covers many telehealth services.

## Q16 — Parathyroid Disorders (p. 289)
Hypercalcemia with low PTH and elevated PTH-related peptide. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia.
- B. Pseudohypoparathyroidism.
- C. Vitamin D deficiency.
- D. Humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Tumor-secreted PTHrP raises calcium and suppresses native PTH. 🔑 "High Ca + low PTH + high PTHrP = malignancy."

## Q17 — Parathyroid Disorders (p. 289)
Tetany, seizures, prolonged QT after neck surgery. Mainstay of follow-up treatment?

- A. Oral calcium and vitamin D supplementation.
- B. Calcimimetics.
- C. Parathyroidectomy.
- D. Bisphosphonates.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Oral calcium + vitamin D.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Post-surgical hypoparathyroidism/hypocalcemia: acute IV calcium then chronic oral calcium + active vitamin D (calcitriol).

## Q18 — Parathyroid Disorders (p. 289)
A 45-year-old, asymptomatic, Ca 12.3 mg/dL, parathyroid adenoma diagnosed. Recommended initial approach?

- A. Monitoring without treatment.
- B. Medical management.
- C. Parathyroidectomy.
- D. No further intervention.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Parathyroidectomy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Calcium >1 mg/dL above upper normal (here ~2 above) is a surgical indication even in asymptomatic primary hyperparathyroidism; surgery is curative.

## Q20 — Cochrane: Antibiotics for COVID-19 (p. 237)
Which statement about azithromycin for COVID-19 is correct?

- A. It improves disease-related mortality in hospitalized patients.
- B. It decreases length of hospitalization.
- C. It does not reduce hospitalization or improve progression in outpatients with mild/asymptomatic infection.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer

> Azithromycin has no role in COVID-19 (no mortality, hospitalization, or progression benefit).

## Q21 — Putting Prevention Into Practice: Dental Caries (USPSTF) (p. 299)
A 4-month-old with recent primary tooth eruption, healthy. How to prevent dental caries per USPSTF?

- A. No intervention needed now.
- B. Check serum fluoride levels.
- C. Begin oral fluoride supplementation.
- D. Begin topical fluoride varnish application to primary teeth.
- E. Begin oral fluoride supplementation and topical fluoride varnish.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Topical fluoride varnish to primary teeth.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF: apply fluoride varnish starting at primary tooth eruption; oral fluoride supplementation only from 6 months if water supply is fluoride-deficient. No serum fluoride testing.

## Q22 — Lown Right Care: ECG in Preparticipation Evaluations (p. 302)
Which statement about screening for sudden cardiac death in young athletes is correct?

- A. Athlete's heart is pathologic and warrants disqualification.
- B. Sudden cardiac death rates are higher in athletes than nonathletes.
- C. The AHA 14-element screening tool has high sensitivity.
- D. Emergency response plans with AED training have been shown to save lives.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — AED-equipped emergency response plans save lives.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Athlete's heart is physiologic (benign); the 14-element history/exam tool has *low* sensitivity; AED access/training is the proven life-saver.

## Q23 — Point-of-Care Guides: Bacterial Meningitis in Children (p. 311)
Which is NOT included in the Dutch Study Group Meningitis Prediction Rule?

- A. Vomiting.
- B. Petechiae.
- C. Elevated C-reactive protein level.
- D. Cerebrospinal fluid protein level of 40 mg/dL.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — CSF protein of 40 mg/dL.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The rule includes vomiting, petechiae, CRP, meningeal signs, cyanosis, and *higher* CSF protein thresholds — 40 mg/dL is normal, not a predictor.

## Q24 — Practice Guideline: ACIP 2022 Immunization Schedules (p. 324)
A healthy 65-year-old. Which vaccine does ACIP recommend?

- A. Hepatitis B regardless of prior vaccination status.
- B. PCV13 and PPSV23.
- C. PCV15 alone.
- D. PCV20 alone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — PCV20 alone.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> 2022 ACIP for pneumococcal-naïve adults ≥65: PCV20 alone, OR PCV15 followed by PPSV23. PCV15 alone or PCV13+PPSV23 are not the current standalone recommendations.

## Q25 — Practice Guideline: Colorectal Cancer Screening (ACG) (p. 327)
A 45-year-old, average risk, wants to avoid colonoscopy. Per ACG, which is primarily recommended?

- A. CT colonography.
- B. Fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) alone.
- C. FIT with multitarget stool DNA testing.
- D. Fecal occult blood testing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — FIT alone.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ACG primary options are colonoscopy or annual FIT; FIT is the preferred noninvasive choice. mt-sDNA/CT colonography are alternatives.
