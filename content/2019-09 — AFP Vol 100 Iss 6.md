## Q1 — Urinary Incontinence in Women: Evaluation and Management (p. 339)

A 49-year-old woman has leakage of small amounts of urine when exercising, sneezing, and laughing, with no other urinary symptoms. Which one of the following is correct regarding her evaluation and treatment?

- A. A urinalysis is not needed because she does not have dysuria.
- B. Measurement of postvoid residual is not traditionally part of the initial evaluation.
- C. Kegel exercises have at least a 29% chance of curing her urinary incontinence (UI).
- D. Mirabegron would be appropriate first-line therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Pelvic floor (Kegel) exercises cure stress UI in at least ~29% of women.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Pelvic floor muscle training is **first-line** for stress UI. Urinalysis **is** part of the basic workup (A wrong), PVR is often measured (B wrong), and mirabegron treats **urge** (overactive bladder), not stress UI (D wrong).

## Q2 — Urinary Incontinence in Women (p. 339)

A caretaker of a 67-year-old patient with a history of stroke reports incontinent episodes that happen only when she is not present to help the patient to the bathroom. Which type of UI does the patient most likely have?

- A. Stress.
- B. Functional.
- C. Transient.
- D. Mixed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Functional incontinence.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Continence physiology is intact, but **mobility/access barriers** (post-stroke) prevent timely toileting → functional incontinence. Addressing access/assistance is the key intervention.

## Q3 — Urinary Incontinence in Women (p. 339)

Which one of the following statements about the pharmacologic treatment of UI is correct?

- A. Duloxetine (Cymbalta) is approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for stress UI.
- B. Pharmacotherapy will not help patients with mixed UI.
- C. Intravesical onabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) treatments provide a permanent cure for urge UI about 50% of the time.
- D. Antimuscarinics are not recommended as first-line pharmacotherapy in older adults.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Antimuscarinics are not recommended as first-line pharmacotherapy in older adults.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Anticholinergic burden (cognitive impairment, falls) makes antimuscarinics a cautious choice in elders (β3-agonists preferred). Duloxetine is **not FDA-approved** for stress UI in the US (A); botox is **temporary** (C); pharmacotherapy **can** help mixed UI (B wrong).

## Q4 — Otitis Media: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 350)

A four-year-old girl has otitis media for the first time in two years. Her symptoms are not severe, but reliability of follow-up is uncertain. Which one of the following treatment options is recommended?

- A. Single dose of intramuscular ceftriaxone.
- B. Observation without initial antibiotic treatment.
- C. Oral amoxicillin/clavulanate (Augmentin).
- D. Oral high-dose amoxicillin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Oral high-dose amoxicillin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **High-dose amoxicillin (80–90 mg/kg/day)** is first-line. Observation requires reliable follow-up (uncertain here, B less appropriate); amoxicillin/clavulanate is for recent antibiotics/treatment failure (C); ceftriaxone for vomiting/failure (A).

## Q5 — Otitis Media (p. 350)

A three-year-old boy has had three episodes of acute otitis media in the past six months. Which one of the following management options is most appropriate?

- A. Antibiotic prophylaxis with amoxicillin/clavulanate for six months.
- B. Antibiotic prophylaxis with low-dose amoxicillin for six months.
- C. Referral for consideration of tympanostomy tube placement.
- D. Tympanostomy tubes if no middle ear effusion is present.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Refer for consideration of tympanostomy tube placement.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Recurrent AOM (≥3 in 6 months or ≥4 in a year) warrants ENT referral for tubes — **especially with effusion**. **Antibiotic prophylaxis is no longer recommended** (resistance) (A, B wrong); tubes are considered with persistent effusion (D wrong).

## Q6 — Pelvic Inflammatory Disease: Diagnosis, Management, and Prevention (p. 357)

Which one of the following statements about the clinical findings in a patient with suspected pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) is correct?

- A. Increased white blood cells on wet prep strongly support the diagnosis of PID.
- B. The presence of bacterial vaginosis or trichomoniasis is helpful in excluding PID.
- C. The cardinal symptoms of PID are periumbilical or right lower quadrant pain.
- D. A negative nucleic acid amplification test for chlamydia and gonorrhea essentially rules out PID.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Increased WBCs on saline wet prep support a diagnosis of PID.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Abundant WBCs on wet mount support PID (their absence argues against it). BV/trichomoniasis **co-occur** with PID (B wrong); PID causes **lower abdominal/pelvic** pain (C wrong); a negative GC/CT NAAT does **not** exclude PID (polymicrobial) (D wrong).

## Q7 — Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (p. 357)

Which one of the following recommendations about the management of PID is correct?

- A. Treatment should be delayed until a definitive diagnosis has been made.
- B. Intrauterine devices that have been in place longer than three weeks should be promptly removed in patients with PID.
- C. Rescreening for sexually transmitted infection is recommended three months after treatment.
- D. First-line treatment of PID includes quinolones for Neisseria gonorrhoeae coverage.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Rescreen for STIs about three months after treatment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Treat **empirically/promptly** to prevent sequelae (A wrong); an IUD can usually **stay** with close follow-up (B wrong); **quinolones are no longer recommended** for gonorrhea (resistance — ceftriaxone-based regimens) (D wrong).

## Q8 — Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (p. 357)

Which one of the following statements about assessing and addressing the risk of PID is correct?

- A. Because of an increased risk of PID, antibiotic prophylaxis should be given to patients undergoing loop electrosurgical excision.
- B. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends annual screening for gonorrhea and chlamydia for all sexually active women younger than 25 years and older women at increased risk.
- C. Patients with HIV require broader antibiotic coverage for the treatment of PID.
- D. Brief counseling is as effective as intensive counseling (more than two hours) in preventing sexually transmitted infections.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — USPSTF: screen all sexually active women <25 (and older at-risk women) for GC/CT.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Screening reduces PID. LEEP doesn't require PID prophylaxis (A); HIV patients respond to **standard** PID regimens (C wrong); **intensive** counseling is more effective than brief counseling for STI prevention (D reversed).

## Q9 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Interventions to Prevent Perinatal Depression (p. 365)

A 25-year-old newly pregnant woman takes acyclovir for genital herpes suppression and reports a history of depression and adolescent sexual abuse; her mood is currently fine and she is off antidepressants. Based on the USPSTF recommendation, which one of the following is the most appropriate course of action to prevent perinatal depression?

- A. Encourage regular physical activity.
- B. Prescribe an antidepressant (e.g., sertraline [Zoloft], nortriptyline [Pamelor]).
- C. Prescribe an omega-3 fatty acid supplement.
- D. Provide counseling or refer to counseling.
- E. Monitor the patient for depression symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Provide or refer for counseling.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF (grade **B**) recommends **counseling interventions** (CBT or interpersonal therapy) for pregnant/postpartum people **at increased risk** — and a history of depression and abuse marks her as at risk. Exercise/omega-3 aren't the recommended preventive interventions (A, C); routine prophylactic antidepressants aren't recommended (B).

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: Dual Antiplatelet Therapy for High-Risk TIA and Minor Stroke — BMJ Rapid Recommendation (p. 378)

According to the BMJ rapid recommendation, which one of the following patients should be started on 10 to 21 days of therapy with aspirin and clopidogrel (Plavix) after a high-risk transient ischemic attack (TIA) or minor stroke?

- A. A 57-year-old man after a TIA with an ABCD2 score of 2.
- B. A 49-year-old woman after a TIA with an ABCD2 score of 6.
- C. A 74-year-old man after a stroke with a National Institutes of Health (NIH) Stroke Scale score of 5.
- D. A 37-year-old woman after a stroke with an NIH Stroke Scale score of 21.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — The TIA patient with an ABCD2 score of 6 (high risk).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Short-course DAPT benefits **high-risk TIA (ABCD2 ≥4)** or **minor stroke (NIHSS ≤3–5)**. An ABCD2 of 2 is low risk (A); NIHSS 5 is borderline/larger; NIHSS 21 is a **major** stroke (hemorrhagic-risk) — not minor (C, D less appropriate).
