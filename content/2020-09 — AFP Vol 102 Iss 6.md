## Q1 — Basal Cell and Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinomas (p. 339)

Which one of the following skin cancer prevention recommendations from the USPSTF is correct?

- A. All adults 25 years and older with fair skin should be counseled on minimizing ultraviolet radiation exposure.
- B. Patients six months to 24 years of age with fair skin should be counseled on minimizing exposure to ultraviolet radiation.
- C. Adult patients should be counseled to perform regular skin self-examination.
- D. Adult patients should be regularly screened for skin cancer.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Counsel fair-skinned patients aged 6 months to 24 years to minimize UV exposure.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF behavioral-counseling **B** recommendation targets ages 6 months–24 years; for adults >24 it's individualized (C statement). USPSTF found **insufficient evidence** for skin self-exam counseling and routine screening (C, D wrong).

## Q2 — Basal Cell and Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinomas (p. 339)

Which one of the following patients should be treated initially with Mohs micrographic surgery?

- A. A patient with low-risk noninvasive subtypes of carcinoma.
- B. A patient with a small superficial basal cell carcinoma.
- C. A patient with a greater than 2-cm basal cell carcinoma lesion on the neck.
- D. A patient with multiple actinic keratoses on the face.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A >2-cm basal cell carcinoma on the neck.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mohs is for **high-risk** tumors — large size, high-risk/cosmetically sensitive locations, aggressive subtypes, recurrence, or immunosuppression. Small superficial/low-risk BCC and actinic keratoses don't need Mohs (A, B, D).

## Q3 — Management of Hypertriglyceridemia (p. 347)

Which one of the following statements about isolated high triglyceride levels (150 to 499 mg/dL) in patients without known cardiovascular disease is correct?

- A. Medical treatment with statins is recommended.
- B. Medical treatment with fibrates is recommended.
- C. Medical treatment with dietary omega-3 fatty acids is recommended.
- D. Medical treatment is not recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Medical treatment is not recommended.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For mild-to-moderate isolated hypertriglyceridemia without ASCVD, **lifestyle modification** (diet, weight loss, alcohol reduction, glycemic control) is the focus, not drugs. Pharmacotherapy targets very high levels (≥500, pancreatitis risk) or established ASCVD.

## Q4 — Management of Hypertriglyceridemia (p. 347)

Which one of the following has been shown to reduce the composite endpoint of cardiovascular death, MI, or nonfatal stroke in patients with established cardiovascular disease and fasting triglycerides between 150 and 499 mg/dL?

- A. Fibrates.
- B. Niacin.
- C. Icosapent (Vascepa).
- D. Dietary omega-3 fatty acids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Icosapent ethyl (Vascepa).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> REDUCE-IT showed icosapent ethyl (high-dose purified EPA) lowers major cardiovascular events in statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides. Fibrates, niacin, and dietary omega-3 supplements have **not** shown this outcome benefit.

## Q5 — Anaphylaxis: Recognition and Management (p. 355)

A 23-year-old with a known peanut allergy presents after accidental exposure with tingling of the lips and abdominal cramping. Vital signs are normal. Which one of the following is the best initial treatment?

- A. Intramuscular antihistamine.
- B. Oral antihistamine.
- C. Intramuscular epinephrine.
- D. Intravenous corticosteroids.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Intramuscular epinephrine.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Two organ systems (mucocutaneous + GI) after a known allergen = anaphylaxis → **IM epinephrine** (anterolateral thigh) is first-line and life-saving even with normal vitals. Antihistamines/steroids are adjuncts, never the initial treatment.

## Q6 — Anaphylaxis (p. 355)

Which one of the following statements about making a diagnosis of anaphylaxis is correct?

- A. Involvement of two organ systems is usually required to meet diagnostic criteria; isolated hypotension in the setting of a known allergen also meets criteria.
- B. Symptom onset in anaphylaxis is typically greater than two hours after exposure.
- C. Gastrointestinal symptoms are more common in adults than in children.
- D. Serum tryptase levels decrease during the first hour of an anaphylactic attack.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Two organ systems are usually required; isolated hypotension after a known allergen also qualifies.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Onset is usually **minutes to ~2 hours** (B wrong); tryptase **rises** early (D wrong); GI symptoms are common across ages (C). The diagnostic criteria are met by multi-system involvement or by hypotension after a known allergen.

## Q7 — Anaphylaxis (p. 355)

Which one of the following statements about biphasic anaphylactic reactions is correct?

- A. Biphasic reactions occur in less than 1% of cases of anaphylaxis.
- B. A biphasic reaction is defined as the recurrence of anaphylaxis within 24 hours.
- C. There is no risk of a biphasic reaction if the patient is treated with corticosteroids.
- D. An observation period of more than six hours can exclude 95% of biphasic reactions.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Observation longer than six hours excludes about 95% of biphasic reactions.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Biphasic reactions occur in up to ~5–20% (A wrong), can recur up to **72 hours** later (B wrong), and **steroids do not reliably prevent** them (C wrong) — so a risk-stratified observation period (≥6 h) is used.

## Q8 — Lown Right Care: Appropriate Use of Opioids for Chronic Pain (p. 335)

Which one of the following statements about chronic pain management is correct?

- A. The CDC guideline on opioids for chronic pain recommends hard limits on morphine milligram equivalents per day that should not be exceeded.
- B. Abrupt discontinuation of chronic opioid therapy is typically preferred to a slow taper.
- C. Use of short-acting opioids for acute pain in the lowest possible dosage for less than seven days may decrease the risk of chronic opioid use.
- D. It is unnecessary to assess pain, function, and adverse effects in patients on a stable dose of opioid therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Short-acting opioids, lowest dose, <7 days for acute pain reduces the risk of chronic use.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The CDC thresholds are **cautionary, not hard limits** (A misreads it); chronic opioids should be **tapered slowly**, not stopped abruptly (B wrong); ongoing assessment of pain/function/harm is essential (D wrong).

## Q9 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Hepatitis C Virus Infection (p. 367)

According to the USPSTF recommendation statement, which one of the following statements about screening for HCV infection is correct?

- A. If the test result is negative and risk factors remain unchanged, the patient should be rescreened in five years.
- B. There is adequate evidence on the timing of repeat testing in people who continue to be at risk of new HCV infection.
- C. There is adequate evidence to recommend one-time testing in all adults and periodic testing in people at continued risk of new HCV infection.
- D. There is inadequate evidence that HCV testing accurately detects HCV infection.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — One-time testing for all adults 18–79, with periodic testing for those at continued risk.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The 2020 USPSTF **B** recommendation expanded HCV screening to all adults 18–79 once, plus periodic testing for ongoing risk. The **optimal interval** for repeat testing is **not** well established (B wrong); HCV testing is accurate (D wrong).

## Q10 — Point-of-Care Guides: Diverticulitis — Predicting Which Patients with Acute Abdominal Pain Have the Disease (p. 371)

Which of the following factors is a predictor of acute diverticulitis in patients with acute abdominal pain?

- A. C-reactive protein level greater than 5 mg/dL (50 mg/L).
- B. Tenderness limited to the left lower quadrant.
- C. Absence of vomiting.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The clinical prediction combines **elevated CRP (>50 mg/L), LLQ-localized tenderness, and absence of vomiting** — together they substantially raise the probability of diverticulitis in acute abdominal pain.

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Chronic Kidney Disease — VA/DoD (p. 378)

A 57-year-old with well-controlled type 2 diabetes and hypertension has an elevated creatinine and an eGFR of 49 mL/min/1.73 m² on routine labs, with no history of CKD. Which one of the following actions is likely to be beneficial?

- A. Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor therapy should be discontinued.
- B. Metformin therapy should be discontinued.
- C. Urate-lowering therapy should be initiated.
- D. A cystatin-C level should be obtained to verify eGFR.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Obtain a cystatin-C–based eGFR to confirm the estimate.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> VA/DoD suggest **cystatin-C** to confirm CKD when a creatinine-based eGFR is borderline (45–59) without other markers. ACE inhibitors are renoprotective and continued (A wrong); metformin is safe down to eGFR 30 (B wrong); asymptomatic hyperuricemia isn't treated to slow CKD (C wrong).
