# AFP CME Quiz — August 2021 (Vol 104, Issue 2)

> [!info] Source & answer provenance
> Extracted from ClinicalKey (Clalit proxy), AFP Vol 104 Iss 2, pp. 128–131, C3. Approved for **12 Prescribed credits**. Official key embedded on the inside back cover (p. C3); every official letter was cross-checked against content and **all 24 aligned** → ✅ Official-verified.

## Q1 — Care of the College Student (p. 141)
Which statement about medications and substance use in college-aged students is correct?

- A. There has been a decrease in students who begin college taking a psychotropic medication.
- B. Stimulant medications are not helpful in treating adult ADHD.
- C. Misuse of controlled medications is rare among college students (lifetime <10%).
- D. Physician-directed interventions can reduce tobacco use among college students.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Physician-directed interventions reduce tobacco use.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Brief clinician counseling reduces smoking. The number on psychotropics has *risen*; stimulants are effective for adult ADHD; controlled-medication misuse is common (well above 10%).

## Q2 — Care of the College Student (p. 141)
Which statement about mental health in college-aged students is correct?

- A. Suicidal ideation is rare among college students.
- B. Depression and anxiety are the most commonly diagnosed mental health conditions.
- C. Student veterans with PTSD are less likely to engage in high-risk drinking.
- D. Most students who died by suicide first sought services in a counseling center.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Depression and anxiety are the most common diagnoses.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> These dominate campus mental health. Suicidal ideation is *not* rare; PTSD raises high-risk drinking; most who die by suicide were *not* prior counseling-center clients (argues for broad screening).

## Q3 — Care of the College Student (p. 141)
Which is supported by a USPSTF recommendation?

- A. Screen for cervical cancer in a sexually active 20-year-old.
- B. Offer HIV PrEP to an average-risk 19-year-old.
- C. Screen for chlamydia in an average-risk 26-year-old woman.
- D. Screen for hepatitis C virus in a 19-year-old.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Screen for HCV in a 19-year-old.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF recommends one-time HCV screening for all adults 18–79. Cervical screening starts at 21; PrEP is for those at high risk; routine chlamydia screening is for sexually active women ≤24 (a 26-year-old needs a risk factor).

## Q4 — Human Papillomavirus: Screening, Testing, Prevention (p. 152)
Which statement about HPV screening is correct?

- A. Most organizations recommend cervical cancer screening every five years in people older than 65.
- B. Evidence about the natural history of anal HPV infection is insufficient to provide screening recommendations.
- C. The USPSTF recommends oral cancer screening in asymptomatic individuals.
- D. HPV tests are FDA-approved for women and men.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Insufficient evidence to recommend anal HPV screening.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Cervical screening generally *stops* at 65 with adequate prior screening; USPSTF does not recommend oral cancer screening; HPV tests are FDA-approved for cervical (women), not men.

## Q5 — HPV (p. 152)
Which statement about the nonavalent HPV vaccine (Gardasil 9) is correct?

- A. It covers only subtypes that cause cervical cancer.
- B. It is available only for females.
- C. It is approved for use as early as nine years of age.
- D. It is recommended for catch-up vaccination in people older than 26.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Approved from age 9.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Gardasil 9 covers oncogenic *and* low-risk (wart-causing) types, is approved for males and females, routinely given at 11–12 (can start at 9). Catch-up is routine through 26; 27–45 is shared decision-making, not a blanket recommendation.

## Q6 — HPV (p. 152)
Which statement about preventing HPV-related lesions is correct?

- A. A three-dose series is recommended for people 15 years and older.
- B. Condoms/dental dams are the primary recommended modality to prevent HPV transmission.
- C. Vaccination is more effective if given after sexual activity begins.
- D. Vaccination is indicated regardless of age or previous HPV exposure.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Three doses for those starting at age ≥15.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Two doses suffice if started before 15; three doses if started at 15+ or immunocompromised. Vaccination works best *before* sexual debut; condoms reduce but don't reliably prevent HPV.

## Q7 — Dysmenorrhea (p. 164)
A 27-year-old, sexually active, with 2 years of progressively worsening menstrual pain and dyspareunia; negative pregnancy test, normal pelvic exam. Most appropriate next step?

- A. One-month trial of acetaminophen three times daily.
- B. Pelvic ultrasonography to look for anatomic abnormalities.
- C. Reassure that menstrual pain is normal.
- D. High-frequency transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Pelvic ultrasonography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Progressive pain plus dyspareunia are red flags for *secondary* dysmenorrhea (e.g., endometriosis), warranting imaging. NSAIDs (not acetaminophen) ± hormones are first-line for *primary* dysmenorrhea.

## Q8 — Dysmenorrhea (p. 164)
Which statement about primary dysmenorrhea is correct?

- A. It is rarely severe enough to cause missed school or work.
- B. It is generally due to an underlying anatomic abnormality.
- C. Smoking is a risk factor.
- D. Exercise and physical activity have no role in treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Smoking is a risk factor.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Primary dysmenorrhea (prostaglandin-mediated, no anatomic cause) is a common cause of missed school/work; exercise helps reduce symptoms.

## Q9 — Dysmenorrhea (p. 164)
Which statement about treating dysmenorrhea is correct?

- A. All NSAIDs are considered equally effective.
- B. Hormonal therapy cannot be combined with NSAIDs.
- C. Contraceptive methods other than oral contraceptives are not effective.
- D. Spinal manipulation is an effective therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — NSAIDs are considered equally effective as a class.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> No single NSAID is clearly superior. NSAIDs and hormones can be combined; non-oral contraceptives (IUD, implant, ring) also help; spinal manipulation lacks evidence.

## Q10 — Breast Cancer Treatment (p. 171)
Which statement about ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) is correct?

- A. Radiation therapy is offered to patients having a mastectomy.
- B. Premenopausal women having a mastectomy should receive an aromatase inhibitor for five years.
- C. Sentinel lymph node biopsy is not necessary if a mastectomy is performed.
- D. Without treatment, it will progress to invasive breast cancer in up to 40% of patients.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Untreated DCIS progresses to invasive cancer in up to ~40%.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Radiation follows breast-conserving surgery, not mastectomy; aromatase inhibitors aren't standard for premenopausal DCIS; SLNB *is* typically done with mastectomy for DCIS (can't be done later).

## Q11 — Breast Cancer Treatment (p. 171)
Which treatment is most appropriate for triple-negative breast cancer?

- A. Aromatase inhibitors.
- B. Chemotherapy.
- C. Tamoxifen.
- D. Trastuzumab (Herceptin).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Chemotherapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Triple-negative tumors lack ER/PR and HER2 targets, so endocrine therapy (AI, tamoxifen) and trastuzumab are ineffective — systemic chemotherapy is the mainstay.

## Q12 — Breast Cancer Treatment (p. 171)
Which statement about osteoporosis medications in breast cancer treatment is correct?

- A. Bisphosphonates or denosumab (Prolia) should be offered to patients with bone metastases.
- B. Denosumab should treat nonmetastatic breast cancer to reduce development of bone metastases.
- C. Bisphosphonates and denosumab have no impact on hypercalcemia.
- D. There is no role for osteoporosis medications in breast cancer.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Offer bisphosphonates/denosumab to patients with bone metastases.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Bone-targeted agents reduce skeletal events in metastatic disease and treat malignant hypercalcemia. They are not used to *prevent* metastases in nonmetastatic disease.

## Q13 — Sinus Node Dysfunction (p. 179)
Which statement about the diagnosis of sinus node dysfunction is correct?

- A. Presyncope, syncope, and lightheadedness are the most common symptoms.
- B. The diagnosis is confirmed when symptoms occur in the absence of documented arrhythmias.
- C. Inability to reach a target exertional heart rate rules out sinus node dysfunction.
- D. The presence of bradycardia-tachycardia syndrome indicates an alternate diagnosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Presyncope/syncope/lightheadedness are most common.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Diagnosis *requires* correlating symptoms with documented arrhythmia; chronotropic incompetence (failure to reach target HR) *supports* SND; bradycardia-tachycardia syndrome is a form of SND.

## Q14 — Sinus Node Dysfunction (p. 179)
A patient with occasional presyncope has bradycardia; labs normal except elevated TSH. Appropriate next step?

- A. Treat the hypothyroidism, with no further intervention if symptoms resolve.
- B. Perform a transthoracic echocardiogram.
- C. Assess for chronotropic incompetence.
- D. Initiate ambulatory cardiac monitoring.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Treat hypothyroidism; no further workup if symptoms resolve.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Reversible causes (hypothyroidism, drugs, electrolytes) must be corrected before diagnosing intrinsic SND — bradycardia often resolves with thyroid replacement.

## Q15 — Sinus Node Dysfunction (p. 179)
For a patient with confirmed stable sinus node dysfunction and left bundle branch block, the first-line initial treatment is:

- A. Single-chamber device placement.
- B. Dual-chamber device placement.
- C. A phosphodiesterase inhibitor.
- D. Oral anticoagulation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Dual-chamber pacemaker.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Symptomatic SND requires permanent pacing; with concurrent conduction disease (LBBB), a dual-chamber device maintains AV synchrony and provides ventricular backup.

## Q16 — Pertussis: Common Questions and Answers (p. 186)
Preferred diagnostic test for pertussis?

- A. Culture.
- B. Direct fluorescent antibody assay.
- C. Polymerase chain reaction.
- D. Serology.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — PCR.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> PCR of a nasopharyngeal sample is sensitive and rapid (best in the first ~3–4 weeks). Culture is specific but slow/insensitive; serology helps only later in illness.

## Q17 — Pertussis (p. 186)
Which statement about antibiotics for treating pertussis is correct?

- A. They improve symptoms if given early.
- B. They are given to prevent transmission to others.
- C. Fluoroquinolones are first-line.
- D. Antibiotics are not recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Given primarily to reduce transmission.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Macrolides (first-line) shorten infectivity but do little for symptoms once the paroxysmal stage begins. Treat early and give post-exposure prophylaxis to close contacts.

## Q18 — Pertussis (p. 186)
Which statement about pertussis vaccination is correct?

- A. Nonmaternal caretakers of infants should be immunized to protect the infant.
- B. Vaccination during pregnancy increases the risk of birth defects.
- C. The CDC makes no recommendation for pertussis vaccination in adolescents.
- D. Vaccination during pregnancy is recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Tdap is recommended during every pregnancy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Maternal Tdap (ideally 27–36 weeks) passes protective antibodies to the newborn — the most effective infant protection. It does not raise birth-defect risk. (Cocooning of caretakers is also encouraged, but maternal vaccination is the keyed primary strategy; Tdap is recommended for adolescents.)

## Q19 — Cochrane: Levonorgestrel IUS for Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (p. 138)
Which statement about the levonorgestrel-releasing IUS (Mirena) for reducing menstrual blood loss is correct?

- A. It is more effective than other medical therapies.
- B. It is clearly more effective than endometrial ablation.
- C. It is clearly more effective than hysterectomy.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — More effective than other medical therapies.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The LNG-IUS outperforms oral medical options for heavy menstrual bleeding, but is *not* clearly superior to surgical options (ablation, hysterectomy) — so "all of the above" is wrong.

## Q20 — Cochrane: Pioglitazone to Prevent/Delay Type 2 Diabetes (p. 139)
Which statement about medications to prevent or delay type 2 diabetes in prediabetes is correct?

- A. Pioglitazone (Actos) does not reduce incidence of diabetes compared with metformin.
- B. Pioglitazone is more effective than placebo in preventing diabetes.
- C. The ADA recommends consideration of thiazolidinediones in patients with prediabetes.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Pioglitazone beats placebo for diabetes prevention, shows no clear advantage over metformin, and the ADA lists TZDs among agents that may be considered — though adverse effects (weight gain, edema, fractures) limit routine use.

## Q21 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Hypertension in Adults (p. 193)
A 23-year-old wellness visit, history of PCOS, BP 110/70 a year ago, BMI 28.2. Per the USPSTF, the appropriate step regarding hypertension screening is:

- A. Screen today because of the PCOS history.
- B. Defer screening 2–4 years because BP was normal one year ago.
- C. Screen today because the BMI is in the overweight range.
- D. Defer screening until age 40.
- E. Ask the patient to begin home BP monitoring.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Screen today because BMI is in the overweight range.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> USPSTF: screen all adults ≥18. Adults 18–39 with normal BP and *no* risk factors can be rescreened every 3–5 years, but overweight/obesity is a risk factor warranting *annual* screening. PCOS is not the qualifying USPSTF risk factor here.

## Q22 — AHRQ: Diagnosis and Initial Management of Acute Colonic Diverticulitis (p. 195)
Which statement about uncomplicated acute diverticulitis is correct?

- A. Antibiotics improve quality of life and reduce recurrence.
- B. Misdiagnosis on CT increases the risk of poor outcomes.
- C. CT of the abdomen has high sensitivity and specificity for diagnosis.
- D. Some antibiotic regimens are clearly superior to others.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — CT has high sensitivity and specificity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CT is the imaging standard. In *uncomplicated* diverticulitis, antibiotics do not clearly improve QoL or reduce recurrence (selected patients can be managed without them), and no regimen is clearly superior.

## Q23 — Practice Guidelines: Management of Gout (ACR update) (p. 209)
Per the ACR, the most effective treatment plan for a patient with a second gout flare in one year?

- A. High-dose colchicine with follow-up to discuss urate-lowering therapy.
- B. Low-dose colchicine with follow-up to discuss urate-lowering therapy.
- C. High-dose colchicine with 100 mg/day allopurinol.
- D. Low-dose colchicine with 100 mg/day allopurinol.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Low-dose colchicine plus allopurinol 100 mg/day.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> ≥2 flares/year is a strong indication for urate-lowering therapy: start allopurinol low (100 mg/day, lower in CKD) and titrate, with low-dose colchicine prophylaxis. Low-dose colchicine is as effective as high-dose with fewer side effects.

## Q24 — Practice Guidelines: Iron Deficiency Anemia (AGA) (p. 211)
Per the AGA, regarding endoscopic studies (beyond H. pylori and celiac testing) in an asymptomatic adult with unexplained iron deficiency anemia:

- A. Endoscopy is not routinely recommended.
- B. EGD with routine gastric and small bowel biopsies should be performed.
- C. Video capsule endoscopy should visualize the entire GI tract.
- D. Bidirectional endoscopy (EGD and colonoscopy) is the primary means of evaluation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Bidirectional endoscopy (EGD + colonoscopy).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Unexplained IDA in adults warrants evaluation for an occult GI source (especially malignancy) via both upper and lower endoscopy. Capsule endoscopy is reserved for ongoing bleeding after a negative bidirectional workup.
