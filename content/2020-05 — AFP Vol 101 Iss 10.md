## Q1 — Atopic Dermatitis: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 590)

Which one of the following statements about atopic dermatitis is correct?

- A. Antihistamines are effective at reducing the pruritus associated with atopic dermatitis.
- B. The typical presentation of atopic dermatitis varies by age.
- C. Emollients with low lipid content are recommended for maintenance therapy.
- D. Bathing should be limited to two or three times per week to prevent drying out the skin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — The typical presentation varies by age.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Infants → face/extensor surfaces; children/adults → flexural creases. Antihistamines don't relieve AD itch (sedation only, A wrong); **high-lipid** emollients are preferred (C wrong); regular bathing **with prompt emollient** is encouraged, not restricted (D wrong).

## Q2 — Atopic Dermatitis (p. 590)

A 10-year-old is having a flare-up of moderate to severe atopic dermatitis, previously treated only with occasional OTC creams. Which one of the following statements about first-line treatment is correct?

- A. Daily bleach baths are recommended during the flare-up.
- B. A 14-day course of oral steroids alone is indicated.
- C. Topical tacrolimus (Protopic) is a reasonable addition to topical steroids.
- D. A seven-day course of prophylactic antibiotics is indicated.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Topical tacrolimus is a reasonable addition to topical steroids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Topical calcineurin inhibitors are steroid-sparing add-ons (especially face/folds). Bleach baths help only **recurrent infection**, not routine flares (A); systemic steroids are avoided (rebound, B); antibiotics only for clear secondary infection (D).

## Q3 — Atopic Dermatitis (p. 590)

Which one of the following statements about treatment of atopic dermatitis is correct?

- A. Maintenance therapy for atopic dermatitis should be continued during flare-ups.
- B. During a flare-up, the highest potency topical steroids available should be used for four weeks.
- C. All patients with atopic dermatitis should be tested for food allergies.
- D. Emollients should not be used in children younger than two years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Maintenance therapy (emollients) should be continued during flare-ups.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Keep emollients going through flares while adding anti-inflammatory therapy. Use the **lowest effective** steroid potency for the shortest time (B wrong); routine food-allergy testing isn't indicated (C wrong); emollients are safe and core therapy in infants (D wrong).

## Q4 — Ectopic Pregnancy: Diagnosis and Management (p. 599)

Which one of the following is an indication for immediate surgical referral of a patient diagnosed with ectopic pregnancy?

- A. Extremely low (less than 1,500 mIU/mL) beta human chorionic gonadotropin (β-hCG) level.
- B. Vaginal spotting.
- C. Peritoneal signs with hemodynamic instability.
- D. Patient is able to adhere to close follow-up plan.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Peritoneal signs with hemodynamic instability.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Signs of **rupture** (peritonitis, hemodynamic instability) mandate emergent surgery. A low β-hCG and the ability to follow up favor medical/expectant management (A, D); spotting alone is not an emergency (B).

## Q5 — Ectopic Pregnancy (p. 599)

Which one of the following groups of baseline laboratory studies should be ordered before initiating methotrexate therapy?

- A. Comprehensive metabolic panel, β-hCG level, and hepatitis B titers.
- B. Complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, and β-hCG level.
- C. β-hCG level, HIV testing, and comprehensive metabolic panel.
- D. Hepatitis B titers, β-hCG level, and complete blood count.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — CBC, comprehensive metabolic panel, and β-hCG.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Methotrexate requires baseline **marrow (CBC), hepatic/renal (CMP)**, and β-hCG assessment because it is myelosuppressive and hepato-/nephrotoxic. Hepatitis titers/HIV aren't part of this baseline set.

## Q6 — Ectopic Pregnancy (p. 599)

Which one of the following is a contraindication to methotrexate treatment for ectopic pregnancy?

- A. Breastfeeding.
- B. Moderate to severe anemia.
- C. Active peptic ulcer disease.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Methotrexate is contraindicated in breastfeeding, significant cytopenias (anemia/leukopenia/thrombocytopenia), active peptic ulcer disease, and hepatic/renal/pulmonary disease, immunodeficiency, or a ruptured/hemodynamically unstable ectopic.

## Q7 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2019 for Primary Care Physicians (p. 608)

Which one of the following statements about cardiovascular screening and prevention is correct?

- A. Fasting lipid levels have better predictive value for cardiovascular outcomes than nonfasting lipid levels.
- B. Statins are effective for primary prevention in patients 75 years and older without cardiovascular disease.
- C. Low-dose aspirin does not reduce all-cause mortality or increase disability-free survival in otherwise healthy older adults.
- D. Aspirin has been shown to reduce cancer mortality in recent trials.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Low-dose aspirin doesn't reduce mortality or increase disability-free survival in healthy older adults.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The ASPREE trial showed no benefit (and more bleeding) from primary-prevention aspirin in healthy elders. Nonfasting lipids are equivalent (A wrong); statin benefit in primary prevention ≥75 is **uncertain** (B wrong); aspirin did not reduce cancer mortality here (D wrong).

## Q8 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2019 (p. 608)

Which one of the following statements about colorectal cancer screening is correct?

- A. Taking aspirin or an NSAID reduces the positive predictive value of fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) for colorectal cancer screening.
- B. Rates of detection of colorectal cancer and advanced adenomas over 10 years are similar between FIT every two years and colonoscopy.
- C. Modeling studies conclude that a FIT-based screening program will result in twice as many colonoscopies as a colonoscopy-based program.
- D. There is no relationship between family history of colorectal cancer and lifetime risk of developing colorectal cancer.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Over 10 years, biennial FIT and colonoscopy have similar CRC/advanced-adenoma detection.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This supports offering FIT as a valid alternative. Aspirin/NSAIDs don't lower FIT's PPV (A wrong); FIT programs use **fewer**, not more, colonoscopies (C wrong); family history **does** raise CRC risk (D wrong).

## Q9 — Top 20 Research Studies of 2019 (p. 608)

Which one of the following statements about infections is correct?

- A. It is not possible to rule out community-acquired pneumonia based on normal vital signs and normal lung examination findings.
- B. It is unusual for cold symptoms to last for longer than one week in children.
- C. Ear infections are associated with a longer duration of symptoms than lower respiratory tract infections.
- D. A five-day course of treatment for streptococcal pharyngitis (strep throat) relieves symptoms as well as a 10-day course.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A five-day course for strep throat relieves symptoms as well as 10 days.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Normal vitals and lung exam make CAP **unlikely** (A reversed); cold symptoms in children often last **>1 week** (B wrong); lower respiratory infections last longer than ear infections (C reversed). (Note: shorter courses help symptoms but 10 days remains standard for eradication/rheumatic-fever prevention.)

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (p. 621)

Based on the USPSTF recommendations, which one of the following statements about the treatment of abdominal aortic aneurysm is correct?

- A. Treatment with antibiotics or beta blockers reduces aneurysm growth.
- B. Patients with a large aneurysm (aortic diameter of 5.5 cm or larger) should undergo surgical repair.
- C. Open surgery is the only way to repair an aneurysm.
- D. Operative mortality associated with abdominal aortic aneurysm is higher in men than in women.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A large aneurysm (≥5.5 cm) should undergo surgical repair.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The ~5.5-cm threshold (or rapid growth/symptoms) triggers repair. No medication reliably slows growth (A wrong); **endovascular (EVAR)** is an alternative to open repair (C wrong); operative mortality is **higher in women** (D reversed).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Heavy Menstrual Bleeding in Adolescents — ACOG (p. 633)

A 13-year-old presents with menarche and has been bleeding through pads every two hours. She is hemodynamically stable. According to ACOG, which one of the following initial treatments is most appropriate?

- A. Intravenous conjugated equine estrogen (Premarin) 50 mg every three hours until bleeding stops.
- B. Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine device (Mirena).
- C. Foley catheter placed in the uterus for up to 24 hours.
- D. Monophasic combined oral contraceptives with 50 mcg of ethinyl estradiol taken every eight hours until bleeding stops.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — High-dose monophasic combined OCPs (50 mcg EE) every 8 hours until bleeding stops.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A hemodynamically **stable** adolescent with acute heavy bleeding can be managed with a tapering high-dose COC regimen. **IV estrogen** is reserved for unstable/severe bleeding (A); intrauterine devices/Foley tamponade aren't first-line acute therapy in an adolescent (B, C).
