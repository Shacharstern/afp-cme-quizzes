## Q1 — Hip Pain in Adults: Evaluation and Differential Diagnosis (p. 81)

A 22-year-old soccer player presents with gradual onset of anterior hip and groin pain that is aggravated by hip flexion and rotation. The patient does not recall a specific injury. Results of the flexion adduction internal rotation (FADIR) and flexion abduction external rotation (FABER) tests are positive. Which one of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- A. Hip osteoarthritis.
- B. Avascular necrosis.
- C. Femoroacetabular impingement.
- D. Intra-abdominal mass.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Femoroacetabular impingement (FAI).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Young athlete + insidious **anterior groin pain** worse with flexion/rotation + **positive FADIR/FABER** is the classic FAI picture. OA is older and shows joint-space loss (A); AVN has risk factors (steroids/alcohol) and AVN-pattern MRI (B); an intra-abdominal mass wouldn't reproduce on provocative hip tests (D).

## Q2 — Hip Pain in Adults (p. 81)

Which one of the following statements about greater trochanteric pain syndrome is correct?

- A. It is an uncommon cause of lateral hip pain.
- B. It commonly affects women 40 to 60 years of age.
- C. There is usually an inciting injury.
- D. It is always associated with bursitis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — It commonly affects women 40 to 60 years of age.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Greater trochanteric pain syndrome (GTPS) is the **most common** cause of lateral hip pain (A wrong), is usually **insidious/overuse** rather than from a single injury (C wrong), and is driven mainly by **gluteal tendinopathy** — bursitis is variable, not obligatory (D wrong).

## Q3 — Hip Pain in Adults (p. 81)

Which one of the following causes of posterior hip pain results from entrapment of the sciatic nerve and/or pudendal nerve by the piriformis muscle, gemelli-obturator internus, or proximal hamstrings?

- A. Deep gluteal syndrome.
- B. Ischiofemoral impingement.
- C. Sacroiliac joint dysfunction.
- D. Hamstring tendinopathy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Deep gluteal syndrome.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Deep gluteal syndrome is non-discogenic sciatic (± pudendal) **nerve entrapment** in the subgluteal space by piriformis, gemelli–obturator internus, or proximal hamstrings — a broader term that includes "piriformis syndrome."

## Q4 — Labor Dystocia in Nulliparous Patients (p. 90)

A 22-year-old patient is in labor, and ultrasonography has confirmed occiput anterior fetal position. Membranes ruptured spontaneously at 4 cm, and labor progressed well until dilation remained unchanged at 7 cm for two hours. Low-dose oxytocin (Pitocin) augmentation was started and contractions continued for more than two hours, but the cervix is still dilated at 7 cm. The patient does not have epidural anesthesia. Which one of the following is the optimal next step?

- A. Change to high-dose oxytocin.
- B. Confirm adequacy of contractions with an intrauterine pressure catheter.
- C. Diagnose arrest of labor and begin preparation for cesarean delivery.
- D. The patient should lay down on their back or side.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Confirm adequacy of contractions with an intrauterine pressure catheter (IUPC).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Active-phase arrest can only be diagnosed with **adequate contractions** (≥200 Montevideo units) and ≥4 cm dilation. Document adequacy with an IUPC *before* calling arrest and proceeding to cesarean (C premature). Escalating oxytocin blindly (A) skips that step.

## Q5 — Labor Dystocia in Nulliparous Patients (p. 90)

A 23-year-old patient is in active labor and receiving epidural anesthesia. Which one of the following statements about pushing during labor is correct?

- A. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists recommends immediate pushing at the start of the second stage of labor.
- B. Delayed pushing (laboring down) increases the rate of spontaneous vaginal delivery.
- C. Pushing in an upright position reduces the likelihood of needing operative delivery.
- D. Closed glottis pushing is more effective than open glottis pushing and reduces the duration of the second stage of labor.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — ACOG recommends immediate pushing at the start of the second stage.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The large Cahill RCT showed **delayed pushing did not increase** spontaneous vaginal delivery and lengthened the second stage → immediate pushing is favored (B wrong). Upright positioning shows no clear operative-delivery benefit with an epidural (C); evidence does not establish closed-glottis superiority (D).

## Q6 — Labor Dystocia in Nulliparous Patients (p. 90)

A 26-year-old nulliparous patient presents at 40 1/2 weeks' gestation with uterine contractions every five to seven minutes. The cervix is 50% effaced and 2 to 3 cm dilated and the amniotic sac is palpable and intact. Which one of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Admittance to labor and delivery.
- B. Amniotomy.
- C. Expectant management and delayed admission.
- D. If the patient does not progress to active labor in one hour, arrest of labor should be diagnosed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Expectant management and delayed admission.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> At 2–3 cm she is in **latent labor**; admitting in latent phase increases interventions/cesarean. Delayed admission until active labor is recommended. Amniotomy in latent phase is not indicated (B); arrest cannot be diagnosed in the latent phase (D).

## Q7 — Dysphagia: Evaluation and Collaborative Management (p. 97)

An 82-year-old recently hospitalized for a stroke is choking and coughing during meals. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Oral feeding by hand is not recommended.
- B. It is appropriate to discuss goals of care with the patient.
- C. Nasogastric tube feeding helps prevent aspiration pneumonia.
- D. Stroke is not a risk factor for dysphagia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — It is appropriate to discuss goals of care with the patient.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Goals-of-care discussion guides feeding decisions in dysphagia. Careful **hand-feeding** is often appropriate (A wrong); **tube feeding does not prevent aspiration pneumonia** (C wrong); stroke is a leading cause of oropharyngeal dysphagia (D wrong).

## Q8 — Dysphagia (p. 97)

Which one of the following is the most common cause of esophageal dysphagia?

- A. Pill esophagitis.
- B. Esophageal stricture.
- C. Distal esophageal spasm.
- D. Gastroesophageal reflux disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Gastroesophageal reflux disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> GERD is the most common cause of esophageal dysphagia and underlies most peptic strictures. Pill esophagitis, motility disorders, and isolated strictures are less common.

## Q9 — Dysphagia (p. 97)

Which one of the following tests is recommended for the initial evaluation of patients with esophageal dysphagia symptoms?

- A. Esophagogastroduodenoscopy.
- B. High-resolution esophageal manometry.
- C. Barium esophagography.
- D. Fiber-optic endoscopic evaluation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> EGD is first-line for esophageal dysphagia: it visualizes, biopsies, and can dilate in one step. Manometry is reserved for suspected motility disorders after a normal EGD (B); barium is adjunctive (C); FEES evaluates **oropharyngeal**, not esophageal, dysphagia (D).

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: Osteoarthritis Management — ACR/Arthritis Foundation (p. 120)

According to ACR and Arthritis Foundation guidelines, which one of the following therapies is most likely to improve pain and function in osteoarthritis of the knee for up to one year?

- A. Exercise.
- B. Glucosamine.
- C. Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation.
- D. Medial wedge orthotics.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Exercise.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Exercise is **strongly recommended** and is the cornerstone of knee OA management with durable benefit. Glucosamine is recommended **against** (B); TENS and lateral/medial wedge insoles are not recommended (C, D).
