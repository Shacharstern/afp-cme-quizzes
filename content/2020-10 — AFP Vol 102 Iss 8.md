## Q1 — Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain: Nonpharmacologic, Noninvasive Treatments (p. 465)

When recommending exercise or movement interventions to a patient with chronic neck and low back pain, which one of the following is correct?

- A. General exercise is helpful for neck pain but not back pain.
- B. Yoga is recommended as the most effective long-term treatment approach.
- C. Any type of low-impact exercise may improve pain and function in patients with low back pain.
- D. The benefit of exercise is limited to patients without radiculopathy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Any type of low-impact exercise may improve low back pain and function.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> No single exercise modality is clearly superior — **the best exercise is the one the patient will do**. Exercise helps both neck and back pain (A wrong); yoga is one option, not uniquely best (B); benefit isn't limited to non-radicular pain (D).

## Q2 — Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain (p. 465)

Psychological therapies such as cognitive behavior therapy or mindfulness-based stress reduction are consistently most effective for improving pain and function in which one of the following conditions?

- A. Chronic neck pain.
- B. Chronic low back pain.
- C. Lumbar radiculopathy.
- D. Knee osteoarthritis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Chronic low back pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CBT and MBSR have the strongest, most consistent evidence in **chronic low back pain** — pain has a major central/psychosocial component. Evidence is weaker for the other conditions listed.

## Q3 — Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain (p. 465)

Which one of the following modalities is most effective in improving pain from knee osteoarthritis?

- A. General exercise.
- B. Knee braces.
- C. Ultrasound.
- D. Low reactive level laser therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — General exercise.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Exercise (land- or water-based, strengthening) is the most effective and strongly recommended nonpharmacologic treatment for knee OA. Braces, ultrasound, and laser have weak or inconsistent evidence.

## Q4 — Outpatient Management of COVID-19: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 478)

Which one of the following laboratory findings is a predictor of severe coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)?

- A. Decreased lymphocyte count.
- B. Decreased neutrophil count.
- C. Decreased d-dimer level.
- D. Decreased C-reactive protein level.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Decreased lymphocyte count (lymphopenia).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Lymphopenia** predicts severe disease, along with **rising** d-dimer, CRP, ferritin, and LDH — all the *opposite* of options C and D, which describe falling inflammatory/coagulation markers.

## Q5 — Outpatient Management of COVID-19 (p. 478)

Which one of the following statements about the treatment of COVID-19 is correct?

- A. Antiviral therapy should be started in outpatients within 48 hours.
- B. Low-dose dexamethasone should be given to hospitalized patients who require supplemental oxygen.
- C. Remdesivir (Veklury) should be prescribed at the onset of symptoms for all patients.
- D. Hydroxychloroquine (Plaquenil) has been proven effective, especially early in the course of illness.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Low-dose dexamethasone for hospitalized patients requiring supplemental oxygen.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The RECOVERY trial showed mortality benefit from dexamethasone **only in those needing oxygen/ventilation**. Hydroxychloroquine is ineffective (D wrong); remdesivir is inpatient/oxygen-dependent, not for everyone at onset (C wrong).

## Q6 — Outpatient Management of COVID-19 (p. 478)

Which one of the following statements about signs and symptoms of COVID-19 is correct?

- A. Anosmia and altered taste are rare.
- B. Persistence of symptoms beyond two weeks is rare.
- C. COVID-19 symptoms can be easily distinguished from those of influenza.
- D. Symptoms are generally milder in children.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Symptoms are generally milder in children.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Children typically have milder illness. **Anosmia/dysgeusia are common** (A wrong); symptoms can persist for weeks ("long COVID," B wrong); COVID-19 and influenza overlap heavily and can't be reliably separated clinically (C wrong).

## Q7 — Inguinal Hernias: Diagnosis and Management (p. 487)

A 47-year-old man presents with a mildly uncomfortable, golf ball–sized bulge in his right groin for one month that increases with prolonged standing and that he can "push back in." Which one of the following statements about the possible diagnosis is correct?

- A. It is most likely a femoral hernia.
- B. Computed tomography is the first-line imaging modality.
- C. Physical examination will likely be sufficient to make the diagnosis.
- D. It is likely an incarcerated hernia in need of immediate surgical referral.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Physical examination will likely be sufficient for diagnosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A reducible groin bulge is diagnosed clinically; imaging (ultrasound first, not CT) is reserved for uncertain cases. Inguinal hernias predominate in men, not femoral (A); a reducible hernia is not incarcerated (D).

## Q8 — Inguinal Hernias (p. 487)

Which one of the following statements about watchful waiting in the management of inguinal hernias is correct?

- A. It is a good option even for painful hernias if the patient is well counseled.
- B. It is not recommended if reducing the hernia is difficult.
- C. It should be done for one year before considering a surgical repair.
- D. Current guidelines do not recommend watchful waiting for any hernia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Watchful waiting is not recommended if the hernia is difficult to reduce.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Watchful waiting suits **minimally symptomatic, easily reducible** hernias. Difficulty reducing signals incarceration risk → repair. Painful hernias should be repaired (A wrong); there's no fixed 1-year rule (C wrong); guidelines **do** allow watchful waiting in select cases (D wrong).

## Q9 — Inguinal Hernias (p. 487)

Which one of the following statements about surgical repair of inguinal hernias is correct?

- A. Mesh techniques are no longer used.
- B. Open techniques have a shorter recovery time than laparoscopic techniques.
- C. No matter which technique is used, patients should not return to work for at least two weeks after the repair.
- D. Laparoscopic techniques are preferred in women.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Laparoscopic repair is preferred in women.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Women have a higher incidence of **femoral hernias**, which laparoscopic (preperitoneal) repair addresses better. Mesh is standard (A wrong); laparoscopic gives **faster** recovery (B wrong); return to work is individualized, often earlier (C wrong).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Primary Care–Based Interventions to Prevent Illicit Drug Use in Youth (p. 493)

Based on the USPSTF recommendation, which one of the following statements about the effects of illicit drug use is correct?

- A. Persistent illicit drug use has a minimal effect on psychosocial and neurocognitive functions.
- B. Illicit drug use does not have an effect on academic performance and education attainment.
- C. No difference in health outcomes occurs when drug use is initiated at a younger age.
- D. Frequent or heavy illicit drug use is associated with increased risk-taking behaviors, including driving under the influence, violence, and unsafe sexual activity.
- E. There is a low prevalence of illicit drug use among children, adolescents, and young adults.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Heavy illicit drug use is linked to risk-taking (impaired driving, violence, unsafe sex).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Drug use impairs neurocognition and academics, is worse with **earlier** initiation, and is prevalent in youth — making A, B, C, and E all false. (Note: USPSTF gives an *I statement* for screening adolescents.)

## Q11 — Point-of-Care Guides: Acute Diverticulitis — Identifying Patients Unlikely to Have Complications (p. 495)

Which one of the following is **not** an independent predictor of complicated disease in patients with acute diverticulitis?

- A. Presence of abdominal guarding.
- B. Elevated white blood cell count.
- C. Lymphopenia.
- D. Elevated C-reactive protein.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Lymphopenia (not an independent predictor).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Independent predictors of complicated diverticulitis include **abdominal guarding, leukocytosis, and elevated CRP** (e.g., CRP >150). Lymphopenia is not part of this prediction set.

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Influenza Vaccination — Updated 2020–2021 ACIP (p. 505)

A 34-year-old patient with a severe egg allergy wants an influenza vaccination for the first time during the COVID-19 pandemic. According to ACIP, which one of the following should they receive?

- A. Two doses of any licensed standard-dose vaccine given four weeks apart.
- B. Any licensed vaccine other than the live attenuated influenza vaccine.
- C. Any licensed vaccine with supervision in a health facility after administration.
- D. Only the cell culture–based inactivated vaccine or the recombinant vaccine.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Any age-appropriate licensed vaccine, administered in a medical setting with supervision.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For **severe** egg allergy, ACIP allows any licensed/recommended flu vaccine but it should be given in a setting able to manage anaphylaxis. Egg-free options (cell-based/recombinant) are reasonable but **not required** (D too restrictive); adults need one dose (A wrong).
