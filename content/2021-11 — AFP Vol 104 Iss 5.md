## Q1 — Recent-Onset Altered Mental Status (p. 461)
A 68-year-old patient presents with acute mental status changes. The adult child reports the parent suddenly lost the ability to name common objects (mobile phone, pencil, drinking glass). Most likely cause of this agnosia and altered mental status?

- A. Delirium.
- B. Dissociative disorder.
- C. Stroke.
- D. Thiamine deficiency.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Stroke.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Sudden-onset focal cortical deficit (agnosia = inability to recognize/name objects) with acute altered mental status points to an acute cerebrovascular event, not the waxing/waning global inattention of delirium.

## Q2 — Recent-Onset Altered Mental Status (p. 461)
An 83-year-old nursing-home resident with dementia is increasingly confused over 1–2 days. UA: 4 WBC/hpf, 3+ bacteria. Afebrile, no urinary symptoms. Correct approach?

- A. Begin empiric antibiotics while awaiting culture.
- B. Begin a standard evaluation for causes of altered mental status because UTI is an unlikely cause in the absence of symptoms.
- C. Obtain a urine culture and treat only if positive.
- D. Repeat testing on a catheterized specimen.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Standard AMS evaluation; UTI unlikely without symptoms.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> This is asymptomatic bacteriuria. Attributing delirium to it anchors prematurely and delays finding the real cause. Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria.

## Q3 — Recent-Onset Altered Mental Status (p. 461)
A patient admitted with altered mental status is confused and disruptive (yelling, trying to leave the bed). Staff fear self-harm or harm to others. Correct **first-line** treatment?

- A. Have a family member stay in the room to try to calm the patient.
- B. Low-dose antipsychotic medication.
- C. Low-dose benzodiazepine.
- D. Soft physical restraints.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Family member to calm the patient.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Non-pharmacologic measures (reorientation, familiar faces, sensory aids) are first-line for delirium agitation. Antipsychotics are reserved for when non-pharmacologic measures fail and there is danger; benzodiazepines worsen delirium (except alcohol/benzo withdrawal).

## Q4 — Vocal Cord Dysfunction (p. 471)
Which statement about vocal cord dysfunction is correct?

- A. More common in males.
- B. Commonly coexists with asthma.
- C. It is a type of asthma.
- D. Most common in older adults.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Commonly coexists with asthma.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> VCD often overlaps with (and is misdiagnosed as) asthma; it is more common in females and younger patients and is a distinct laryngeal disorder, not a form of asthma.

## Q5 — Vocal Cord Dysfunction (p. 471)
Patients with VCD typically present with which one of the following?

- A. Expiratory stridor.
- B. Increased work of breathing.
- C. Dyspnea.
- D. Oxygen desaturation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Dyspnea.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> VCD produces dyspnea with **inspiratory** (not expiratory) stridor, typically *without* hypoxemia or increased work of breathing — a clue distinguishing it from true airway obstruction.

## Q6 — Vocal Cord Dysfunction (p. 471)
Diagnostic standard for VCD if asthma is unlikely?

- A. Empiric onabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) injection.
- B. Pulmonary function testing.
- C. Upper endoscopy.
- D. Nasolaryngoscopy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Nasolaryngoscopy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Direct visualization of paradoxical vocal-cord adduction during inspiration is the diagnostic standard.

## Q7 — Medical Clearance for Dental Procedures (p. 476)
Before a tooth extraction, which is an indication for antibiotic prophylaxis?

- A. Poorly controlled diabetes.
- B. History of joint replacement.
- C. Prosthetic heart valve.
- D. Ascites in a patient with cirrhosis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Prosthetic heart valve.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Prosthetic valves are a high-risk cardiac condition warranting endocarditis prophylaxis. Joint replacement no longer routinely requires prophylaxis.

## Q8 — Medical Clearance for Dental Procedures (p. 476)
A patient with diabetes on dialysis asks about dental care. Which statement is correct?

- A. Proper oral care improves mortality.
- B. Dental visits should preferably be scheduled on the same day as dialysis.
- C. NSAIDs are preferred for pain management.
- D. Glycemic control is required before dental cleanings.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Proper oral care improves mortality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Periodontal disease is linked to worse outcomes in dialysis patients. Dental care should be on **non-dialysis** days (heparin/bleeding risk); NSAIDs are avoided in renal disease.

## Q9 — Medical Clearance for Dental Procedures (p. 476)
A patient is scheduled for elective gingival surgery. Which precautionary step should be taken?

- A. Postpone in patients with cardiac stenting within the past year.
- B. Patients on bisphosphonates should discontinue before surgery.
- C. Non-aspirin antiplatelet therapy should be stopped one week before.
- D. Postpone in patients undergoing head and neck radiation therapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Postpone if cardiac stenting within the past year.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Recent stents mandate uninterrupted dual antiplatelet therapy; defer elective dental surgery. Do **not** stop antiplatelets or bisphosphonates routinely.

## Q10 — Fetal Growth Restriction (p. 486)
Which patient should have formal ultrasonography to look for fetal growth restriction?

- A. 9 weeks’ gestation, previous pregnancy 6 years ago.
- B. 15 weeks’ gestation with uncertain fundal height.
- C. 17 weeks’ gestation with trisomy 18 found on cell-free DNA.
- D. 26 weeks’ gestation with a fundal height of 24 cm.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Trisomy 18 on cfDNA.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Chromosomal abnormalities are a strong risk factor for FGR and warrant serial growth ultrasonography. A fundal height only ~2 cm below dates is within the normal margin.

## Q11 — Fetal Growth Restriction (p. 486)
Which finding suggests increased risk of pregnancy loss and that early delivery should be considered?

- A. Patient declines amniocentesis for chromosomal microarray.
- B. Estimated fetal weight at the 15th percentile.
- C. Estimated abdominal circumference at the 15th percentile.
- D. Decreased flow by umbilical artery Doppler.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Decreased umbilical artery Doppler flow.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Absent/reversed end-diastolic umbilical artery flow signals placental insufficiency and high loss risk — a trigger for considering delivery. The 15th percentile is not yet abnormal (<10th defines FGR).

## Q12 — Fetal Growth Restriction (p. 486)
Which intervention is recommended for children affected by fetal growth restriction?

- A. Monitor height/weight more frequently than the standard well-child schedule.
- B. Use high-calorie formulas to catch up to standard growth curves as early as possible.
- C. Refer children younger than two years to an endocrinologist if length is below the third percentile.
- D. Use special growth charts published for growth-restricted children.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Refer to endocrinology if length <3rd percentile by age 2.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most FGR infants catch up by age 2; those who do not (length <3rd percentile) warrant endocrine evaluation for growth hormone therapy. Forced rapid catch-up raises later metabolic risk.

## Q13 — End-Stage Renal Disease: Medical Management (p. 493)
Referral to nephrology should be considered when eGFR falls below which level?

- A. 10 mL/min/1.73 m².
- B. 20 mL/min/1.73 m².
- C. 30 mL/min/1.73 m².
- D. 40 mL/min/1.73 m².

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — 30 mL/min/1.73 m² (CKD stage 4).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Refer at eGFR <30 to plan for renal replacement / vascular access.

## Q14 — End-Stage Renal Disease (p. 493)
Preferred treatment for type 2 diabetes in patients with ESRD?

- A. Insulin.
- B. Metformin.
- C. Glyburide.
- D. Exenatide (Byetta).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Insulin.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Metformin is contraindicated (lactic acidosis), glyburide accumulates causing hypoglycemia, and exenatide is renally cleared. Insulin is titratable and preferred in ESRD.

## Q15 — End-Stage Renal Disease (p. 493)
Which statement about protein in end-stage renal disease is correct?

- A. Unintentional weight loss ≥5% over six months is diagnostic of protein-energy wasting.
- B. Patients with ESRD should be counseled to eat a protein-restricted diet.
- C. Protein-energy wasting has a neutral association with mortality.
- D. BMI <23 kg/m² and serum albumin <3.8 g/dL is a sign of protein-energy wasting.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Low BMI (<23) + low albumin (<3.8) signals protein-energy wasting.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Once on dialysis, protein needs *increase*; protein-energy wasting strongly predicts mortality. Weight loss alone is supportive but not solely diagnostic.

## Q16 — Breast Implants: Common Questions (p. 500)
Which presentation is most appropriately treated with antibiotics in patients with breast implants?

- A. Breast redness and pain within the first four weeks after surgery.
- B. Brain fog, joint pain, rashes, and fatigue.
- C. Notable change in the size of one breast.
- D. Fever, drenching night sweats, and weight loss over six months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Early post-op redness and pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Early cellulitis/surgical-site infection warrants antibiotics. Unilateral enlargement → evaluate for seroma/BIA-ALCL; systemic B-symptoms → lymphoma workup; vague systemic complaints ("breast implant illness") are not an antibiotic indication.

## Q17 — Breast Implants (p. 500)
Which recommendation for patients with breast implants is correct?

- A. A woman with augmentation implants should not breastfeed.
- B. Routine MRI should replace mammographic screening.
- C. Textured implants should be removed to prevent BIA-ALCL.
- D. Additional views should be included in routine mammographic cancer screening.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Include implant-displacement (additional) views in screening mammography.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Implants obscure tissue, so extra (Eklund) views are added. Breastfeeding is safe; MRI does not replace mammography; asymptomatic textured implants are not prophylactically removed.

## Q18 — Breast Implants (p. 500)
An asymptomatic patient had silicone implants placed three years ago. Correct management?

- A. Routine screening for implant rupture five to six years postoperatively.
- B. Removal of implant if there is a palpable capsule.
- C. MRI for secondary prevention of BIA-ALCL.
- D. Replacement of implants within the next one to two years regardless of symptoms.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Screen for silent rupture at 5–6 years post-op.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> FDA recommends rupture screening (MRI or ultrasound) starting 5–6 years after silicone implant placement, then every 2–3 years. Implants are not replaced on a fixed schedule if asymptomatic.

## Q19 — Cochrane: Coenzyme Q10 for Heart Failure (p. 458)
Which statement about coenzyme Q10 supplementation is correct?

- A. Starting it during hospitalization for acute heart failure reduces all-cause mortality.
- B. It is not recommended because it increases all-cause mortality.
- C. It reduces hospitalizations for heart failure.
- D. It is not associated with adverse effects.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Reduces heart failure hospitalizations.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Low-quality evidence suggests CoQ10 may reduce HF hospitalizations; mortality benefit is uncertain.

## Q20 — Cochrane: Basal Cell Carcinoma — Surgical vs Nonsurgical (p. 459)
Your patient has a slowly enlarging (<1 cm over 15 years) basal cell carcinoma on the back. Which statement about treatment is correct?

- A. Recurrence after standard surgical excision is similar to recurrence after Mohs micrographic surgery.
- B. Recurrence after imiquimod (Aldara) is lower than after any surgical excision.
- C. Observer-rated cosmetic results are better with surgery than with imiquimod.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Recurrence after standard excision ≈ recurrence after Mohs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For low-risk BCC, standard excision and Mohs have comparable recurrence. Imiquimod has *higher* recurrence than surgery, and cosmetic outcomes with imiquimod are often rated *better*, not worse.

## Q21 — AHRQ: Acute Treatments for Episodic Migraine (p. 509)
Which statement about acute treatments for episodic migraine is correct?

- A. Acetaminophen provides no more pain relief than placebo.
- B. Low and high doses of triptans have similar effectiveness.
- C. Opioids have greater adverse effects than other treatments.
- D. Triptans are preferred in pregnancy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Opioids carry greater adverse effects.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Opioids are not first-line for migraine and have more adverse effects. Acetaminophen does beat placebo; higher triptan doses are more effective (with more side effects); triptans are not first-choice in pregnancy.

## Q22 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Vitamin D Deficiency (p. 515)
Per the USPSTF, which statement about risk factors for low vitamin D is correct?

- A. People with BMI <25 are at higher risk than people who are obese.
- B. Frequent sun exposure is paradoxically associated with higher risk.
- C. Younger people have higher risk than older people.
- D. Black people have a higher risk of low vitamin D than White people.
- E. Known risk factors account for almost all variability in 25(OH)D levels.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Black individuals have higher risk of low measured 25(OH)D.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Risk rises with obesity, older age, and low sun exposure; known factors explain only part of the variability. (USPSTF: insufficient evidence — "I" statement — for screening asymptomatic adults.)

## Q23 — Practice Guidelines: Asthma (NHLBI 2020 update) (p. 531)
A 13-year-old with 8-year history of moderate persistent asthma, 2 exacerbations this year, on daily ICS/formoterol + albuterol PRN. Which additional option does the NHLBI recommend?

- A. Add a long-acting muscarinic antagonist.
- B. Refer for bronchial thermoplasty.
- C. Recommend a HEPA-filter vacuum.
- D. Use the ICS/formoterol inhaler as both daily and as-needed treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — SMART: ICS/formoterol as both maintenance and reliever.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The 2020 NHLBI update endorses Single Maintenance And Reliever Therapy (SMART) with one ICS/formoterol inhaler for moderate–severe persistent asthma in patients ≥4 years.

## Q24 — Practice Guidelines: Dietary Guidelines 2020–2025 (p. 533)
Per the 2020–2025 Dietary Guidelines, which recommendation is appropriate for a pregnant patient eating a standard diet?

- A. Avoid peanut products during pregnancy to reduce infant peanut allergy.
- B. Aim for a standard 25–35 lb weight gain regardless of starting weight.
- C. Eat fish regularly but avoid larger fish such as king mackerel and swordfish.
- D. Increase iron via non-heme supplements instead of heme iron from animal sources.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Eat fish regularly, avoid high-mercury large fish (king mackerel, swordfish).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> 8–12 oz/week of low-mercury seafood is encouraged for omega-3s. Peanut avoidance is *not* recommended; weight-gain targets are individualized to pre-pregnancy BMI; heme iron is better absorbed.
