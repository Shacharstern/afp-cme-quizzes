## Q1 — Barrett Esophagus: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 383)
Which one of the following is a risk factor for Barrett esophagus?

- A. Female sex.
- B. Alcohol use.
- C. Aspirin use.
- D. Family history of Barrett esophagus or esophageal adenocarcinoma.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Family history of Barrett esophagus or esophageal adenocarcinoma.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Risk factors are male sex, white race, age >50, central obesity, smoking, chronic GERD, and family history. Female sex is protective; aspirin/NSAID use is associated with *lower* risk; alcohol is not a consistent risk factor (unlike for squamous cell cancer).

- C. A 55-year-old woman who has had GERD symptoms for four years.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — 55-year-old man with obesity and chronic/frequent GERD.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> ACG screens chronic GERD plus ≥2 risk factors (male, age >50, white, obesity, smoking, family history). The obese man clears that bar; the women/younger patients without clustered risks do not.

## Q3 — Barrett Esophagus (p. 383)
Which one of the following statements about the treatment of Barrett esophagus is correct?

- A. Endoscopic therapy is recommended for all patients with Barrett esophagus.
- B. Endoscopic therapy for Barrett esophagus is not associated with adverse effects.
- C. Endoscopic therapy or surveillance is recommended for patients with low-grade dysplasia.
- D. Endoscopic surveillance alone is recommended for patients with high-grade dysplasia.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Endoscopic therapy or surveillance for low-grade dysplasia.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> No dysplasia → surveillance only. Low-grade → endoscopic eradication *or* surveillance. High-grade → endoscopic eradication therapy (not surveillance alone). Endoscopic therapy carries risks (stricture, bleeding, perforation).

## Q4 — Schizophrenia (p. 388)
A mother brings in her 17-year-old son worried about a family history of psychosis. Which one is most consistent with the diagnosis of schizophrenia?

- A. Delusional behavior lasting for three days.
- B. A six-month history of hallucinations and social withdrawal.
- C. Lack of interest in academic studies despite good grades.
- D. A high score on a validated scale.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Six-month history of hallucinations and social withdrawal.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> DSM-5 requires ≥6 months of continuous signs with ≥1 month of active symptoms. Three days = brief psychotic disorder. No single scale or score is diagnostic.

## Q5 — Schizophrenia (p. 388)
Which one of the following statements about the treatment of schizophrenia is correct?

- A. Early treatment leads to better outcomes.
- B. Treatment can usually be tapered off over time.
- C. Cannabis use can delay expression of schizophrenia.
- D. Coordination with social services has not been found to improve medication adherence.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Early treatment leads to better outcomes.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Shorter duration of untreated psychosis predicts better outcomes. Treatment is usually long-term/lifelong; cannabis *accelerates/precipitates* onset; coordinated specialty care improves adherence.

## Q6 — Schizophrenia (p. 388)

- A. First-generation antipsychotics have fewer extrapyramidal symptoms than second-generation.
- B. Tardive dyskinesia should be treated with olanzapine/samidorphan (Lybalvi).
- C. Metformin can reduce body mass index in patients taking second-generation antipsychotics.
- D. Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) is contraindicated with antipsychotic use.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Metformin can reduce BMI with second-generation antipsychotics.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> First-gen agents cause *more* EPS. TD is treated with VMAT2 inhibitors (valbenazine, deutetrabenazine), not Lybalvi. Diphenhydramine is actually used for acute dystonia.

## Q7 — Endometriosis: Evaluation and Treatment (p. 397)
A 19-year-old has long-standing dysmenorrhea with rectovaginal induration. Appropriate next diagnostic step?

- A. Serum cancer antigen 125.
- B. Laparoscopic investigation.
- C. Transvaginal ultrasonography.
- D. Magnetic resonance imaging.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Transvaginal ultrasonography.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> First-line imaging for suspected endometriosis. Laparoscopy is no longer required to start empiric treatment; CA-125 lacks specificity; MRI is reserved for deep infiltrating disease/surgical planning.

## Q8 — Endometriosis (p. 397)
A 36-year-old with mild endometriosis is concerned about fertility. Which statement is correct?

- A. A trial of ovulation suppression will enhance fertility.
- B. In vitro fertilization should not be considered.
- C. Laparoscopic ablation has been shown to improve fertility.
- D. They should wait for their endometriosis to wane before attempting to conceive.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Laparoscopic ablation has been shown to improve fertility.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Hormonal ovulation suppression does NOT improve fertility (and delays conception). IVF is an option. Endometriosis does not "wane" with waiting.

## Q9 — Endometriosis (p. 397)
Which one of the following is considered first-line treatment of endometriosis?

- A. Dietary modification, exercise, and acupuncture.
- B. Combined hormonal contraceptives.
- C. A trial of aromatase inhibitors.
- D. A trial of gonadotropin-releasing hormone receptor agonists.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Combined hormonal contraceptives.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> CHCs (± NSAIDs) are first-line. GnRH agonists/antagonists and aromatase inhibitors are second-line due to cost and adverse effects.

## Q14 — PMR and GCA (p. 420)

> [!success]- Reveal answer
