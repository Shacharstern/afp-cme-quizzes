## Q1 — Fibromyalgia (p. 137)
Which one of the following findings is consistent with a diagnosis of fibromyalgia?

- A. Cognitive symptoms for one month.
- B. Isolated muscle tenderness in the right shoulder for six months.
- C. Diffuse joint swelling for three months.
- D. Sleep problems for four months.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Sleep problems for four months.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Fibromyalgia requires *widespread* symptoms present ≥3 months — unrefreshing sleep, fatigue, and cognitive ("fibro fog") symptoms are core. Isolated/localized tenderness (B) and joint *swelling* (C, implies inflammatory arthritis) are inconsistent; symptoms <3 months (A) don't meet duration.

## Q2 — Fibromyalgia (p. 137)
Which one of the following medications has been FDA-approved for the treatment of fibromyalgia?

- A. Amitriptyline.
- B. Cyclobenzaprine.
- C. Gabapentin.
- D. Milnacipran (Savella).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Milnacipran (Savella).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The three FDA-approved fibromyalgia drugs: **duloxetine, milnacipran, pregabalin**. Amitriptyline, cyclobenzaprine, and gabapentin are used off-label but not FDA-approved.

## Q3 — Acute Otitis Externa (p. 145)
Which one of the following statements about topical antibiotics for acute otitis externa is correct?

- A. They are less effective than oral antibiotics.
- B. Quinolones should never be used when a perforated tympanic membrane is suspected.
- C. Topical antibiotics with corticosteroids are preferred over antibiotics alone.
- D. No evidence suggests that one topical antibiotic preparation is more effective than another.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — No one topical preparation is proven more effective than another.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Topical antibiotics are *more* (not less) effective than oral; fluoroquinolone drops are the safe choice when a perforation is suspected (non-ototoxic); adding a steroid is optional, not clearly preferred.

## Q4 — Acute Otitis Externa (p. 145)
Which one of the following statements about oral antibiotics for acute otitis externa is correct?

- A. They are indicated only when infection has spread beyond the ear canal or the patient is immunocompromised.
- B. Oral plus topical is superior to either alone.
- C. Oral cephalosporins and amoxicillin/clavulanate cover the most common pathogens.
- D. They deliver higher antimicrobial concentration to the external ear than topical agents.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Oral antibiotics only when infection extends beyond the canal or the patient is immunocompromised.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Uncomplicated AOE is treated topically. Oral agents poorly penetrate the canal and don't cover the usual pathogens (Pseudomonas, S. aureus) well — reserved for spreading cellulitis, diabetes/immunocompromise, or malignant otitis externa.

## Q5 — Skin Conditions in Pregnancy (p. 152)
A patient develops irregular darkening of the central face during pregnancy (melasma). Which statement applies?

- A. It improves with sun exposure.
- B. It is unlikely to recur in future pregnancies.
- C. Combined topical tretinoin, hydroquinone, and corticosteroids are effective in the postpartum period.
- D. Topical Centella asiatica and hyaluronic acid with daily massage hasten resolution.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Triple topical therapy (tretinoin + hydroquinone + corticosteroid) is effective postpartum.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> Melasma *worsens* with sun, commonly *recurs*, and the triple combination (Kligman's formula) is used **postpartum** — tretinoin is avoided during pregnancy. Option D is a plausible-sounding distractor; this item is on the to-verify list.

## Q6 — Skin Conditions in Pregnancy (p. 152)
A third-trimester patient has vaginal condylomata acuminata. Which treatment is unsafe in pregnancy?

- A. Cryotherapy.
- B. Laser therapy.
- C. Imiquimod.
- D. Podophyllotoxin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Podophyllotoxin (podophyllin).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Podophyllin/podophyllotoxin are antimitotic and contraindicated in pregnancy (teratogenic, maternal toxicity). Cryotherapy, laser, and TCA are the pregnancy-safe options for genital warts.

## Q7 — Skin Conditions in Pregnancy (p. 152)
Which condition warrants delivery at 36 to 37 weeks of gestation?

- A. Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy.
- B. Active herpes simplex lesions.
- C. Condylomata acuminata.
- D. Hidradenitis suppurativa.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> ICP carries a rising stillbirth risk near term, so delivery at 36–37 weeks is recommended (earlier with very high bile acids). Active HSV affects mode (cesarean), not timing, of delivery.

## Q8 — Trigger Point Management (p. 159)
Which one of the following statements about trigger point injection is correct?

- A. It is first-line therapy for myofascial trigger points.
- B. No single pharmacologic agent or mixture has been proven superior.
- C. It has been shown to be superior to noninvasive management.
- D. The placebo effect does not appear to contribute to the pain relief.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — No single injectate has proven superior.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Even dry needling and saline can work, so no agent (anesthetic, steroid, mixture) is clearly best. Injection is *not* first-line (conservative care is), is not proven superior to noninvasive care, and a placebo component clearly contributes.

## Q9 — Peptic Ulcer Disease / H. pylori (p. 165)
A 62-year-old has several weeks of new epigastric pain and mild microcytic anemia. Most appropriate plan?

- A. Serologic H. pylori testing and treat if positive.
- B. Upper endoscopy.
- C. Trial of a PPI with further workup if no improvement.
- D. Urea breath testing for H. pylori and treat if positive.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Upper endoscopy.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Alarm features — age ≥60 with **new** dyspepsia, plus iron-deficiency anemia — mandate prompt endoscopy to exclude malignancy. "Test-and-treat" or empiric PPI is only for younger patients without alarm features.

## Q10 — Peptic Ulcer Disease / H. pylori (p. 165)
A 55-year-old has epigastric burning after regular NSAID use. Which statement is correct?

- A. Naproxen is the safest NSAID for GI adverse effects.
- B. Noninvasive H. pylori testing would not help this patient.
- C. An H₂ blocker would not reduce his risk of peptic ulcer disease.
- D. Taking a proton pump inhibitor would reduce his risk of peptic ulcer disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — A PPI reduces his risk of peptic ulcer disease.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> PPIs are the most effective NSAID-ulcer prophylaxis. Naproxen carries high GI risk (celecoxib/ibuprofen lower); H. pylori testing *is* useful (synergistic ulcer risk with NSAIDs); H₂ blockers offer some, but inferior, protection.

## Q11 — Pharmacologic Treatment of Depression (p. 173)
A 34-year-old has taken paroxetine 50 mg daily for one year (first episode). Which statement about discontinuation is correct?

- A. Paroxetine is less likely than other SSRIs to cause discontinuation syndrome.
- B. Insomnia and hyperarousal are not associated with discontinuation syndrome.
- C. Discontinuation syndrome occurs in more than 75% of patients who stop SSRIs.
- D. CBT during medication taper can reduce the risk of relapse.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — CBT during the taper reduces relapse risk.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Paroxetine's short half-life makes it one of the *highest-risk* SSRIs for discontinuation syndrome (flu-like symptoms, insomnia, hyperarousal, "brain zaps"). The syndrome affects a minority (~20%), not >75%. Slow taper + CBT mitigates relapse.

## Q12 — Pharmacologic Treatment of Depression (p. 173)
Which one of the following statements about antidepressant use in older adults is correct?

- A. Antidepressants are not effective in patients older than 65.
- B. Fluoxetine is the preferred antidepressant in this age group.
- C. Older patients should start at higher doses than younger adults.
- D. SSRIs and SNRIs have been associated with hyponatremia in older adults.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — SSRIs/SNRIs are associated with hyponatremia (SIADH) in older adults.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> "Start low, go slow." Antidepressants *are* effective in the elderly; fluoxetine's long half-life and interactions make it a poor first choice; and SSRI/SNRI-induced hyponatremia is an important, often-tested adverse effect.

## Q13 — Cochrane: Topical Corticosteroids for Eczema (p. 133)

## Q14 — Cochrane: gFOBT vs FIT for CRC Screening (p. 134)
Which statement about FITs vs guaiac-based FOBTs for average-risk adults is correct?

- A. FITs are more sensitive than gFOBTs for detecting colorectal cancer.
- B. FITs are highly sensitive for detecting advanced neoplasia.
- C. FITs require the same dietary alterations as gFOBTs.
- D. FITs are more cumbersome than gFOBTs in obtaining a sample.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — FITs are more sensitive than gFOBTs for colorectal cancer.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> FIT detects human globin specifically — more sensitive for CRC, **no dietary/medication restrictions**, and easier sampling. Sensitivity for advanced *adenomas* remains modest (so B is wrong).
