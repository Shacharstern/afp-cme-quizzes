## Q1 — Apophysitis and Osteochondrosis (p. 610)

Which one of the following findings in an eight-year-old who is active in school sports and has leg pain should prompt consideration for more serious disease and appropriate referral?

- A. Swelling over the tibial tuberosity.
- B. Anterior knee pain irritated by jumping.
- C. Midfoot tenderness and swelling.
- D. Night pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Night pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Night pain** (and rest pain, systemic symptoms, weight loss) is a red flag for malignancy/infection → referral and imaging. The other findings fit benign apophysitis/osteochondrosis (Osgood-Schlatter, jumper's knee, Köhler/Sever).

## Q2 — Apophysitis and Osteochondrosis (p. 610)

Which one of the following statements about medial epicondyle apophysitis in baseball pitchers is correct?

- A. Increasing the pitch count may hasten pain resolution.
- B. Recurrence rates are low.
- C. An annual four-month rest from pitching is recommended.
- D. Immobilization and surgery are usually required.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — An annual ~four-month rest from pitching is recommended.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> "Little League elbow" is an overuse injury — **relative rest and pitch-count limits** are key. Increasing pitch counts worsens it (A); recurrence is **common** without load management (B); most cases resolve **without** surgery (D).

## Q3 — Apophysitis and Osteochondrosis (p. 610)

A 10-year-old girl presents with gradually developing posterior heel pain that is often aggravated by running. Which one of the following diagnostic or treatment approaches is correct?

- A. The squeeze test.
- B. Calcaneal radiography.
- C. Magnetic resonance imaging.
- D. Immobilization.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — The (calcaneal) squeeze test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Posterior heel pain in a running child is **Sever disease (calcaneal apophysitis)** — a clinical diagnosis confirmed by the **mediolateral squeeze test**. Imaging isn't needed routinely (B, C), and treatment is activity modification/stretching, not immobilization (D).

## Q4 — Depression and Anxiety Disorders: Benefits of Exercise, Yoga, and Meditation (p. 620)

Which one of the following statements about yoga for the treatment of depression and anxiety is correct?

- A. It is effective as monotherapy for treating mild anxiety.
- B. It is not an effective treatment for people with panic disorder.
- C. One 60-minute session per week can be effective for treating depression.
- D. Exercise-based yoga is more effective than meditation-based yoga in treating perinatal depression.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — One 60-minute yoga session per week can be effective for depression.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Yoga is an **adjunct** (not established monotherapy, A), can help panic/anxiety (B wrong), and the exercise- vs. meditation-based comparison in perinatal depression isn't established (D).

## Q5 — Depression and Anxiety Disorders (p. 620)

Which one of the following statements about meditation for the treatment of depression and anxiety is correct?

- A. It has a positive effect in people with mood and anxiety disorders.
- B. Positive effects are generally sustained no more than two to three weeks.
- C. Evidence suggests that mindfulness works only when integrated into a larger therapeutic framework.
- D. There is convincing evidence that it can be recommended as a first-line treatment for social anxiety disorders.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Meditation has a positive effect in mood and anxiety disorders.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mindfulness meditation helps as an adjunct with **sustained** benefit (B wrong) and works **on its own or integrated** (C overstated); evidence isn't strong enough to make it **first-line** for social anxiety (D).

## Q6 — Depression and Anxiety Disorders (p. 620)

Which one of the following statements about exercise for the treatment of depression and anxiety is correct?

- A. Exercise is more effective than yoga for the treatment of major depressive disorder.
- B. There is good evidence that resistance training is more effective than interval training for the treatment of depression.
- C. Anxiety disorders respond better than depressive disorders to exercise.
- D. Exercise may be recommended even though robust evidence of mental health benefits is lacking.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Exercise may be recommended despite the lack of robust, high-quality evidence.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Given its broad health benefits and low harm, exercise is a reasonable adjunct even though the mental-health trial evidence is modest. Head-to-head superiority claims (A, B) and the anxiety-vs-depression comparison (C) aren't established.

## Q7 — Lice and Scabies: Treatment Update (p. 635)

Which one of the following statements about the diagnosis and management of scabies is correct?

- A. Infants may present with pustules on the palms and soles.
- B. Oral ivermectin (Stromectol) is the first-line treatment.
- C. A one-time treatment is sufficient.
- D. Pruritus should resolve promptly with treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Infants may present with pustules/vesicles on the palms and soles.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **Topical permethrin** is first-line (oral ivermectin is alternative, B); treatment is **repeated in ~1 week** (C wrong); **post-scabietic pruritus persists for weeks** after successful treatment (D wrong).

## Q8 — Lice and Scabies (p. 635)

A mother has been treating her child for head lice using a lice comb. Careful inspection identifies yellow to white egg casings but no live lice on the hair shafts. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. The child should be treated presumptively with permethrin 1% (Nix).
- B. The child should be excluded from school or day care.
- C. Diagnosis requires visualization of a live louse.
- D. Prompt chemical treatment of the home environment is recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Active infestation requires visualizing a live louse.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Old **nits alone don't confirm active infestation** — so no presumptive treatment (A). "No-nit" school exclusion is **not recommended** (B); environmental chemical treatment is unnecessary (lice die off the host quickly, D).

## Q9 — Lice and Scabies (p. 635)

Which one of the following is the first-line pharmacologic treatment for head lice?

- A. Permethrin 1%.
- B. Ivermectin 0.5% (Sklice).
- C. Lindane.
- D. Benzyl alcohol 5% (Ulesfia).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Permethrin 1% (Nix).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Permethrin 1% (or pyrethrins) is first-line for head lice. Topical ivermectin and benzyl alcohol are alternatives; **lindane is neurotoxic and avoided** (C).

## Q10 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Screening for Intimate Partner Violence, Elder Abuse, and Abuse of Vulnerable Adults (p. 649)

A 32-year-old woman presents for a health maintenance visit with no medical history and no substance use. According to the USPSTF recommendation on screening for intimate partner violence (IPV), how should you proceed?

- A. Ask about IPV only if she reports symptoms suggestive of physical, sexual, or psychological aggression.
- B. Screen for IPV because she is a woman of reproductive age.
- C. Do not screen for IPV because she is not in a formal relationship.
- D. Do not screen for IPV because the harms outweigh the benefits.
- E. Do not screen for IPV because she has no risk factors.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Screen because she is a woman of reproductive age.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF (grade **B**) recommends screening **all women of reproductive age** for IPV, regardless of symptoms, relationship status, or apparent risk (A, C, D, E wrong).

## Q11 — Practice Guidelines: Chronic Cough After Acute Viral Bronchiolitis — ACCP (p. 654)

Which one of the following management options is recommended by the American College of Chest Physicians (ACCP) for chronic cough in children after viral bronchiolitis?

- A. Hypertonic saline is a preferred treatment.
- B. Children with chronic cough should have a trial of inhaled corticosteroids.
- C. A one-week course of targeted antibiotic therapy should be used regardless of etiology.
- D. Evaluation for cough pointers that suggest an underlying cause can guide treatment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Evaluate for "cough pointers" that suggest an underlying cause to guide treatment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Persistent post-bronchiolitis cough should prompt a search for **specific underlying causes** rather than empiric hypertonic saline (A), routine inhaled steroids (B), or blanket antibiotics (C).
