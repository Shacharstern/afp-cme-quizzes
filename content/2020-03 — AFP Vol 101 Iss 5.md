## Q1 — Point-of-Care Ultrasonography (p. 275)

Which one of the following statements about point-of-care ultrasonography (POCUS) for skin and soft tissue infections is correct?

- A. Computed tomography is considered more sensitive than POCUS for diagnosing abscess.
- B. It increases the failure-to-diagnose rate for cellulitis and abscess.
- C. It decreases inappropriate incision and drainage of cellulitis without abscess.
- D. Ultrasonography cannot effectively rule out the presence of foreign bodies.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — POCUS decreases inappropriate I&D of cellulitis without abscess.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> POCUS distinguishes abscess (drainable) from cellulitis (not), improving management. It is **sensitive** for abscess (A wrong), **improves** diagnostic accuracy (B wrong), and is excellent for **detecting foreign bodies**, especially radiolucent ones (D wrong).

## Q2 — Point-of-Care Ultrasonography (p. 275)

For which one of the following findings does POCUS have the highest sensitivity in detecting cholecystitis?

- A. Gallstones.
- B. Gallbladder wall thickening.
- C. Sludge in gallbladder.
- D. Common bile duct dilation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Gallstones.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> POCUS is most sensitive for **cholelithiasis** (the prerequisite finding in most cholecystitis). Wall thickening, sludge, and CBD dilation are supportive but less sensitive/specific signs.

## Q3 — Sexual Health History: Techniques and Tips (p. 286)

According to the USPSTF, intensive behavioral counseling to reduce the risk of sexually transmitted infections is recommended for which one of the following groups?

- A. All adults.
- B. All adolescents.
- C. All women.
- D. All sexually active adolescents.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All sexually active adolescents.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends intensive behavioral STI counseling for **all sexually active adolescents** and adults at increased risk — not all adolescents/adults universally (A, B, C overbroad).

## Q4 — Sexual Health History (p. 286)

According to the USPSTF, screening for chlamydia and gonorrhea is recommended for which one of the following groups?

- A. All sexually active women.
- B. All sexually active men.
- C. Sexually active women 24 years and younger.
- D. Sexually active men 24 years and younger.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Sexually active women 24 years and younger.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The USPSTF recommends chlamydia/gonorrhea screening for **all sexually active women ≤24** (and older women at increased risk). Evidence is **insufficient** for routine screening in men (B, D).

## Q5 — Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding in Adults (p. 294)

A patient who has melena is admitted to the emergency department. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Nasogastric lavage should be performed to determine the source of bleeding.
- B. A blood urea nitrogen/creatinine ratio greater than 36 favors a diagnosis of upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding.
- C. If the patient's Glasgow-Blatchford Bleeding Score is greater than 1, the patient can be treated as an outpatient.
- D. Use of erythromycin has been shown to increase length of hospitalization.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A BUN/creatinine ratio >36 favors an upper GI source.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Digested blood raises BUN out of proportion to creatinine. NG lavage is no longer recommended to localize bleeding (A); a Glasgow-Blatchford score of **0** (not >1) supports outpatient care (C); pre-endoscopy **erythromycin shortens** stay by clearing the stomach (D reversed).

## Q6 — Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding (p. 294)

A patient with upper GI bleeding from peptic ulcer disease is hemodynamically unstable. Aside from anticoagulation for DVT, he has no significant history. His INR is 1.5 and hemoglobin is 8 g/dL. Which one of the following recommendations is correct?

- A. Endoscopy should be delayed until his coagulopathy is corrected.
- B. The patient should immediately be given a blood transfusion.
- C. Endoscopy should be performed urgently after hydration and stabilization.
- D. Epinephrine monotherapy is the endoscopic treatment of choice.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Perform endoscopy urgently after hydration and stabilization.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Resuscitate first, then early endoscopy (within ~24 h). A mildly elevated INR (≤2.5) shouldn't delay it (A); a **restrictive** transfusion threshold (~7 g/dL) applies, so 8 doesn't mandate transfusion (B); epinephrine must be **combined** with a second hemostatic method, never used alone (D).

## Q7 — Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding (p. 294)

Which one of the following statements about the role of proton pump inhibitors in patients with upper GI bleeding is correct?

- A. Oral proton pump inhibitors are as effective as intravenous treatment.
- B. Proton pump inhibitors should be continued indefinitely after discharge.
- C. Proton pump inhibitors are of limited benefit and can be avoided in low-risk lesions.
- D. Proton pump inhibitors given within the first 72 hours of upper GI bleeding increase the risk of rebleeding.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Oral PPIs are as effective as intravenous PPIs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Oral and IV PPIs give comparable outcomes for most upper GI bleeding. PPIs **reduce** rebleeding (D reversed), are beneficial (not "avoided," C), and are continued for a defined course based on the lesion, not indefinitely (B).

## Q8 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Vitamin and Mineral Supplementation for Maintaining Cognitive Functioning in Cognitively Healthy Adults (p. 272)

A cognitively healthy 65-year-old asks if she should add anything to her diet to prevent dementia. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Vitamin C supplementation will decrease her risk of dementia.
- B. Vitamin D supplementation will decrease her risk of dementia.
- C. Vitamin E supplementation will decrease her risk of dementia.
- D. No vitamin or mineral supplementation has been shown to decrease the risk of dementia in older adults who are cognitively healthy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — No vitamin or mineral supplement has been shown to reduce dementia risk in healthy older adults.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found no cognitive benefit from vitamin/mineral supplementation in cognitively healthy adults (A, B, C wrong) — counsel a balanced diet and vascular risk-factor control instead.

## Q9 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Music Therapy for Depression (p. 273)

A 45-year-old asks about music therapy for depression and anxiety. Which one of the following statements is correct?

- A. Active music therapy added to standard care is more effective than standard care alone for depressive symptoms.
- B. Receptive music therapy added to standard care is more effective than standard care alone for depressive symptoms.
- C. Active music therapy added to standard care is more effective than standard care alone for symptoms of anxiety.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Adding music therapy (active or receptive) to standard care improves depressive symptoms and anxiety more than standard care alone — a reasonable low-risk adjunct.

## Q10 — Practice Guidelines: Tuberculosis Screening, Testing, and Treatment in U.S. Health Care Professionals — CDC (p. 312)

According to the CDC, when should a health care professional receive tuberculosis testing after a negative initial baseline test?

- A. Annually.
- B. After returning from a two-month vacation through various areas of New Zealand.
- C. After exposure without proper protection to a patient with tuberculosis.
- D. After returning from a 14-day medical mission to Haiti.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — After an unprotected exposure to a patient with tuberculosis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The 2019 CDC update **dropped routine annual testing** for health care personnel without ongoing exposure (A wrong). Post-exposure testing is indicated. New Zealand is low-incidence (B); a brief Haiti trip alone doesn't mandate testing per these criteria (D).
