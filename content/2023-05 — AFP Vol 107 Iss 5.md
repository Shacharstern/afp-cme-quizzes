## Q1 — Allergic Rhinitis: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 466)
First-line treatment for patients with allergic rhinitis?

- A. Oral antihistamine.
- B. Intranasal corticosteroid spray.
- C. Montelukast.
- D. Oral or nasal decongestants.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Intranasal corticosteroid spray.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Intranasal corticosteroids are the most effective single agent and are first-line for allergic rhinitis. Montelukast is inferior and carries a neuropsychiatric boxed warning; decongestants are for short-term use only.

## Q2 — Allergic Rhinitis (p. 466)
Which statement about intranasal corticosteroids is correct?

- A. They are similarly effective to oral antihistamines.
- B. They have no clinically important drug interactions.
- C. They may be used in children six months of age.
- D. They are similarly effective whether used on a regular or as-needed basis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Similarly effective whether used on a regular or as-needed basis.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> As-needed use can be comparably effective to scheduled dosing. They are **more** effective than oral antihistamines (A wrong), can have interactions (e.g., with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors like ritonavir — B wrong), and aren't approved down to 6 months of age (C wrong).

## Q3 — Evaluating the Child With a Limp (p. 474)
A 6-year-old boy with a limp points to his left hip; antalgic gait, negative radiograph, no trauma. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Septic arthritis of the hip.
- B. Nonaccidental trauma.
- C. Growth plate injury.
- D. Transient synovitis of the hip.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Transient synovitis of the hip.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Transient (toxic) synovitis is the most common cause of atraumatic hip pain/limp in young children, often post-viral, with a **well-appearing** child and normal radiographs. Septic arthritis would show systemic toxicity and abnormal inflammatory markers.

## Q4 — Evaluating the Child With a Limp (p. 474)
A 4-year-old with nonlocalized limp, no infection concern. Per ACR Appropriateness Criteria, which initial imaging is "usually appropriate"?

- A. Radiography of the femur of both legs.
- B. Radiography of the tibia and fibula of both legs.
- C. Ultrasonography of both legs.
- D. ACR considers no imaging "usually appropriate" here.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Radiography of the tibia and fibula of both legs.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For a young child with a nonlocalized limp, tibia/fibula radiographs are "usually appropriate" — this is the classic location for an occult **toddler's fracture** (spiral tibial fracture).

## Q5 — Disability Evaluations (p. 490)
A woman gets a crush injury to her lower leg **while cliff diving on vacation**. Which program would most likely provide paid disability benefits?

- A. Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA).
- B. Personal disability insurance.
- C. Workers' compensation.
- D. World Health Organization.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Personal disability insurance.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The injury is recreational, not work-related, so **workers' compensation doesn't apply**. FMLA provides job-protected **unpaid** leave, and the WHO isn't a benefits payer. Private disability insurance is the source of paid benefits here.

## Q6 — Disability Evaluations (p. 490)
Minimum qualification to perform an FMLA evaluation (e.g., for a patient caring for a spouse undergoing radiation)?

- A. A medical license.
- B. Independent medical examiner certification.
- C. Board certification in occupational medicine.
- D. Subspecialization in the treatment modality.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A medical license.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Any licensed physician can complete an FMLA certification; no special certification or subspecialty is required.

## Q7 — Acute Shoulder Injuries in Adults (p. 503)
A 17-year-old football player with his **first** anterior shoulder dislocation. Most appropriate treatment?

- A. Reduction with return to game if pain is minimal.
- B. Reduction and six weeks of sling immobilization.
- C. Reduction followed by referral for surgical treatment.
- D. Reduction and return to game if no axillary nerve deficit.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Reduction followed by referral for surgical treatment.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Young athletes (especially contact-sport males under ~20–25) have a **very high recurrence rate** after a first anterior dislocation; early surgical stabilization referral reduces recurrence. Prolonged immobilization doesn't meaningfully lower recurrence, and same-game return is inappropriate.

## Q8 — Acute Shoulder Injuries (p. 503)
A 70-year-old with a proximal humerus fracture, <1 cm displacement and ~10° angulation. Most appropriate treatment?

- A. Open reduction and internal fixation.
- B. Closed reduction and physical therapy for functional recovery.
- C. Casting for 12 weeks.
- D. Joint replacement surgery.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Conservative management (sling) with physical therapy for functional recovery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Most proximal humerus fractures are **minimally displaced** and managed nonoperatively with brief immobilization and **early physical therapy**. Surgery (ORIF/arthroplasty) is reserved for significantly displaced/angulated or complex fractures; prolonged casting causes stiffness.

## Q9 — Dizziness: Evaluation and Management (p. 514)
A 70-year-old with positional dizziness triggered by movement; Dix-Hallpike shows transient upbeat-torsional nystagmus. Which treatment statement is correct?

- A. Evaluate for acute coronary syndrome.
- B. Pharmacologic intervention is indicated.
- C. Vitamin D supplementation accelerates recovery.
- D. A combination of vestibular rehabilitation and repositioning maneuvers is recommended.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Vestibular rehabilitation plus repositioning maneuvers.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Upbeat-torsional nystagmus on Dix-Hallpike = **posterior-canal BPPV**. Treat with canalith repositioning (Epley) ± vestibular rehab. Vestibular suppressants are discouraged (they delay compensation), and this isn't a cardiac presentation.

## Q10 — Dizziness (p. 514)
A 60-year-old man with tinnitus and recurrent spontaneous vertigo; exam shows unidirectional horizontal nystagmus. Correct next step?

- A. Audiogram.
- B. Switch to a high-salt diet.
- C. Repositioning maneuvers.
- D. Labyrinthectomy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Audiogram.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Tinnitus + spontaneous episodic vertigo + unidirectional nystagmus suggests **Ménière disease**; an audiogram documenting low-frequency sensorineural hearing loss supports the diagnosis. Management uses a **low-salt** diet (not high), repositioning is for BPPV, and labyrinthectomy is a last resort.

## Q11 — Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia: Evaluation and Treatment (p. 525)
A newborn discharged yesterday was jaundiced but below the phototherapy threshold at discharge. Which statement is correct?

- A. All infants require a repeat bilirubin at the follow-up appointment.
- B. The difference between the measured bilirubin and the hour-specific phototherapy threshold determines when to reevaluate and repeat measurements.
- C. The size of that difference does not affect the recommended frequency of repeat assays.
- D. Neurotoxicity risk factors do not change the hour-specific phototherapy thresholds.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — The gap between measured bilirubin and the hour-specific threshold sets the follow-up timing.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The **smaller the margin** below the phototherapy threshold, the sooner the recheck. Not all infants need a repeat level (A wrong), the difference very much affects timing (C wrong), and neurotoxicity risk factors **lower** the thresholds (D wrong).

## Q12 — Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia (p. 525)
Which statement about phototherapy is correct?

- A. Fluorescent lights are less effective than newer LED lights.
- B. Phototherapy carries a short-term risk of constipation.
- C. Phototherapy is associated with a small increased risk of seizures.
- D. In most newborns, bilirubin should be rechecked no sooner than 24 hours after starting phototherapy.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — A small increased risk of seizures.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Observational data link phototherapy with a small absolute increase in childhood seizure risk. A short-term GI effect is **diarrhea** (not constipation), and bilirubin is typically rechecked well within 24 hours of starting phototherapy.

## Q13 — Cochrane: Low-Dose CT Lung Cancer Screening (p. 462)
For a patient with a 20-pack-year smoking history, which statement about low-dose CT screening is correct?

- A. Screening reduces lung cancer–related deaths.
- B. Many patients undergo unnecessary invasive testing.
- C. USPSTF recommends annual low-dose CT for adults 50–80 with a 20-pack-year history who smoke or quit within 15 years.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> LDCT reduces lung-cancer mortality (NLST), the trade-off is **false positives → unnecessary invasive workup**, and the USPSTF criteria are exactly as stated (ages 50–80, ≥20 pack-years, current or quit <15 years).

## Q14 — Insomnia Therapy: Listening to Music (p. 463)
Which statement about listening to music for insomnia is correct?

- A. Outcomes are better in studies lasting ≥22 days than in those up to 21 days.
- B. There is no difference in outcomes among patients with different insomnia etiologies.
- C. Music combined with relaxation instructions outperformed music alone.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — No difference in outcomes across different insomnia etiologies.** ✅ Official (key letter) · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The embedded key reads B: benefit appears regardless of the underlying cause of insomnia. ⚠️ The duration (A) and combined-with-relaxation (C) claims are plausible distractors — confirm the exact Cochrane wording against the print PDF.

## Q15 — AHRQ Review: Telehealth for Women's Preventive Services (p. 535)
A 27-year-old with transportation/scheduling barriers wants a women's health visit. Which telehealth statement is correct?

- A. Telehealth alone is inferior to in-person care for interpersonal-violence patients and should only supplement in-person care.
- B. Telehealth alone is inferior for contraception management and should only supplement in-person care.
- C. There is sufficient evidence to state telehealth is not associated with potential harm.
- D. Using telehealth to supplement contraceptive care is similar to in-person care alone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Telehealth-supplemented contraceptive care is comparable to in-person care alone.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The AHRQ review found telehealth for contraception is comparable to in-person care. The "inferior" claims (A, B) overstate the evidence, and C overstates certainty about absence of harm.

## Q16 — Practice Guidelines: Antithrombotics in GI Bleeding (ACG) (p. 552)
A 65-year-old post-MI on **secondary-prevention aspirin** presents with GI bleeding. What does the ACG primarily recommend?

- A. Continue aspirin and transfuse platelets.
- B. Continue aspirin and treat conservatively.
- C. Stop aspirin and transfuse platelets.
- D. Stop aspirin and treat conservatively.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Continue aspirin and treat conservatively.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For patients on aspirin for **secondary** cardiovascular prevention, the ACG advises **not** interrupting it during GI bleeding — discontinuation raises cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. Platelet transfusion isn't routinely indicated in non-thrombocytopenic patients.

## Q17 — Practice Guidelines: NAFLD (AACE) (p. 554)
A 45-year-old with T2DM and obesity (BMI 42), elevated FIB-4 and increased liver stiffness, minimal alcohol, no other liver disease. Per AACE, which intervention most likely improves liver fibrosis over 5 years?

- A. A 52-week intensive lifestyle program.
- B. Bariatric surgery.
- C. A GLP-1 agonist.
- D. The Mediterranean diet.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Bariatric surgery.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> With a BMI of 42 and NASH-related fibrosis, **bariatric/metabolic surgery** produces the most durable long-term improvement in liver fibrosis. GLP-1 agonists and dietary/lifestyle measures help steatosis and metabolic parameters but have weaker long-term fibrosis evidence.
