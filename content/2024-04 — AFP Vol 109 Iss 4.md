## Q1 — Audiometry Interpretation for Hearing Loss in Adults (p. 316)
A 43-year-old with hypertension and tobacco use reports not hearing as well as before. Which one of the following statements about hearing loss in this patient is correct?

- A. The patient should wait until 50 years of age for routine screening.
- B. Tobacco use is a risk factor for hearing loss.
- C. Testing should not be performed in the primary care setting.
- D. The finger rub and whisper tests are as accurate as an audiometer for detecting hearing loss.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Tobacco use is a risk factor for hearing loss.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Smoking (and hypertension/cardiovascular disease) is an established risk factor for sensorineural hearing loss. Symptomatic patients should be evaluated regardless of age, primary care can perform initial testing, and bedside whisper/finger-rub tests are useful screens but less accurate than audiometry.

## Q2 — Audiometry Interpretation (p. 316)
Which one of the following statements about types of hearing loss is correct?

- A. Narrowing of the external auditory canal may cause conductive hearing loss.
- B. Damage to the cochlea may cause conductive hearing loss.
- C. Mixed hearing loss refers to hearing loss of both high and low frequencies.
- D. A gap between bone and air conduction on an audiogram is due to sensorineural hearing loss.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — External-canal narrowing can cause conductive hearing loss.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Conductive loss arises from outer/middle-ear transmission problems (canal narrowing, cerumen, effusion). Cochlear damage causes *sensorineural* loss (B wrong); "mixed" means combined conductive + sensorineural, not high/low frequencies (C wrong); an air–bone gap signifies *conductive* loss (D wrong).

## Q3 — Preventive Care for Men Who Have Sex With Men (p. 324)
A 23-year-old man reports anal receptive intercourse with one partner. Which one of the following statements about PrEP for HIV is correct?

- A. All MSM should be offered PrEP.
- B. Most MSM who qualify for PrEP in the United States receive it.
- C. PrEP should be offered to MSM who have had syphilis in the past 2 years.
- D. PrEP should be offered to MSM who inconsistently use condoms with a partner whose HIV status is unknown.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Offer PrEP to MSM with inconsistent condom use and a partner of unknown HIV status.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> PrEP is indicated by risk, not for *all* MSM (A wrong); uptake remains low, so "most who qualify receive it" is false (B wrong); a bacterial STI within the past ~6 months (not "2 years") is an indication (C wrong). Inconsistent condom use with an unknown-status partner is a classic PrEP indication.

## Q4 — Preventive Care for MSM (p. 324)
Postexposure prophylaxis (PEP) for HIV should be initiated within which one of the following time frames after a sexual encounter with a partner whose HIV status is positive or unknown?

- A. 24 hours.
- B. 72 hours.
- C. 1 week.
- D. 1 month.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Within 72 hours.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> HIV PEP must start as soon as possible and no later than 72 hours after exposure, then continue for 28 days. Beyond 72 hours efficacy falls off sharply.

## Q5 — Management of Type 2 Diabetes With Noninsulin Pharmacotherapy (p. 333)
Which one of the following medications is first-line treatment for a patient with type 2 diabetes mellitus?

- A. Semaglutide (Rybelsus; Wegovy).
- B. Glyburide.
- C. Metformin.
- D. Dapagliflozin (Farxiga).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Metformin.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Metformin remains first-line for most patients (efficacy, weight-neutrality, low cost, safety). GLP-1 agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors are added/preferred when there is ASCVD, heart failure, CKD, or obesity; glyburide (sulfonylurea) is later-line.

## Q6 — Management of Type 2 Diabetes (p. 333)
A 45-year-old man (BMI 35) with type 2 diabetes cannot tolerate metformin, takes glimepiride, and has ASCVD with a stent placed 2 years ago. Which one of the following medications is indicated?

- A. Semaglutide.
- B. Acarbose.
- C. Metformin.
- D. Alogliptin (Nesina).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Semaglutide (GLP-1 receptor agonist).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> With established ASCVD plus obesity, a GLP-1 agonist (or SGLT2 inhibitor) with proven cardiovascular benefit is indicated. Metformin is already not tolerated; acarbose and DPP-4 inhibitors (alogliptin) lack cardiovascular-event reduction.

## Q8 — Streptococcal Pharyngitis (p. 343)
Which one of the following is recommended as a first-line antibiotic for the treatment of GABHS pharyngitis?

- A. Penicillin or amoxicillin.
- B. Azithromycin.
- C. Clarithromycin.
- D. Clindamycin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Penicillin or amoxicillin.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Penicillin/amoxicillin remain first-line (no documented GABHS penicillin resistance, narrow spectrum, low cost). Macrolides (azithromycin, clarithromycin) and clindamycin are reserved for penicillin allergy and carry higher resistance.

## Q9 — Lumbar Spinal Stenosis: Diagnosis and Management (p. 350)
A patient has long-standing cramping, burning, tingling low back pain. Which one of the following best supports the diagnosis of spinal stenosis?

- A. Pain worsens with leaning forward.
- B. Narrow-based gait.
- C. Symptoms worsen with walking or prolonged standing.
- D. Hip pain with limited range of motion.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Symptoms worsen with walking or prolonged standing (neurogenic claudication).** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> Stenosis classically *improves* with forward flexion/sitting (canal widens) and worsens with standing/extension/walking — so A is the opposite. Stenosis causes a *wide*-based gait (B wrong), and limited-ROM hip pain points to hip OA (D wrong).

## Q10 — Lumbar Spinal Stenosis (p. 350)
A 70-year-old with MRI-confirmed spinal stenosis prefers conservative treatment for waxing-and-waning symptoms. Which one of the following nonsurgical treatments should be used initially for short-term pain improvement?

- A. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).
- B. Nasal calcitonin.
- C. Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation.
- D. Acupuncture.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — NSAIDs.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> NSAIDs are the reasonable initial pharmacologic option for short-term symptom relief alongside activity modification and physical therapy. Nasal calcitonin is not effective, and TENS/acupuncture have weak evidence for stenosis.

## Q13 — Practice Guidelines: Beers Criteria Update — American Geriatrics Society (p. 374)
A 66-year-old presents with a new pulmonary embolism without hemodynamic compromise. According to the Beers criteria, which one of the following anticoagulants would be safest to start?

- A. Apixaban (Eliquis).
- B. Aspirin.
- C. Rivaroxaban (Xarelto).
- D. Warfarin.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Apixaban.** 🧠 Generated · Confidence: High
> The updated Beers criteria flag rivaroxaban (higher major/GI bleeding in older adults) and favor apixaban for VTE/AF in this population; warfarin is harder to manage with more bleeding/interactions. Aspirin is not a treatment for acute PE.
