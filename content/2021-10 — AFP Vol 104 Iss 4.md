## Q1 — Onychomycosis: Rapid Evidence Review (p. 359)
Recommended first-line systemic therapy for onychomycosis?

- A. Terbinafine using pulse dosing.
- B. Terbinafine using continuous dosing.
- C. Itraconazole (Sporanox) using pulse dosing.
- D. Itraconazole using continuous dosing.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Continuous terbinafine.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Continuous oral terbinafine (250 mg/day × 6 wk fingernails, 12 wk toenails) has the highest cure rates and is first-line.

## Q2 — Onychomycosis (p. 359)
Which statement about the diagnosis of onychomycosis is correct?

- A. Clinical diagnosis is generally adequate without laboratory testing.
- B. Biopsy with periodic acid–Schiff (PAS) stain is the preferred diagnostic test.
- C. Potassium hydroxide (KOH) preparation is the preferred diagnostic test.
- D. Fungal culture is the preferred diagnostic test.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — KOH preparation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> AFP names KOH microscopy as the preferred initial confirmatory test before starting oral therapy. *Nuance:* PAS-stained nail clippings have the highest sensitivity and culture identifies species — KOH is favored for cost/availability as the first step. Answer confirmed.

## Q3 — Onychomycosis (p. 359)
Which statement about the management of onychomycosis is correct?

- A. The relapse rate after treatment is less than 10%.
- B. Topical therapies should be tried before prescribing oral medications.
- C. There is good evidence for menthol-camphor ointment and tea tree oil but not other home remedies.
- D. Nail removal may be indicated when medical therapy is unsuccessful.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Nail removal when medical therapy fails.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Relapse exceeds 20%; oral therapy is more effective than topical for most; home remedies lack good evidence. Mechanical/surgical removal is an option for refractory disease.

## Q4 — Hepatitis A (p. 368)
Appropriate post-exposure prophylaxis for a person exposed to HAV?

- A. HAV vaccine for a healthy nine-month-old infant.
- B. Immunoglobulin for a healthy six-year-old child.
- C. HAV vaccine for a 20-year-old man with neomycin allergy.
- D. Immunoglobulin and HAV vaccine for an otherwise healthy 45-year-old man with chronic liver disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Both Ig and vaccine for chronic liver disease / age >40.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Healthy persons 12 mo–40 yr get vaccine alone; those >40, immunocompromised, or with chronic liver disease get **both** Ig and vaccine. Infants <12 mo and neomycin-allergic patients receive Ig (not vaccine).

## Q5 — Hepatitis A (p. 368)
Which statement about infection with HAV is correct?

- A. Children younger than six are more likely to be symptomatic than older children and adults.
- B. It is most commonly transmitted through contact with the feces of an infected individual.
- C. Chronic infection is common with HAV.
- D. HAV is only found in the stool after jaundice develops.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Fecal-oral transmission.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Children <6 are usually *asymptomatic*; HAV causes no chronic carrier state; viral shedding precedes jaundice (peak infectivity before symptoms).

## Q6 — Hepatitis A (p. 368)
Which person should receive the HAV vaccine?

- A. A child younger than six months.
- B. A healthy woman traveling to sub-Saharan Africa with a neomycin allergy.
- C. A couple honeymooning in New Zealand.
- D. A healthy 50-year-old man traveling to China for work.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Healthy adult traveling to an endemic country (China).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Travel to high-endemicity regions warrants vaccination. Neomycin-allergic travelers get Ig instead; New Zealand is low-risk; <6-month infants get Ig.

## Q7 — Bioterrorism (p. 376)
Which method is most likely to be used in a major bioterrorism attack?

- A. Aerosolized release.
- B. Distribution of toxin-contaminated food.
- C. Mailed distribution of contaminated powder.
- D. Widespread release of infected animal vectors.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Aerosolized release.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Aerosol dispersal maximizes exposure over a wide area and is the principal concern for high-impact agents (e.g., anthrax).

## Q8 — Bioterrorism (p. 376)
Which statement about smallpox in a bioterrorism event is correct?

- A. A smallpox vaccine can lessen severity, but only if given before symptom onset, ideally within four days of exposure.
- B. The initial symptom is a blistering rash on the face, forearms, and oral mucosa.
- C. There are no FDA-approved drugs for treating smallpox.
- D. Vaccination of pregnant patients exposed to smallpox is absolutely contraindicated.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Post-exposure vaccine within ~4 days lessens severity.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Smallpox begins with a prodrome (fever, myalgia) and a *maculopapular* rash that progresses; tecovirimat is FDA-approved; in true exposure, pregnancy is only a *relative* contraindication to vaccination.

## Q9 — Bioterrorism / Botulism (p. 376)
A patient has acute diplopia, dysarthria, then bilateral arm weakness — you suspect botulism. Which statement is correct?

- A. Botulism antitoxin is only available through CDC consultation.
- B. The antitoxin is univalent and works against only one toxin form.
- C. The antitoxin is given to reverse symptoms.
- D. The antitoxin should not be given until laboratory confirmation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Antitoxin obtained through the CDC.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The heptavalent antitoxin is released via CDC, *halts progression* (does not reverse existing paralysis), and must be given empirically without waiting for confirmation.

## Q10 — Diabetes-Related Foot Infections (p. 386)
Which statement about the microbiology of diabetes-related foot infections is correct?

- A. Gram-negative bacteria are the most common organisms.
- B. MRSA is rarely seen.
- C. More than one-half are polymicrobial.
- D. A superficial wound culture usually identifies the pathogen.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Over half are polymicrobial.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Gram-positive cocci (esp. *S. aureus*, including MRSA) predominate but mixed flora is typical; deep tissue/bone cultures (not superficial swabs) best identify pathogens.

## Q11 — Diabetes-Related Foot Infections (p. 386)
A 42-year-old with type 2 diabetes has a new infected ulcer over the right first metatarsal head. Which statement about the diagnostic evaluation is correct?

- A. Hard or gritty resistance to probing with a sterile metal instrument is highly suggestive of osteomyelitis.
- B. MRI is the initial imaging study of choice for suspected osteomyelitis.
- C. Scoring systems describe infections to others but do not predict outcomes.
- D. Transcutaneous oximetry should not be used in vascular assessment.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Positive probe-to-bone test.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Striking bone on probing strongly suggests osteomyelitis. Plain radiographs (not MRI) are the initial imaging; validated scoring systems do predict outcomes; transcutaneous oximetry aids vascular assessment.

## Q12 — Diabetes-Related Foot Infections (p. 386)
Which statement about treating diabetes-related foot infections is correct?

- A. All should be treated initially with IV antibiotics.
- B. Empiric therapy should include gram-negative coverage in severe infections.
- C. Infections with osteomyelitis treated with amputation require longer antibiotic courses.
- D. Vascular interventions in severe PVD will not change recurrence risk.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Cover gram-negatives empirically in severe infections.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Mild infections can be treated orally/outpatient; complete amputation (removing infected bone) *shortens* antibiotic duration; revascularization improves healing and lowers recurrence.

## Q13 — Osteomyelitis: Diagnosis and Treatment (p. 395)
Which provides a definitive diagnosis of osteomyelitis?

- A. The probe-to-bone test.
- B. Plain film radiography of the affected bone.
- C. MRI (with or without contrast) of the affected bone.
- D. Biopsy with positive culture from the affected bone.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Bone biopsy with positive culture.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Bone biopsy with histopathology/culture is the diagnostic gold standard and guides targeted antibiotics.

## Q14 — Osteomyelitis (p. 395)
Which finding is consistent with a diagnosis of chronic osteomyelitis?

- A. Systemic symptoms.
- B. Rapid onset (development over days).
- C. The presence of fistulous tracts from skin to bone.
- D. Fever.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Sinus/fistulous tracts to bone.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Chronic osteomyelitis is indolent with draining sinus tracts, sequestra, and few systemic signs; fever and rapid onset characterize *acute* disease.

## Q15 — Osteomyelitis (p. 395)
Most commonly identified pathogen in osteomyelitis?

- A. *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.
- B. Methicillin-sensitive *Staphylococcus aureus*.
- C. Methicillin-resistant *S. aureus*.
- D. *Serratia marcescens*.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — MSSA.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> *S. aureus* (most often methicillin-sensitive) is the predominant cause across age groups.

## Q16 — Medical Advice for Commercial Air Travel (p. 403)
Which person should NOT travel by air this week?

- A. A 24-year-old with an uncomplicated singleton pregnancy at 34 weeks.
- B. A 25-year-old with a recent seizure and antiepileptic changes within the past week.
- C. A 62-year-old who had retinal detachment repair one month ago.
- D. A 15-day-old healthy term infant.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Recent seizure with medication changes in the past week.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Recently uncontrolled seizures (within ~1–2 weeks) contraindicate flying. Uncomplicated pregnancy is fine until ~36 weeks; intraocular surgery 1 month prior has resolved; a 15-day-old term infant may fly.

## Q17 — Medical Advice for Commercial Air Travel (p. 403)
A patient with stable COPD on a baseline 2 L/min oxygen asks for clearance for extended air travel. Next best step?

- A. Office pulse oximetry, clear if reassuring.
- B. Refer to pulmonology for a Hypoxia Altitude Simulation Test.
- C. Advise against flying if unable to walk 500 meters.
- D. Advise doubling oxygen to 4 L/min while flying and clear them.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Double the in-flight oxygen flow and clear.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Patients already on home oxygen can simply increase the flow rate (commonly double) during flight. HAST is reserved for borderline patients *not* already on supplemental oxygen.

## Q18 — Medical Advice for Commercial Air Travel (p. 403)
A patient is at moderate risk for DVT during air travel. In addition to walking every two hours, avoiding alcohol, and hydration, what should you recommend?

- A. Wear compression stockings throughout travel.
- B. Give 40 mg LMWH (enoxaparin) on the day of travel and the day after.
- C. Recommend flying with a companion.
- D. No additional recommendations are needed.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Graduated compression stockings.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Compression stockings are advised for moderate-risk travelers. Pharmacologic prophylaxis (LMWH) is reserved for high-risk patients.

## Q19 — Cochrane: Schizophrenia Maintenance Antipsychotics (p. 355)
Your patient worries about weight gain and movement disorders from maintenance antipsychotics. The benefit that outweighs the harms is which one of the following?

- A. Increased possibility of employment.
- B. Decreased risk of relapse and hospitalization.
- C. Decreased risk of death from suicide.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Reduced relapse and hospitalization.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Maintenance antipsychotics clearly reduce relapse/rehospitalization; effects on employment and suicide mortality are not established, so "all of the above" is incorrect.

## Q20 — Cochrane: Interventions to Improve CPAP Use in OSA (p. 356)
Which intervention increases use of CPAP machines?

- A. Motivational enhancement therapy.
- B. Stages-of-change (transtheoretical) assessment.
- C. Automated CPAP feedback triggering a supportive follow-up appointment.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Educational, motivational, and supportive (including telemonitoring-triggered) interventions each improve CPAP adherence.

## Q21 — Putting Prevention into Practice: Diet & Activity Counseling for CVD Prevention (p. 411)
The USPSTF recommends offering behavioral counseling for CVD prevention to individuals with which cardiovascular risk factor?

- A. Abnormal blood glucose level.
- B. Obesity.
- C. Smoking.
- D. Estimated 10-year cardiovascular risk of 7.5% or greater.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Estimated 10-year CVD risk ≥7.5%.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The 2020 USPSTF "B" recommendation targets adults with CVD risk factors, defined to include an estimated 10-year CVD risk ≥7.5%. *Nuance:* obesity, abnormal glucose, and smoking are addressed by separate USPSTF statements (intensive behavioral therapy, prediabetes screening, tobacco cessation). ## Q22 — Practice Guidelines: Neonatal Resuscitation (AHA update) (p. 425)
Per the AHA, the most time-critical intervention when neonatal resuscitation is required?

- A. Intubation.
- B. Nasal and oral suctioning.
- C. Positive pressure ventilation.
- D. Tactile stimulation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Positive pressure ventilation.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Effective ventilation of the lungs is the single most important and time-critical step in neonatal resuscitation; bradycardia is almost always due to inadequate ventilation.

## Q23 — Practice Guidelines: VTE Management (ASH) (p. 429)
Per ASH, the most important factor when deciding whether to continue anticoagulation indefinitely after 3–6 months of treatment for an initial VTE?

- A. No associated transient provoking factors.
- B. High risk on a clinical prognostic risk score.
- C. Residual thrombus on ultrasound after treatment.
- D. Elevated d-dimer after anticoagulant use.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Unprovoked VTE (no transient provoking factor).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The provoked-vs-unprovoked distinction drives the decision: VTE provoked by a transient factor → stop after 3–6 months; unprovoked VTE → consider indefinite anticoagulation. Residual thrombus and d-dimer are adjuncts, not the primary determinant.
