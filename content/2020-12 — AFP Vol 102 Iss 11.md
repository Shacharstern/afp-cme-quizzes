## Q1 — Seasonal Affective Disorder: Common Questions and Answers (p. 668)

Which one of the following is a possible risk factor for seasonal affective disorder?

- A. Age older than 60 years.
- B. Male sex.
- C. Family history.
- D. Living at a more southern latitude.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Family history.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> SAD risk rises with **family history, female sex, younger age, and more northern (higher) latitude**. Older age, male sex, and southern latitude are the opposite of the risk profile (A, B, D wrong).

## Q2 — Seasonal Affective Disorder (p. 668)

Which one of the following has been shown in clinical trials to be an effective treatment for seasonal affective disorder?

- A. Cognitive behavior therapy.
- B. Tricyclic antidepressants.
- C. Vitamin D supplementation.
- D. Sleep hygiene and exercise.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Cognitive behavior therapy.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> CBT (with light therapy and SSRIs/bupropion) has trial evidence in SAD, and CBT shows more durable benefit across seasons. Vitamin D is not effective (C); TCAs aren't first-line (B); sleep hygiene/exercise alone lack established trial efficacy here (D).

## Q3 — Seasonal Affective Disorder (p. 668)

Which one of the following has been shown to be effective for the long-term prevention of seasonal affective disorder?

- A. Infrared light therapy.
- B. Bright white light therapy.
- C. Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy.
- D. Bupropion (Wellbutrin).

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Bupropion (Wellbutrin).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Extended-release **bupropion is FDA-approved for prophylaxis** of SAD, started before the symptomatic season. Light therapy treats active episodes but isn't established for long-term prevention here; infrared light and MBCT lack evidence.

## Q4 — Top POEMs of 2019 Consistent with Choosing Wisely (p. 673)

Which one of the following statements about musculoskeletal conditions is correct?

- A. Adding ibuprofen and codeine to acetaminophen improves short-term relief of pain from an acute musculoskeletal injury.
- B. Adverse events are similar in patients taking ibuprofen plus codeine and acetaminophen compared with those taking acetaminophen alone.
- C. Platelet-rich plasma injections are not beneficial for rotator cuff disease.
- D. Platelet-rich plasma injections are superior to hyaluronic acid injections for knee osteoarthritis.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — PRP injections are not beneficial for rotator cuff disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Consistent with Choosing Wisely, PRP showed no benefit for rotator cuff disease and **no superiority over hyaluronic acid** for knee OA (D wrong). Adding ibuprofen/codeine to acetaminophen didn't improve relief but **increased** adverse events (A, B wrong).

## Q5 — Top POEMs of 2019 (p. 673)

Which one of the following statements about respiratory disease is correct?

- A. Patients with COPD exacerbations whose care is guided by a C-reactive protein test are less likely to receive an antibiotic than patients who receive usual care.
- B. Patients hospitalized with asthma who receive antibiotics on admission have lower risk of treatment failure and 30-day mortality.
- C. Patients hospitalized with asthma who receive antibiotics on admission have shorter hospitalizations and lower costs.
- D. Patients with community-acquired pneumonia who receive longer courses of antibiotics have lower relapse rates than patients treated for five days.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — CRP-guided care reduces antibiotic use in COPD exacerbations.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Point-of-care CRP testing safely **lowers antibiotic prescribing** in COPD exacerbations. Routine antibiotics for asthma admissions add cost/length-of-stay without benefit (B, C wrong); short (5-day) courses are non-inferior in CAP (D wrong).

## Q6 — Top POEMs of 2019 (p. 673)

Which one of the following statements about screening and preventive care is correct?

- A. Older people with stable CAD and atrial fibrillation who had a CABG or PCI a year ago or longer have lower mortality on rivaroxaban (Xarelto) plus aspirin than those taking rivaroxaban monotherapy.
- B. Adding ultrasonography to screening mammography does not increase breast cancer detection compared with mammography alone.
- C. Use of omega-3 fatty acid supplements reduces the risk of cancer and cardiovascular events.
- D. Out-of-office blood pressure monitoring is not a significant contributor to emergency department visits for high blood pressure.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Adding ultrasonography to screening mammography does not increase detection (per this POEM).** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: Medium ⚠️
> The Choosing Wisely message: supplemental US adds harms (false positives) without a meaningful detection gain in this study. The AFIRE-type data show rivaroxaban **monotherapy** is preferable (A wrong); omega-3 supplements don't prevent cancer/CVD — VITAL (C wrong). *(Teaching nuance: supplemental US can raise detection in dense breasts; the POEM's population/endpoint drives answer B.)*

## Q7 — Parkinson Disease (p. 679)

Which one of the following statements about the presentation of Parkinson disease is correct?

- A. A typical pill-rolling tremor begins bilaterally.
- B. Motor symptom response to a trial of levodopa strongly supports a diagnosis of Parkinson disease.
- C. Cogwheel rigidity is less common than lead pipe rigidity.
- D. A physical examination is not helpful in determining a diagnosis of Parkinson disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — A clear motor response to levodopa strongly supports Parkinson disease.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Levodopa responsiveness is a supportive diagnostic feature. PD tremor is classically **asymmetric/unilateral** at onset (A wrong); **cogwheel** rigidity is the characteristic finding (C wrong); the exam is central to diagnosis (D wrong).

## Q8 — Parkinson Disease (p. 679)

A 67-year-old patient is diagnosed with Parkinson disease based on classic motor symptoms that do not currently cause impairment. Which one of the following statements about initial therapy is correct?

- A. Amantadine alone is the best choice for initial therapy.
- B. Combination therapy that includes levodopa is indicated.
- C. No pharmacologic therapy is indicated.
- D. Starting monoamine oxidase-B inhibitors will slow the progression of Parkinson disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — No pharmacologic therapy is indicated yet.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Treatment is driven by **functional impairment/quality of life**, not the diagnosis itself — defer drugs while symptoms are non-disabling. No agent (including MAO-B inhibitors) is proven **neuroprotective/disease-modifying** (D wrong).

## Q9 — Parkinson Disease (p. 679)

Which one of the following statements about nonpharmacologic treatment of Parkinson disease is correct?

- A. Deep brain stimulation has no significant risks.
- B. Deep brain stimulation is not recommended for patients with postural instability.
- C. Exercise has not been shown to be effective for the treatment of Parkinson disease.
- D. A low-protein diet is recommended for all patients with Parkinson disease.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — DBS is not recommended for patients with postural instability.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> DBS helps levodopa-responsive motor symptoms but **does not improve axial/postural instability** and carries surgical risk (A wrong). Exercise **is** beneficial (C wrong); protein redistribution (not universal low-protein) is used selectively (D wrong).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Low-Protein Diets for Adults Without Diabetes Who Have CKD (p. 665)

A 63-year-old patient with stage 4 CKD asks if consuming less protein would be beneficial. Which one of the following statements about protein-restrictive diets is correct?

- A. Very low-protein diets (0.3 to 0.4 g/kg/day) reduce the risk of progressing to end-stage renal disease.
- B. Protein-restrictive diets have been associated with weight loss during the first few months and small amounts of protein energy wasting.
- C. Following a very low-protein diet does not reduce the risk of mortality.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The Cochrane review found very-low-protein diets reduce progression to ESRD (A) but do **not** reduce mortality (C) and can cause early weight loss/mild protein-energy wasting (B) — so adherence and nutrition must be balanced.

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Beta Blockers for Suspected or Diagnosed Acute MI (p. 666)

A 57-year-old patient with poorly controlled hypertension has been diagnosed with acute coronary syndrome. Which one of the following statements about the use of beta blockers in this patient is correct?

- A. Beta blockers are harmful compared with placebo.
- B. Beta blockers have no clear benefit in patients with acute coronary syndrome.
- C. Beta blockers reduce short- and long-term morbidity and mortality.
- D. Guidelines for the management of acute coronary syndrome recommend against the use of beta blockers in the first 24 hours.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — Beta blockers reduce short- and long-term morbidity and mortality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> In ACS/MI, beta blockers improve outcomes (avoid only in acute decompensated HF, bradycardia, or cardiogenic shock). They are beneficial, not harmful (A, B wrong), and guidelines **recommend** early use once stable (D wrong).

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Management of Acute Pain from Non–Low Back Musculoskeletal Injuries — AAFP/ACP (p. 697)

A 22-year-old patient fell while bicycling and the injuries appear limited to contusions. According to AAFP/ACP, which one of the following treatments is most effective at reducing pain over the next week while minimizing adverse effects?

- A. A topical nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID).
- B. Massage therapy.
- C. Acetaminophen and an oral NSAID.
- D. Tramadol.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — A topical NSAID.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> AAFP/ACP give **topical NSAIDs** the strongest recommendation for acute non–low back MSK injury — best balance of efficacy and safety. Opioids/tramadol are discouraged (D); oral NSAID combos add systemic risk (C); massage lacks comparable evidence (B).
