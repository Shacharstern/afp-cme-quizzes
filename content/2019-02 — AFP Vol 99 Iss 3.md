## Q1 — Gastroenteritis in Children (p. 159)

A 13-month-old child presents to urgent care with what appears to be mild viral gastroenteritis. You estimate he has 3% to 4% dehydration. Which one of the following would be the preferred initial rehydration fluid for this child?

- A. Half-strength apple juice.
- B. Intravenous fluid therapy.
- C. Solution of 1 teaspoon salt and 4.5 teaspoons sugar in 1 L of water.
- D. World Health Organization oral rehydration solution.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Half-strength (dilute) apple juice followed by preferred fluids.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> For **minimal/mild** dehydration in a child who will drink, dilute apple juice/preferred fluids improves adherence and reduces treatment failure. WHO ORS is for **moderate** dehydration in resource-limited/higher-acuity settings; IV fluids for failure of oral therapy or severe dehydration (B, D); home salt-sugar recipes risk dosing errors (C).

## Q2 — Gastroenteritis in Children (p. 159)

Which one of the following statements about ondansetron (Zofran) therapy for acute gastroenteritis in children is correct?

- A. It is associated with a decreased risk of diarrhea.
- B. Hypokalemia can increase the risk of QT prolongation in children taking ondansetron.
- C. Promethazine or metoclopramide (Reglan) are preferred over ondansetron when treating children younger than five years.
- D. Although often used, ondansetron has not been shown to decrease further vomiting in children.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Hypokalemia increases the QT-prolongation risk with ondansetron.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Ondansetron **reduces vomiting** and the need for IV fluids (D wrong) and may slightly **increase** diarrhea (A wrong); it is **preferred over** promethazine/metoclopramide in young children (C reversed) but warrants caution with electrolyte disturbances.

## Q3 — Gastroenteritis in Children (p. 159)

Which one of the following statements about the rotavirus vaccine is correct?

- A. Preterm infants should receive the vaccine based on a schedule adjusted for corrected age.
- B. The entire vaccine series should be completed by eight months of age.
- C. The minimal dosing interval is two weeks.
- D. It is associated with a significantly increased risk of intussusception.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Complete the rotavirus series by eight months of age.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Rotavirus vaccine uses **chronologic** (not corrected) age (A wrong), a **4-week** minimum interval (C wrong), and carries only a **very small** intussusception risk far outweighed by benefit (D overstated).

## Q4 — First Trimester Bleeding: Evaluation and Management (p. 166)

Which one of the following statements about laboratory testing in women with first trimester bleeding is correct?

- A. All women with Rh-negative blood types require Rhₒ(D) immune globulin (Rhogam).
- B. Low serum progesterone levels are useful for ruling out ectopic pregnancy.
- C. The amount of expected increase in the β subunit of human chorionic gonadotropin (β-hCG) level over 48 hours depends on the baseline level.
- D. The β-hCG level typically plateaus or decreases by approximately eight weeks' gestation.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **C — The expected 48-hour β-hCG rise depends on the baseline level.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Lower starting levels rise faster (proportionally) than higher ones. Rh immune globulin in early bleeding is **debated/selective**, not universal (A); low progesterone suggests a **nonviable** pregnancy but doesn't rule out ectopic (B); β-hCG **peaks around 8–11 weeks** then declines (D mistimes it).

## Q5 — First Trimester Bleeding (p. 166)

Which one of the following statements about the management of early pregnancy loss is correct?

- A. The β-hCG levels in completed early pregnancy loss must always be followed to zero.
- B. Women should wait six months before attempting to conceive again.
- C. Psychological support has been shown to have a significant positive impact on well-being after early pregnancy loss.
- D. More than 90% of women with incomplete abortion will complete the process within four weeks with no intervention.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Over 90% of incomplete abortions resolve within four weeks with expectant management.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> hCG need not always be tracked to zero in clearly completed loss (A); there's **no requirement to delay** conception (B); formal psychological interventions haven't shown clear benefit on well-being (C).

## Q6 — First Trimester Bleeding (p. 166)

Which one of the following statements about ectopic pregnancy is correct?

- A. Medical and surgical management result in similar long-term fertility rates.
- B. Patients rarely develop vaginal bleeding before the pregnancy ruptures.
- C. Expectant management should be continued only if the β-hCG level continues to decrease by 50% or more every 48 hours.
- D. Ectopic pregnancies with cardiac activity noted on ultrasonography can be treated with methotrexate.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Medical and surgical management yield similar long-term fertility.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Vaginal bleeding **commonly** precedes rupture (B wrong); **fetal cardiac activity is a contraindication** to methotrexate (D wrong); expectant management thresholds are lower than a 50%/48-h decline requirement (C overstated).

## Q7 — Renal Cell Carcinoma: Diagnosis and Management (p. 179)

Which one of the following statements about the evaluation of microscopic hematuria is correct?

- A. A workup for etiology is not warranted unless microscopic hematuria has been noted on at least three urine specimens.
- B. Triple-phase helical computed tomography is the imaging study of choice.
- C. Routine urine cytology is recommended for initial evaluation of asymptomatic microscopic hematuria.
- D. No further evaluation is warranted if infection is found.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **B — Triple-phase (multiphase) helical CT urography is the imaging study of choice.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> A single confirmed episode of unexplained microscopic hematuria warrants workup (A wrong); **urine cytology isn't routine** initially (C); and infection doesn't fully explain persistent hematuria — re-evaluate after treatment (D wrong).

## Q8 — Renal Cell Carcinoma (p. 179)

Which one of the following is a risk factor for renal cell carcinoma?

- A. Occupational exposure to trichloroethylene increases the risk of, and mortality from, renal cell carcinoma.
- B. von Hippel-Lindau disease only increases the risk of non–renal cell carcinoma kidney cancers.
- C. Obesity is a protective factor for renal cell carcinoma.
- D. Hypotension.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Trichloroethylene exposure increases RCC risk and mortality.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> **von Hippel-Lindau** strongly predisposes to clear cell **RCC** (B wrong); **obesity and hypertension** (not hypotension) are risk factors (C, D wrong); smoking is another major risk factor.

## Q9 — Renal Cell Carcinoma (p. 179)

Which one of the following statements describes the presentation of renal cell carcinoma?

- A. Less than 10% of patients present with paraneoplastic syndromes.
- B. Gross hematuria is a common early presenting sign.
- C. Renal cell carcinoma never presents with pain.
- D. More than one-half of renal cell carcinoma diagnoses are incidentally discovered during thoracoabdominal imaging.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — More than half of RCCs are found incidentally on imaging.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The classic triad (flank pain, hematuria, palpable mass) is **late and uncommon**; most are incidental. Paraneoplastic syndromes occur in up to ~20% (A understates), and RCC **can** cause pain (C wrong).

## Q10 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Complementary and Integrative Treatments for Pain Management in Labor (p. 154)

Which one of the following statements about the use of complementary and integrative treatments for pain management during labor is correct?

- A. Yoga worsens patient satisfaction and pain during latent labor.
- B. Instruction on relaxation had no effect on pain during latent labor.
- C. There are more adverse effects in patients treated with relaxation techniques compared with usual care.
- D. Hypnosis reduces the use of pain medications in labor, but it does not affect reported pain.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — Hypnosis reduces pain-medication use but doesn't change reported pain.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Relaxation techniques **help** pain in latent labor (B wrong) without added harm (C wrong), and yoga **improves** satisfaction (A reversed).

## Q11 — Cochrane for Clinicians: Oral Immunotherapy for Egg Allergy (p. 156)

Which one of the following statements about patients who have immunoglobulin E–mediated egg allergy is correct?

- A. Providing steadily increasing doses of egg protein over an extended period of time decreases egg allergy symptoms in children.
- B. There is a high risk of adverse effects among patients who undergo oral immunotherapy.
- C. Once desensitized, patients who avoid egg protein may redevelop egg sensitivity.
- D. All of the above.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **D — All of the above.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> Egg oral immunotherapy **desensitizes** children but carries a **high rate of allergic adverse effects**, and protection **wanes** if regular exposure stops — so it must be weighed carefully.

## Q12 — Practice Guidelines: Urinary Incontinence — Screening Recommendation from the WPSI (p. 194)

Which one of the following statements about the Women's Preventive Services Initiative (WPSI) recommendation to screen for urinary incontinence is correct?

- A. Screening should assess the presence of urinary incontinence and effects on activities and quality of life.
- B. Screening methods have been supported by high-quality evidence.
- C. Women with a positive screening result should be referred for treatment only if symptoms persist longer than six months.
- D. The WPSI found that screening for urinary incontinence is cost-effective.

> [!success]- Reveal answer
> **A — Screen for the presence of incontinence and its impact on activities/quality of life.** ✅ Official-verified · Confidence: High
> The WPSI recommends **annual** screening assessing presence and impact. The evidence base is **limited** (B overstated); there's no 6-month waiting rule before evaluation (C); cost-effectiveness wasn't the stated basis (D).
